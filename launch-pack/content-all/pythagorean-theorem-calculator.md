H1: Pythagorean Theorem Calculator
The Pythagorean theorem relates the three sides of a right triangle: the square of the longest side (the hypotenuse) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides (the legs). Enter two sides and this calculator finds the third, either the hypotenuse from the two legs or a missing leg from the hypotenuse and the other leg.
How to use this calculator
1. Find: "Hypotenuse c (legs a, b)" or "Leg a (hypotenuse c, leg b)".
2. Side a and Side b:
For the hypotenuse: enter the two legs.
For a missing leg: enter the hypotenuse in the first box and the known leg in the second. The hypotenuse must be longer than the leg.
Result: the missing side, to six decimal places.
The formula
a² + b² = c²
c = √(a² + b²)
a = √(c² − b²)
a, b: the legs (the sides forming the right angle)
c: the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle; always the longest side)
The theorem works only for right triangles (one 90° angle). For other triangles, use the law of cosines in the triangle calculator.
Worked examples
Example 1: legs 3 and 4
c = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5
Example 2: missing leg
Hypotenuse 13, leg 5: a = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12
Example 3: a ladder
A 4 m ladder leans against a wall with its foot 1 m out. It reaches √(16 − 1) = 3.872983 m up the wall.
Example 4: TV screen size
A 65-inch TV is measured diagonally. With a 16:9 aspect ratio, the screen is about 56.65 in wide and 31.87 in high (scale the 16-9-√337 triangle up to a 65 in diagonal).
Example 5: checking a square corner
Builders use the 3-4-5 rule: measure 3 units along one wall and 4 along the other; if the diagonal is 5, the corner is square. For a room 12 ft by 16 ft, the diagonal should be 20.00 ft.
Example 6: unit square
Legs 1 and 1 give a hypotenuse of √2 ≈ 1.414214.

Understanding your results
Units: all sides use the same unit; the result does too.
Irrational results like √2 never end; round to suit your measurement precision.
Is it a right triangle? If a² + b² = c² for the measured sides, yes. If a² + b² > c², the triangle is acute; if less, obtuse.
3D: the space diagonal of a box is √(l² + w² + h²); see the distance calculator.

Tips and common mistakes
Using the theorem on non-right triangles.
Putting the hypotenuse in the wrong box when finding a leg.
Adding sides before squaring. 3 + 4 = 7, but the hypotenuse is 5.
Mixing units such as feet and inches.
Frequently asked questions
What is the Pythagorean theorem?
In a right triangle, a² + b² = c².
How do I find the hypotenuse?
Square both legs, add, and take the square root.
How do I find a missing leg?
Subtract the known leg's square from the hypotenuse's square and take the root.
Does it work for all triangles?
No, only right triangles.
What is a Pythagorean triple?
Three whole numbers that satisfy a² + b² = c², such as 3, 4, 5.
Why must the hypotenuse be longest?
It's opposite the largest angle (90°).
