H1: Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator
Gaining a healthy amount of weight in pregnancy supports your baby's growth and lowers the risk of complications for both of you. How much is "healthy" depends mainly on your weight before pregnancy. This calculator uses your pre-pregnancy BMI to show the total weight gain recommended for a single baby by the US Institute of Medicine (IOM, now the National Academy of Medicine), and gives a rough figure for where you might be by your current week.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric or imperial.
2. Pre-pregnancy weight: your weight before you became pregnant (or at your first appointment if you don't know it).
3. Height.
4. Current pregnancy week (1–42).
Results:

Total recommended gain for a full-term pregnancy, in kg and lb
Approximate gain by your current week (a simple mid-range estimate)

Step 3: gain by week. The calculator takes the middle of your range and scales it by week ÷ 40. This is a simplification: in practice most weight is gained after the first trimester. The IOM assumes 0.5 to 2 kg in the first trimester, then the weekly rates above.
Worked examples
Example 1: 65 kg, 165 cm, week 20
BMI = 65 ÷ 1.65² = 23.9 (healthy weight)
Recommended total gain: 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb)
Calculator's by-week estimate: 13.75 × 20 ÷ 40 = 6.9 kg
IOM pattern for comparison: 0.5–2 kg by week 13 plus 0.35–0.50 kg a week for 7 weeks = about 2.9–5.5 kg
Example 2: 90 kg, 165 cm, week 30
BMI = 33.1 (obese)
Recommended total gain: 5–9 kg
By-week estimate: 7 × 30 ÷ 40 = 5.3 kg
Example 3: 50 kg, 165 cm, week 28
BMI = 50 ÷ 1.65² = 18.4 (underweight)
Recommended total gain: 12.5–18 kg (28–40 lb)
By-week estimate: 15.25 × 28 ÷ 40 = 10.7 kg
Example 4: 150 lb, 5 ft 4 in, week 36
150 lb = 68.0 kg; 5 ft 4 in = 162.6 cm
BMI = 25.7 (overweight)
Recommended total gain: 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb)
By-week estimate: 9.25 × 36 ÷ 40 = 8.3 kg (18.4 lb)
A BMI just over 25 moves the recommended range down by several kilograms, so check that your pre-pregnancy weight and height are accurate.

Understanding your results
Ranges, not targets. Gaining a little above or below the range isn't automatically a problem; your midwife or doctor will look at the trend.
Too little gain is linked to a higher risk of a small baby and preterm birth.
Too much gain is linked to gestational diabetes, high blood pressure, a large baby, caesarean birth and retaining weight after birth.
Twins or more: the ranges above are for one baby. The IOM gives provisional ranges for twins (for example 17–25 kg for a healthy BMI), which this calculator doesn't show.
Morning sickness can mean little or no gain, or a small loss, in the first trimester.
Eating in pregnancy
You don't need to "eat for two". Most guidance suggests no extra calories in the first trimester, about 340 extra kcal a day in the second and about 450 in the third (US). The NHS says extra energy is only needed in the last three months, about 200 kcal a day. Focus on nutrient-rich foods, take folic acid (400 micrograms a day until 12 weeks) and vitamin D, and avoid alcohol.
Country notes
US: ACOG endorses the IOM 2009 guidelines.
Canada: Health Canada uses the same IOM ranges and BMI categories.
UK: NICE doesn't set official weight gain targets; midwives usually weigh you at booking and focus on healthy eating and activity. Many UK clinicians still refer to the IOM ranges.
Tips and common mistakes
Using your current weight instead of pre-pregnancy weight.
Dieting to lose weight during pregnancy. Not recommended unless advised by your care team.
Comparing week-by-week with friends. Patterns vary; the trend matters most.
Ignoring rapid gain. Sudden swelling or fast weight gain can be a sign of pre-eclampsia; contact your maternity unit.
Frequently asked questions
How much weight should I gain during pregnancy?
It depends on your pre-pregnancy BMI: 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb) for a healthy BMI, less if you were overweight, more if underweight.
How much should I gain in the first trimester?
About 0.5 to 2 kg for most people.
Is it safe to lose weight while pregnant?
Generally no, unless your doctor recommends it.
How much weight do I lose after birth?
About 5 to 6 kg straight away (baby, placenta, fluid), with more over the following weeks.
Does BMI work in pregnancy?
Use pre-pregnancy BMI only; BMI during pregnancy isn't meaningful.
What if I'm expecting twins?
Gain ranges are higher; ask your care team.
How much extra should I eat?
Typically none in the first trimester and modest amounts later.
