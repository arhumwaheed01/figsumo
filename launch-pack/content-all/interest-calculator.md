H1: Interest Calculator
This calculator works out the interest earned on savings, or owed on a sum, over a period of time, using either simple interest or compound interest with monthly compounding. Enter the principal, the annual rate and the time in years, then choose the interest type. The result shows the interest, the total amount and, for comparison, what the other method would give.
How to use this calculator
1. Principal: the starting amount, whether a deposit or a sum borrowed.
2. Annual rate: the yearly interest rate as a percentage.
3. Time (years): the period. Quarter-years are fine; enter 1.5 for 18 months.
4. Interest type: Simple (interest only on the principal) or Compound (monthly) (interest added to the balance every month and then earning interest itself).
Results: the interest, the total amount (principal plus interest), the principal, a comparison figure from the other method, and a breakdown chart.
The formulas
Simple interest
I = P × r × t
Compound interest, monthly
A = P × (1 + r ÷ 12)^(12 × t)
I = A − P
P: principal
r: annual rate as a decimal
t: time in years
A: total amount at the end
Simple interest grows in a straight line. Compound interest curves upward because each month's interest is calculated on a larger balance.
Worked examples
Example 1: $10,000 at 5% for 3 years
Compound (monthly): A = 10,000 × (1 + 0.05 ÷ 12)^36 = $11,614.72, so interest = $1,614.72
Simple: I = 10,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $1,500.00
Compounding adds $114.72 over three years.
Over 20 years the gap grows: simple interest gives $10,000.00, monthly compounding $17,126.40.
Example 2: $2,500 at 4.25% for 18 months (1.5 years)
Simple: 2,500 × 0.0425 × 1.5 = $159.38
Compound (monthly): $164.26
Understanding your results
Interest is before tax. In most taxable accounts, interest is taxed as income in the year it is credited.
Total amount is what the balance would be at the end (or what you'd owe, for a loan with no payments).
Compare other method shows how much compounding matters for your figures.
The rate is assumed fixed. Variable savings rates change; re-run with new rates as they move.
Simple or compound: which applies?
Simple interest is used for some short-term loans, many car loans in the US (as simple-interest amortizing loans), bonds' coupon payments, and some promissory notes.
Compound interest applies to most savings accounts, CDs and GICs, credit cards (daily compounding) and investments that reinvest income.
Monthly compounding is assumed here. Many savings accounts compound daily and pay monthly, which gives a very similar result. If you have an APY or AER, use the compound interest calculator with annual compounding so the rate isn't compounded twice.
How banks actually credit interest
Most savings accounts calculate interest every day on the closing balance and credit it monthly. The daily rate is the annual rate divided by 365 (some institutions use 360). For $10,000 at 5%, one day's interest is 10,000 × 0.05 ÷ 365 = $1.37. Over a 30-day month that adds up to $41.10, very close to the monthly-compounding figure the calculator uses ($41.67 for the first month). The small difference comes from day counts, not a different method.
If you add or withdraw money during the period, interest changes from that day. The calculator assumes the balance only changes through interest. For regular deposits, use the savings calculator.
Interest on borrowing
For a loan or card with no repayments, the total amount shows what the debt would grow to. Card balances usually compound daily at rates far above savings rates, so the growth is much faster. At 22% compounded monthly, $2,000 grows to $3,846.52 in 3 years if nothing is paid. Use the credit card calculator to see the effect of minimum payments.
Country notes
United States: banks must disclose APY under the Truth in Savings Act (Regulation DD). Interest of $10 or more from a bank is usually reported on Form 1099-INT.
Canada: interest income is fully taxable unless earned inside a TFSA, RRSP or similar registered plan.
United Kingdom: savings rates are quoted as AER. The Personal Savings Allowance lets basic-rate taxpayers earn £1,000 of interest tax-free a year (higher-rate £500, additional-rate £0), and cash ISAs are tax-free.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering months as years. Convert months to years (18 months = 1.5).
Comparing a simple rate with a compound one. Convert both to an effective annual rate first.
Forgetting tax. Interest is often taxable, which lowers your real return.
Ignoring inflation. A 4% interest rate with 3% inflation is about a 1% real return.
Quick check
Check whether the rate you're quoted is nominal or effective.
Frequently asked questions
What is the difference between simple and compound interest?
Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus interest already added, so it grows faster.
How often does interest compound in this calculator?
Monthly, when you choose the compound option.
Can I use this for a loan?
Yes, to see the interest that builds up if nothing is repaid. For loans with regular payments, use the loan calculator.
How do I calculate interest for part of a year?
Enter the time as a fraction of a year: 6 months is 0.5.
Is APY simple or compound?
APY is the effective annual rate including compounding. It lets you compare accounts that compound at different frequencies.
Does the result include tax?
No. Subtract tax at your marginal rate if the interest is taxable.
Why is my bank's figure slightly different?
Banks usually compound daily and use exact day counts, and some round each day's interest. The calculator's monthly method gives a close estimate.
