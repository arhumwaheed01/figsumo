H1: Volume Calculator
Volume is the amount of space a 3D object takes up, measured in cubic units. Use it for container capacity, material quantities, shipping sizes or homework. Choose a shape, enter its dimensions, and the calculator returns the volume.

Example: a water tank
A cylindrical tank 0.6 m across (r = 0.3 m) and 1.2 m tall holds 0.3393 m³ = about 339 litres (1 m³ = 1,000 L).
Example: an aquarium
A tank 60 × 30 × 35 cm holds 63,000 cm³ = 63 litres, or about 16.6 US gallons. In practice you'll fill it to a little below the rim.
Example: a concrete slab
A slab 10 ft × 12 ft × 6 in (0.5 ft) needs 60 ft³ = 2.22 cubic yards (27 ft³ = 1 yd³). The concrete calculator adds waste allowance and bag counts.

Understanding your results
Cubic units: if you entered centimetres, the answer is cm³.
Mixed units: convert everything first (6 inches = 0.5 ft) or the result will be wrong.
Wall thickness: a container's internal volume is smaller than its outside dimensions suggest.
Mass from volume: multiply by density; see the density calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Using diameter as radius makes a cylinder or cone four times too big and a sphere eight times.
Forgetting the ⅓ for cones and pyramids.
Converting cubic units linearly. 1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³.
Mixing US and imperial gallons.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US: liquid volumes are usually in US gallons (231 in³, about 3.785 L) and cubic yards for bulk materials. A US gallon is smaller than an imperial gallon.
Canada: metric is official, so tanks are sold in litres and concrete is ordered in cubic metres. You'll still see cubic yards and US-style gallons on some imported products.
UK: litres and cubic metres are standard. Older references to "gallons" mean the imperial gallon, 4.546 L.
More practice
Cylinder, radius 3 and height 10: π × 3² × 10 = 282.74 cubic units.
Cone with the same base and height: one third of that, 94.25.
Sphere, radius 6: ⁴⁄₃ × π × 6³ = 904.78.
The cone is always exactly one third of the cylinder that encloses it, which is a useful way to check your answer.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find the volume of a box?
Multiply length × width × height.
What is the volume of a cylinder?
πr²h.
How many litres are in a cubic metre?
1,000.
How do I convert cubic feet to cubic yards?
Divide by 27.
Why is a cone a third of a cylinder?
It follows from integrating its shrinking circular cross-sections, and can be shown by filling a cylinder with three cones of water.
Can I calculate a pyramid?
Not yet. Use (1/3) × base area × height.
