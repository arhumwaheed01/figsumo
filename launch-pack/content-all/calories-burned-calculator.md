H1: Calories Burned Calculator
How many calories does an activity burn? This calculator estimates energy used during exercise or everyday activity from its MET value, your body weight and how long you do it. METs (metabolic equivalents) are a standard way of expressing how hard an activity is compared with sitting quietly.
How to use this calculator
1. Units and body weight.
2. Activity MET value: 1 MET is roughly the energy you use at rest. Look up the activity's MET in the table below or the Compendium of Physical Activities.
3. Duration (minutes).
Results: calories burned (estimate) in kcal, and the formula used.
The formula
Calories (kcal) = MET × Weight (kg) × Time (hours)
One MET is defined as about 1 kcal per kilogram of body weight per hour (equivalent to 3.5 ml of oxygen per kg per minute). An activity of 8 METs uses about eight times the energy of resting.
The result is gross energy: it includes the calories you'd have burned anyway at rest. To estimate the extra calories from exercise alone, use (MET − 1) instead.

Worked examples
Example 1: 45-minute run at 8 METs, 70 kg
Calories = 8 × 70 × 0.75 = 420 kcal
Net of resting energy: 7 × 70 × 0.75 = 368 kcal
Example 2: 30-minute brisk walk, 180 lb
180 lb = 81.6 kg
Calories = 3.5 × 81.6 × 0.5 = 143 kcal
Walking 30 minutes, 5 days a week, at 70 kg burns about 612 kcal a week in total.
Example 3: a weekday cycling commute
A 75 kg rider commutes 25 minutes each way, five days a week, at a leisurely 6.8 METs:
Per ride: 6.8 × 75 × 25/60 = 212 kcal
Per week (10 rides): 2,125 kcal, or 1,812 kcal above resting energy use
That's 250 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity, which more than meets the 150-minute weekly guideline on its own.

How long does it take to burn off a snack?
A 250 kcal chocolate bar needs about 250 ÷ (3.5 × 70) × 60 = 61 minutes of walking for a 70 kg person, or 22 minutes of running at 6 mph. That comparison is one reason food choices usually matter more than exercise alone for weight control.
Understanding your results
Estimates, not measurements. METs are averages for adults; fitness, efficiency, terrain, temperature and technique change real energy use. Individual results can differ by 20% or more.
Heavier people burn more for the same activity because moving more mass takes more energy.
Wearables estimate from heart rate and movement and may disagree with MET-based estimates.
Weight management: exercise calories add to your daily energy expenditure. Use the TDEE calculator for total daily needs and the calorie deficit calculator to plan changes. Don't simply "eat back" every estimated exercise calorie; the estimate may be generous.
Physical activity guidelines
United States: adults should aim for at least 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate-intensity activity, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days (Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans).
Canada: the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate to vigorous aerobic activity a week, muscle strengthening twice a week, and limiting sedentary time.
United Kingdom: the Chief Medical Officers advise at least 150 minutes of moderate or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, with strength activities on at least 2 days.
Moderate activities are roughly 3 to 5.9 METs; vigorous activities are 6 METs or more.
Calories and kilojoules on labels
US and Canadian food labels show energy as "Calories", meaning kilocalories. UK labels show both kilojoules (kJ) and kilocalories (kcal), with kJ first. To compare a UK label with this calculator, divide kJ by 4.184: a 1,757 kJ meal is about 420 kcal, the same as Example 1's run.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting the whole gym session when half was rest between sets.
Using a vigorous MET for a moderate effort.
Forgetting to convert pounds to kilograms (the calculator handles this when imperial units are selected).
Double-counting resting calories already in your TDEE.
Frequently asked questions
How are calories burned calculated?
By multiplying the activity's MET value by your weight in kg and the time in hours.
What is a MET?
A metabolic equivalent: the energy cost of an activity as a multiple of resting energy use.
How many calories does walking burn?
At 3 mph (about 3.5 METs), a 70 kg person burns about 120 kcal in 30 minutes.
Does a heavier person burn more calories?
Yes, for the same activity and duration.
Are fitness tracker calories accurate?
They're estimates too, and studies find wide errors for energy expenditure.
Should I eat back exercise calories?
It depends on your goals; many people eat back only part of the estimate.
