H1: Average Calculator
The average, or arithmetic mean, is the most common way to summarise a list of numbers with one value. Teachers use it for grades, businesses for sales and costs, and everyone for things like average spending or fuel use. Enter your numbers and this calculator gives the mean, the sum and the count.
How to use this calculator
Type or paste your numbers, separated by commas, for example 10, 20, 30, 40. Negative numbers and decimals are fine.
Results: average (mean), sum and count.
The formula
Mean = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) ÷ n
x₁ … xₙ are the values
n is how many values there are
Worked examples
Example 1: 10, 20, 30, 40
Sum = 100; count = 4; mean = 100 ÷ 4 = 25.
Example 2: test scores 72, 85, 90, 64 and 88
Sum = 399; mean = 399 ÷ 5 = 79.8.
To average 80 over six tests, the sixth score would need to be 80 × 6 − 399 = 81.
Mean, median and mode
The mean is one of three common "averages":
Mean: sum ÷ count
Median: the middle value when sorted
Mode: the most frequent value
They can differ a lot when there are outliers. Salaries of $32,000, $35,000, $38,000, $41,000 and $250,000 have a mean of $79,200 but a median of $38,000. The median is the better "typical" value here. Use the mean, median, mode and range calculator to see all of them.
Weighted averages
When some values count more than others, multiply each by its weight and divide by the total weight. A course graded 40% on coursework (85), 35% on a midterm (78) and 25% on a final (92) has a weighted average of 0.40 × 85 + 0.35 × 78 + 0.25 × 92 = 84.30. The simple mean of the three marks would be different. This calculator gives the simple mean; for weighted marks, see the grade calculator.
Geometric and harmonic means
Two other means are useful in specific cases:
Geometric mean for growth rates: multiply the values and take the nth root. Returns of +50% and −50% have an arithmetic mean of 0%, but the money actually falls from 100 to 75. Using growth factors 1.5 and 0.5, the geometric mean is √0.75 ≈ 0.866, a loss of about 13.4% a year.
Harmonic mean for rates over the same distance: driving 60 km at 60 km/h and 60 km at 40 km/h gives an average speed of 48 km/h, not 50.
Where averages are used
Grades: the average of assignments or a GPA (GPA calculator)
Finance: average monthly spending, average return (for returns over several years, a geometric average is more accurate)
Science: averaging repeated measurements to reduce random error
Sport: batting averages, average pace
Tips and common mistakes
Averaging percentages or rates from groups of different sizes. Weight them by group size.
Averaging averages. The mean of two class averages isn't the overall mean unless the classes are the same size.
Leaving in typos. One extra zero can change the mean dramatically.
Using commas as thousands separators. Enter 1500, not 1,500, because commas separate values.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate an average?
Add the numbers and divide by how many there are.
What's the difference between mean and average?
In everyday use they're the same; "average" can also mean median or mode.
Can the average be a number not in the list?
Yes; the mean of 1 and 2 is 1.5.
How do I find a weighted average?
Multiply each value by its weight, add them, and divide by the sum of the weights.
Does a zero count?
Yes; zero is a value and increases the count.
What's a good average to report with outliers?
The median is often better.
