H1: Scientific Calculator
A scientific calculator handles the functions that a basic calculator can't: trigonometry, logarithms, powers, roots, factorials and constants. FigSumo's works in your browser with the on-screen keypad or your keyboard, and keeps your last answer and a memory value for multi-step problems.

How to use it
1. Choose Deg or Rad before any trig calculation.
2. Type an expression with the keys, or by typing digits, operators, ^ and brackets on your keyboard.
3. Press = (or Enter) to evaluate. Back (Backspace) removes the last character; AC (Escape) clears.
4. Use Ans to bring the previous result into a new expression, and M+ / M− / MR to add to, subtract from and recall a stored value.
The calculator follows the standard order of operations: brackets, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. When in doubt, add brackets: 2^(1/3) is not the same as 2^1/3.
Worked examples
Example 1: height of a tree
Standing 20 m away, you measure the angle to the treetop as 35°. In Deg mode, 20 × tan(35) = 14.00 m (plus your eye height).
Example 2: inverse trig
sin⁻¹(0.5) in Deg mode returns 30°.
Example 3: pH from concentration
pH = −log[H⁺]. For a hydrogen-ion concentration of 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L, enter −log(3.2 EXP −4) → pH 3.49.
Example 4: continuous compounding
$5,000 at 4% for 10 years, compounded continuously: 5000 × e^(0.04 × 10) = $7,459.12.
Example 5: factorials
10! = 3,628,800, the number of ways to arrange 10 books on a shelf.
Example 6: Deg vs Rad
sin(30) gives 0.5 in Deg mode but -0.98803162 in Rad mode, because 30 radians is about 1,719°.
Understanding the functions
log vs ln: log is base 10 (log 1000 = 3); ln is base e ≈ 2.71828 (ln e = 1). For other bases, use log(x) ÷ log(b) or the log calculator.
EXP enters powers of ten: 6.02 EXP 23 = 6.02 × 10²³. It's not the same as eˣ.
ʸ√x takes any root: the 4th root of 81 is 3.
n! works on non-negative whole numbers; 0! = 1.
% converts to a hundredth; for percentage changes, the percentage calculator is clearer.
RND returns a random number between 0 and 1.
Tips and common mistakes
Wrong angle mode is the most common trig error. Check the Deg/Rad switch first.
Missing brackets around fractions in exponents and denominators: 1/(2×3) vs 1/2×3.
Negative bases: (−3)^2 = 9, but −3^2 = −9 under standard precedence.
Undefined results: tan(90°), log(0) and √ of a negative number have no real value; the calculator returns an error.
Floating-point rounding: results like 0.30000000000000004 come from binary arithmetic; round as needed.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
School exam rules on calculators differ. US tests such as the SAT and ACT publish their own lists of permitted calculators; UK GCSE has both calculator and non-calculator papers; Canadian provincial exams set their own rules. An online calculator like this one is for study and homework, so check what's allowed before a test.
Frequently asked questions
Is this calculator in degrees or radians?
Either; use the Deg/Rad switch.
How do I enter scientific notation?
Use EXP: 3.2 EXP −4 means 3.2 × 10⁻⁴.
What's the difference between log and ln?
log is base 10; ln is base e.
Can I use my keyboard?
Yes: digits, + − * /, ^, brackets, Enter, Backspace and Escape.
How do I reuse my last answer?
Press Ans.
