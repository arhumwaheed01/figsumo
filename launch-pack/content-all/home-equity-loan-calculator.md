H1: Home Equity Loan Calculator
A home equity loan lets you borrow a lump sum against the equity in your home and repay it in fixed monthly installments, usually at a fixed rate. It is sometimes called a second mortgage. This calculator gives the monthly payment, the total of payments and the total interest for the amount, rate and term you enter, with an amortization chart. The section below shows how to work out how much a lender might let you borrow.
How to use this calculator
1. Loan amount: the lump sum you plan to borrow. Check it against your equity limit first (Example 2).
2. Interest rate: the rate in the lender's offer. Use the APR to include fees.
3. Loan term (years) and Loan term (months): common terms are 5 to 30 years.
Results: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest paid, the rate used and a chart of the balance over time.
The formula
PMT = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Total interest = PMT × n − P
P: loan amount
r: annual rate ÷ 12
n: years × 12 + months
How much can you borrow? Lenders cap the combined loan-to-value (CLTV):
CLTV = (Mortgage balance + Home equity loan) ÷ Home value
Maximum loan = Home value × CLTV limit − Mortgage balance
Worked examples
Example 1: $20,000 over 5 years at 7.5%
PMT = $400.76

Example 2: maximum loan at 85% CLTV, then the payment
Home value $450,000; mortgage balance $260,000; lender's CLTV limit 85%.
Maximum loan = 450,000 × 0.85 − 260,000 = $122,500.00

Understanding your results
Monthly payment is principal and interest on the second loan only. Your first mortgage payment continues as before.
Total interest assumes you keep the loan for the full term.
The chart shows the balance falling slowly at first, then faster, as more of each payment goes to principal.
Home equity loan, HELOC or cash-out refinance?
Home equity loan: one lump sum, fixed rate and payment. Suits a single known cost, such as a roof replacement.
HELOC: draw as needed, variable rate, often interest-only for years; compare with the HELOC calculator.
Cash-out refinance: one new, larger first mortgage. Worth considering when current mortgage rates are close to or below your existing rate; see the refinance calculator.
Costs to expect
Home equity loans can carry closing costs similar to a mortgage: an appraisal, origination fee, title search and recording fees. Some lenders waive them in exchange for a higher rate or an early-closure fee if you repay within a few years. In the US, the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure set these out. Put the fees into the APR calculator to compare offers fairly.
Country notes
United States: interest may be deductible only if the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the loan, within the overall mortgage debt limit (IRS Publication 936). Federal law gives a three-day right of rescission on most home equity loans on your primary residence.
Canada: fixed-sum second mortgages are less common than HELOCs; borrowers usually refinance or use a HELOC. Federally regulated lenders are limited to 80% loan-to-value on refinancing under OSFI B-20.
United Kingdom: the equivalent is a second-charge mortgage or a further advance from your existing lender. Both are regulated by the FCA, and lenders must check affordability.
Tips and common mistakes
Borrowing the maximum by default. A lower CLTV leaves a safety margin if home prices fall.
Comparing rates without fees. Use APR.
Using equity for short-lived spending. A 15-year loan for a holiday means paying for it long after it's over.
Forgetting the risk. The loan is secured on your home.
Questions to ask the lender
Ask whether the rate is fixed, what closing costs and appraisal fees apply, and whether there's a prepayment penalty or an early-closure fee. Ask how much the lender will let you borrow as a share of your home's value, since limits of 80% to 90% combined loan-to-value are common. Get the answer in writing and compare at least two lenders, using the APR as well as the rate.
Frequently asked questions
How much can I borrow with a home equity loan?
Typically up to 80%–90% CLTV, minus what you owe on your mortgage, subject to credit and income checks. Lenders set their own limits.
Are home equity loan rates fixed?
Usually, yes. That's the main difference from a HELOC.
Is home equity loan interest tax-deductible?
In the US, only if the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home securing the loan, within IRS limits.
How long does it take to get a home equity loan?
Often a few weeks, because the lender needs an appraisal and title work.
Can I pay it off early?
Usually, but check for prepayment or early closure fees.
What happens if I sell the house?
Both the first mortgage and the home equity loan are repaid from the sale proceeds.
