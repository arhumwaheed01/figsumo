H1: Horsepower Calculator
Horsepower measures how fast an engine can do work. Torque, the twisting force at the crankshaft, tells you how hard it pulls. The two are tied together by engine speed: horsepower is torque multiplied by RPM, scaled by a constant. Enter torque and RPM and this calculator gives the horsepower.
How to use this calculator
1. Torque (lb·ft): at a given engine speed, from a dyno sheet or manufacturer spec (default 250).
2. Engine speed (RPM): the speed at which that torque is produced (default 5,500).
Result: horsepower, to one decimal place.
The formula
hp = torque (lb·ft) × RPM ÷ 5,252
Where 5,252 comes from: one horsepower is 33,000 ft·lbf per minute (James Watt's figure for a working horse), and one revolution is 2π radians, so 33,000 ÷ 2π ≈ 5,252.
A consequence: on any dyno chart plotted in lb·ft and hp, the torque and power curves cross at 5,252 rpm.
Metric equivalent
kW = torque (N·m) × RPM ÷ 9,549
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
250 × 5,500 ÷ 5,252 = 261.8 hp (about 195.2 kW, or 265.4 PS).
Example 2: finding torque
An engine makes 300 hp at 6,000 rpm. Torque at that speed = 300 × 5,252 ÷ 6,000 = 262.6 lb·ft.
Example 3: a turbo-diesel (metric)
400 N·m at 3,000 rpm → 400 × 3,000 ÷ 9,549 = 125.7 kW (168.5 hp).
Example 4: an electric motor
EVs are usually rated in kW: a 150 kW motor is about 201 hp. Electric motors produce peak torque from zero rpm, which is why they feel quick off the line.

Torque vs horsepower
Torque gets the car moving and pulling a load: high torque at low RPM makes towing easy.
Horsepower sets the top speed and how quickly you can accelerate at high speed.
Gearing multiplies torque at the wheels, which is why a lower gear gives stronger acceleration.
Understanding your results
Peak power and peak torque occur at different RPMs. Use torque at the same RPM you're calculating for.
Crank vs wheel: dyno figures at the wheels are 10–20% lower than crank ratings because of drivetrain losses.
Gross vs net: pre-1972 US ratings were gross and higher than today's SAE net ratings.
For an estimate from engine size, see the engine displacement calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Using N·m with the 5,252 constant. Convert first (1 lb·ft = 1.3558 N·m) or use 9,549 for kW.
Mixing peak torque with peak-power RPM.
Comparing PS and hp directly (1 PS = 0.986 hp).
Quick check
A 150 kW electric motor is 150 ÷ 0.7457 = 201.2 hp.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
US and Canadian car ads quote SAE net horsepower. UK ads usually quote PS or bhp, and electric cars are often rated in kW, so convert before you compare models.
Frequently asked questions
How do I convert torque to horsepower?
Multiply torque by RPM and divide by 5,252.
Why 5,252?
It's 33,000 ft·lbf/min divided by 2π.
How many watts in a horsepower?
About 745.7 for mechanical horsepower.
What's the difference between hp and PS?
PS is metric horsepower, about 1.4% smaller.
How do I convert kW to hp?
Divide by 0.7457 (or multiply by 1.341).
Why do torque and horsepower curves cross at 5,252 rpm?
At that speed, the formula makes hp equal to torque.
