H1: Density Calculator
Density is how much mass is packed into a given volume. It explains why a steel bolt sinks while a log floats, and it's used to identify materials, convert between weight and volume, and estimate the load of a delivery. Enter a mass and a volume and this calculator gives the density in kilograms per cubic metre and grams per cubic centimetre.
How to use this calculator
1. Mass (kg): default 2.5.
2. Volume (L): litres, default 1.
Results: density in kg/m³ and in g/cm³ (which equals kg/L and g/mL).
The formula
ρ = m ÷ V
ρ (rho): density
m: mass
V: volume
Rearranged: m = ρ × V, and V = m ÷ ρ.
The calculator converts litres to cubic metres (1 L = 0.001 m³), so density in kg/m³ is mass ÷ (volume ÷ 1,000). Dividing by 1,000 again gives g/cm³.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
2.5 kg in 1 L = 2,500 kg/m³ = 2.5 g/cm³, close to glass or granite. In US units that's about 156.1 lb/ft³.
Example 2: is it gold?
A ring weighs 10.5 g and displaces 0.66 mL of water: 10.5 ÷ 0.66 = 15.91 g/cm³. That's within the typical range for 18-carat gold alloys (about 15–16 g/cm³ [VERIFY by alloy]) and well below pure gold's 19.3. Displacement on small items is imprecise, so an assay is the reliable test.
Example 3: mass from density
An oak board 0.75 m × 0.3 m × 20 mm has a volume of 0.0045 m³. At about 600–750 kg/m³ (oak varies), it weighs roughly 2.7 kg at 600 kg/m³.
Example 4: aluminium sheet
2 litres (0.002 m³) of aluminium at 2,700 kg/m³ weighs 5.4 kg.

Specific gravity is density relative to water, so a material with SG 2.5 has a density of 2.5 g/cm³.
Understanding your results
Temperature matters, especially for liquids and gases. Water is densest at about 4 °C.
Bulk density of gravel, soil or grain includes air gaps and is much lower than the solid material's density; use supplier figures for loose materials (see the gravel calculator).
Measure volume by displacement for irregular objects: the rise in water level equals the object's volume.
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing units, such as grams with litres (that gives g/L, which equals kg/m³).
Using weight in pounds without converting to kilograms.
Ignoring air gaps in loose materials.
Practice
A 500 g block measuring 5 × 4 × 10 cm has a density of 500 ÷ 200 = 2.5 g/cm³.
Frequently asked questions
What is the formula for density?
Mass divided by volume.
What is the density of water?
About 1,000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³).
How do I convert g/cm³ to kg/m³?
Multiply by 1,000.
How do I find mass from density?
Multiply density by volume.
Why does ice float?
It's about 8% less dense than liquid water.
What is specific gravity?
Density divided by the density of water.
