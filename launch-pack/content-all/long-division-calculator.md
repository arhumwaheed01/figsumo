H1: Long Division Calculator
Long division breaks a division problem into a series of small steps, one digit at a time. This calculator divides one integer by another and gives the whole-number quotient, the remainder, and the decimal answer, so you can check homework or split quantities exactly.
How to use this calculator
1. Dividend: the number being divided (whole number).
2. Divisor: the number you're dividing by (whole number, not zero).
Results: quotient (whole part), remainder and decimal (to eight places).
The relationship
Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder,  0 ≤ Remainder < Divisor
Check any answer by multiplying back: 7 × 14 + 2 = 100.
How to do long division by hand
Repeat four steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.
Example 1: 100 ÷ 7
1. 7 goes into 10 once. Write 1. 1 × 7 = 7; 10 − 7 = 3.
2. Bring down 0 → 30. 7 goes into 30 four times. Write 4. 4 × 7 = 28; 30 − 28 = 2.
3. No digits left: quotient 14, remainder 2.
To continue into decimals, add a decimal point and bring down zeros: 20 ÷ 7 = 2 r 6, 60 ÷ 7 = 8 r 4, … giving 14.28571429. The digits 142857 repeat forever.
Example 2: 4,567 ÷ 12
1. 12 into 45 → 3 (36), remainder 9
2. Bring down 6 → 96 → 8 (96), remainder 0
3. Bring down 7 → 7 → 0, remainder 7
4. Quotient 380, remainder 7; decimal 380.58333333
Example 3: 98,765 ÷ 43
Quotient 2296, remainder 37.
Expressing the answer
With a remainder: 100 ÷ 7 = 14 r 2
As a mixed number: 14 2/7 (remainder over divisor); see the fraction calculator
As a decimal: 14.28571429 (rounded); see the rounding calculator
Which one you need depends on the problem. Sharing 100 sweets among 7 children: 14 each with 2 left over. Splitting a $100 bill seven ways: $14.29 each (rounded).
Understanding your results
Negative numbers: the quotient is truncated toward zero and the remainder has the same sign as the dividend. −100 ÷ 7 gives quotient −14 and remainder −2 (since 7 × −14 − 2 = −100). Some textbooks prefer a positive remainder (−15 r 5).
Decimal display is rounded to eight places; repeating decimals continue beyond that.
Integers only. To divide decimals by hand, multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 first: 4.5 ÷ 0.3 = 45 ÷ 3 = 15.
Divisibility shortcuts
A remainder of 0 means the divisor is a factor. Quick checks: a number is divisible by 3 if its digit sum is (4+5+6+7 = 22, so 4,567 isn't), by 4 if its last two digits are, and by 9 if its digit sum is. See the factor calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting a zero in the quotient when the divisor doesn't go into a partial dividend (step 3 of Example 2).
A remainder bigger than the divisor means the last digit of the quotient is too small.
Misaligning digits when writing by hand.
Dividing by zero: it's undefined, so the calculator shows an error.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US and Canadian schools write long division with the divisor to the left of a bracket. Some UK schools teach "bus stop" short division for one-digit divisors.
Frequently asked questions
What is the remainder?
What's left over after dividing as many whole times as possible.
How do I check a long division answer?
Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder; you should get the dividend.
How do I turn a remainder into a decimal?
Keep dividing, bringing down zeros after the decimal point.
Can I divide decimals?
Scale both numbers to whole numbers first.
Why does 100 ÷ 7 repeat?
Because 7 doesn't divide any power of 10, the remainders cycle.
What does "r" mean?
Remainder.
