H1: Gravel Calculator
Whether you're laying a path, a driveway or a drainage bed, gravel is sold by volume (cubic yards) or by weight (tons). This calculator converts the area and depth you want to cover into both, so you can compare quotes from suppliers that price either way.
How to use this calculator
1. Area length (ft) and area width (ft): default 15 × 10.
2. Depth (in): default 4.
Results: estimated gravel in tons and cubic yards, with a note on the density assumed.
The formulas
volume (ft³) = length × width × depth ÷ 12
cubic yards = ft³ ÷ 27
tons = ft³ × 100 lb ÷ 2,000
The tonnage assumes about 100 lb per cubic foot (1.35 tons per cubic yard), a typical figure for loose gravel. Actual densities run from about 95 to 110 lb/ft³ depending on stone type, size and moisture; ask your supplier for theirs.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
15 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 50 ft³ = 1.85 yd³ ≈ 2.5 tons
Example 2: a parking pad
A 40 ft × 12 ft parking pad topped up 3 in deep is 120 ft³ = 4.44 yd³ ≈ 6.00 tons. Allowing 15% for compaction: 6.90 tons.
Example 3: bagged gravel
Bags are often 0.5 ft³. A 15 × 10 ft area at 2 in deep needs 50 bags, which is why bulk delivery is cheaper for anything bigger than a small bed.
Example 4: metric (UK, Canada)
6 m × 3 m at 100 mm deep = 1.8 m³. At about 1.6 tonnes per m³, that's roughly 2.88 tonnes. UK merchants often sell 850 kg "jumbo" bulk bags.
How deep should gravel be?
Use
Typical depth
Decorative top layer
2 in (5 cm)
Footpath
2–3 in on a compacted base
Patio or shed base
4 in compacted sub-base
Driveway
4–6 in sub-base plus 2 in top layer (often more on soft ground)
French drain
Per design; often 12 in+
These are common practice, not engineering specifications.
Types of gravel
Crushed stone (#57, MOT Type 1 in the UK): angular, compacts well; for bases and driveways.
Pea gravel: small and rounded; comfortable underfoot but shifts, so edge it.
River rock: larger rounded stones for decoration and drainage.
Decomposed granite: fine, packs into a firm path.
Understanding your results
Compaction: crushed base can compact 10–20%; order extra for driveways and sub-bases.
Waste: add 5–10% for uneven ground and edges.
Weight limits: a pickup truck bed or trailer may not carry what the volume suggests. A ton of gravel is only about 0.74 yd³.
The chart splits volume and weight for reference only.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering depth in feet (the box is inches).
Ignoring compaction for base layers.
Assuming all stone weighs the same.
Skipping landscape fabric under decorative gravel, which lets weeds through.
Quick check
One cubic yard is about 0.765 m³.
Frequently asked questions
How much gravel do I need for a 10 × 10 area?
At 4 in deep, about 33 ft³ (1.23 yd³), or 1.67 tons.
How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?
About 1.35 at 100 lb/ft³; 1.3–1.5 depending on the stone.
How deep should a gravel driveway be?
Often 4–6 in of base plus a 2 in top layer.
Can I enter metres?
Not yet; convert, or use the metric example above.
Should I order extra?
Yes, 5–10% plus compaction for base layers.
What's the difference between tons and tonnes?
A US short ton is 2,000 lb (907 kg); a metric tonne is 1,000 kg.
