H1: College Cost Calculator
This calculator projects what a college or university education will cost by the time a student enrolls. Enter today's annual cost, the years until enrollment, an education inflation rate and the number of years of study. It returns the estimated cost of the first year at enrollment and the total cost of the whole program.
How to use this calculator
1. Annual cost today: the full yearly cost of attendance at the kind of school you have in mind: tuition and fees, plus housing, food, books and transport if you want the full picture. College websites publish a "cost of attendance"; in the US, the College Navigator database lists them.
2. Years until enrollment: for a newborn, about 18; for a high-school sophomore, about 3.
3. Education inflation: how fast you expect costs to rise per year. Historic tuition increases have often outpaced general inflation, but the gap has varied a lot by decade and country [VERIFY a current long-run figure from the source you rely on, e.g. College Board "Trends in College Pricing"].
4. Years of college: usually 4 for a US bachelor's degree, 3 or 4 in the UK, 4 in Canada.
Results: estimated total college cost and first year cost (at enrollment).
The formula
Each year of study is inflated from today to the year it is paid:
Cost in study year k = C × (1 + g)^(Y + k),  k = 0, 1, …, N − 1
Total cost = Σ cost in each study year
C: annual cost today
g: education inflation as a decimal
Y: years until enrollment
N: years of college
The first year is C × (1 + g)^Y. Later years keep rising because inflation continues while the student is enrolled.
Worked examples
Example 1: $30,000 a year today, enrollment in 10 years, 5% inflation, 4 years
Year 1: 30,000 × 1.05^10 = $48,866.84
Year 2: $51,310.18
Year 3: $53,875.69
Year 4: $56,569.47
Total: $210,622.18
At today's prices four years would cost $120,000. Inflation adds about three quarters of that again over the 14 years.
Example 2: $12,000 a year today, 5 years away, 4% inflation, 4 years
First year: $14,599.83
Total: $61,997.67 (compared with $48,000.00 at today's prices)
To have the entire total saved by enrollment, earning 5% a year compounded monthly, you would need to put aside about $911.65 a month for five years. That is a cautious target: in practice the later years are paid while the remaining savings keep earning.
Understanding your results
First year cost is the bill for the year the student starts, in future dollars.
Total cost is the sum of all years in future dollars. It is not adjusted for financial aid, scholarships, grants or tax credits, which can reduce the net price substantially.
Sensitivity: inflation compounds, so small changes in the rate move the total a lot over long horizons. Try two or three rates.
Sticker price vs net price: many students pay less than the published cost of attendance. In the US, every college must offer a net price calculator on its website.
Ways to pay
Savings plans: in the US, 529 plans grow tax-free when used for qualified education expenses. In Canada, an RESP attracts the Canada Education Savings Grant of 20% on the first $2,500 contributed per year per child (up to $500 a year, $7,200 lifetime). In the UK, a Junior ISA allows £9,000 a year for a child [VERIFY current JISA limit].
Financial aid and scholarships: apply early. US students file the FAFSA; Canadian students apply through their province's student aid program; UK students apply to Student Finance.
Student loans: borrowing fills the gap, but compare repayment costs with the student loan calculator.
Work and cash flow: part-time work and paying some costs from income during study reduce what must be saved up front.
Country notes
United States: published costs vary widely between in-state public, out-of-state public and private colleges. The College Scorecard and College Navigator show costs, net prices and outcomes.
Canada: tuition is set by provinces and institutions; Statistics Canada publishes average tuition by province each year. The RESP grant rules are set out by Employment and Social Development Canada.
United Kingdom: in England, tuition fees for home students are capped by the government (£9,535 a year for 2025/26 [VERIFY 2026/27 cap]), and most students take a tuition fee loan, repaid through the tax system after graduation. Living costs are covered partly by maintenance loans. Scotland does not charge eligible Scottish students tuition fees.
Tips and common mistakes
Using tuition only. Housing and food can cost as much as tuition; include them for a realistic budget.
Ignoring aid. Use school net price calculators to estimate what you'd really pay.
Setting inflation at zero. Even modest rises compound over 10 to 18 years.
Waiting to start saving. The earlier contributions begin, the more growth does the work.
Frequently asked questions
What inflation rate should I use for college costs?
Look at recent published increases for the type of school you're considering, and run the calculator at a low and a high rate. There is no single correct figure.
Does the total include financial aid?
No. It is the full projected cost before grants, scholarships or loans.
Why does each year cost more than the last?
Prices keep rising while the student is enrolled, so year four is inflated for three more years than year one.
How much should I save each month?
Divide the total among your sources (savings, aid, loans, income). For the savings share, the savings calculator can find the monthly deposit to reach a target.
Should I include room and board?
Yes, if you expect to pay it. Living costs are a large part of the total, especially for students who live away from home.
Can I use this for graduate school?
Yes. Enter the annual cost, the years until the program starts and its length.
