H1: Currency Converter
This converter changes an amount from one currency to another using exchange rates you enter. It works through the US dollar: you give the value of each currency per 1 USD, and it calculates the cross rate between them. That makes it handy for checking a bank's or card provider's rate against a published reference rate, budgeting a trip, or converting a price quoted abroad.
How to use this converter
1. Amount: the sum you want to convert, in the "from" currency.
2. From currency (units per 1 USD): how many units of your starting currency equal one US dollar. For US dollars, enter 1.
3. To currency (units per 1 USD): how many units of the target currency equal one US dollar.
Results: converted amount in the target currency and the amount in USD (estimate).
Where to get the rates: central banks publish daily reference rates, including the Federal Reserve (H.10 release), the Bank of Canada and the European Central Bank. Rates change throughout the day, so note the date of the rate you use. The tool does not fetch live rates.
The formula
Amount in USD = Amount ÷ From rate
Converted amount = Amount in USD × To rate
Cross rate (to per 1 from) = To rate ÷ From rate
Both rates are quoted against the dollar, so dividing one by the other gives the exchange rate between the two currencies directly.
Worked examples
The rates below are illustrations, not current market rates.
Example 1: CAD to GBP through the dollar
Suppose 1 USD = 1.38 CAD and 1 USD = 0.75 GBP. Convert C$500:
Amount in USD = 500 ÷ 1.38 = $362.32
Converted = $362.32 × 0.75 = £271.74
Cross rate: 0.75 ÷ 1.38 = 0.5435 GBP per CAD
Example 2: how much a card's exchange margin costs
You spend US$1,000 on a trip. At a mid-market rate of 1.38 CAD per USD, that is C$1,380.00. A provider whose rate is 2.5% worse than mid-market gives you only C$1,345.50 of value for the same Canadian dollars, a hidden cost of C$34.50. Enter the provider's rate and the reference rate to see the gap for your own amounts.
Converting the other way
To go from USD to another currency, enter 1 as the "from" rate. To find how many dollars one unit of a currency buys, divide 1 by its rate: at 1.38 CAD per USD, one Canadian dollar is 0.7246 USD.
Understanding your results
Converted amount uses exactly the rates you entered. If you entered mid-market rates, your bank or exchange bureau will usually give you less.
Amount in USD is an intermediate step; it is useful if you also want the dollar value.
Rates move. A conversion is only valid for the moment of the rate you used.
Mid-market, buy and sell rates
The mid-market rate is halfway between the prices at which banks buy and sell a currency. Consumers rarely get it. Providers make money through:
a margin (spread) built into the rate, often the largest cost;
fees, fixed or percentage-based;
dynamic currency conversion, where a shop or ATM abroad offers to charge you in your home currency. Its rate is usually worse; choosing the local currency is normally cheaper.
To compare offers, convert the same amount at each provider's rate after fees and see which gives the most foreign currency.
Budgeting a trip or purchase abroad
For travel, convert your daily budget at a cautious rate, about 3% worse than the reference rate, so card margins and ATM fees don't push you over. For a large purchase or a transfer (a house deposit, tuition, a car), small rate differences matter: 1% of $50,000 is $500. Get quotes from two or three providers on the same day, convert the same amount with each provider's rate here, and subtract their fees before choosing.
Country notes
United States: the Federal Reserve publishes noon buying rates in the H.10 release (weekly) for many currencies. Remittance transfers abroad must disclose the exchange rate and fees under the CFPB's Remittance Rule.
Canada: the Bank of Canada publishes daily average exchange rates, which the CRA accepts for converting foreign income on tax returns.
United Kingdom: the Bank of England publishes daily spot rates, and HMRC publishes monthly exchange rates for customs and VAT purposes.
Tips and common mistakes
Rate the wrong way round. Rates here are units per 1 USD. If a site shows "1 CAD = 0.72 USD", convert it: 1 ÷ 0.72 = 1.389 CAD per USD.
Mixing a buy rate with a sell rate. Use the same type of rate for both currencies.
Ignoring fees. Add fixed fees in the original currency before converting.
Old rates. Note the date. A rate from last month can be several percent off.
Frequently asked questions
Does this converter use live rates?
No. You enter the rates, so you control which reference you compare against. Central bank websites publish daily rates.
Why convert through US dollars?
Most rates are quoted against the dollar, so it is the easiest common reference. The cross rate between any two currencies follows from their dollar rates.
What is the mid-market rate?
The midpoint between the buy and sell prices on the wholesale market. It is the fairest benchmark for comparing what a provider charges.
Why did my bank give me a different amount?
Banks add a margin to the rate and may charge fees. The converter shows the result at the rate you entered, without fees.
How do I convert a price into my currency?
Enter the foreign price as the amount, its currency's per-USD rate as the "from" rate, and your currency's per-USD rate as the "to" rate.
Which rate should I use for tax purposes?
Use the rate your tax authority specifies: in the US, the IRS accepts yearly average or spot rates; in Canada, the Bank of Canada rate; in the UK, HMRC's published rates where they apply.
