H1: Protein Calculator
Protein builds and repairs muscle and other tissues, and makes enzymes and hormones. How much you need depends mostly on your body weight and how active you are. This calculator gives a daily protein target in grams based on your weight and goal, along with the calories that protein provides.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric (kg) or imperial (lb).
2. Body weight.
3. Goal:
General health: 0.8 g per kg
Active / endurance: 1.2 g per kg
Strength / muscle gain: 1.6 g per kg
High intake: 2.0 g per kg
Results: daily protein (grams), calories from protein and the guideline used.
How it works
Protein (g/day) = body weight (kg) × grams per kg
Calories from protein = protein g × 4
Weights in pounds are converted with 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg.
0.8 g/kg is the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for adults set by the US National Academies, used in the US and Canada. It's the amount that meets the needs of nearly all healthy adults, not an optimal figure for athletes. The higher settings reflect sports nutrition position stands, which suggest 1.2–2.0 g/kg for active people.
Worked examples
Example 1: 70 kg, active
70 × 1.2 = 84 g a day
Calories from protein: 336 kcal
At the RDA (0.8 g/kg), the same person needs 56 g.
On a 2,200 kcal diet, 84 g is about 15% of calories.
Example 2: 180 lb, strength training
180 lb = 81.65 kg
× 1.6 = 131 g a day (523 kcal)

Understanding your results
Spread it out. Muscle protein synthesis responds best to about 0.3–0.4 g/kg per meal over three to four meals. For 70 kg, that's 21–28 g per meal.
More isn't always better. Benefits for muscle growth tend to plateau around 1.6 g/kg for most people; intakes above 2 g/kg are rarely needed.
Weight loss: higher protein (1.2–1.6 g/kg or more) helps preserve muscle in a calorie deficit and can help with fullness.
People with obesity: basing the target on your actual weight can overestimate needs. Some dietitians use a goal or adjusted weight instead.
Older adults: some experts recommend 1.0–1.2 g/kg for people over 65 to help limit muscle loss.

Country notes
US and Canada: RDA 0.8 g/kg for adults; 71 g a day during pregnancy (or 1.1 g/kg) [VERIFY current DRI table].
UK: the Reference Nutrient Intake (RNI) is 0.75 g/kg a day for adults, about 56 g for men and 45 g for women.
Nutrition labels in all three countries list protein per serving or per 100 g.
Protein on food labels
US: the Nutrition Facts label lists protein in grams. A % Daily Value, based on 50 g a day, is only required when a protein claim is made or the food is for young children.
Canada: the Nutrition Facts table lists protein in grams but gives no % Daily Value for it.
UK: labels list protein per 100 g, and the reference intake for an average adult is 50 g a day.
These label figures are general reference amounts. Your own target from the calculator may be higher, especially if you train regularly.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting all of a food's weight as protein. 100 g of chicken has about 31 g of protein, not 100 g.
Relying on supplements. Food sources also provide fibre, vitamins and minerals; powders are a convenience, not a requirement.
Ignoring kidney disease. People with chronic kidney disease may need to limit protein; ask your doctor.
Forgetting plant proteins. Beans, lentils, tofu, nuts and whole grains all count.
Frequently asked questions
How much protein do I need a day?
At least 0.8 g per kg of body weight for most adults, more if you're very active or training for strength.
How much protein to build muscle?
About 1.6 g/kg is a widely used target for people doing resistance training.
Can I eat too much protein?
Healthy adults tolerate up to about 2 g/kg well; very high intakes offer little extra benefit.
Should I use lean body mass?
Some people do, especially with high body fat. See the lean body mass calculator.
Is plant protein as good as animal protein?
A varied plant diet can meet needs; some plant proteins are lower in certain amino acids, so mix sources.
Do I need protein right after a workout?
Total daily intake matters most; a meal with protein within a few hours of training is enough.
