H1: Basic Calculator
This basic calculator works out an arithmetic expression you type, such as 2 + 3 * 4 or (250 - 40) / 7. It follows the standard order of operations, so you can enter a whole calculation in one line instead of pressing keys one at a time.
How to use this calculator
1. Type an expression using numbers and these symbols:
+ add, - subtract
* multiply, / divide
^ power (for example 2^8)
( and ) brackets
2. The result appears below. If the expression can't be read, you'll see a message listing the symbols to use.
Order of operations
The calculator applies the standard rules, often remembered as PEMDAS in the US, BEDMAS in Canada and BIDMAS or BODMAS in the UK:
1. Brackets (parentheses)
2. Exponents / indices / orders (powers)
3. Multiplication and division, left to right
4. Addition and subtraction, left to right
Multiplication doesn't come before division; they have equal priority and are worked left to right. The same applies to addition and subtraction.

The fourth row is the famous viral puzzle "48 ÷ 2(9 + 3)". Under the standard left-to-right rule, the answer is 288. Some people get 2 by treating 2(9 + 3) as a single unit; writing the expression with explicit symbols, as the calculator requires, removes the ambiguity.
Negative numbers and powers
-3^2 is usually read as −(3²) = -9, while (-3)^2 = 9. Add brackets when you mean to square a negative number. This is a common source of disagreement between calculators, so brackets are the safest choice.
Everyday uses
Shopping: 3 * 4.99 + 2 * 1.25 for a basket total
Discounts: multiply by 0.8 for 20% off, or use the percentage calculator
Splitting bills: (62.40 + 9.60) / 4
Units: 5280 * 3 for feet in three miles
Recipes: 250 * 1.5 to scale an ingredient by half again
Tips and common mistakes
Using x for multiplication. Use *.
Commas in numbers. Type 1240, not 1,240.
Missing brackets. 100 - 20 / 4 is 95, not 20.
Percent sign. Write 15% as 0.15 or 15/100.
Rounding too early. Keep full values until the end, then round with the rounding calculator.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
Decimal point: the calculator uses a full stop for decimals, as in the US, English Canada and the UK. If you're used to the comma decimal common in Quebec French and much of Europe, type 2.5 rather than 2,5.
Thousands separators: leave them out whatever your local style; 1240 is read correctly, while 1,240 is not.
The mnemonic you were taught (PEMDAS, BEDMAS, BIDMAS or BODMAS) gives the same results. Only the words differ: "parentheses" or "brackets", and "exponents", "indices" or "orders".
Quick checks before you trust a result
Estimate first. 48.7 × 21 should be roughly 50 × 20 = 1,000; the exact answer is 1,022.7.
Reverse the operation. If 1,054 ÷ 0.85 gives 1,240, the original discount calculation was right.
Watch the size of the answer when dividing by a decimal: 30 ÷ 0.5 is 60, larger than 30.
Frequently asked questions
Does this calculator follow PEMDAS/BODMAS?
Yes. Brackets, powers, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.
Is multiplication done before division?
No. They're done left to right in the order they appear.
How do I enter a power?
Use ^, for example 2^10 = 1,024.
Can I use decimals and negative numbers?
Yes, for example -4.5 * 2.
Why does my phone calculator give a different answer?
Some simple calculators work strictly left to right as you press keys and ignore precedence.
Is there a scientific version?
Yes, the scientific calculator adds trig, logs, roots and memory.
