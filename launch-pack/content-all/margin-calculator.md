H1: Margin Calculator
Gross margin is the share of each sale left after paying for the goods or services sold. This calculator takes revenue and cost of goods sold (COGS) and returns gross profit and gross margin. Business owners use it to check pricing, compare products, and see how discounts or cost increases affect profit.
How to use this calculator
1. Revenue: sales for the item, product line or period, excluding sales tax or VAT collected for the government.
2. Cost of goods sold: the direct cost of what you sold, such as purchase price or materials, direct labor, inbound shipping and packaging. Overheads like rent and marketing are not part of COGS.
Results: gross profit and gross margin.
The formula
Gross profit = Revenue − COGS
Gross margin = Gross profit ÷ Revenue × 100
Margin is always measured against the selling price (revenue). That is the key difference from markup, which is measured against cost:
Markup = Gross profit ÷ COGS × 100
To set a price that gives a target margin:
Price = Cost ÷ (1 − Target margin)
Worked examples
Example 1: revenue $250,000, COGS $150,000
Gross profit = 250,000 − 150,000 = $100,000.00
Gross margin = 100,000 ÷ 250,000 = 40.00%
The same profit expressed as markup = 100,000 ÷ 150,000 = 66.67%
Example 2: pricing a product for a 35% margin
A product costs $18.
Price = 18 ÷ (1 − 0.35) = $27.69
Gross profit per unit: $9.69; markup: 53.85%
A common mistake is to add 35% to cost instead: 18 × 1.35 = $24.30, which gives a margin of only 25.93%.

Understanding your results
Gross profit is the money available to pay overheads (rent, salaries, marketing) and leave a profit.
Gross margin lets you compare products and businesses of different sizes. Higher is better, but "good" margins vary widely by industry: grocery retail runs on thin margins while software can be very high.
Gross margin is not net profit. Operating expenses, interest and tax come out after it.
Margin and discounting
Discounts hit margin hard. Selling a $100 item that costs $60 gives a 40% margin. A 10% discount to $90 drops the margin to 33.33%, and gross profit falls from $40 to $30 per unit, so you would need 33.3% more unit sales to make the same gross profit. Before running a sale, check the volume you'd need with the break-even calculator.

Ways to improve gross margin
Raise prices where customers value the product; even small rises flow straight to gross profit.
Cut direct costs by negotiating supplier prices, ordering in larger quantities or reducing waste and returns.
Change the mix: promote higher-margin products and bundles.
Control discounting: set a floor price below which staff can't discount.
Charge for extras such as delivery, installation or rush orders instead of absorbing them.
Track margin monthly by product. A slow drift down often signals rising supplier costs that haven't been passed on.
Country notes
United States and Canada: exclude sales tax, GST/HST and PST from revenue; they belong to the government, not the business.
United Kingdom: for VAT-registered businesses, calculate margin on prices excluding VAT. Use the VAT calculator to strip VAT out of a gross price first. (The VAT "margin scheme" for second-hand goods is a different, tax-specific concept.)
Tips and common mistakes
Confusing margin and markup. A 50% markup is a 33.33% margin.
Leaving costs out of COGS. Forgetting shipping, payment fees or packaging overstates margin.
Including sales tax in revenue. It inflates both revenue and margin.
Looking only at percentage. A lower-margin product that sells in volume can produce more gross profit.
Margin or markup for pricing? Either works if you use it consistently. Retailers often think in margin because it relates directly to sales revenue; many trades and manufacturers use markup on cost. Convert between them with the table above.
Frequently asked questions
What is the difference between margin and markup?
Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price; markup is profit as a percentage of the cost. The same sale has a lower margin than markup.
How do I calculate gross margin?
Subtract COGS from revenue, divide by revenue, and multiply by 100.
How do I price for a specific margin?
Divide the cost by (1 − target margin as a decimal). For a 40% margin on a $30 cost: 30 ÷ 0.60 = $50.
What is a good gross margin?
It depends on the industry and business model. Compare with businesses like yours and with your own history.
Can margin be negative?
Yes, if COGS exceeds revenue, meaning each sale loses money before overheads.
Does gross margin include overheads?
No. Operating margin and net margin include overheads and other costs.
How do I calculate margin from a price that includes VAT or GST?
Remove the tax first. For UK VAT at 20%, divide the gross price by 1.2; for Ontario HST at 13%, divide by 1.13. Then calculate margin on the net price.
What is contribution margin?
Price minus all variable costs per unit (COGS plus variable selling costs such as commissions and payment fees). It is the figure used in break-even analysis.
