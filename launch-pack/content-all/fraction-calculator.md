H1: Fraction Calculator
This fraction calculator adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides two fractions and gives the answer in lowest terms, plus its decimal value. You can also enter whole numbers.
How to use this calculator
1. First fraction: type it as numerator/denominator, such as 3/4. Whole numbers like 2 work too.
2. Operation: +, −, × or ÷.
3. Second fraction.
Results: result (reduced) and decimal to eight places.
Mixed numbers: convert to an improper fraction first. 1¾ = (1 × 4 + 3)/4 = 7/4.
The rules
Add:       a/b + c/d = (ad + bc) / bd
Subtract:  a/b − c/d = (ad − bc) / bd
Multiply:  a/b × c/d = ac / bd
Divide:    a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c = ad / bc
After each operation, the calculator divides the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor to reduce the fraction, and moves any minus sign to the numerator.
Worked examples
Example 1: 1/2 + 1/3
(1 × 3 + 1 × 2) / (2 × 3) = 5/6
Result: 5/6; decimal 0.83333333
Using the least common denominator gives the same answer: 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, so the sum is 5/6.
Example 2: 3/4 ÷ 2/5
Flip the second fraction and multiply: 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8
Result: 15/8 (1.87500000), or 1⅞ as a mixed number
Example 3: 1¾ − ⅚
1¾ = 7/4
7/4 − 5/6 = (42 − 20)/24 = 22/24 → 11/12 (0.91666667)
Example 4: 2/3 × 9/10
18/30 → 3/5
Tip: cancel before multiplying (3 into 9, 2 into 10) to keep numbers small.
Converting fractions
Fraction → decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator (the calculator shows this). See the long division calculator.
Decimal → fraction: write it over a power of 10 and reduce: 0.375 = 375/1000 = 3/8.
Fraction → percentage: multiply the decimal by 100. 5/6 ≈ 83.33%. See the percentage calculator.
Improper → mixed: divide; the quotient is the whole part and the remainder over the denominator is the fraction. 15/8 = 1 remainder 7 → 1⅞.
Understanding your results
Lowest terms: the numerator and denominator share no factor other than 1.
Whole-number results are shown without a denominator (for example, 1/2 + 1/2 = 1).
Repeating decimals are rounded at eight places; the fraction is exact.
Dividing by zero (a second fraction equal to 0 in ÷ mode) gives an error.
Where fractions come up
Cooking: scaling ⅔ cup by 1½.
Construction: adding measurements like 5⅜ in + 2¾ in.
Probability: chances like 1/6 on a die.
Music: time signatures and note lengths.
Tips and common mistakes
Adding denominators. 1/2 + 1/3 isn't 2/5.
Forgetting to flip the second fraction when dividing.
Leaving answers unreduced. 18/30 should be 3/5.
Typing a mixed number with a space; convert it first.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
Terminology: "mixed number" is standard in all three countries. US materials often say "improper fraction" for 7/4, as do UK schools, although some UK texts call it a "top-heavy fraction".
Lowest terms: US and Canadian worksheets ask you to "reduce" or "simplify"; UK ones ask you to write the fraction "in its simplest form". The calculator always gives lowest terms.
Measurements: US and Canadian tape measures and recipes still use fractions of an inch and cup (⅝ in, ¾ cup), so fraction arithmetic is everyday DIY maths there. UK recipes and building plans are almost entirely metric.

Frequently asked questions
How do I add fractions with different denominators?
Find a common denominator, convert both fractions, then add the numerators.
How do I divide fractions?
Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
How do I simplify a fraction?
Divide the top and bottom by their greatest common factor.
Can I enter mixed numbers?
Convert them to improper fractions (7/4 for 1¾).
Can I use negative fractions?
Yes, type a minus sign, such as −3/4.
Why is the decimal rounded?
Many fractions have repeating decimals; the fraction result is exact.
