H1: Blood Alcohol (BAC) Calculator
Blood alcohol concentration (BAC) is the percentage of your blood that is alcohol. This calculator estimates BAC from the number of standard drinks, your body weight and sex, and the time since you started drinking, using the Widmark formula. It's an educational estimate only: many factors affect real BAC, and no calculator can tell you whether you're safe to drive.
How to use this calculator
1. Sex: men and women distribute alcohol differently on average.
2. Units and body weight.
3. Standard drinks consumed: the calculator counts each standard drink as 14 grams of pure alcohol (the US definition).
4. Hours since first drink.
Results: estimated BAC (%), alcohol consumed in grams and a safety note.
The formula (Widmark)
BAC (%) = [Alcohol (g) ÷ (Body weight (g) × r)] × 100 − 0.015 × Hours
Alcohol: standard drinks × 14 g
r: the Widmark factor, the share of body weight in which alcohol distributes: 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women (averages)
0.015: an average elimination rate of 0.015 percentage points per hour
The result can't go below zero.
What's a standard drink (US)? About 12 fl oz of 5% beer, 5 fl oz of 12% wine, or 1.5 fl oz of 40% spirits. Many real drinks are larger: a strong craft beer or a generous pour of wine can be 1.5 to 2 standard drinks.
Worked examples
Example 1: man, 80 kg, 4 drinks over 2 hours
Alcohol = 4 × 14 = 56 g
Peak contribution = 56 ÷ (80,000 × 0.68) × 100 = 0.103%
Minus 2 × 0.015 = 0.030
Estimated BAC = 0.073%
At 0.015 an hour, it would take about 4.9 more hours to fall to zero.
In imperial units, 180 lb is about 81.6 kg.
Example 2: woman, 65 kg, 3 drinks over 1 hour
Estimated BAC = 0.102%, falling to zero in about 6.8 hours.
Understanding your results
Effects begin well below legal limits. Judgment, reaction time and coordination start to be affected at low BACs, around 0.02% to 0.05%.
Individual variation is large. Food in the stomach, drinking speed, medications, body composition, age, liver function and genetics all change real BAC. The elimination rate varies roughly from 0.010 to 0.020 per hour between people.
BAC can still be rising after you stop drinking, as alcohol is absorbed.
Only time lowers BAC. Coffee, cold showers and food after drinking don't speed elimination.
Legal limits for driving
United States: 0.08% in every state except Utah, which uses 0.05%. Commercial drivers: 0.04%. Drivers under 21: zero tolerance laws (typically 0.00% to 0.02%).
Canada: 0.08% is the federal criminal limit; most provinces impose administrative penalties from 0.05%. Many provinces require zero BAC for new and young drivers.
United Kingdom: 0.08% (80 mg per 100 ml of blood) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; 0.05% in Scotland.
Being under the limit doesn't mean you're safe or legal to drive if you're impaired. The safest choice is not to drive after drinking.
Example 3: three UK pints
A pint (568 ml) of 5% beer contains 568 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 22.4 g of alcohol, which is 1.6 US standard drinks or 2.8 UK units. For a 75 kg man who drinks three pints over three hours:
Alcohol: 3 × 22.4 = 67.2 g, so enter 4.8 standard drinks
67.2 ÷ (75,000 × 0.68) × 100 − 0.015 × 3 = 0.087%
That's above the drink-drive limit in Scotland (0.05%) and in the rest of the UK (0.08%), even though "three pints" sounds moderate.
Units of measurement for BAC
US laws express BAC as grams of alcohol per 100 ml of blood, written as a percentage (0.08%). Canadian and UK laws write the same limit as 80 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood. The UK also sets breath limits: 35 micrograms of alcohol per 100 ml of breath in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and 22 micrograms in Scotland. Roadside breath tests estimate blood alcohol from breath, so results can differ slightly from a blood test.
Standard drinks in other countries
The calculator uses the US 14 g standard drink. In Canada a standard drink is about 13.45 g of alcohol [VERIFY], and in the UK one unit is 8 g (10 ml) of alcohol; a pint of 5% beer is about 2.8 units (about 22 g). Convert to US standard drinks by dividing grams of alcohol by 14.
Health guidance
United States: the Dietary Guidelines advise that adults who choose to drink limit intake to 2 drinks or fewer a day for men and 1 or fewer for women [VERIFY current guideline wording].
Canada: Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health (2023) describes risk rising with each drink, with 2 or fewer drinks a week as low risk.
United Kingdom: the UK Chief Medical Officers advise not regularly drinking more than 14 units a week.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting glasses instead of standard drinks.
Assuming you're fine after a meal. Food slows absorption but doesn't remove alcohol.
Driving the morning after a heavy night; BAC may still be above the limit.
Mixing alcohol with medication without checking interactions.
Frequently asked questions
How accurate is a BAC calculator?
It gives a rough average estimate. Your actual BAC could be significantly higher or lower.
How long does it take for alcohol to leave the body?
On average about 0.015 percentage points of BAC per hour, so a BAC of 0.08% takes roughly 5 hours to reach zero.
What is the legal limit?
0.08% in most of the US, Canada (criminal) and England, Wales and Northern Ireland; 0.05% in Utah and Scotland.
Why do women reach higher BAC than men?
On average, women have less body water per kilogram, so alcohol is more concentrated.
Can I sober up faster?
No. Only time reduces BAC.
Is it safe to drive under the limit?
Not necessarily. Impairment begins at low levels.
