H1: Business Loan Calculator
Use this calculator to estimate the payments on a term loan for a business: equipment finance, a bank term loan, an SBA-backed loan in the US or a government-backed loan elsewhere. Enter the amount, interest rate and term. You get the monthly payment, the total you will repay, the total interest and a chart of how the balance falls over time.
How to use this calculator
1. Loan amount: the principal you will borrow. If the lender deducts a fee from the funds, still enter the full amount borrowed (see the APR note below).
2. Interest rate: the annual rate on the term sheet or offer letter. For a variable-rate loan, enter today's rate and re-run at a higher one to test affordability.
3. Loan term (years) and Loan term (months): the repayment period. Equipment loans often match the asset's useful life; working-capital loans tend to be shorter.
Results: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest paid, the rate used, and an amortization chart.
The formula
The calculator assumes a fixed-rate loan repaid in equal monthly instalments:
PMT = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
P: loan amount
r: annual interest rate ÷ 12
n: years × 12 + months
Interest each month = remaining balance × r. The rest of the payment repays principal. Total interest = PMT × n − P.
Worked examples
Example 1: $20,000 over 5 years at 7.5%
n = 60, r = 0.075 ÷ 12
PMT = $400.76
Total of payments $24,045.54; total interest $4,045.54
Example 2: $150,000 equipment loan at 9.25% for 10 years, with a 3% fee
n = 120, r = 0.0925 ÷ 12
PMT = $1,920.49 a month; total repaid $230,458.90; interest $80,458.90
If the lender deducts a 3% fee ($4,500.00) from the funds, the effective APR is about 9.97%. The APR calculator does this conversion.
Can the business afford it? Lenders often look at the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR): cash available for debt service ÷ debt payments. If the business generates $2,400 a month of operating cash flow before debt payments, DSCR = 2,400 ÷ $1,920.49 = 1.25. Many lenders want to see comfortably more than 1.0; the required level varies by lender and loan type.
Understanding your results
Monthly payment is principal and interest. It excludes fees, insurance required by the lender, and any personal guarantee costs.
Total interest is the full cost of borrowing over the term if you make every scheduled payment. Prepayment can reduce it, unless the loan has a prepayment penalty.
The chart shows the balance falling slowly at first and faster later, because early payments are mostly interest.
Other types of business finance
Not all business funding is a fixed monthly term loan, and the calculator only models that type.
Line of credit: draw and repay as needed; interest only on what you use. Payments vary.
Merchant cash advance and short-term loans: often priced with a factor rate rather than an interest rate. For example, $50,000 at a factor of 1.25 means repaying $62,500.00. Spread over 12 equal monthly payments of $5,208.33, that equals an APR of roughly 43.3%. Convert factor rates before comparing offers.
Invoice financing: borrowing against unpaid invoices, priced as a percentage of invoice value.
Asset finance and leasing: the equipment is the security; payment profiles can include a balloon.
Country notes
United States: the SBA guarantees loans made by participating lenders, including 7(a) and 504 loans, with maximum rates and fees set by SBA rules [VERIFY current SBA maximum rates and guarantee fees on sba.gov]. Business loan interest is generally deductible as a business expense, subject to the business interest limitation for larger businesses (section 163(j), reported on IRS Form 8990).
Canada: the Canada Small Business Financing Program shares lending risk with banks for loans to buy equipment, property and improvements; BDC is a federal Crown corporation lending to small businesses.
United Kingdom: the British Business Bank runs government-backed schemes delivered through accredited lenders, such as the Growth Guarantee Scheme [VERIFY scheme name and status]. Business loans are not covered by the Consumer Credit Act in most cases, so protections are fewer than for personal loans.
Tips and common mistakes
Match the term to the asset. Don't finance a short-lived asset over a long term.
Ask for the APR or total cost of credit, not just the rate, especially with fees or factor rates.
Check prepayment terms and personal guarantees before you sign.
Stress-test the payment. Re-run with a rate two or three points higher, and with lower revenue.
Keep the cash buffer. Paying a large down payment can leave the business short of working capital.
Frequently asked questions
What interest rate should I expect on a business loan?
It depends on the lender, the business's trading history, credit and collateral, and the type of loan. Get several quotes and compare APRs.
Does the result include fees?
No. Enter fees in the APR calculator to see their effect on the true cost.
Can I model a variable-rate loan?
Only one rate at a time. Run the calculator at the current rate and at higher rates to see a range.
How do I convert a factor rate to an APR?
Multiply the amount by the factor rate to get total repayment, then use the APR calculator with the actual payment schedule. Factor-rate products often have very high APRs because the term is short.
Is business loan interest tax-deductible?
Interest on money used for business purposes is usually deductible against business income in the US, Canada and the UK, subject to each country's rules. Check with an accountant.
What is a balloon payment?
A large final payment that repays the remaining balance. This calculator assumes a fully amortizing loan with no balloon.
