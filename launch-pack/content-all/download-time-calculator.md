H1: Download Time Calculator
How long will a file take to download (or upload)? Enter its size and your connection speed and this calculator gives the time. It's handy for planning large game downloads, video uploads, cloud backups or file transfers before a deadline.
How to use this calculator
1. File size (MB): in megabytes (default 850). For gigabytes, multiply by 1,000 (a 50 GB game = 50,000 MB).
2. Download speed (Mbps): megabits per second, the figure your provider advertises or a speed test reports (default 100).
Results: download time in minutes and seconds, and the same in seconds.
The formula
time (seconds) = file size (MB) × 8 ÷ speed (Mbps)
The × 8 converts bytes (B, used for file sizes) to bits (b, used for connection speeds). The calculator uses decimal megabytes (1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes) and assumes the full speed is available.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
850 MB × 8 = 6,800 megabits ÷ 100 Mbps = 68 seconds (1 min 8 s)
Example 2: a 50 GB game
50,000 MB × 8 ÷ 100 Mbps = 4,000 s = 66.7 minutes. At a more realistic 90 Mbps actual throughput: 74.1 minutes.
Example 3: streaming-quality video
A 7 GB 4K film on a 25 Mbps connection: 37.3 minutes.
Example 4: uploading
Upload speeds are often much lower. A 2 GB video upload at 20 Mbps takes about 13.3 minutes. Enter your upload speed in the speed box.
Bits, bytes and units
Unit
Meaning
b (bit)
Smallest unit; speeds use bits
B (byte)
8 bits; file sizes use bytes
Mbps
Megabits per second
MB/s
Megabytes per second = Mbps ÷ 8
1 GB
1,000 MB (decimal)
1 GiB
1,024 MiB (binary), about 1,073.74 decimal MB
Steam and some browsers show download rates in MB/s: 12.5 MB/s equals 100 Mbps. Operating systems may report file sizes in binary units (Windows labels GiB as "GB"), so a file can look about 7% bigger or smaller depending on the tool.
Why real downloads are slower
Protocol overhead: TCP/IP and encryption use some bandwidth, often 5–10%.
Wi-Fi: walls, distance and interference cut speeds well below your plan.
Server limits: the source may cap speed per user.
Shared connections: other devices streaming or updating.
Peak times: busier networks in the evening.
Run a speed test first and use the measured figure for a better estimate.
Typical connection speeds
Broadband plans range from under 10 Mbps on older DSL to 1,000 Mbps (1 Gbps) or more on fibre. The US FCC's benchmark for broadband is 100 Mbps download and 20 Mbps upload (set in 2024), and Ofcom defines UK "superfast" broadband as at least 30 Mbps [VERIFY current definitions].
Tips and common mistakes
Confusing MB and Mb. A 100 Mbps connection downloads 12.5 MB per second, not 100.
Entering GB as MB. 5 GB is 5,000 MB.
Using the advertised "up to" speed; measured speeds are more realistic.
Forgetting upload speed for backups and video uploads.
Frequently asked questions
How long does 1 GB take at 100 Mbps?
About 80 seconds (1,000 × 8 ÷ 100).
What's the difference between Mbps and MB/s?
Mbps is megabits; MB/s is megabytes. Divide Mbps by 8 to get MB/s.
Why is my download slower than the estimate?
Overhead, Wi-Fi, server limits and other users on your network.
Can I use this for uploads?
Yes, enter your upload speed.
Does it use 1,000 or 1,024?
Decimal units (1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes).
How do I convert GB to MB?
Multiply by 1,000.
