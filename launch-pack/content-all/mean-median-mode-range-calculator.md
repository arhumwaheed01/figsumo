H1: Mean, Median, Mode & Range Calculator
Mean, median, mode and range are the four quickest ways to summarise a list of numbers. Three describe the "centre" of the data and one describes its spread. Paste in your numbers and this calculator returns all four, plus the minimum, maximum and count.
How to use this calculator
1. Numbers (comma-separated): for example 8, 3, 5, 3, 9, 3. Decimals and negative numbers are fine.
Results: mean, median, mode, range, and a table with minimum, maximum and count.
Definitions and formulas
Mean (average): the sum divided by the count. x̄ = Σx ÷ n
Median: the middle value when the data are sorted. With an even count, it's the average of the two middle values.
Mode: the value(s) that occur most often. If several values tie for most frequent, all are shown; if no value repeats, there's no mode (shown as "—").
Range: maximum − minimum.
Worked examples
Example 1: 8, 3, 5, 3, 9, 3
Sorted: 3, 3, 3, 5, 8, 9 (count 6)
Mean = 31 ÷ 6 = 5.166667
Median = (3 + 5) ÷ 2 = 4 (average of the 3rd and 4th values)
Mode = 3 (appears three times)
Range = 9 − 3 = 6
Example 2: two modes
2, 4, 4, 6, 6: 4 and 6 each appear twice, so the data are bimodal and the calculator lists 4, 6. Mean 4.4, median 4, range 4.
Example 3: why the median matters
Salaries of five people: $52,000, $48,000, $61,000, $55,000 and $250,000.
Mean: $93,200.00
Median: $55,000.00
One high salary pulls the mean far above what most people earn; the median better describes the typical person. This is why income statistics usually report medians.
Which measure should you use?
Situation
Best measure
Symmetric data, no outliers (test scores, heights)
Mean
Skewed data or outliers (incomes, house prices)
Median
Categories or most popular choice (shoe sizes, survey answers)
Mode
Quick sense of spread
Range
The range is sensitive to a single extreme value. For a more robust measure of spread, use the standard deviation or interquartile range; see the standard deviation calculator and statistics calculator.
Understanding your results
Mean and median equal suggests roughly symmetric data.
Mean above median suggests a right skew (a long tail of high values); below suggests a left skew.
Rounding: results are shown to six decimals.
Count confirms the calculator read every number you entered.
Practice problem
Quiz scores 7, 8, 10, 7: mean 8.00, median 7.5, mode 7, range 3.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting to sort before finding the median.
Taking the middle of an even list as one value; average the two middle numbers.
Saying "the mode" when there are several or none.
Typing separators other than commas, such as semicolons.
Including blank entries or units (like "kg") in the list.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
UK GCSE often calls the mean "the average" and asks for it from a frequency table. US and Canadian courses use "mean", "median", "mode" and "range" in the same way.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find the median of an even set of numbers?
Average the two middle values after sorting.
Can there be more than one mode?
Yes. Data can be bimodal or multimodal, and the calculator lists them all.
What if no number repeats?
There's no mode; the calculator shows "—".
Is average the same as mean?
In everyday use, yes. "Average" can also mean median or mode in some contexts; see the average calculator for weighted averages.
What does the range tell me?
How spread out the data are, from smallest to largest.
Can I use negative numbers?
Yes.
