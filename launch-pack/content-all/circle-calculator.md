H1: Circle Calculator
A circle is every point at the same distance (the radius) from a centre. Enter the radius and this calculator returns the circle's area, circumference and diameter, rounded to four decimal places.
How to use this calculator
1. Radius: the distance from the centre to the edge, in any unit. If you know the diameter, halve it first.
Results: area (in square units), circumference (in the same unit as the radius) and diameter.
The formulas
Diameter       d = 2r
Circumference  C = 2πr = πd
Area           A = πr²
r: radius
π (pi): the ratio of any circle's circumference to its diameter, about 3.14159265. It's irrational, so its decimals never end or repeat.
Working backwards:
From area: r = √(A ÷ π)
From circumference: r = C ÷ (2π)
From diameter: r = d ÷ 2
Worked examples
Example 1: radius 5
Area = π × 5² = 78.5398 square units
Circumference = 2π × 5 = 31.4159
Diameter = 10
Example 2: a 12-inch pipe opening
Diameter 12 in, so r = 6 in.
Area = π × 36 = 113.10 in²
Circumference = π × 12 = 37.70 in
Example 3: finding the radius from the area
A circular patio must cover 50 m².
r = √(50 ÷ π) = 3.9894 m, so the diameter is 7.9788 m.
Example 4: from the circumference
A tree trunk measures 100 cm around.
r = 100 ÷ 2π = 15.9155 cm; cross-section area ≈ 795.77 cm²
Example 5: is a 16-inch pizza twice a 14-inch one?
14-inch: π × 7² = 153.94 in²
16-inch: π × 8² = 201.06 in²
The 16-inch has 1.31 times the area, not twice. Area grows with the square of the radius.
Understanding your results
Units: area is in square units. A radius in feet gives an area in square feet.
Rounding: results show four decimal places; round to suit your measurement precision.
Doubling the radius doubles the circumference but quadruples the area.
Parts of a circle
Chord: a straight line joining two points on the circle; the diameter is the longest chord.
Arc: part of the circumference. Arc length = 2πr × (angle ÷ 360°). For r = 5 and 60°, that's 5.2360.
Sector: a "pizza slice". Sector area = πr² × (angle ÷ 360°). For r = 5 and 60°, 13.0900.
Tangent: a line touching the circle at one point, at right angles to the radius there.
Practical uses
Material for round tables, rugs, patios and garden beds (area).
Edging, fencing or trim around a circle (circumference).
Pipe and duct flow areas.
Wheel travel: one turn moves a wheel forward by its circumference. For 3D shapes like cylinders and spheres, see the volume calculator and surface area calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the diameter as the radius, which makes the area four times too large.
Using 3.14 for precise work. It's fine for rough estimates; the calculator uses full precision.
Mixing units. Convert everything to one unit first.
Forgetting to square the radius in the area formula.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US and Canadian classes use "circumference" and "area"; UK GCSE uses the same words but often asks for answers "in terms of π" (such as 56.25π). Pizza and cake-tin sizes are quoted by diameter in inches in the US and by diameter in centimetres in the UK.
Check: a circle of radius 7.5 has area π × 7.5² = 176.71 (56.25π) and circumference 2π × 7.5 = 47.12 (15π).
Frequently asked questions
What is the formula for the area of a circle?
A = πr².
How do I find the circumference?
C = 2πr, or π times the diameter.
How do I get the radius from the area?
Divide the area by π and take the square root.
What is π?
The ratio of circumference to diameter, approximately 3.14159.
Can I enter the diameter?
Enter half of it as the radius.
Why is the area in square units?
Area measures a two-dimensional surface.
