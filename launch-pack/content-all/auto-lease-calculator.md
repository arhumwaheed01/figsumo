H1: Auto Lease Calculator
This calculator estimates the monthly payment on a car lease from the negotiated price, the residual value, the money factor, the term and any down payment. It splits the payment into its two parts, depreciation and the rent (finance) charge, so you can see what you are paying for and check a dealer's quote.
How to use this calculator
1. MSRP / capitalized cost: the price the lease is based on. Use the negotiated selling price, not the sticker price, plus any fees rolled into the lease. It appears on the lease worksheet as "gross capitalized cost".
2. Residual value: what the leasing company expects the car to be worth at the end of the lease, in dollars. It is usually quoted as a percentage of MSRP; multiply to get the dollar figure.
3. Money factor: the lease's interest charge in decimal form, for example 0.00125. Ask the dealer for it; it is not always printed on the contract.
4. Lease term (months): commonly 24, 36 or 39 months.
5. Down payment: cash, rebates or trade-in equity that reduce the capitalized cost.
The results show the estimated monthly lease payment, the implied APR (money factor × 2,400) and the total of monthly payments.
The formula
A lease payment (before tax) has two parts:
Net cap cost   = Capitalized cost − Down payment
Depreciation   = (Net cap cost − Residual) ÷ Term
Rent charge    = (Net cap cost + Residual) × Money factor
Payment        = Depreciation + Rent charge
Depreciation is the part of the car's value you use up, spread evenly over the term.
Rent charge is the finance cost. Adding net cap cost and residual and multiplying by the money factor is the standard shortcut for the average balance times a monthly rate.
Money factor ≈ APR ÷ 2,400. So a money factor of 0.00125 is roughly a 3% APR.
Worked examples
Example 1: $38,000 car, $22,000 residual, 36 months, $2,000 down
Net cap cost = 38,000 − 2,000 = $36,000
Depreciation = (36,000 − 22,000) ÷ 36 = $388.89
Rent charge = (36,000 + 22,000) × 0.00125 = $72.50
Payment = $461.39 a month before tax
Implied APR: 0.00125 × 2,400 = 3.00%
36 payments total $16,610.00; with the $2,000 down payment, $18,610.00 for three years of use
Example 2: $45,000 car, 55% residual, 39 months, money factor 0.0021, nothing down
Residual = 55% × 45,000 = $24,750
Depreciation = (45,000 − 24,750) ÷ 39 = $519.23
Rent charge = (45,000 + 24,750) × 0.0021 = $146.47
Payment = $665.71 before tax (implied APR 5.04%)
With an 8% sales tax on each payment (a common US approach), add $53.26 for a total of $718.96
Understanding your results
Monthly payment excludes sales tax, registration and insurance. Add tax at your local rate.
Implied APR helps you compare the lease's finance cost with a loan. A dealer quoting a payment without the money factor can hide a high rate inside it.
Total of monthly payments is what you pay for the use of the car, excluding the down payment and fees. At the end you return the car, or buy it for the residual value.
A high residual lowers the payment because you use up less value. A low money factor lowers the rent charge.
Lease vs buy
Leasing usually means lower monthly payments than a loan on the same car, because you only pay for the depreciation during the term plus the rent charge. But you own nothing at the end, and mileage limits and wear charges apply. If you keep cars for many years, buying (see the auto loan calculator) usually costs less overall. Leasing can suit drivers who want a new car every few years and drive a predictable number of miles.
Ending a lease early is often expensive: you may owe the remaining payments or the difference between the payoff amount and the car's value, plus an early termination fee. Read the early-termination section of the contract before signing.
Country notes
United States: most states tax each monthly payment rather than the full price; a few tax the full cost up front [VERIFY your state's rule with its revenue department]. The CFPB and FTC publish consumer guides to vehicle leasing, and federal Regulation M requires lease disclosures.
Canada: GST/HST (and PST/QST where applicable) is charged on lease payments. Leases often quote a "lease rate" as an annual percentage instead of a money factor; divide by 2,400 to convert.
United Kingdom: the closest products are personal contract hire (PCH), where you never own the car, and PCP finance, which has an optional final "balloon" payment. UK PCH prices usually include VAT. Use this calculator as a guide only; UK quotes often state an initial rental of several months' payments.
Tips and common mistakes
Negotiate the price, not the payment. The capitalized cost is negotiable just like a purchase price.
Check the money factor against the lender's base rate for your credit tier.
Watch for rolled-in extras. Acquisition fees, add-on products and negative equity raise the cap cost.
Pick a realistic mileage allowance. Excess-mile charges (often quoted per mile) can cost more than a higher allowance.
Large down payments are risky. If the car is written off early, the down payment is usually lost; gap coverage typically protects the lender's balance, not your cash.
Frequently asked questions
What is a money factor?
The lease equivalent of an interest rate, written as a small decimal. Multiply it by 2,400 to get an approximate APR.
How do I find the residual value?
The leasing company sets it, usually as a percentage of MSRP for a given term and mileage. Ask the dealer, then multiply by the MSRP to enter it in dollars.
Does a down payment lower the payment?
Yes. It reduces the net capitalized cost, which lowers both the depreciation and the rent charge.
Does the result include sales tax?
No. Multiply the payment by your local tax rate and add it, or check how your state or province taxes leases.
Why is my dealer's payment higher?
Common reasons: a higher cap cost (fees or add-ons), a higher money factor, sales tax, or a lower residual than you assumed.
Can I buy the car at the end?
Most leases include a purchase option at the residual value plus any purchase fee. Compare it with the car's market value at the time.
