H1: TDEE Calculator
Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories you burn in a day, including everything from breathing to exercise. Eat about this many calories and your weight should stay roughly the same. This calculator estimates your TDEE from your age, sex, height, weight and activity level, and shows calorie targets for gradual weight loss and gain.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric or imperial.
2. Age, sex, weight and height.
3. Activity level:
Sedentary: little or no exercise (× 1.2)
Light: exercise 1–3 days a week (× 1.375)
Moderate: 3–5 days a week (× 1.55)
Active: 6–7 days a week (× 1.725)
Very active: hard daily exercise or a physical job (× 1.9)
Results: BMR, TDEE (maintenance), weight-loss target (TDEE − 500) and weight-gain target (TDEE + 500), with a chart splitting BMR and activity.
How it works
BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor):
Men:   BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5
Women: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161
W = weight in kg, H = height in cm, A = age in years.
TDEE = BMR × activity factor.
TDEE has four parts: BMR (usually 60–70% of the total), the thermic effect of food (about 10%), exercise, and non-exercise activity such as walking, standing and fidgeting (NEAT). The activity factor bundles the last three.
Worked examples
Example 1: man, 30, 80 kg, 180 cm, moderate activity
BMR = 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5 = 1,780 kcal
TDEE = 1,780 × 1.55 = 2,759 kcal
Activity adds 979 kcal
Weight loss (−500): 2,259 kcal; weight gain (+500): 3,259 kcal

Example 4: how TDEE falls as weight drops
If the man in Example 1 loses 10 kg, his BMR falls by 10 × 10 = 100 kcal to 1,680 kcal, and his moderate-activity TDEE falls to 2,604 kcal, 155 kcal less than before. A 2,259 kcal target that was 500 below maintenance at 80 kg is only 345 below at 70 kg, which is one reason weight loss slows. Recalculate as your weight changes.
Understanding your results
It's an estimate. Mifflin-St Jeor predicts resting energy within about 10% for most adults, and activity factors add more uncertainty.
Test and adjust. Eat at your TDEE for two to three weeks and track your weight. If it's rising, your real TDEE is lower; if it's falling, it's higher.
500 kcal a day below TDEE is roughly 0.45 kg (about 1 lb) a week using the old 7,700 kcal per kg rule. In practice, weight loss slows over time as your body adapts and you get lighter; the NIH Body Weight Planner models this.
Don't go too low. Very low-calorie diets (under about 800 kcal a day) should only be followed under medical supervision. Many guidelines suggest not going below about 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without advice.
Weight gain: a surplus of 250–500 kcal with resistance training favours muscle over fat.
Choosing an activity level
Most people overestimate their activity. An office worker who goes to the gym three times a week is usually "light" to "moderate", not "active". Fitness trackers can help, but their calorie estimates can be off by 20% or more.
Country notes
US: Dietary Guidelines for Americans list estimated calorie needs from about 1,600 to 2,400 for adult women and 2,000 to 3,000 for adult men, depending on age and activity.
UK: reference intakes on labels are based on 2,000 kcal for women and 2,500 kcal for men.
Canada: Health Canada uses the same Dietary Reference Intakes (energy equations) as the US.
From TDEE to macros
Once you've chosen a calorie target, you can split it between protein, carbohydrate and fat. For 2,259 kcal at 30% protein, 40% carbohydrate and 30% fat:
Protein: 2,259 × 0.30 ÷ 4 = 169 g
Carbohydrate: 2,259 × 0.40 ÷ 4 = 226 g
Fat: 2,259 × 0.30 ÷ 9 = 75 g
The macro calculator does this split for you with other ratios.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting exercise twice: choosing a high activity level and also adding workout calories from a tracker.
Using outdated weight. Recalculate after every 5 kg change.
Expecting linear weight loss. Water and glycogen cause day-to-day swings; look at weekly averages.
Frequently asked questions
What is TDEE?
Total daily energy expenditure: your BMR plus calories from digestion and activity.
What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?
BMR is energy at complete rest; TDEE includes everything you do in a day. See the BMR calculator.
How do I lose weight with TDEE?
Eat below it; about 500 kcal less a day is a common starting point.
How accurate is TDEE?
It's an estimate; adjust based on your weight trend over several weeks.
Which BMR formula does this use?
Mifflin-St Jeor, recommended by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics for healthy adults.
Should I eat back exercise calories?
Not if you've already chosen an activity level that includes your exercise.
Does TDEE change with age?
Yes; BMR falls by about 5 kcal a day per year of age in the formula, and muscle loss with age lowers it further.
