H1: Age Calculator
How old are you, exactly? This calculator gives your age in completed years, as years, months and days, and as a total count of days, between your date of birth and any other date. Use it for forms, eligibility checks, milestones or simply curiosity.
How to use this calculator
1. Date of birth: pick or type the date (YYYY-MM-DD).
2. Calculate age as of: the date you want the age on. It's set to 1 January 2026 by default; change it to today, a future date (how old will I be on…?) or a past one.
Results: age in years, years, months and days, and total days, with a breakdown table.
The as-of date must be on or after the birth date.
How age is calculated
Age is counted in completed units, the way it's used legally and on official forms:
1. Years: the number of full birthdays reached by the as-of date.
2. Months: full months since the last birthday.
3. Days: remaining days since the last "monthiversary".
4. Total days: the calendar difference between the two dates, including every leap day in between.
Because months have 28 to 31 days, the months-and-days figure depends on where the count starts; that's why two tools can differ by a day in edge cases, while total days always agree.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
Born 15 June 1990, age on 1 January 2026:
Last birthday: 15 June 2025 → 35 years
15 June to 15 December = 6 months; 15 December to 1 January = 17 days
35 years, 6 months, 17 days, or 12,984 days in total (1,854 weeks, about 311,616 hours)
Example 2: a different as-of date
Born 10 March 1985, age on 15 October 2026: 41 years, 7 months, 5 days, or 15,194 days.
Example 3: "How old will I be?"
Want to know your age on a retirement date or at a child's graduation? Enter that future date as the as-of date.
Age rules worth knowing
Birthdays on 29 February: in non-leap years, many jurisdictions treat the legal birthday as 28 February or 1 March; the rule varies by country and purpose [VERIFY for your jurisdiction].
"Age next birthday", used by some insurers, is your completed age plus one.
East Asian age reckoning, where a baby is one at birth, is different: South Korea standardised on international age for legal purposes in June 2023.
Full retirement age for US Social Security, UK State Pension age and CPP eligibility all depend on exact birth date; see the retirement calculator.
Common uses
Checking age limits for school entry, driving, voting or alcohol.
Pension and benefits eligibility.
Insurance quotes and travel visas.
Calculating a pet's or a building's age.
Counting days for a milestone such as 10,000 days old (about 27 years and 4 months).
Understanding your results
Total days counts the as-of date but not the birth date (the same as subtracting dates).
Time zones and birth times are ignored; the calculation is by calendar date.
For a plain day count between any two dates, use the day counter; to add or subtract days, use the date calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Leaving the as-of date at its default when you want today's age.
Typing dates in the wrong order (MM/DD vs DD/MM); the picker uses year-month-day.
Rounding up: someone who is 17 years and 11 months is 17, not 18.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate my exact age?
Enter your birth date and today's date.
How many days old am I?
The "total days" result shows it.
Does it account for leap years?
Yes; every 29 February between the dates is counted.
Can I calculate age on a future date?
Yes. Set the as-of date in the future.
Why does my age in months and days differ from another site?
Months have different lengths, so methods vary at month ends. The years and total days should match.
What if I was born on 29 February?
The calculator uses calendar arithmetic; check local rules for legal purposes.
