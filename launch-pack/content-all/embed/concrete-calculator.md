What the calculator does
Visitors enter the length and width of a slab in feet and its depth in inches. The tool returns the volume in cubic yards and cubic feet:
Volume (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12
Volume (yd³) = ft³ ÷ 27
Ready-mix suppliers in the US and Canada sell by the cubic yard, so the cubic-yard figure is the one to quote when ordering.
Examples
A 12 × 10 ft patio, 4 in deep: 40.00 ft³ = 1.48 yd³. With a 10% allowance for spillage and uneven ground, order about 1.63 yd³.
A 20 × 20 ft garage slab, 6 in deep: 200.00 ft³ = 7.41 yd³.

Depths are common practice, not engineering advice; local building codes and the ground conditions decide what's needed. Pointing visitors to these typical depths alongside the widget helps them enter realistic numbers and reduces under-ordering, which is the costliest mistake on a pour day.

Tips for site owners
Explain the allowance. Visitors often order exactly the calculated volume; most contractors add 5–10% for waste, over-excavation and spillage. A short note under the widget helps.
Mention minimum loads. Many ready-mix suppliers have minimum orders or short-load fees for small volumes; bagged concrete may be cheaper for under about 1 yd³.
Bag counts: an 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 ft³ [VERIFY: yield varies by brand], so the 12 × 10 ft example would need roughly 67 bags.
Metric visitors: the widget currently uses feet and inches; UK and other metric users will need a metric version (in development).
