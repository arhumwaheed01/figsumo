H1: Free Love Calculator Widget
A love calculator is a light-hearted game: type two names and get a "compatibility" percentage. It's popular on entertainment blogs, wedding and dating sites, school fair pages and Valentine's Day posts. This generator gives you the code to add FigSumo's love calculator to your own site, free.
How to use the embed generator
1. First name and second name: try any two names to preview the score.
2. Embed width (px): 280 to 720 (default 400).
3. Copy the embed code:
<iframe src="https://figsumo.com/love-calculator" width="400" height="420" style="border:0;border-radius:8px" loading="lazy" title="Love calculator"></iframe>
With the sample names Alex and Jordan, the preview shows 28%.
How the score is made
There's no science here. The calculator:
1. Joins the two names, lower-cases them and removes spaces.
2. Turns the letters into a number with a simple hash (a repeatable scrambling formula).
3. Maps that number to a score between 13% and 98%.
So the same pair always gets the same score, capital letters don't matter ("alex" and "JORDAN" also give 28%), and nothing is random. The order of names does change the result in the current version: Jordan first and Alex second gives 60%.
Be clear it's a game
A compatibility percentage from two names has no meaning for real relationships. If you embed it, label it as entertainment, for example "Just for fun!", especially on sites with younger visitors. It works best as an icebreaker, a party game or a social media prompt.

Privacy
Names are processed in the visitor's browser to calculate the score. Even so, remind visitors not to enter anything they wouldn't want to share, and see FigSumo's privacy policy for the embedded page.
Notes for US, Canada and UK site owners
If children are likely to use your site, privacy rules apply in each country: COPPA in the US, PIPEDA and provincial privacy laws in Canada, and the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code (the Children's Code) in the UK. The love calculator doesn't ask for anything except two names, but your own page may collect other data through comments, analytics or ads. Review those settings and your cookie notice before featuring a game aimed at younger visitors.
Testing before you publish
1. Paste the code on a draft page and type a couple of name pairs to see the score appear.
2. Check the widget at your column width on a phone; 300–320 px fits most mobile layouts.
3. Add a short caption above it, such as "Just for fun: the score comes from the letters in the names".
4. Confirm your site's content security policy allows frames from figsumo.com.
Tips and common mistakes
Placing it without a label. Tell visitors it's just for fun.
Making it too narrow. Below 280 px the inputs become cramped.
Forgetting mobile. Check it on a phone before publishing.
Frequently asked questions
Is the love calculator accurate?
No. It's a game, and the score comes from a formula applied to letters, not from anything about the people.
Why does the same pair always get the same score?
The formula is fixed, so identical inputs always produce the same result.
Does swapping the names change the score?
Currently, yes. A future update will make the order irrelevant.
Is the widget free?
Yes, there's no fee or sign-up.
Can I change the score range?
No, scores always fall between 13% and 98%.
Is it suitable for children?
The content is harmless, but label it as just for fun.
