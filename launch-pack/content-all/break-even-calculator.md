H1: Break-Even Calculator
The break-even point is the sales level at which revenue exactly covers costs, so you make neither a profit nor a loss. Knowing it tells you how many units you must sell before a product, shop or project starts to make money, and how sensitive that is to price and costs. This calculator works out break-even units and revenue, the sales needed for a target profit, and your margin of safety.
How to use this calculator
1. Fixed costs: costs that don't change with sales volume over the period, such as rent, salaries, insurance, software and loan payments. Use one period consistently (a month or a year).
2. Price per unit: what you charge, excluding sales tax or VAT.
3. Variable cost per unit: costs that rise with each sale, such as materials, packaging, shipping, card fees and sales commission.
4. Target profit (optional): the profit you want for the period.
5. Expected sales (optional): to calculate the margin of safety.
Results: contribution margin per unit, contribution margin ratio, break-even units, break-even revenue, units for target profit and margin of safety.
The formulas
Contribution margin (CM) = Price − Variable cost per unit
CM ratio = CM ÷ Price
Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ CM
Break-even revenue = Fixed costs ÷ CM ratio
Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ CM
Margin of safety = (Expected sales − Break-even sales) ÷ Expected sales
Round break-even units up to the next whole unit: selling a fraction of a unit isn't possible.
Worked examples
Example 1: a small product business
Fixed costs $12,000 a month; price $50; variable cost $20.
CM = $30 a unit; CM ratio 60%
Break-even = 12,000 ÷ 30 = 400 units a month
Break-even revenue = $20,000.00
To make $6,000 profit: (12,000 + 6,000) ÷ 30 = 600 units
If you expect to sell 600 units, the margin of safety is 200 units, or 33.33%: sales could fall that much before you make a loss.
Example 2: what if price or costs change?
Cut the price to $45: CM falls to $25 and break-even rises to 480.00, so 480 units.
Variable cost rises to $24: CM $26, break-even 461.54, so 462 units.
A small change in contribution margin moves the break-even point a lot. That's why discounting can be expensive.
Example 3: service business using revenue
A consultancy has $12,000 of monthly fixed costs and a 60% contribution margin ratio (40% of each sale goes on direct costs). Break-even revenue = 12,000 ÷ 0.6 = $20,000.00 a month.
Operating leverage
Above break-even, every extra unit adds its full contribution margin to profit. At 600 units, Example 1 makes $6,000.00; at 660 units (10% more), it makes $7,800.00, a 30% jump in profit from a 10% rise in sales. High fixed costs magnify both gains and losses.
Understanding your results
Break-even is a planning tool. It assumes price and variable cost per unit stay constant, which may not hold with volume discounts or price changes.
Multiple products: use a weighted-average contribution margin based on your sales mix.
Cash vs profit: break-even here is accounting profit. Cash flow also depends on timing of payments, stock purchases and loan principal.
Taxes: the target profit is before income tax. To hit an after-tax target, divide it by (1 − tax rate) first.
Choosing what's fixed and what's variable
Some costs are mixed. A phone plan with a base fee plus usage, or staff paid a salary plus overtime, have fixed and variable parts. Split them as best you can. Over a long enough horizon, most costs become variable, so match the period to the decision you're making.
Tips and common mistakes
Leaving card fees, shipping or commission out of variable costs.
Mixing periods, such as annual fixed costs with monthly unit sales.
Using a price that includes VAT or sales tax.
Forgetting the owner's pay in fixed costs, which makes the business look profitable when it isn't.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate the break-even point?
Divide fixed costs by the contribution margin per unit (price minus variable cost).
What is contribution margin?
The amount each sale contributes toward fixed costs and profit: price minus variable cost.
What is break-even revenue?
The sales value needed to cover all costs: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio.
What if my variable cost is higher than my price?
You can never break even; each sale increases the loss. Raise the price or cut costs.
What is margin of safety?
How far sales can fall before you reach break-even, as a percentage of expected sales.
How is break-even different from payback period?
Break-even is about sales volume in a period; payback is about how long it takes to recover an upfront investment. See the payback period calculator.
