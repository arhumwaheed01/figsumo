H1: Roofing Calculator
Roofing materials are sold by the "square", which is 100 square feet of roof surface. A roof's real surface is larger than the footprint of the house because it slopes. This calculator adjusts the footprint for pitch, adds a waste allowance, and tells you the area and the number of squares to order.
How to use this calculator
1. Roof length (ft) and roof width (ft): the horizontal footprint the roof covers, including overhangs (default 40 × 30).
2. Pitch rise (in per 12 in run): for example 6 for a 6/12 roof (default 6).
3. Waste factor (%): extra for cuts, starter rows, ridges and mistakes (default 10).
Results: roof area with waste (ft²) and roofing squares, plus a chart splitting the sloped surface and the waste allowance.
The formulas
pitch factor = √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²)
sloped area = length × width × pitch factor
area with waste = sloped area × (1 + waste ÷ 100)
squares = area with waste ÷ 100
The pitch factor comes from the Pythagorean theorem: for each 12 in of horizontal run, the roof surface runs along the hypotenuse of a 12-by-rise triangle.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
Pitch factor for 6/12 = √1.25 = 1.1180
Sloped area = 40 × 30 × 1.1180 = 1,341.64 ft²
With 10% waste = 1,476 ft² = 14.76 squares
Asphalt shingles commonly come 3 bundles per square: about 45 bundles
Example 2: steeper roof, with overhangs
A 42 × 32 ft house with 1 ft overhangs all round (44 × 34 ft footprint), 8/12 pitch (factor 1.2019), 15% waste for a hip roof: 1,798 ft² sloped, 2,068 ft² with waste, 20.68 squares.
Example 3: metric pitch
A 10 m × 8 m footprint at a 35° pitch, common in the UK: factor = 1 ÷ cos 35° = 1.2208, so about 97.7 m² of roof.

The formula works for any roof with a single pitch throughout, whether gable or hip, because every face shares the same slope. For roofs with sections at different pitches, calculate each section separately and add.
Waste allowance
Simple gable: about 10%.
Hip roofs, valleys, dormers: 15–20%.
Patterned or architectural shingles: follow the manufacturer's guidance.
What else to order
Underlayment: synthetic rolls often cover 4–10 squares each [VERIFY product].
Starter strip, ridge cap and drip edge: measured in linear feet along eaves, rakes and ridges.
Ice and water shield: many US and Canadian codes require it along eaves in cold climates.
Nails: typically 4–6 per shingle; high-wind areas require more.
Understanding your results
Measure the footprint including overhangs; building plans or aerial measurements help.
The chart is area, not cost.
Local codes (the IRC in the US, the National Building Code of Canada, UK Building Regulations) set minimum slopes for each roofing material.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the house's floor area without overhangs.
Ignoring pitch: a 12/12 roof has 41% more surface than its footprint.
Under-ordering waste on complex roofs.
Working on a roof without fall protection. Steep or high roofs are a job for professionals.
Frequently asked questions
What is a roofing square?
100 square feet of roof surface.
How do I calculate roof area from pitch?
Multiply the footprint by √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²).
How many bundles are in a square?
Usually 3 for standard asphalt shingles.
How much waste should I add?
10% for simple roofs; 15–20% for complex ones.
What does 6/12 pitch mean?
The roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run.
How do I measure pitch?
Hold a level 12 in horizontally against a rafter and measure the vertical rise.
