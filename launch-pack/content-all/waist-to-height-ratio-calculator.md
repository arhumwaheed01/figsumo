H1: Waist-to-Height Ratio Calculator
Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) compares the size of your waist with your height. It's a simple way to check for excess fat around the middle (central or abdominal fat), which is linked to type 2 diabetes, heart disease and high blood pressure. The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends it alongside BMI, with one easy rule: keep your waist to less than half your height.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: centimetres or inches.
2. Waist: measured as described below.
3. Height: without shoes.
Results: your waist-to-height ratio, its health risk category, and the waist size that would equal half your height.
How to measure your waist
NICE and the NHS describe the method:
1. Find the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips.
2. Wrap a tape measure around your middle at a point halfway between them (for many people, this is just above the belly button).
3. Breathe out naturally and measure. Don't pull the tape tight or hold your stomach in.
NICE's own worked example: a 38 in waist and a 67 in height (5 ft 7 in) give 0.57.
Use the same tape and the same point each time to track change.
The formula
WHtR = waist ÷ height
Both measurements must be in the same unit, so the result has no units and is the same in centimetres or inches.

Worked examples
Example 1: waist 84 cm, height 175 cm
84 ÷ 175 = 0.48 → healthy
Half of height: 87.5 cm, so this waist is comfortably below it.
Example 2: waist 38 in, height 64 in (5 ft 4 in)
38 ÷ 64 = 0.59 → increased health risk
Half of height is 32 in, so reducing the waist by 6 in would bring the ratio to 0.5.
Example 3: waist 101 cm, height 162 cm
0.62 → high health risk
Example 4: man, 6 ft (72 in), waist 40 in
40 ÷ 72 = 0.56 → increased health risk
Half of height is 36 in, so a 4 in smaller waist would bring the ratio to 0.5.
Example 5: tracking a change
A woman 168 cm tall reduces her waist from 92 cm to 86 cm. Her ratio falls from 0.55 to 0.51, moving from the increased-risk band into the healthy band.

Why waist-to-height ratio?
It accounts for height. A 90 cm waist means something different on someone 160 cm tall than on someone 190 cm tall.
It detects central fat that BMI misses. People with a "healthy" BMI can still carry risky abdominal fat.
One cut-off for everyone. Unlike waist circumference alone, the 0.5 threshold works across sexes and ethnic groups in research.
Predicts risk well. Studies have found WHtR predicts cardiometabolic risk at least as well as BMI or waist size alone.
Understanding your results
A ratio of 0.5 or more doesn't mean you have a disease; it means your risk is higher and it may be worth discussing with a doctor, especially with other risk factors such as high blood pressure or a family history of diabetes.
Changes over time matter. Losing even a few centimetres from your waist reduces risk.
Pregnancy: WHtR isn't meaningful during pregnancy.
Children: NICE applies the same bands to children and young people aged 5 and over. The NHS has a separate waist-to-height calculator for children.
Country notes
UK: NICE guideline NG246 (overweight and obesity management, 2025; the waist-to-height recommendations date from 2022) advises adults with a BMI under 35 to measure their own WHtR and to seek advice if it shows increased risk.
US: the CDC and NIH use waist circumference: higher risk above 40 in (102 cm) for men and 35 in (88 cm) for most women.
Canada: Obesity Canada and Health Canada guidance use waist circumference with similar cut-offs.
Tips and common mistakes
Measuring at the belt line. Many people wear trousers below the natural waist.
Measuring after a large meal.
Mixing units. Use centimetres for both or inches for both.
Frequently asked questions
What is a healthy waist-to-height ratio?
Between 0.4 and 0.49 for adults.
How do I calculate my waist-to-height ratio?
Divide your waist measurement by your height, in the same units.
Is waist-to-height ratio better than BMI?
It's a useful addition: it shows fat distribution, which BMI doesn't.
What does a ratio over 0.5 mean?
Increased health risk from central fat; over 0.6 is high risk.
Does it work for men and women?
Yes, the same thresholds apply.
How can I reduce my waist size?
A combination of a calorie deficit, regular physical activity and less alcohol helps reduce abdominal fat.
