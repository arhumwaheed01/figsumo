H1: Fat Intake Calculator
Fat is an essential nutrient. It supplies energy, helps you absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, and provides fatty acids your body can't make. It also has more than twice the calories of carbohydrate or protein, so portions add up quickly. This calculator turns your daily calories and a chosen percentage into a daily fat target in grams.
How to use this calculator
1. Daily calories: your maintenance calories or a weight-loss target. The TDEE calculator can estimate this.
2. Fat as % of calories: 10–70% (default 30%).
Results: daily fat intake in grams and calories from fat.
The formula
Fat (g) = Daily calories × Fat % ÷ 100 ÷ 9
Each gram of fat provides about 9 kcal.
Worked examples
Example 1: 2,000 kcal at 30%
2,000 × 0.30 = 600 kcal from fat
600 ÷ 9 = 67 g of fat a day
Example 2: 2,500 kcal at 35%
2,500 × 0.35 ÷ 9 = 97 g
Example 3: 1,800 kcal at 20%
40 g, the lower end of the recommended range.

On 2,000 kcal, 20–35% is 44–78 g of total fat. Saturated fat under 10% is under 22 g in the US; the UK reference intake on labels is 20 g of saturated fat and 70 g of total fat for an adult.
Types of fat
Unsaturated fats (olive and rapeseed oils, nuts, seeds, avocado, oily fish) are linked with better heart health when they replace saturated fat.
Omega-3 fats (salmon, mackerel, sardines, walnuts, flaxseed) are recommended regularly; the NHS advises two portions of fish a week, one oily.
Saturated fat (butter, fatty meat, cheese, coconut and palm oil) raises LDL cholesterol.
Trans fats (partially hydrogenated oils) are the most harmful; the US and Canada have banned added partially hydrogenated oils.
Understanding your results
Very low fat (under 20%) can make it harder to get essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins.
Higher fat (over 35%) is common in low-carb diets; the type of fat matters more than the amount for heart health.
Your target is a daily average. Some days will be higher, some lower.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting cooking oil. A tablespoon of oil is about 14 g of fat (about 120 kcal).
Comparing grams of fat with grams of food. 100 g of cheese has about 30–35 g of fat, not 100 g.
Assuming "low fat" means low calorie. Some low-fat products add sugar.

Reading food labels in the US, Canada and UK
US Nutrition Facts: the % Daily Value for total fat is based on 78 g a day and for saturated fat on 20 g, both for a 2,000 kcal diet.
Canada Nutrition Facts table: the % Daily Value uses 75 g for fat and 20 g for saturated plus trans fat. Health Canada describes 5% DV or less as "a little" and 15% DV or more as "a lot".
UK front-of-pack labels: traffic-light colours show fat per 100 g. Food is "high" (red) in fat above 17.5 g per 100 g and "low" (green) at 3 g or less; for saturates, high is above 5 g and low is 1.5 g or less.
Labels give fat per serving, so multiply by the number of servings you actually eat before comparing with your target.
Frequently asked questions
How many grams of fat should I eat a day?
On a 2,000 kcal diet, about 44–78 g (20–35% of calories).
How do I convert fat percentage to grams?
Multiply calories by the percentage, then divide by 9.
Is a high-fat diet bad?
Not necessarily; what matters most is the type of fat and total calories.
How much saturated fat is too much?
Under 10% of calories in the US, and no more than 11% in the UK.
Do I need fat to lose weight?
Yes, some fat is essential; reducing it moderately can help lower calories.
What counts as a healthy fat?
Mainly unsaturated fats from plant oils, nuts, seeds and fish.
