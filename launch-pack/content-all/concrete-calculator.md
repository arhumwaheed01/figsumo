H1: Concrete Calculator
Ordering concrete is a balance: too little and you're left with a cold joint halfway through the pour; too much and you're paying for waste. This calculator converts a slab's length, width and depth into the cubic yards a ready-mix supplier needs, plus cubic feet for working out bagged concrete.
How to use this calculator
1. Length (ft) and width (ft): the slab's outline (default 12 × 10).
2. Depth (in): thickness in inches (default 4).
Results: cubic yards (highlighted) and cubic feet, with a chart splitting the volume into whole cubic yards and leftover cubic feet.
The formula
ft³ = length × width × (depth ÷ 12)
yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27
Depth is divided by 12 to turn inches into feet; there are 27 ft³ in a cubic yard (3 × 3 × 3).
Worked examples
Example 1: the default patio
12 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 40 ft³ = 1.48 yd³. Adding 10% for waste and uneven ground: order 1.63 yd³, which most suppliers will round to the nearest quarter or half yard.
Example 2: in bags
Bagged mix yields roughly 0.60 ft³ per 80 lb bag and 0.45 ft³ per 60 lb bag (check the bag). For 40 ft³: 67 × 80 lb or 89 × 60 lb bags, before waste. At that volume, ready-mix delivery is usually easier.
Example 3: a two-car garage
24 × 24 ft at 5 in deep = 8.89 yd³.
Example 4: fence post footings (round)
Not yet built in; work it out by hand. A 12 in diameter hole 3 ft deep: π × 0.5² × 3 = 2.36 ft³ each. Four posts: 9.42 ft³ = 0.35 yd³ (bagged mix is the practical choice).
Example 5: metric
A 4 m × 3 m slab, 100 mm thick: 4 × 3 × 0.1 = 1.2 m³. Enter the dimensions in feet and inches to use the calculator, or work in metres directly as shown.
Planning the pour
Waste allowance: 5–10% for flat, well-prepared ground; more for irregular shapes or soft subgrade.
Sub-base: most slabs sit on 4 in or so of compacted gravel; see the gravel calculator.
Thickness at edges: thickened edges or footings add volume; calculate them separately and add.
Minimum orders: many ready-mix suppliers charge a short-load fee under a few cubic yards [VERIFY with local suppliers].
Reinforcement: mesh or rebar is set by the job and local code, not by volume.
Concrete in the US, Canada and UK
US: ready-mix is ordered in cubic yards; bags in pounds.
Canada: suppliers usually quote cubic metres (1 yd³ ≈ 0.7646 m³); bags in kilograms.
UK: ready-mix in cubic metres; bags of 20–25 kg, with yields printed on the bag.
Understanding your results
The whole yards / extra feet chart helps when a supplier sells in whole or half yards.
Results assume a rectangle with a uniform depth. For L-shapes, split into rectangles and add the volumes.
Uneven ground is the biggest cause of shortfalls: a slab meant to be 4 in that averages 4.5 in needs 12.5% more.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering depth in feet. The depth box is inches.
Skipping the waste allowance.
Measuring the top of the forms rather than the excavated depth.
Forgetting footings and thickened edges.
Frequently asked questions
How much concrete do I need for a 10 × 10 slab, 4 in thick?
33.33 ft³, or 1.23 yd³.
How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?
27.
How many 80 lb bags make a cubic yard?
About 45, at 0.60 ft³ per bag.
How thick should a patio be?
Commonly 4 in; driveways 4–6 in. Check local code.
Should I order extra?
Yes, typically 5–10%.
Can it do round columns?
Not yet; use πr²h as shown above.
