H1: Mortgage Calculator
The monthly payment on a home loan is more than principal and interest. Enter a price, a down payment and a rate, and this calculator adds property tax, homeowners insurance, private mortgage insurance (PMI) and HOA dues. It then shows the full monthly cost, the interest over the life of the loan, and the month your PMI can come off.
How to use this calculator
1. Home price and down payment. Type the down payment in dollars or as a percent. The loan amount is the price minus the down payment.
2. Interest rate and term. Use the note rate from your Loan Estimate (not the APR) and the term in years: 30, 20, 15 or a custom length.
3. Property tax. Enter the yearly bill, or a percent of the price if you don't know it yet. Your county assessor or the listing usually has last year's figure.
4. Homeowners insurance. Enter the annual premium from a quote.
5. PMI. If you put down less than 20% on a conventional loan, the calculator turns PMI on automatically and starts it at 0.5% of the loan per year. This is a placeholder: real PMI rates depend on your credit score, loan-to-value and insurer, so replace it with your quote.
6. HOA and extra payments (optional). Add monthly HOA dues, and any extra monthly, yearly or one-time principal payments.
The result updates as you type. You can copy a link that reproduces your exact inputs.
The formula
The principal-and-interest (P&I) part of a fixed-rate mortgage uses the standard amortizing-loan formula:
M = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
M: monthly principal and interest payment
P: loan amount (price − down payment)
r: monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12 (6.5% → 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167)
n: number of monthly payments = years × 12
Each month, the interest due is the current balance × r. The rest of M reduces the balance. On top of P&I, the calculator adds:
Monthly total = M + (annual property tax ÷ 12) + (annual insurance ÷ 12) + (PMI rate × loan ÷ 12) + HOA
PMI is dropped from the total in the month the scheduled balance reaches 78% of the original home value. That is the point where federal law generally requires automatic termination for conventional loans on a principal residence.
Worked examples
Example 1: 20% down, no PMI. Home price $400,000, down payment $80,000 (20%), loan $320,000, 6.5% for 30 years, property tax 1.1% of price ($4,400/yr), insurance $1,800/yr.
r = 0.065 / 12 = 0.00541667; n = 360
P&I: M = $2,022.62
Property tax: 4,400 ÷ 12 = $366.67
Insurance: 1,800 ÷ 12 = $150.00
Monthly total: $2,539.28
Total interest over 30 years: $2,022.62 × 360 − $320,000 = $408,142.36 (the calculator rounds the final payment, so the exact figure can differ by a few cents)
In the first payment, $1,733.33 goes to interest (320,000 × 0.00541667) and only $289.28 to principal.
Example 2: 10% down, with PMI. Same home, $40,000 down, loan $360,000, PMI 0.5% per year (illustrative).
P&I: $2,275.44
PMI: 360,000 × 0.005 ÷ 12 = $150.00
Tax and insurance as before: $516.67
Monthly total: $2,942.11
The scheduled balance falls to $320,000 (80% of the $400,000 original value) after payment 95 (just under 8 years). From then you can ask your servicer to cancel PMI. It reaches $312,000 (78%) after payment 109, when PMI should end automatically if you are current on payments.
Understanding your results
Monthly payment is what leaves your account each month, provided your lender collects tax and insurance through escrow. Escrow amounts change when tax bills or premiums change; the P&I part of a fixed-rate loan does not.
Total interest shows how much the term costs you. In Example 1, a 15-year term at the same rate would raise P&I to $2,787.54 a month but cut total interest to $181,757.84, less than half of the 30-year figure. If a lender quotes a lower rate for the shorter term, the gap grows further.
Rate sensitivity: on the same $320,000 loan, 6.0% gives $1,918.56 and 7.0% gives $2,128.97. Half a point moves the payment by roughly $100 a month.
Amortization schedule: early payments are mostly interest. Extra principal payments made early save the most.
Ways to pay the loan off sooner
Extra monthly principal: even a small fixed amount, made from the first year, shortens the loan noticeably. Use the extra-payment fields to see the new payoff month.
Bi-weekly payments: paying half the monthly amount every two weeks gives 26 half-payments a year, which equals 13 monthly payments. Check first that your servicer applies each half-payment straight away, not just once a month, and that it doesn't charge a fee for the service.
Lump sums: a bonus or tax refund applied to principal lowers every later interest charge.
Shorter term at refinance: see the refinance calculator.
Before paying extra, check your note for any prepayment penalty, and consider whether higher-interest debt or an emergency fund should come first. The calculator shows the interest saved. It can't tell you whether that is the best use of the money.

Tips and common mistakes
Leaving out taxes, insurance and PMI. Add them to see the full monthly housing cost.
Comparing rates without points and fees. Use the APR or a loan comparison.
Assuming an adjustable rate stays low. Run a scenario at a higher rate after the fixed period.
Stretching to the maximum loan. A lower payment leaves room for repairs and savings.
Forgetting to shop around. Quotes from several lenders on the same day often differ.
Frequently asked questions
Does the monthly payment include property tax and insurance?
Only if you enter them. Many lenders collect them monthly through an escrow account, so including them gives a more realistic monthly cost. The P&I figure on its own is what the loan formula produces.
When can I stop paying PMI?
For most conventional loans on a principal residence that closed on or after 29 July 1999, you can ask your servicer in writing to cancel PMI once the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value. It must end automatically at 78%, provided you are current on payments. There are other conditions, such as a good payment history and no second liens. FHA and VA loans follow different rules.
Why is my lender's payment different from this one?
Lenders may round differently, start interest on a different date, or include escrow amounts based on actual tax bills. They may also add a cushion to escrow. Use your Loan Estimate as the reference.
Is a 15-year mortgage better than a 30-year?
It costs far less interest, but the monthly payment is higher. In Example 1, the 15-year P&I is $764.92 a month more. Which suits you depends on your budget and other goals; this calculator only shows the trade-off.
How much do extra payments save?
It depends on the size and timing. Enter an extra monthly amount and compare total interest with and without it. On the amortization calculator, an extra $200 a month on a $250,000, 6%, 30-year loan saves about $86,233 in interest and pays the loan off 93 months early.
What is the difference between interest rate and APR?
The interest rate determines your payment. The APR also folds in certain fees, such as points and origination charges, so you can compare offers. Enter the interest rate in this calculator.
Does this include closing costs?
No. Closing costs (lender fees, title, appraisal, prepaid items) are listed on your Loan Estimate and are paid at closing. They don't change the monthly payment unless they are financed into the loan.
