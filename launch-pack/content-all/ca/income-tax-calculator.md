H1: Canadian Income Tax Calculator (2026)
Every Canadian taxpayer outside Quebec pays two income taxes, calculated on the same T1 return: federal tax and provincial or territorial tax. Each has its own brackets and its own non-refundable credits. This calculator works out both for 2026 using CRA-published rates. It shows the tax at each step, your average rate and the marginal rate that applies to your next dollar.
How to use this calculator
1. Province or territory where you lived on 31 December 2026.
2. Employment income and other income (interest, rental, self-employment net income).
3. Deductions: RRSP contributions, union dues and similar.
4. CPP and EI: calculated automatically from employment income. If you are self-employed, you pay both the employee and employer shares of CPP, and the calculator uses those rules.
5. Results show federal tax, provincial tax, the total, the average rate and the marginal rate.
The formula
Taxable income   = total income − deductions (RRSP, enhanced CPP, CPP2, union dues…)
Federal tax      = federal brackets(taxable income) − 14% × federal credit amounts
Provincial tax   = provincial brackets(taxable income) − lowest provincial rate × provincial credit amounts
                   (+ surtax / health premium where applicable)
Average rate     = (federal + provincial tax) ÷ total income

Worked examples
Example 1: Ontario, $120,000 employment income.
CPP $4,230.45, of which $711 is the enhanced part. CPP2 $416. EI $1,123.07.
Taxable income: 120,000 − 711 − 416 = $118,873
Federal brackets: 14% × 58,523 + 20.5% × 58,522 + 26% × 1,828 = 8,193.22 + 11,997.01 + 475.28 = $20,665.51
Federal credits: 14% × (16,452 + 3,519.45 + 1,123.07 + 1,501) = $3,163.37. The BPA is not reduced, because net income is below $181,440. Federal tax $17,502.14
Ontario: basic tax $7,999.83, surtax $635.74, Health Premium $750 → $9,385.57
Total income tax ≈ $26,887.71, an average rate of about 22.4%
Marginal rate on the next dollar: 26% federal + 11.16% Ontario, plus the 20% + 36% surtax on the Ontario portion ≈ 43.4%
Example 2: Ontario, $75,000 employment income.
Taxable income $74,273 (after $711 enhanced CPP and $16 CPP2)
Federal tax $8,258.60 · Ontario tax $4,446.06, including the $750 Health Premium and no surtax
Total income tax $12,704.65, an average rate of 16.9%
Marginal rate: 20.5% + 9.15% = 29.65%
Understanding your results
Average rate: your total tax as a share of income. This is what you pay overall.
Marginal rate: what you'd pay on an extra dollar. Use it to value an RRSP contribution: at a 29.65% marginal rate, a $1,000 RRSP deduction saves about $296.50.
Refund or balance owing depends on what was withheld at source. Compare the total with box 22 of your T4.
Not included: most non-refundable credits beyond the basic ones (tuition, medical, donations, disability), dividend tax credits, capital gains, OAS clawback, and refundable benefits.
Country notes
Quebec: residents file a separate provincial return with Revenu Québec and receive a federal abatement. Not supported yet.
Capital gains: half of a capital gain is included in income. A proposed increase to two-thirds was cancelled in March 2025. A dedicated capital gains calculator is planned.

Tips and common mistakes
Applying your top rate to all income. Canadian brackets are marginal, federally and provincially.
Forgetting non-refundable credits. The basic personal amount and CPP/EI credits reduce tax.
Overlooking RRSP deductions. Contributions lower taxable income.
Using the wrong province. Your province of residence on 31 December applies for the whole year.
Frequently asked questions
Which province's rates apply to me?
Generally, the province or territory where you lived on 31 December of the tax year, even if you moved during the year.
What is the federal basic personal amount for 2026?
$16,452. It is reduced gradually for net income above $181,440, to a minimum of $14,829 at $258,482 and above.
Why are credits calculated at 14%?
Federal non-refundable credits are worth the lowest federal rate multiplied by the credit amount. For 2026 that rate is 14%.
What's the difference between my marginal and average rate?
The average rate is total tax ÷ income. The marginal rate applies to your next dollar, and it is higher because of progressive brackets and Ontario's surtax.
Does this include CPP and EI?
CPP and EI are calculated because they generate credits and deductions. They are payroll contributions, not income tax, so they are shown separately. Your total payroll deductions are on the take-home pay calculator.
How does an RRSP contribution change my tax?
It is deducted from income, so it saves tax at your marginal rate, up to your deduction limit. The 2026 RRSP dollar limit is $33,810, but your own limit is on your notice of assessment.
