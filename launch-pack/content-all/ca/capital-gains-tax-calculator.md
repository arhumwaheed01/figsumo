H1: Canada Capital Gains Tax Calculator
In Canada, only half of a capital gain is taxable. When you sell shares, a cottage, a rental property or other capital property for more than its adjusted cost base, you add 50% of the gain (the taxable capital gain) to your income and pay tax at your normal federal and provincial rates. This calculator estimates the gain, the taxable portion and the extra tax it adds to your 2026 return.
How to use this calculator
1. Province or territory of residence on 31 December.
2. Other taxable income: employment, pension, interest and other income for the year.
3. Proceeds of disposition: the sale price (or fair market value for gifts and deemed dispositions).
4. Adjusted cost base (ACB): what you paid, plus costs to acquire and capital improvements.
5. Outlays and expenses: commissions, legal fees and other selling costs.
6. Capital losses: current-year losses and net capital losses carried forward.
Results: capital gain, taxable capital gain, incremental federal and provincial tax, and the effective tax rate on the gain.
How it works
Capital gain = Proceeds − ACB − Outlays and expenses
Taxable capital gain = Capital gain × 50%
Tax on gain = Tax(Other income + Taxable gain) − Tax(Other income)
The inclusion rate stays at one-half: the proposal to raise it to two-thirds was cancelled on 21 March 2025. Because the taxable gain is added to your income, the tax depends on your marginal rate, which varies by province and income (including surtaxes and health premiums in Ontario).
Capital losses: half of a capital loss is an allowable capital loss. It can only offset taxable capital gains, not other income. Unused net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely.
Worked examples
Example 1: selling shares in Alberta
Other income $75,000. You sell shares for $30,000 with an ACB of $9,500 and $500 of commissions.
Capital gain = $20,000.00; taxable capital gain = $10,000.00
Your income rises from $75,000 to $85,000. In 2026, that range is taxed at 20.5% federally and 10% in Alberta, a 30.5% combined marginal rate.
Tax on the gain = $3,050.00, an effective rate of 15.25% on the whole gain.
Example 2: working out the ACB
You bought 50 shares at $20 ($20 commission) and later 60 shares at $25 ($10 commission). Your ACB per share is the total cost ÷ total shares = $23.00. Canada uses this average cost method for identical properties, so each sale uses the average, not the price of a specific lot.
Example 3: a cottage held for 10 years
You owned a cottage and a city home for 10 years and designate the cottage as your principal residence for 6 of those years. The exempt share of a $300,000 gain is (6 + 1) ÷ 10, or $210,000.00. The remaining $90,000.00 is a capital gain, so $45,000.00 is taxable. A family can designate only one principal residence per year.
Losses
A $4,000 capital loss gives an allowable capital loss of $2,000.00, which reduces taxable capital gains this year, in the previous three years, or in future years.
Exemptions and sheltered accounts
Principal residence exemption: gains on your home are generally exempt for years you designate it as your principal residence. You must report the sale on Schedule 3 even if it's fully exempt.
TFSA, RRSP, RRIF and FHSA: gains inside these accounts aren't capital gains for tax purposes. See the TFSA calculator.
Lifetime capital gains exemption: qualified small business corporation shares and qualified farm or fishing property can be eligible for an exemption of up to $1.25 million of gains [VERIFY current limit]. Large exempt gains can trigger alternative minimum tax.
Gifts of listed securities to charity: the taxable gain is generally nil.
Deemed dispositions
You can owe tax without selling: when you give property to someone other than your spouse, change a property's use (for example, a home becomes a rental), emigrate, or at death (with a rollover to a surviving spouse).
Understanding your results
Incremental tax is the difference between your tax with and without the gain, which captures bracket changes.
Instalments: a large gain may mean you need to pay tax by instalments next year.
Reporting: report dispositions on Schedule 3 of your T1 return; brokers issue T5008 slips but they don't always show the correct ACB.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the T5008 cost without checking it against your records.
Superficial loss rule: a loss is denied if you or an affiliated person buy the same property within 30 days before or after the sale and still hold it 30 days later.
Forgetting reinvested distributions that increase ACB, which makes you pay tax twice.
Assuming the principal residence exemption is automatic. You must report the sale and designate.
Frequently asked questions
What is the capital gains inclusion rate in Canada?
50%. The proposed increase to two-thirds was cancelled.
How much tax will I pay on a capital gain?
Half the gain is added to income and taxed at your marginal rate, so the effective rate is roughly half your marginal rate.
Is the sale of my home taxable?
Usually not, if it was your principal residence for every year you owned it. You still report it.
Can capital losses reduce my salary income?
No, except in the year of death and the year before. They offset taxable capital gains.
What is the adjusted cost base?
Your cost, including acquisition costs and improvements, averaged across identical properties.
Are capital gains in a TFSA taxable?
No.
