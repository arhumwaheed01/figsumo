H1: Canada Take-Home Pay Calculator (2026)
In Canada, your employer takes four things off each paycheque: federal income tax, provincial or territorial income tax, Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions and Employment Insurance (EI) premiums. Above a certain level of earnings there is also a second CPP contribution, CPP2. This calculator applies the 2026 CRA figures for your province and shows your net pay for the year and for each pay period.
How to use this calculator
1. Province or territory of employment. Quebec is not supported yet, because it uses QPP, QPIP and its own provincial tax.
2. Gross annual salary, or an hourly rate with hours.
3. Pay frequency: weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly or monthly.
4. Deductions (optional): RRSP contributions made through payroll and union dues. Both reduce taxable income.
5. Results show each deduction for the year and per pay, with your net pay.
The formula
CPP (employee), 2026:
CPP  = 5.95% × (min(earnings, $74,600) − $3,500)      → maximum $4,230.45
CPP2 = 4%    × (min(earnings, $85,000) − $74,600)     → maximum $416.00
The 5.95% is made up of 4.95% "base" and 1% "first additional". The base part earns a non-refundable credit. The enhanced 1% and all of CPP2 are deducted from income instead.
EI (outside Quebec), 2026:
EI = 1.63% × min(insurable earnings, $68,900)          → maximum $1,123.07
Income tax:
Taxable income = salary − enhanced CPP − CPP2 − RRSP − union dues
Federal tax    = federal brackets(taxable) − 14% × (BPA $16,452 + CPP base + EI + Canada employment amount $1,501)
Provincial tax = provincial brackets(taxable) − lowest provincial rate × (provincial BPA + CPP base + EI)
                 + any provincial surtax or premium (e.g., Ontario surtax and Ontario Health Premium)
Net pay        = salary − CPP − CPP2 − EI − federal tax − provincial tax − other deductions
2026 federal brackets: 14% to $58,523 · 20.5% to $117,045 · 26% to $181,440 · 29% to $258,482 · 33% above. The federal basic personal amount is $16,452, reduced gradually to $14,829 for net income between $181,440 and $258,482.
Ontario 2026: 5.05% to $53,891 · 9.15% to $107,785 · 11.16% to $150,000 · 12.16% to $220,000 · 13.16% above. Basic personal amount $12,989. Surtax: 20% of basic Ontario tax over $5,818, plus 36% over $7,446. The Ontario Health Premium is up to $900 a year.
Worked examples
Example 1: Ontario, $75,000 salary, no other deductions.
CPP: 5.95% × (74,600 − 3,500) = $4,230.45. Base part $3,519.45; enhanced part $711.00.
CPP2: 4% × (75,000 − 74,600) = $16.00
EI: 1.63% × 68,900 = $1,123.07
Taxable income: 75,000 − 711 − 16 = $74,273
Federal: brackets give $11,421.97. Credits: 14% × (16,452 + 3,519.45 + 1,123.07 + 1,501) = $3,163.37. Federal tax $8,258.60
Ontario: brackets give $4,586.45. Credits: 5.05% × (12,989 + 3,519.45 + 1,123.07) = $890.39. Basic Ontario tax $3,696.06, which is under $5,818, so there is no surtax. Health Premium $750. Ontario total $4,446.06
Net pay: $56,925.82 a year, or $4,743.82 a month, or about $2,189.45 bi-weekly
Total deductions are $18,074.18, which is 24.1% of gross pay.
Example 2: Ontario, $120,000 salary.
CPP $4,230.45 · CPP2 at the maximum, $416 · EI $1,123.07
Taxable income $118,873; federal tax $17,502.14
Basic Ontario tax $7,999.83 → surtax $635.74 → plus OHP $750 → Ontario total $9,385.57
Net pay: $87,342.77 a year, or $7,278.56 a month
At this income, the Ontario surtax adds about 8% to basic Ontario tax.
Understanding your results
CPP and EI stop for the year once you reach the maximums. This often happens in the autumn for higher earners, and your later paycheques rise.
Per-pay figures are annual amounts divided evenly. Payroll software uses CRA's formulas each period, so individual paycheques can differ slightly.
Your tax return reconciles everything. Credits such as tuition, medical expenses or donations can change the final result.
Marginal rate: in Example 1, the next dollar is taxed at 20.5% federal + 9.15% Ontario = 29.65% (before CPP and EI).
Country notes
Provinces: every province and territory has its own brackets and basic personal amount. The calculator uses the CRA's 2026 table for each one. Surtaxes and premiums currently apply in Ontario. Other provincial credits and reductions are flagged where they apply.
Quebec: not supported yet. Quebec residents contribute to QPP instead of CPP, pay QPIP premiums, pay a lower EI rate and file a separate provincial return with Revenu Québec.
United States / UK: see the US paycheck calculator and the UK take-home pay calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting provincial tax. It varies a lot by province.
Ignoring CPP and EI maximums. Deductions stop once you reach them, so later paycheques rise.
Comparing salaries across provinces without tax. The same gross pay gives different net pay.
Leaving out benefits deductions such as group insurance or pension contributions.
Frequently asked questions
What is CPP2?
It is a second CPP contribution, at 4% on earnings between the year's maximum pensionable earnings ($74,600) and the year's additional maximum ($85,000) in 2026. It funds a higher CPP pension later. The maximum employee contribution in 2026 is $416.
Why is part of CPP a deduction and part a credit?
Base contributions (4.95%) earn a non-refundable tax credit. Enhanced contributions (the extra 1% and CPP2) are deducted from income, which gives more tax relief to higher earners.
Does an RRSP contribution increase my take-home pay?
If it is deducted through payroll, your employer can reduce the tax withheld right away. Personal contributions are deducted on your tax return and usually produce a refund instead. The RRSP calculator estimates that refund.
What is the Ontario Health Premium?
It's an amount added to Ontario income tax, based on taxable income. It is $0 up to $20,000 and rises in steps to a maximum of $900 above $200,600.
Why is my EI deduction different?
If you work in Quebec, the EI rate is lower because QPIP covers parental benefits. Some employment is not insurable. Your employer's payroll determines this.
Does this include the Canada Workers Benefit or GST/HST credit?
No. Those are paid through the tax return or by separate payments. They are not part of payroll deductions.
