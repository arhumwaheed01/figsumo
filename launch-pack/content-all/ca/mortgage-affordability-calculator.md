H1: Canadian Mortgage Affordability Calculator
How much mortgage can you qualify for in Canada? Lenders check two ratios, gross debt service (GDS) and total debt service (TDS), and they test them at a stress-test rate above your actual mortgage rate. This calculator applies those rules to your income and costs and shows the maximum monthly payment, the maximum mortgage and an estimated maximum purchase price for your down payment.
How to use this calculator
1. Gross annual household income: before tax, for everyone on the mortgage.
2. Monthly debt payments: car loans, student loans, lines of credit, and credit card minimums (lenders often use 3% of card balances).
3. Property tax (annual) and heating (monthly): estimates for the home you're looking at.
4. Condo fees (monthly): if applicable.
5. Down payment: cash you'll put down.
6. Mortgage rate: the contract rate you've been offered.
7. Amortization: 25 years, or 30 where eligible.
Results: qualifying rate, maximum monthly mortgage payment (the lower of the GDS and TDS limits), maximum mortgage, estimated maximum price, and the payment at your contract rate.
How it works
Ratios (CMHC guideline limits [VERIFY]):
GDS = (Mortgage payment + Property tax + Heating + 50% of condo fees) ÷ Gross monthly income ≤ 39%
TDS = (GDS costs + All other debt payments) ÷ Gross monthly income ≤ 44%
Stress test: you must qualify at the minimum qualifying rate, the higher of your contract rate plus 2 percentage points or 5.25%. This applies to insured and uninsured mortgages at federally regulated lenders.
Maximum mortgage: the present value of the maximum payment at the qualifying rate over the amortization. Canadian fixed-rate mortgages compound semi-annually by law, so the monthly rate is (1 + rate ÷ 2)^(1/6) − 1.
Worked example
Household income $120,000 ($10,000 a month); property tax $4,800 a year ($400 a month); heating $100 a month; car payment $500 a month; contract rate 4.5% over 25 years.
1. Qualifying rate = max(4.5% + 2%, 5.25%) = 6.5%
2. GDS limit: 39% × 10,000 = $3,900.00 − 400 − 100 = $3,400.00 for the mortgage
3. TDS limit: 44% × 10,000 = $4,400.00 − 400 − 100 − 500 = $3,400.00
4. Maximum payment = $3,400.00 (both limits are equal here)
5. Maximum mortgage at 6.5% over 25 years = $507,596.19
6. Your actual payment at 4.5% would be $2,809.41
Maximum price:
With 20% down, the price could be up to $634,495.23 (down payment $126,899.05).
With $60,000 down, the insured mortgage including a 3.10% CMHC premium can't exceed $507,596.19, so the base loan is $492,333.84 (premium $15,262.35) and the maximum price is about $552,333.84 (LTV 89.1%). The minimum down payment at that price is $30,233.38, so $60,000 is enough.
Effect of more debt: with $1,100 a month of other debts instead of $500, the TDS limit drops to $2,800.00 and the maximum mortgage to $418,020.39. Paying off a car loan before applying can raise what you qualify for.
A 30-year amortization at the same payment would allow $542,782.06, where eligible.
Understanding your results
This is a maximum, not a recommendation. Lenders may approve less based on credit score, employment history and their own policies.
The stress test is the binding constraint for most buyers. It reduces borrowing power by about a fifth compared with qualifying at the contract rate.
Cash to close: budget for land transfer tax, legal fees, inspection and adjustments (often 1.5% to 4% of the price). See the land transfer tax calculator.
Down payment under 20% means CMHC or private mortgage insurance, a price cap of $1.5 million and a minimum down payment of 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the rest. See the CMHC insurance calculator.
Ways to increase what you can afford
Pay down or close high-payment debts before applying.
Save a larger down payment, using the FHSA and RRSP Home Buyers' Plan (FHSA calculator).
Add a co-borrower with income.
Choose a home with lower property tax or condo fees.
Tips and common mistakes
Using net income. Lenders use gross income.
Forgetting heating costs and condo fees in GDS.
Qualifying at your contract rate. The stress test applies.
Spending to the limit. Leave room for maintenance, rate changes at renewal and savings.
Before you apply
Pull your credit report from Equifax and TransUnion and correct any errors, because your score affects both approval and rate. Keep your employment stable through closing, and avoid new car loans or credit cards that would raise your debt ratios. If you're self-employed, lenders usually want two years of Notices of Assessment. A pre-approval can hold a rate for 90 to 120 days, which protects you if rates rise while you house-hunt. [VERIFY]
Frequently asked questions
What is the mortgage stress test?
A rule requiring you to qualify at the higher of your contract rate plus 2 points or 5.25%.
What GDS and TDS ratios do lenders use?
CMHC's limits are 39% GDS and 44% TDS [VERIFY]; some lenders use different limits for uninsured mortgages.
How much house can I afford on $100,000 a year?
It depends on debts, property tax, rate and down payment. Enter your figures; with no other debts, low property tax and a 4.5% contract rate, a maximum mortgage in the mid-$400,000s is typical.
Does the stress test apply at renewal?
Not if you renew with your current lender. Switching lenders at renewal for an uninsured mortgage generally no longer requires re-qualifying under the stress test [VERIFY].
Why does Canada use semi-annual compounding?
The Interest Act requires fixed-rate mortgage rates to be stated as calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance, and lenders quote semi-annual compounding.
Do lenders count child support or rental income?
Often, with documentation; policies vary.
