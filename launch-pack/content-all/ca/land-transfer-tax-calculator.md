H1: Land Transfer Tax Calculator
Land transfer tax is a provincial (and in some cities, municipal) tax paid by the buyer when property changes hands. It's due on closing and can't be added to your mortgage, so it's one of the largest cash costs of buying a home in Canada. This calculator estimates provincial and municipal land transfer tax for your purchase, including first-time buyer rebates.
How to use this calculator
1. Province: each province sets its own tax and calls it something different (land transfer tax in Ontario, property transfer tax in British Columbia, the "welcome tax" in Quebec).
2. City: select Toronto (or Montreal) where a municipal tax applies on top.
3. Purchase price: the value of the consideration, usually the sale price.
4. First-time buyer: for provincial and municipal rebates or exemptions where eligible.
Results: provincial tax, municipal tax, rebates, and total due at closing.
How it works
Land transfer tax is marginal: each slice of the price is taxed at its own rate, and the slices are added together. Ontario's rates (for transfers on or after 1 January 2017) are:

Worked examples
Example 1: $400,000 in Ontario (outside Toronto)
0.5% × 55,000 = $275.00
1.0% × 195,000 = $1,950.00
1.5% × 150,000 = $2,250.00
Total = $4,475.00
Example 2: $600,000 in Ontario
The first $400,000 costs $4,475.00, plus 2% × 200,000 = $4,000.00
Provincial tax = $8,475.00 (1.41% of the price)
In Toronto, the municipal land transfer tax uses matching rates at this price [VERIFY], so the total roughly doubles to $16,950.00. A first-time buyer eligible for the full Ontario refund (up to $4,000 [VERIFY]) and Toronto rebate (up to $4,475 [VERIFY]) would pay about $8,475.00.
Example 3: a $2.5 million detached home in Ontario
Provincial tax = $48,975.00, including 2.5% on the portion over $2 million.
Example 4: $900,000 in British Columbia
1% × 200,000 = $2,000.00
2% × 700,000 = $14,000.00
Property transfer tax = $16,000.00, compared with $14,475.00 provincial tax in Ontario
Example 5: an Ontario first-time buyer at $400,000
The Ontario first-time buyer refund covers up to $4,000 of provincial tax [VERIFY current maximum]. On Example 1's $4,475.00, the buyer would pay about $475.00. At $600,000, the refund still covers only $4,000 of the $8,475.00 bill.

Other provinces
British Columbia: property transfer tax of 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million and 3% above, plus a further 2% on residential value over $3 million [VERIFY]. On a $900,000 home that's $16,000.00 before any exemption. First-time buyers and buyers of new homes may qualify for full or partial exemptions under price limits [VERIFY current thresholds].
Quebec: the duties on transfers of immovables ("welcome tax") use brackets that are indexed each year, with higher municipal rates in Montreal and some other cities [VERIFY].
Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland and Labrador: each charges a provincial or municipal deed transfer tax with its own rates [VERIFY].
Alberta and Saskatchewan: no land transfer tax; buyers pay land titles registration fees based on value, which are much smaller.
Understanding your results
Payable at closing, typically through your lawyer or notary.
Not added to the mortgage. Budget for it in cash alongside the down payment, legal fees, inspection and moving costs.
Rebates may be paid at closing or claimed afterwards, depending on the province and how your lawyer registers the transfer.
CMHC premium isn't included. See the CMHC insurance calculator.
First-time buyer rules (summary)
Eligibility usually requires that you've never owned a home anywhere, that you're a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and that you'll occupy the home within a set period (Ontario requires occupation within nine months) [VERIFY]. If a spouse has owned a home while being your spouse, the refund may be reduced or unavailable.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting the municipal tax in Toronto. It can double the bill.
Assuming rebates cover everything. On pricier homes, the rebate covers only part of the tax.
Leaving it out of the cash budget. Lenders won't lend it to you.
Using last year's rates. Rebates and thresholds are adjusted from time to time.
Notes for US and UK readers
US: many states and some counties and cities charge a real estate transfer tax or deed stamps, usually a small percentage of the price. Depending on local custom, the seller often pays it, unlike Canada, where the buyer pays. See the closing cost calculator.
UK: the equivalent is Stamp Duty Land Tax in England and Northern Ireland, LBTT in Scotland and LTT in Wales, also paid by the buyer. See the Stamp Duty calculator.
Frequently asked questions
Who pays land transfer tax?
The buyer.
How is land transfer tax calculated in Ontario?
Marginally: 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, 2% and 2.5% on successive portions of the price.
Do I pay land transfer tax in Alberta?
No. Alberta charges land titles registration fees instead.
Can I add land transfer tax to my mortgage?
No. It's due in cash at closing.
How much is the first-time buyer rebate?
Up to $4,000 in Ontario and up to $4,475 in Toronto [VERIFY]; other provinces differ.
Is land transfer tax deductible?
Not for a principal residence. For a rental or business property, it's generally added to the property's cost base.
