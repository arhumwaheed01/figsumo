H1: CMHC Insurance Calculator
In Canada, a mortgage with a down payment below 20% must carry mortgage default insurance. Lenders buy it from CMHC or a private insurer (Sagen, Canada Guaranty) and pass the premium on to you, usually by adding it to the mortgage. This calculator works out your minimum down payment, loan-to-value ratio, the premium and the insured mortgage amount, plus any provincial sales tax due on the premium.
How to use this calculator
1. Purchase price: the price of the home.
2. Down payment: in dollars or as a percentage.
3. Down payment source: traditional (your savings, RRSP Home Buyers' Plan, FHSA, a gift from family) or non-traditional (for example, borrowed funds). Non-traditional sources carry a higher premium at the highest loan-to-value band.
4. Province: some provinces charge sales tax on the premium.
5. Amortization: 25 years, or 30 years where eligible.
Results: minimum down payment, loan-to-value (LTV), premium rate, premium amount, total insured mortgage and PST payable at closing.
How it works
Minimum down payment (federal rules):
5% of the first $500,000
10% of the portion from $500,000 up to $1.5 million
Homes of $1.5 million or more need 20% and can't be insured

Worked examples
Example 1: $500,000 home with 10% down
Loan = 500,000 − 50,000 = $450,000; LTV = 90%
Premium = 450,000 × 3.10% = $13,950.00
Insured mortgage = $463,950.00
In Ontario, PST at 8% on the premium = $1,116.00, payable at closing [VERIFY rate].
Spread over a 25-year mortgage at 4.5%, the premium adds roughly $77.21 a month to the payment.
Example 2: $800,000 home at the minimum down payment
Minimum down = 5% × 500,000 + 10% × 300,000 = $55,000.00 (6.875% of the price)
Loan $745,000.00; LTV 93.125% → 4.00%
Premium = $29,800.00; insured mortgage $774,800.00
With 15% down instead, the loan is $680,000.00 at 2.80%, a premium of $19,040.00. That's $10,760.00 less in premium, on top of the smaller loan.
Example 3: $1,000,000 home at the minimum down payment
Minimum down = 5% × 500,000 + 10% × 500,000 = $75,000.00 (7.5%)
Loan $925,000.00; LTV 92.5% → 4.00%
Premium = $37,000.00; insured mortgage $962,000.00
In Ontario, PST at 8% on the premium = $2,960.00, paid at closing [VERIFY rate]
Example 4: a few dollars across an LTV band
On a $600,000 home:
$59,000 down (9.83%): loan $541,000, LTV 90.17% → 4.00%, premium $21,640.00
$60,000 down (10%): loan $540,000, LTV 90.00% → 3.10%, premium $16,740.00
An extra $1,000 of down payment saves $4,900.00 of premium. Check where you sit against the band edges before finalising your down payment.
Understanding your results
The premium is a one-time charge, usually added to the mortgage, so you pay interest on it.
PST isn't added to the mortgage. Where it applies, you pay it in cash at closing.
It protects the lender, not you. If you default, the insurer pays the lender.
Insured mortgages often get lower rates than uninsured ones because the lender's risk is lower.
Stress test: insured borrowers must qualify at the higher of the contract rate plus 2 percentage points or 5.25%. See the mortgage affordability calculator.
Eligibility rules to know
Purchase price under $1.5 million.
Owner-occupied (1 to 4 units; small rental properties have separate rules).
Maximum amortization 25 years, or 30 years for first-time buyers and buyers of new builds [VERIFY current eligibility].
Gross debt service (GDS) and total debt service (TDS) ratio limits apply.
Tips and common mistakes
Using 5% for a home over $500,000. The 10% tier applies above $500,000.
Forgetting the PST in Ontario, Quebec or Saskatchewan.
Assuming 20% down is always best. It avoids the premium, but keeping cash for closing costs, land transfer tax and an emergency fund matters too.
Crossing an LTV band by a few dollars. A slightly bigger down payment can drop you into a lower premium rate.
Notes for US and UK readers
US: the closest equivalent is private mortgage insurance (PMI) on conventional loans with less than 20% down. It's usually a monthly charge rather than a one-time premium, and it can be cancelled once the loan reaches 80% of the original value, and ends automatically at 78%. FHA loans charge an upfront premium plus an annual one. See the PMI calculator.
UK: borrowers don't normally buy mortgage default insurance. Instead, lenders charge higher interest rates at higher loan-to-value ratios, and 95% mortgages are priced well above 75% ones. A government-backed mortgage guarantee scheme has supported 95% lending since 2021 [VERIFY current status].
Canadian buyers can't avoid the premium with less than 20% down, so comparing the premium with the extra time needed to save 20% is the key decision.
Frequently asked questions
When is CMHC insurance required?
When your down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price.
How much is CMHC insurance?
Between 2.80% and 4.00% of the loan for most buyers with less than 20% down (4.50% at 90.01–95% LTV with a non-traditional down payment).
Can I add the premium to my mortgage?
Yes, and most buyers do. Provincial sales tax on the premium can't be added.
Is CMHC insurance refundable?
Generally no, although some insurers offer partial refunds or portability if you move and port the mortgage.
What's the minimum down payment in Canada?
5% up to $500,000, 10% on the portion from $500,000 to $1.5 million, and 20% for $1.5 million or more.
Do private insurers charge the same?
Sagen and Canada Guaranty generally price close to CMHC, but check with your lender.
