H1: Percentage Calculator
Percentages express a part of a whole as a number out of 100. This calculator handles the four most common percentage questions: finding a percentage of a number, working out what percentage one number is of another, and increasing or decreasing a number by a percentage.
How to use this calculator
1. Calculation: choose one of:
What is X% of Y?
X is what % of Y?
Y increased by X%
Y decreased by X%
2. X: the percentage, or the part (in the second mode).
3. Y: the whole or starting value.
Result: the answer, with a simple breakdown chart.
The formulas
X% of Y            = X ÷ 100 × Y
X as a % of Y      = X ÷ Y × 100
Y increased by X%  = Y × (1 + X ÷ 100)
Y decreased by X%  = Y × (1 − X ÷ 100)
Two more you may need:
Percentage change     = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100
Percentage difference = |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100
Worked examples
Example 1: what is 15% of 80?
0.15 × 80 = 12.00
Example 2: 45 is what percent of 60?
45 ÷ 60 × 100 = 75.00%
Example 3: increase 250 by 8%
250 × 1.08 = 270.00
Example 4: decrease 120 by 25%
120 × 0.75 = 90.00
Example 5: percentage change
A price rises from 45 to 54: (54 − 45) ÷ 45 × 100 = 20.00%.
Example 6: reversing a percentage
A jacket costs 90 after a 25% discount. The original price is 90 ÷ 0.75 = 120.00, not 90 × 1.25. Similarly, removing 20% VAT from 100 gives 100 ÷ 1.2 = 83.33. See the VAT calculator.
Understanding your results
Increases and decreases aren't symmetric. Up 10% then down 10% from 100 gives 99.00, not 100.
Percentage points vs percent: a rate rising from 4% to 5% is up 1 percentage point, but 25% in relative terms.
Percentages over 100% are fine for increases and comparisons (150 is 300% of 50).
Percentage difference compares two values with no "original"; for 40 and 50 it's 22.2222%.
Everyday uses

Grades: marks as a percentage of the total.
Finance: interest rates, returns, inflation.
Statistics: survey results and shares of a total.
Mental maths shortcuts
10%: move the decimal point one place left (10% of 80 = 8).
5%: half of 10%.
1%: move the decimal two places.
X% of Y = Y% of X: 8% of 25 is the same as 25% of 8 = 2.
Tips and common mistakes
Dividing by the wrong base. "What percent of Y" divides by Y.
Reversing a discount by adding the same percentage back.
Adding percentages from different bases (a 10% rise on one item and 20% on another isn't a 30% rise overall).
Confusing percent change with percentage points.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate a percentage of a number?
Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100.
How do I find what percent one number is of another?
Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.
How do I add a percentage to a number?
Multiply by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal (1.08 for 8%).
How do I calculate percentage change?
(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100.
How do I reverse a percentage increase?
Divide by 1 plus the rate.
Is 50% of 20 the same as 20% of 50?
Yes; both are 10.
