H1: HELOC Calculator
A home equity line of credit (HELOC) lets you borrow against your home's equity as you need it, up to a credit limit. Most HELOCs have two phases: a draw period (often 10 years) when you can borrow and usually pay interest only, and a repayment period (often 20 years) when you can no longer draw and must repay principal and interest. This calculator shows the payment in each phase, the jump between them, the total interest and how much credit remains available.
How to use this calculator
1. HELOC credit limit: the maximum the lender has approved. If you don't have one yet, estimate it from your equity (see below).
2. Amount drawn: how much you expect to borrow. It must not exceed the limit.
3. Interest rate: the current rate on the line. HELOC rates are usually variable: an index (in the US, typically the prime rate) plus a margin.
4. Draw period (years): from your HELOC agreement; 10 years is common.
5. Repayment period (years): commonly 15 or 20 years.
Results: interest-only monthly payment during the draw period, repayment-phase payment, the payment increase at the switch, total interest and available credit.
The formula
Draw period (interest only):
Payment = Balance × Rate ÷ 12
Repayment period (amortizing):
Payment = B × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
B: balance at the end of the draw period; r: rate ÷ 12; n: repayment years × 12
Available credit = Credit limit − Amount drawn
The calculator assumes the whole amount is drawn at the start, only interest is paid during the draw period, and the rate stays constant.
Worked examples
Example 1: $40,000 drawn on a $100,000 line at 8.75%, 10-year draw, 20-year repayment
Interest-only payment = 40,000 × 0.0875 ÷ 12 = $291.67 a month
Available credit: $60,000.00
Repayment payment over 240 months: $353.48 a month
Payment increase at the end of the draw period: $61.82
Total interest over 30 years: $79,836.23
That jump catches many borrowers by surprise. Paying some principal during the draw period reduces it.
Example 2: how big a line can I get, and what if rates rise?
Lenders cap the combined loan-to-value (CLTV): all loans secured on the home ÷ its value. With a home worth $500,000, a mortgage balance of $280,000 and an 85% CLTV cap, the maximum line is 500,000 × 0.85 − 280,000 = $145,000.00.
Drawing $60,000 at 9.75% costs $487.50 a month interest-only. If the prime rate rises two points, the rate becomes 11.75% and the payment rises to $587.50, an extra $100.00 a month.
Understanding your results
Interest-only payment is the minimum during the draw period on many HELOCs. It doesn't reduce what you owe.
Repayment-phase payment is what you'll pay once drawing ends, if the balance is unchanged and the rate is the same.
Total interest depends heavily on the rate path. Treat it as an illustration.
Available credit is unused limit you could still draw (subject to the lender's terms; lenders can freeze or reduce lines in some situations).
HELOC vs home equity loan vs cash-out refinance
HELOC: flexible draws, variable rate, interest-only option. Suits ongoing or uncertain costs (a renovation in stages).
Home equity loan: a lump sum at a fixed rate with fixed payments; see the home equity loan calculator.
Cash-out refinance: replaces your mortgage with a larger one. It can make sense when the new mortgage rate is similar to or lower than your current rate; see the refinance calculator.
Country notes
United States: interest on a HELOC is deductible only if the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home securing it, and the total qualifying mortgage debt is within limits (IRS Publication 936). Lenders must give the CFPB's "What you should know about home equity lines of credit" booklet.
Canada: HELOCs are often combined with a mortgage in a "readvanceable" product. Federally regulated lenders can lend up to 65% of the home's value on a HELOC, within an 80% total loan-to-value limit including the mortgage (OSFI B-20) [VERIFY current B-20 wording]. Interest is generally not tax-deductible unless the money is borrowed to earn investment or business income.
United Kingdom: HELOCs are uncommon. Homeowners usually take a further advance from their mortgage lender, remortgage, or take a second-charge mortgage, all regulated by the FCA.
Tips and common mistakes
Planning only for the interest-only payment. Budget for the repayment-phase payment.
Ignoring variable rates. Test the payment at a rate 2–3 points higher.
Using a HELOC for everyday spending. The loan is secured on your home; falling behind can lead to foreclosure.
Missing fees. Some HELOCs charge annual fees, early closure fees or minimum draw requirements.
Frequently asked questions
How is a HELOC payment calculated?
During the draw period, usually interest only: balance × rate ÷ 12. In the repayment period, as an amortizing loan over the remaining term.
How much can I borrow with a HELOC?
Typically up to a CLTV limit set by the lender, such as 80% to 90% of the home's value minus your mortgage balance, subject to credit and income checks.
Is HELOC interest tax-deductible?
In the US, only when the funds are used to buy, build or substantially improve the home, within IRS limits.
Can the rate change?
Most HELOCs have variable rates that move with an index such as the prime rate. Some lenders let you fix the rate on part of the balance.
What happens at the end of the draw period?
You can no longer borrow, and payments switch to principal plus interest, which can raise the payment sharply.
Can I pay off a HELOC early?
Usually yes, but check for early closure fees.
