H1: Payment Calculator
This payment calculator answers the two questions people most often ask about a loan: "what will my payment be?" and "if I pay a fixed amount each month, how long until it's paid off?" Enter the loan amount, interest rate and term to get the monthly payment, or enter the amount you can afford each month to find how many payments it will take.
How to use this calculator
Fixed term (find the payment)
1. Loan amount: the principal you will borrow or currently owe.
2. Interest rate: the annual rate on the loan.
3. Loan term (years) and Loan term (months): the repayment period.
Fixed payment (find the time)
1. Loan amount and interest rate as above.
2. Monthly payment: the amount you'll pay each month.
Results: monthly payment (or number of payments), total of payments, total interest paid, and an amortization chart.
The formulas
Payment for a fixed term
PMT = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Number of payments for a fixed payment
n = −ln(1 − P × r ÷ PMT) ÷ ln(1 + r)
P: loan amount
r: annual rate ÷ 12
n: number of monthly payments
PMT: monthly payment
The term formula only works if PMT is more than one month's interest (P × r). If it isn't, the balance never falls. When n isn't a whole number, the last payment is smaller than the others.

Understanding your results
Monthly payment covers principal and interest. Taxes, insurance and fees are extra.
Total interest is the full cost of borrowing if you keep to the schedule.
A shorter term means a higher payment but much less interest.
The chart shows how the balance falls slowly at first, then faster.
Monthly vs bi-weekly payments
Some lenders allow payments every two weeks. On the $20,000 loan at 7.5%, a 5-year schedule of 130 bi-weekly payments costs $184.71 each, with total interest of $4,012.32, slightly less than monthly because the balance falls a little sooner. A different idea is to pay half the monthly payment ($200.38) every two weeks: that adds up to 13 monthly payments a year and pays the loan off months early. Check that your lender applies bi-weekly payments as they arrive rather than holding them until the monthly due date.
Choosing a payment
A comfortable payment leaves room in your budget for savings and surprises. Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio; you should also check the full monthly budget with the budget calculator. If the payment for the term you want is too high, the options are a longer term (more interest), a smaller loan, or a lower rate.
Country notes
United States: for consumer loans, the Truth in Lending disclosure shows the payment schedule, APR and finance charge.
Canada: lenders must disclose the cost of borrowing. Canadian mortgages compound semi-annually, so for a mortgage use the Canadian mortgage calculator.
United Kingdom: credit agreements show the APR and total amount payable. You have a legal right to repay a regulated personal loan early; the lender may charge compensation of up to 1% of the amount repaid early, or 0.5% if less than a year of the agreement remains [VERIFY on FCA guidance].
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing up rate and APR. Use the interest rate for the payment; compare offers by APR.
Choosing the longest term by default. It keeps payments low but costs the most.
Paying just above the interest. On high-rate debt, small payments take years; test larger amounts.
Forgetting fees rolled into the loan amount.
Quick check
Check that the term is entered in years or months as the form expects.
Frequently asked questions
How is a monthly loan payment calculated?
With the amortization formula PMT = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), using the monthly rate and number of payments.
How long will it take to pay off my loan with a fixed payment?
Use the fixed-payment mode, or the formula n = −ln(1 − P × r ÷ PMT) ÷ ln(1 + r).
Why does a small payment increase save so much?
Every extra dollar goes to principal, which stops interest being charged on it for the rest of the loan.
Does the payment include insurance or taxes?
No. It is principal and interest only.
Can I use this for a mortgage?
Yes, for principal and interest on a fixed-rate loan. The mortgage calculator adds taxes and insurance.
What if the payment doesn't cover the interest?
The loan will never be repaid at that payment. Increase the payment or look for a lower rate.
What happens if I miss a payment?
Interest keeps building on the unpaid balance, and late fees and credit-report damage can follow. If you can't pay, contact the lender before the due date to ask about options.
Is a fixed or variable rate better for planning payments?
A fixed rate gives the same payment for the whole term, which makes budgeting easy. A variable rate can start lower but the payment can rise; test higher rates if you choose one.
