H1: UK Capital Gains Tax Calculator
Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is charged on the profit when you sell or give away an asset that has risen in value, such as shares outside an ISA, a second home or buy-to-let property, or business assets. This calculator estimates CGT for the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) after the annual exempt amount, splitting your gains between the 18% and 24% rates based on your income.
How to use this calculator
1. Taxable income: your income for the year minus your Personal Allowance and any other Income Tax reliefs. On a £45,000 salary with the standard £12,570 allowance, that's £32,430.00.
2. Total gains: the sum of gains on disposals in the tax year (sale proceeds minus purchase cost and allowable costs such as fees, Stamp Duty and improvements).
3. Allowable losses: losses in the same year, plus any unused losses brought forward.
4. Asset type: residential property, other assets, or gains qualifying for Business Asset Disposal Relief.
Results: annual exempt amount used, gain taxed at 18%, gain taxed at 24%, total CGT and the effective rate.

Residential property and other assets now use the same rates. The basic rate band is £37,700.
How it works
1. Gains − losses − £3,000 annual exempt amount = taxable gains.
2. Add taxable gains to taxable income.
3. The part of the gains that falls within the £37,700 basic rate band is taxed at 18%; the rest at 24%.
You can set the exempt amount and losses against the gains charged at the higher rate first, which minimises the tax.
Worked examples
Example 1: £45,000 salary and a £20,000 gain on shares
Taxable income £32,430.00; remaining basic rate band = 37,700 − 32,430 = £5,270.00
Taxable gains = 20,000 − 3,000 = £17,000
18% on £5,270.00 = £948.60
24% on £11,730.00 = £2,815.20
CGT = £3,763.80
Example 2: higher-rate taxpayer with a £10,000 gain
Taxable income £60,000 uses the whole basic rate band.
CGT = 24% × (10,000 − 3,000) = £1,680.00
Example 3: selling a business
A sole trader sells their business with a £100,000 qualifying gain. With Business Asset Disposal Relief at 18% (lifetime limit £1 million of gains), CGT = £17,460.00 after the annual exempt amount.
Example 4: a home that was let for part of the time
You owned a flat for 10 years, lived in it for 6, then let it out, and made a £150,000 gain. Private Residence Relief covers the years you lived there plus the final 9 months: 6.75 ÷ 10 of the gain, or £101,250.00. The remaining £48,750.00 is chargeable (letting relief now applies only if you shared occupancy with the tenant).
Example 5: sharing a gain with a spouse
Shares with a £20,000 gain are moved to joint names before sale (transfers between spouses and civil partners are free of CGT). Each partner has a £10,000 gain, less their own £3,000 exempt amount, so £7,000 is taxable each.
If both are basic-rate taxpayers with enough band left: 18% × 7,000 × 2 = £2,520.00
If one higher-rate partner sold alone: 24% × (20,000 − 3,000) = £4,080.00
Example 6: losses brought forward
A £10,000 gain this year and £12,000 of losses carried forward from earlier years: carried-forward losses are used only to bring the gain down to the £3,000 exempt amount. So £7,000 of the losses is used, no tax is due, and £5,000 of losses carries forward to later years. Losses must be reported to HMRC within four years of the end of the tax year they arose in to be usable.
What's exempt
Your main home, if Private Residence Relief applies in full.
ISAs and pensions: no CGT on gains inside them. See the ISA calculator.
UK government gilts, Premium Bonds, private cars, and most personal possessions worth £6,000 or less.
Gifts to your spouse or civil partner (no gain, no loss) and to charity.
Reporting and paying
UK residential property: report and pay any CGT within 60 days of completion using HMRC's online service.
Other assets: report on your Self Assessment return by 31 January after the tax year, or use the real-time CGT service if you don't file a return.
Keep records of purchase costs, fees and improvements.
Understanding your results
Effective rate = CGT ÷ total gains. It's lower than 18% for small gains because of the exempt amount.
Scottish taxpayers still use the UK basic rate band for CGT, because CGT isn't devolved.
Pension contributions and Gift Aid extend your basic rate band, which can move gains from 24% to 18%.
Tips and common mistakes
Wasting the annual exempt amount. It can't be carried forward; spread disposals across tax years where you can.
Not claiming losses. Losses must be reported within four years to be used.
Forgetting allowable costs like Stamp Duty, legal fees and improvements.
Missing the 60-day deadline for property.
Bed and breakfasting: shares bought back within 30 days are matched to the sale, so you can't crystallise a loss that way.
Frequently asked questions
What is the CGT allowance for 2026/27?
£3,000 for individuals.
What are the CGT rates for 2026/27?
18% within the basic rate band, 24% above it.
Do I pay CGT on my home?
Usually not, if it has been your only or main residence throughout.
How long do I have to pay CGT on property?
60 days from completion for UK residential property.
Can my spouse and I both use the allowance?
Yes. Transferring assets between spouses is tax-free, so couples can use two allowances and both basic rate bands.
Do I pay CGT on shares in an ISA?
No.
