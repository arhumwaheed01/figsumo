H1: LTT Calculator (Wales)
Land Transaction Tax (LTT) is the Welsh equivalent of Stamp Duty. It's paid by buyers of property in Wales and collected by the Welsh Revenue Authority. This calculator works out LTT on a residential purchase using the main rates or, for second homes and buy-to-let, the higher residential rates.
How to use this calculator
1. Purchase price: the chargeable consideration, usually the agreed price.
2. Rates: main residential rates if this will be your only home (or replaces your main home), or higher rates if you'll own two or more homes.
Results: LTT with the tax in each band, the total and the effective rate.

Higher rates are a separate table, not a surcharge added to the main rates. Wales has no first-time buyer relief; the £225,000 nil-rate band applies to every buyer paying main rates.
How it works
LTT = Σ (portion of price in each band × band rate)

When higher rates apply
You'll own two or more residential properties worth £40,000 or more at the end of the day of purchase, and the new one doesn't replace your main residence.
Companies and most other non-individual buyers pay higher rates on residential property.
If you buy a new main home before selling your old one, you pay higher rates but can claim a refund if you sell the previous home within the allowed period [VERIFY current period with the WRA].
Property owned anywhere in the world counts.
Paying LTT
Your solicitor or conveyancer submits the LTT return and pays the tax to the Welsh Revenue Authority, normally within 30 days of completion. A return is needed for most residential purchases of £40,000 or more.

Wales has the highest nil-rate band of the three, so buyers below about £350,000 pay less than in England (the two are equal at £350,000, at £7,500). Above that, the 6% and 7.5% Welsh rates make LTT the larger bill. The comparison only matters if you're choosing between areas near a border; the tax depends on where the property is, not where you live.
Understanding your results
Cash cost at completion. Budget for LTT alongside legal fees and surveys.
The effective rate helps compare prices across bands.
England, Scotland and Northern Ireland use different systems: see the Stamp Duty calculator for England and Northern Ireland and the LBTT calculator for Scotland.
Tips and common mistakes
Applying English first-time buyer relief in Wales. It doesn't exist here.
Adding a surcharge to main rates instead of using the higher-rates table.
Forgetting overseas property when deciding whether higher rates apply.
Not reclaiming higher rates after selling your old main home.
Reliefs and special cases
The calculator covers standard residential purchases. Some situations need different treatment and usually a conveyancer's advice:
Multiple dwellings bought in one transaction, which may be taxed differently.
Mixed-use property, such as a shop with a flat above, which uses non-residential rates.
Shared ownership and other schemes where you buy a share of the property.
Transfers between spouses or on divorce, which are often exempt.
Gifts where no money changes hands, unless the transfer includes taking on a mortgage.
The Welsh Revenue Authority publishes guidance on each of these [VERIFY current WRA guidance before relying on it].
What to have ready
Have the agreed price, the completion date and details of any other residential property you own. In Wales there's no first-time buyer relief, so the main rates apply to everyone buying a single home. If you'll own more than one home, the higher residential rates apply. Your solicitor files the return with the Welsh Revenue Authority and pays the tax, normally within 30 days. [VERIFY]
Frequently asked questions
Do first-time buyers pay LTT?
Only on the portion above £225,000, the same as other main-rate buyers. There's no separate relief.
How much is LTT on a £250,000 house?
£1,500 at main rates (6% on £25,000).
What are the higher rates for second homes in Wales?
From 5% up to 17% across six bands, since 11 December 2024.
Who collects LTT?
The Welsh Revenue Authority.
When do I pay LTT?
Usually within 30 days of completion, through your solicitor.
Does LTT apply to commercial property?
Yes, with separate non-residential rates.
