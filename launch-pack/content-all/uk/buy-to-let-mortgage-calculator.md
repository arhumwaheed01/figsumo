H1: Buy-to-Let Mortgage Calculator
Buy-to-let mortgages are assessed mainly on the rent the property will earn rather than your salary. Lenders apply a rental cover test (the interest coverage ratio, or ICR) at a stressed interest rate to decide how much you can borrow. This calculator works out your loan, loan-to-value, monthly payment on interest-only or repayment terms, and the maximum loan the rent supports under the ICR test you enter.
How to use this calculator
1. Property value or purchase price.
2. Deposit: typically at least 25% for buy-to-let.
3. Interest rate and term.
4. Repayment type: interest-only (most common for buy-to-let) or capital repayment.
5. Expected monthly rent: from local letting agents or comparable listings.
6. Lender ICR (%) and stress rate (%): these vary by lender and product. 125% is common for basic-rate taxpayers and limited companies; 145% for higher-rate taxpayers. Stress rates are often around 5% to 5.5%, or the pay rate for five-year fixes [VERIFY with your lender or broker].
Results: loan, LTV, monthly payment, maximum loan by rental cover, a pass/fail check and the gross rental yield.
How it works
Loan = Value − Deposit
Interest-only payment = Loan × Rate ÷ 12
Repayment payment = Loan × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Maximum loan (rental cover) = Annual rent ÷ (ICR × Stress rate)
Gross yield = Annual rent ÷ Value
The lender will lend the lower of the LTV limit and the rental-cover limit.
Worked examples
Example 1: £300,000 property, 25% deposit, £1,500 rent
Loan = £225,000.00 (75% LTV)
Interest-only at 4.8%: £900.00 a month
Capital repayment over 25 years: £1,289.24 a month
Rental cover at 145% and 5.5%: maximum loan = 18,000 ÷ (1.45 × 0.055) = £225,705.33. The loan passes, just.
The test needs rent of at least £1,495.31 a month for this loan.
Gross yield: 6.00%
At a 125% ICR (for example, a limited company or basic-rate taxpayer), the same rent supports up to £261,818.18.
Example 2: how much rent for a £250,000 loan?
At 145% and 5.5%: 250,000 × 1.45 × 0.055 ÷ 12 = £1,661.46 a month.
Example 3: tax on the rental profit (individual landlord)
Rent £18,000 a year, letting and maintenance costs £2,700, mortgage interest £10,800, higher-rate taxpayer:
Taxable rental profit (interest not deductible): £15,300.00
Tax at 40% less a 20% credit on the interest (£2,160.00): £3,960.00
Cash left after tax: £540.00
Through a limited company, mortgage interest is deductible and profit is taxed at Corporation Tax rates (19% on profits up to £50,000: £855.00 here), but extracting the money as dividends adds personal tax. Get advice before choosing a structure.
Example 4: the basic-rate ICR
For a basic-rate taxpayer or a limited company at a 125% ICR and the same 5.5% stress rate, a £250,000 loan needs rent of 250,000 × 1.25 × 0.055 ÷ 12 = £1,432.29 a month, £229.17 less than at 145%.
Example 5: gross and net yield
Example 1's property at £300,000 with £1,500 a month rent has a gross yield of 18,000 ÷ 300,000 = 6.00%. After £10,800 of mortgage interest and £2,400 of other costs, the pre-tax profit is £4,800. On the £75,000 deposit, that's a return of 6.40% before tax, stamp duty and fees, and before any change in the property's value.
Understanding your results
Rental cover is often the binding limit, especially in high-price, low-yield areas.
Interest-only means the loan isn't repaid. You need a plan to clear it at the end of the term, usually by selling.
Fees: buy-to-let products often have arrangement fees of 1% to 3% of the loan; adding them to the loan affects the rental cover test.
Voids and costs: the ICR test ignores your other costs. Use the rental property calculator for cash flow.
Costs and rules for landlords
Stamp Duty: in England and Northern Ireland, additional properties carry a 5% surcharge on top of standard SDLT rates. Scotland adds an 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement and Wales has higher LTT rates; see the LTT calculator.
Section 24: individual landlords can't deduct mortgage interest; they get a basic-rate tax credit instead.
Capital Gains Tax applies when you sell, at 18% or 24% on residential property gains; see the CGT calculator.
Regulation: most buy-to-let mortgages aren't regulated by the FCA, except "consumer buy-to-let" (for example, letting a former home). Landlords must meet safety, deposit protection and licensing rules.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the pay rate instead of the stress rate in the ICR test.
Forgetting the higher ICR for higher-rate taxpayers.
Assuming rent will rise to cover a rate increase at remortgage.
Ignoring the tax change that removed mortgage interest deductions for individuals.
Notes for US and Canadian readers
US: investors often use conventional investment-property mortgages with 15% to 25% down, or "DSCR" loans sized on rental income, a similar idea to the UK rental cover test. Mortgage interest on rentals is deductible against rental income.
Canada: a rental property that you won't live in normally needs at least 20% down, because CMHC insurance is generally limited to owner-occupied homes of up to four units. Lenders often count only part of the expected rent toward qualifying [VERIFY lender policy].
Frequently asked questions
How much deposit do I need for buy-to-let?
Usually at least 25%, although some lenders accept 20% or less at higher rates.
What is the ICR?
The interest coverage ratio: the rent must cover the stressed interest cost by a set percentage, often 125% or 145%.
Can I get an interest-only buy-to-let mortgage?
Yes, most buy-to-let mortgages are interest-only.
Is my salary relevant?
Some lenders require a minimum personal income, often around £25,000, and may use it to top up a rental shortfall.
Should I buy through a limited company?
It can suit higher-rate taxpayers with several properties, but costs, rates and extraction taxes differ. Take advice.
What yield is good for buy-to-let?
Gross yields vary widely by region; compare net yield after costs, not just gross.
