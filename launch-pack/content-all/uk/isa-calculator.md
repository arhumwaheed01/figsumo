H1: ISA Calculator (2026/27)
An Individual Savings Account (ISA) shelters interest, dividends and capital gains from UK tax. In 2026/27 you can put in up to £20,000 across all your ISAs. A Lifetime ISA (LISA) adds a 25% government bonus on up to £4,000 of that. This calculator tracks how your allowance is split, adds any LISA bonus, and projects tax-free growth for cash or investments.
How to use this calculator
1. Your age: used for the LISA checks and the cash ISA change coming in 2027.
2. Planned contributions for 2026/27 by ISA type: cash, stocks and shares, Lifetime and innovative finance. The calculator warns you if the total goes over £20,000.
3. Contribution pattern: a lump sum or monthly.
4. Expected rate: an AER for cash, or an assumed average return for investments (try a range).
5. Years to project, and whether to repeat the same contribution each year.
The formula
Allowance check (2026/27):
cash + stocks & shares + LISA + innovative finance ≤ £20,000
LISA ≤ £4,000 (counts inside the £20,000)
LISA bonus = 25% × LISA contributions (max £1,000 a year)
Growth (annual contributions at year-end):
FV = B × (1 + r)^n + C × ((1 + r)^n − 1) / r
For monthly saving, the calculator uses the monthly equivalent rate.
LISA withdrawal charge: if you withdraw for any reason other than buying your first home, reaching 60 or terminal illness, a 25% charge applies to the amount withdrawn.
Worked examples
Example 1: using the full allowance for 10 years. £20,000 a year, 5% a year, 10 years, starting from £0:
FV = 20,000 × (1.05^10 − 1) / 0.05 = £251,557.85
Contributions: £200,000
Tax-free growth: £51,557.85
A smaller plan: £5,000 a year at 4% for 10 years gives £60,030.54. Of that, £10,030.54 is interest that would otherwise count towards your Personal Savings Allowance.
Example 2: the Lifetime ISA bonus, and the cost of the withdrawal charge.
Pay in £4,000 → bonus £1,000 → £5,000 in the account (before growth)
If you withdraw that £5,000 for a non-qualifying reason, the 25% charge is £1,250, so you get back £3,750. That is £250 less than you paid in. The charge removes the bonus and takes 6.25% of your own money.
Example 3: a cash ISA versus the Personal Savings Allowance. A higher-rate taxpayer with £30,000 at 4.5% earns £1,350 of interest a year. Outside an ISA, £500 is covered by the Personal Savings Allowance and £850 is taxed at 40%, costing £340.00 a year. In a cash ISA, the tax is nil. A basic-rate taxpayer earning the same interest would pay 20% on £350 (£70.00).
Example 4: a stocks and shares ISA over 20 years. £300 a month at an assumed 6% average annual return (compounded monthly) grows to about £138,612.27, from £72,000 of contributions. All growth and dividends are tax-free, and nothing needs to go on a tax return.
Understanding your results
Allowance used / remaining: across all ISA types for the current tax year. Unused allowance doesn't carry forward after 5 April.
Projected value: with the LISA bonus included where applicable. Investment values can fall as well as rise.
Tax saved: for comparison, the calculator can estimate the tax you'd pay on the same interest or dividends outside an ISA. It uses your band and the Personal Savings and Dividend Allowances.
Country notes
LISA rules: you must be 18 or over but under 40 to open one, and you can pay in until you turn 50. For a first home, the property must cost £450,000 or less, and you must buy it at least 12 months after your first payment in.
Cash ISA change: from 6 April 2027, the annual cash ISA limit for people under 65 falls to £12,000, within the £20,000 overall limit. For those 65 or older, cash ISA saving stays within the full £20,000. The calculator will apply this from the 2027/28 tax year.
Comparable accounts: Canada's TFSA and the US Roth IRA.
Notes for US and Canadian readers
The nearest equivalents are the Roth IRA in the US, which also gives tax-free growth but has income limits and withdrawal rules (see the Roth IRA calculator), and the TFSA in Canada, whose unused room carries forward, unlike the ISA allowance (see the TFSA calculator). UK residents who move abroad can keep an ISA but can't add new money while non-resident, and the other country may tax the income.
Tips and common mistakes
Paying more than £20,000 into ISAs in one tax year. The excess isn't tax-free and must be removed.
Withdrawing from a Lifetime ISA early. The 25% charge takes back more than the bonus.
Moving money between ISAs by withdrawing it. Use an ISA transfer so it keeps its tax-free status.
Leaving the allowance unused. It doesn't carry forward after 5 April.
Frequently asked questions
What is the ISA allowance for 2026/27?
£20,000 in total across cash, stocks and shares, innovative finance and Lifetime ISAs. Up to £4,000 of it can go into a Lifetime ISA.
Can I pay into more than one ISA in a year?
Yes. GOV.UK says you can save up to £20,000 in one account or split it across several accounts, as long as the total stays within the limit. The exception is the Lifetime ISA: you can pay into only one in a tax year. If your ISA is "flexible", money you take out and put back in the same tax year doesn't use up extra allowance.
How does the Lifetime ISA bonus work?
The government adds 25% of what you pay in, up to £1,000 a year on £4,000 of contributions, until you turn 50.
What happens if I take money out of a Lifetime ISA early?
Unless it's for a qualifying first-home purchase, after age 60 or due to terminal illness, a 25% withdrawal charge applies. As Example 2 shows, you can get back less than you paid in.
Do I pay tax on ISA interest or gains?
No UK Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax is due on interest, dividends or gains inside an ISA, and you don't need to declare them.
Is the cash ISA limit changing?
Yes. From 6 April 2027, people under 65 will be able to put at most £12,000 a year into cash ISAs, within the £20,000 overall limit.
