H1: UK Income Tax Calculator (2026/27)
UK Income Tax is charged on three kinds of income in a fixed order: earnings and pensions first, then savings interest, then dividends. Each kind has its own allowances and, for dividends, its own rates. This calculator applies the 2026/27 HMRC figures in that order. It shows how much of each band you use, the tax on each slice, and your effective and marginal rates. Scottish taxpayers get the Scottish bands for earnings.
How to use this calculator
1. Employment, self-employment and pension income: your total for the tax year.
2. Savings interest from bank accounts and bonds held outside ISAs.
3. Dividends from shares held outside ISAs and pensions.
4. Scottish taxpayer? Tick this if you live in Scotland.
5. Gross pension contributions under relief at source, and Gift Aid donations (optional): these extend your basic-rate band.
The formula
Adjusted net income (ANI)  = total income − gross relief-at-source pension contributions − grossed-up Gift Aid
Personal Allowance          = £12,570 − max(0, (ANI − £100,000) ÷ 2)       (zero at £125,140)
Order of taxation           = non-savings income → savings → dividends
Basic-rate band             = £37,700 (+ gross pension contributions / Gift Aid)
Non-savings income (England, Wales, NI): 20% on the first £37,700 of taxable income, 40% up to £125,140, 45% above. Scotland uses six bands: 19%, 20%, 21%, 42%, 45% and 48%.
Savings:
Starting rate for savings: up to £5,000 at 0%, reduced £1 for £1 by non-savings income above the Personal Allowance
Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate, £0 for additional-rate
Above these, savings are taxed at 20%, 40% or 45%
Dividends: the first £500 is taxed at 0%. Above that, the rate is 10.75% in the basic band, 35.75% in the higher band and 39.35% in the additional band.
Worked examples
Example 1: £60,000 salary, England.
Taxable income: 60,000 − 12,570 = £47,430
20% × 37,700 = £7,540.00
40% × (47,430 − 37,700) = 40% × 9,730 = £3,892.00
Income Tax: £11,432.00. Effective rate 19.05%; marginal rate 40%.
Add £5,000 of dividends: the salary already uses the whole basic-rate band, so the dividends fall in the higher band. £500 is covered by the dividend allowance, and £4,500 × 35.75% = £1,608.75 of dividend tax. Total tax is £13,040.75.
Example 2: £110,000 salary, England.
Personal Allowance: 12,570 − (110,000 − 100,000) ÷ 2 = £7,570
Taxable income: £102,430
20% × 37,700 = £7,540 · 40% × 64,730 = £25,892
Income Tax: £33,432. The marginal rate between £100,000 and £125,140 is effectively 60%.
A gross pension contribution of £10,000 under relief at source would bring adjusted net income back to £100,000 and restore the full allowance.
Scottish comparison, £40,000 salary: Scottish tax is £5,551.07, compared with £5,486.00 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. See the take-home pay calculator for the full breakdown.
Understanding your results
Band usage chart: how much of each band your income fills, coloured by income type. This explains why the same dividend can be taxed at 10.75% for one person and 35.75% for another.
Effective rate: total Income Tax ÷ total income.
Marginal rate: the tax on your next £1 of the type you select. It includes the Personal Allowance taper.
What isn't included: National Insurance (see the take-home pay calculator), Capital Gains Tax, the High Income Child Benefit Charge, the Marriage Allowance and the Blind Person's Allowance.
Country notes
Scotland: Scottish rates apply to earnings, pensions and rental profit. Savings and dividends use the UK rates and bands.
Wales: Welsh rates of Income Tax currently match England and Northern Ireland.
Tax year: UK tax years run from 6 April to 5 April. For calendar-year systems, see the US and Canadian calculators.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting the Personal Allowance taper. Between £100,000 and £125,140 the effective rate is 60%.
Using rest-of-UK bands for a Scottish taxpayer. Scotland sets its own bands on earnings.
Mixing gross and net pension contributions. Relief-at-source contributions extend your basic rate band.
Leaving out savings and dividend income. They're taxed after earnings, at their own rates.
Assuming the tax code is right. Check it in your HMRC personal tax account.
Frequently asked questions
What are the UK Income Tax bands for 2026/27?
Outside Scotland: Personal Allowance £12,570; basic rate 20% on the next £37,700; higher rate 40% up to £125,140; additional rate 45% above. Scotland has six rates, from 19% to 48%.
How does the £100,000 taper work?
The Personal Allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000, so it is gone at £125,140. Pension contributions and Gift Aid reduce adjusted net income and can bring back some or all of the allowance.
How much savings interest can I earn tax-free?
Basic-rate taxpayers can earn £1,000, and higher-rate taxpayers £500, under the Personal Savings Allowance. Additional-rate taxpayers get no allowance. People with low earnings may also get up to £5,000 under the starting rate for savings. Interest inside an ISA is always tax-free.
How are dividends taxed?
After the £500 dividend allowance, dividends are taxed at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35%, depending on which band they fall in once your other income is counted.
Do I need to file a Self Assessment return?
Many employees don't, because PAYE collects the tax. You may need to file if you have untaxed income such as self-employment, rental, or savings or dividends above the allowances. HMRC's online checker gives a definitive answer.
Why is my effective rate so much lower than my band?
Because the Personal Allowance and the lower bands apply first. At £60,000, the effective rate is about 19% even though the top slice is taxed at 40%.
