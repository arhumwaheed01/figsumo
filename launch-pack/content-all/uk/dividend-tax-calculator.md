H1: UK Dividend Tax Calculator
Dividends from UK and overseas companies are taxed at their own rates, after a small tax-free dividend allowance. This calculator estimates the tax on your dividends for 2026/27 (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027), taking account of your other income, the Personal Allowance and the bands your dividends fall into. It's useful for company directors who pay themselves in dividends and for investors with shares outside an ISA.
How to use this calculator
1. Salary and other income: employment income, self-employed profit, pensions and rental profit (non-savings income).
2. Savings interest (optional): taxed before dividends.
3. Dividends: total dividends received in the tax year.
4. Scottish taxpayer: for how your other income is taxed. Dividends themselves use UK-wide rates.
Results: dividend allowance used, dividends taxed at each rate, total dividend tax and the effective rate on dividends.

The Personal Allowance is £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000.
How it works
Income is taxed in a fixed order: non-savings income first, then savings, then dividends on top. So dividends fall into whichever band your other income has reached.
1. Use the Personal Allowance against other income first (any unused allowance covers dividends).
2. The first £500 of dividends is taxed at 0%, but it still uses up band space.
3. Remaining dividends are taxed at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35% according to the band they fall in.
Worked examples
Example 1: £40,000 salary and £10,000 of dividends
Taxable salary = 40,000 − 12,570 = £27,430.00, leaving £10,270.00 of basic rate band
Dividends: £500 at 0%, then £9,500 at 10.75% (all within the remaining band)
Dividend tax = £1,021.25
Example 2: company director taking £12,570 salary and £50,000 dividends
The salary uses the Personal Allowance.
Dividends: £500 at 0%, £37,200 at 10.75% = £3,999.00, and the remaining £12,300 above the basic rate band at 35.75% = £4,397.25
Dividend tax = £8,396.25, an effective rate of 16.79% on the dividends
Directors should also consider employer and employee National Insurance on salary and Corporation Tax on company profits, which this calculator doesn't model.
Example 3: higher-rate taxpayer with £5,000 of dividends
Salary £60,000 means all dividends are in the higher rate band.
Tax = 35.75% × (5,000 − 500) = £1,608.75
Example 4: £100,000 salary and £20,000 of dividends
Adjusted net income is £120,000, so the Personal Allowance falls by (120,000 − 100,000) ÷ 2 = £10,000, to £2,570
Salary uses the basic rate band and part of the higher rate band; all dividends fall in the higher rate band
Dividend tax = 35.75% × 19,500 = £6,971.25
The dividends also cost £10,000 of Personal Allowance, which adds £4,000 of income tax on the salary at 40%. In total, the £20,000 of dividends increases the tax bill by about £10,971.25, an effective rate of 54.9%. Pension contributions that reduce adjusted net income below £100,000 can restore the allowance.

Shares in an ISA
Dividends from shares held in an ISA or pension are tax-free and don't use the dividend allowance. Moving investments into an ISA (up to £20,000 a year) can remove the tax entirely; see the ISA calculator.
Paying dividend tax
Up to £10,000 of dividends: if you don't file a Self Assessment return, ask HMRC to change your tax code, or tell them through your personal tax account.
Over £10,000: you must file a Self Assessment return.
Self Assessment tax is due by 31 January after the tax year, with payments on account for the next year if your bill is over £1,000 and less than 80% of your tax is collected at source.
Understanding your results
The allowance isn't a band extension. The £500 at 0% still counts toward the basic rate limit.
Income above £100,000 reduces the Personal Allowance, which increases tax on both salary and dividends; the effective marginal rate in that range is higher than the headline rate.
High Income Child Benefit Charge and other income tests use total income including dividends.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting dividends sit on top of other income.
Assuming the allowance covers £500 of tax. It covers £500 of dividends.
Overlooking a spouse's allowance and bands. Holding shares jointly or in the lower earner's name can reduce tax (subject to settlements rules for family companies).
Ignoring ISA wrappers for investments.
Notes for US and Canadian readers
The UK system differs from both North American approaches. In the US, qualified dividends are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on taxable income, with ordinary dividends taxed as regular income. In Canada, eligible dividends from Canadian corporations are "grossed up" and then reduced by a dividend tax credit, which can make low-income dividend tax very small or even negative. UK residents receiving US or Canadian dividends may suffer withholding tax abroad, which can usually be credited against UK tax up to the treaty rate [VERIFY treaty rates].
Frequently asked questions
What is the dividend allowance for 2026/27?
£500.
What are the dividend tax rates for 2026/27?
10.75% basic, 35.75% higher and 39.35% additional rate.
Do I pay tax on dividends in an ISA?
No.
Do I pay National Insurance on dividends?
No. Dividends aren't subject to NI.
How do I pay dividend tax?
Through a tax code change for smaller amounts, or Self Assessment if dividends exceed £10,000 or you already file.
Do Scottish rates apply to dividends?
No. Dividends use UK-wide rates, although Scottish rates apply to your other income.
