H1: Salary Sacrifice Calculator
Salary sacrifice (also called salary exchange) is an agreement to give up part of your salary in return for a non-cash benefit from your employer, most often a pension contribution. Because your taxable pay falls, you pay less Income Tax and National Insurance, and your employer saves employer NI too. This calculator shows the effect on your take-home pay and how much each pound into your pension really costs you in 2026/27.
How to use this calculator
1. Annual salary: before the sacrifice.
2. Amount to sacrifice: in pounds or as a percentage of salary.
3. Scottish taxpayer: Scottish Income Tax bands differ.
4. Student loan plan: sacrifice reduces the pay used for student loan repayments.
5. Employer NI passed on: the share of the employer's 15% NI saving it adds to your pension (0% if it keeps the saving).
Results: take-home pay before and after, Income Tax saved, employee NI saved, student loan reduction, employer NI saved, total pension contribution and the net cost to you.
How it works
New salary = Salary − Sacrifice
Net cost = Sacrifice − Income Tax saved − NI saved − Student loan saved

Worked examples
Example 1: £50,000 salary, 5% (£2,500) into a pension
Income Tax saved: 20% × 2,500 = £500.00
NI saved: 8% × 2,500 = £200.00
Net cost to you: £1,800.00 for £2,500 in your pension
Employer NI saved: £375.00. If the employer passes it all on, your pension gets £2,875.00.
Compare a relief-at-source personal pension: you pay £2,000.00 and HMRC adds basic-rate relief to make £2,500.00, but there's no NI saving. Salary sacrifice is £200 cheaper here.
With a Plan 2 student loan, your repayments also fall by £225.00, bringing the net cost down to £1,575.00.
Example 2: bringing a £60,000 salary down to the higher-rate threshold
Sacrifice £9,730.00 to reduce pay to £50,270:
Tax saved at 40%: £3,892.00; NI saved at 2%: £194.60
Net cost: £5,643.40, so each £1 into the pension costs 58p.
Example 3: £110,000 salary, sacrificing £10,000
Pay drops to £100,000, restoring £5,000 of Personal Allowance.
Income Tax saved: 40% × 10,000 + 40% × 5,000 = £6,000.00
NI saved: £200
Net cost: £3,800.00 for £10,000 in the pension
Example 4: £30,000 salary, 3% (£900) into a pension
Income Tax saved: 20% × 900 = £180.00
NI saved: 8% × 900 = £72.00
Net cost to you: £648.00
Employer NI saved: 15% × 900 = £135.00; if passed on, the pension receives £1,035.00
That's £1,035 in the pension for £648 of take-home pay, a 60% uplift before any investment growth.

Scottish taxpayers
Scotland has more Income Tax bands, including a 21% intermediate rate, a 42% higher rate, a 45% advanced rate and a 48% top rate [VERIFY 2026/27 Scottish bands]. National Insurance is the same UK-wide, so a Scottish higher-rate taxpayer saves 42% + 2% = 44% on each pound sacrificed. Tick the Scottish taxpayer box to use these rates.
Other salary sacrifice benefits
Electric car schemes: low benefit-in-kind rates make EV leases through salary sacrifice popular. A £400-a-month lease sacrificed by a higher-rate taxpayer saves about £2,016.00 a year in tax and NI before the benefit-in-kind charge.
Cycle to work, childcare (for schemes that started before October 2018) and extra holiday can also be sacrificed.
Things to check
Minimum wage: the sacrifice can't reduce your cash pay below the National Minimum or Living Wage (£12.71 an hour from April 2026 for workers aged 21+).
Mortgages and credit: lenders see your lower salary, which may reduce how much you can borrow.
Statutory pay: maternity, paternity and sick pay can be based on the lower salary.
Life cover and pay-linked benefits: check whether they use the pre-sacrifice salary.
Annual allowance: total pension contributions over £60,000 a year (tapered for high incomes) can trigger a tax charge.
Future changes: the government announced a £2,000-a-year cap on NI-free salary sacrifice into pensions from April 2029 [VERIFY]. Income Tax relief isn't affected by that announcement.
Understanding your results
Take-home falls by less than the sacrifice, because tax and NI also fall.
Higher earners gain most from the tax relief; basic-rate taxpayers gain most from the NI saving relative to relief at source.
Child Benefit: sacrifice reduces adjusted net income, which can reduce or remove the High Income Child Benefit Charge.
Tips and common mistakes
Sacrificing below the minimum wage. Cash pay can't fall below the National Living Wage.
Forgetting the effect on mortgage applications. Lenders may use your lower salary.
Assuming the employer passes on its NI saving. Ask; many don't.
Ignoring the annual allowance if you're a high earner making large contributions.
Frequently asked questions
Is salary sacrifice worth it?
For pension contributions, it's usually the cheapest way to save because you save NI as well as Income Tax.
Does salary sacrifice affect my State Pension?
Not as long as your earnings stay above the Lower Earnings Limit, so you still get NI credits.
Can I change my salary sacrifice amount?
Usually at set times (often once a year) or after a life event, depending on your employer's scheme.
Does salary sacrifice reduce student loan repayments?
Yes, because repayments are based on your reduced pay.
Do Scottish taxpayers save more?
The relief depends on Scottish bands: 21% for intermediate, 42% for higher and 45% for advanced rates.
Will my employer pass on its NI saving?
Some do, some don't. Ask HR.
