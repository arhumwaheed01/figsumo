H1: UK Take-Home Pay Calculator (2026/27)
Most employees in the UK pay Income Tax and National Insurance through PAYE, and many also repay a student loan and pay into a workplace pension. This calculator applies the 2026/27 rates from HMRC to your salary. It handles the separate Scottish bands, the withdrawal of the Personal Allowance above £100,000, and the three ways pension contributions are commonly taken. It then shows your take-home pay per year, month and week.
How to use this calculator
1. Annual salary before deductions, including any regular bonus you want counted.
2. Where you pay tax: England, Wales or Northern Ireland, or Scotland. This depends on where you live, not where you work.
3. Student loan plan(s): Plan 1, 2, 4 or 5, and/or Postgraduate Loan. If you don't know which plan you are on, check your account with the Student Loans Company.
4. Pension: your contribution as a percent, and how it is taken. Choose net pay (taken before tax), relief at source (taken after tax, with basic-rate relief added by the provider) or salary sacrifice (reduces salary before tax and NI).
5. Tax code (optional): leave as 1257L unless your payslip shows something else.
The formula
Personal Allowance (PA)  = £12,570 − max(0, (adjusted net income − £100,000) ÷ 2)
Taxable income            = salary − pension (net pay / sacrifice) − PA
Income Tax                = bands applied to taxable income
NI (employee, cat. A)     = 8% × (earnings between £12,570 and £50,270) + 2% × earnings above £50,270
Student loan              = 9% × (earnings above plan threshold); Postgraduate Loan 6% above £21,000
Take-home                 = salary − pension − Income Tax − NI − student loan

Worked examples
Example 1: £40,000, England, Plan 2, no pension.
Taxable income: 40,000 − 12,570 = £27,430 → Income Tax 20% = £5,486.00
NI: 8% × (40,000 − 12,570) = £2,194.40
Student loan: 9% × (40,000 − 29,385) = £955.35
Take-home: £31,364.25 a year, £2,613.69 a month
Same salary in Scotland: 19% × 3,967 + 20% × 12,989 + 21% × 10,474 = £753.73 + £2,597.80 + £2,199.54 = £5,551.07 tax, so take-home is £31,299.18, £65.07 less than in England.
With a 5% pension (£2,000) by net pay arrangement: taxable income falls to £25,430, so tax is £5,086.00. NI and the student loan are unchanged, because a net pay pension doesn't reduce earnings for those. Take-home is £29,764.25. The £2,000 contribution cost £1,600 of take-home pay.
Example 2: £110,000, England, no student loan or pension.
Income is £10,000 over £100,000, so the PA falls by £5,000 to £7,570
Taxable income £102,430: 20% × 37,700 + 40% × 64,730 = £7,540 + £25,892 = £33,432
NI: 8% × 37,700 + 2% × 59,730 = £3,016 + £1,194.60 = £4,210.60
Take-home: £72,357.40 a year, £6,029.78 a month
Between £100,000 and £125,140, each extra £1 costs 40p in tax plus 20p from losing 50p of allowance. With NI, that is an effective 62% marginal rate. Pension contributions in this range recover allowance as well as saving tax.
Understanding your results
Monthly take-home assumes the same pay every month and a cumulative tax code. One-off bonuses, emergency tax codes or a mid-year start can make individual payslips differ.
Effective marginal rate: shown for your next £1. For a basic-rate taxpayer on Plan 2 above the threshold, it is 20% + 8% + 9% = 37%.
Pension method matters: salary sacrifice also saves NI. Relief-at-source schemes give basic-rate relief automatically; higher-rate taxpayers claim the extra relief through Self Assessment or by contacting HMRC.
Employer costs: your employer separately pays 15% NI above £5,000 of your earnings. It does not affect your take-home pay.
Country notes
Scotland: Scottish taxpayers pay Scottish Income Tax rates on non-savings, non-dividend income. NI and student loan rules are UK-wide.
Wales: Welsh rates are currently the same as England's.

Tips and common mistakes
Using the wrong tax code. An emergency code can overtax you; check with HMRC.
Forgetting student loan deductions. Select your plan.
Ignoring pension contributions. They change both tax and take-home pay.
Using rest-of-UK rates in Scotland. Tick the Scottish taxpayer option.
Frequently asked questions
What is the Personal Allowance for 2026/27?
£12,570. It falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000, and reaches zero at £125,140.
How much National Insurance do employees pay?
Category A employees pay 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, and 2% above that. Different NI categories apply in some cases, for example people over State Pension age, who pay no employee NI.
When does student loan repayment start?
When your income goes over your plan's threshold. In 2026/27 that is £26,900 for Plan 1, £29,385 for Plan 2, £33,795 for Plan 4 and £25,000 for Plan 5. You repay 9% of income above the threshold. The Postgraduate Loan is 6% above £21,000.
Does salary sacrifice increase take-home pay compared with other pension methods?
For the same pension contribution, it usually leaves you more take-home pay because it also saves employee NI. It can affect other things linked to salary, such as some benefits or mortgage affordability, so check before switching.
Why does my payslip show a different tax?
Your tax code may not be 1257L. You might have taxable benefits, an underpayment being collected, or a bonus in that month. Your payslip and your personal tax account show your actual code.
Is the Scottish rate based on where I work?
No, it is based on where you live. If you live in Scotland and work in England, you pay Scottish rates. Your tax code starts with "S".
What about the High Income Child Benefit Charge?
If you or your partner claim Child Benefit and one of you has adjusted net income over £60,000, a charge applies. It claws back 1% of the Child Benefit for every £200 over £60,000, and all of it at £80,000. This calculator doesn't include it.
