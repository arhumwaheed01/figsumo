H1: LBTT Calculator (Scotland)
Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) replaced Stamp Duty Land Tax in Scotland in 2015. Buyers pay it on residential and commercial purchases above certain prices, and it's collected by Revenue Scotland. This calculator works out LBTT on a Scottish home purchase, including first-time buyer relief and the Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS) for second homes and buy-to-let.
How to use this calculator
1. Purchase price: the chargeable consideration, usually the price agreed.
2. First-time buyer: if you've never owned a home anywhere in the world and will live in this one.
3. Additional dwelling: if you'll own more than one home at the end of the day of purchase and aren't replacing your main residence.
Results: LBTT with the amount in each band, ADS where it applies, the total and the effective rate.

First-time buyer relief raises the nil-rate band to £175,000, saving up to £600.
Additional Dwelling Supplement: 8% of the whole price for transactions on or after 5 December 2024 (contracts entered on or after that date), where the price is £40,000 or more.
How it works
LBTT is charged on each slice of the price at that slice's rate, then the slices are added.
LBTT = Σ (portion of price in band × band rate)
ADS = 8% × total price (if it applies)

Additional Dwelling Supplement: key points
It applies if, at the end of the day of the purchase, you own two or more dwellings and you're not replacing your main residence.
If you buy a new main home before selling your old one, you pay ADS but can claim a repayment if you sell the previous main home within 36 months [VERIFY current period].
It applies to companies buying residential property in most cases.
Properties worth under £40,000 are ignored when counting dwellings.
Paying LBTT
Your solicitor normally submits the LBTT return and pays the tax to Revenue Scotland within 30 days of the effective date (usually the date of entry). A return is required for most residential purchases of £40,000 or more, even when no tax is due.
Understanding your results
Cash cost: LBTT is paid upfront, usually from your own funds. Some lenders allow you to borrow more to cover it, but it then accrues interest.
Effective rate shows tax as a share of the price.
Leases and non-residential property use different tables.
Tips and common mistakes
Using English SDLT rates for a Scottish property. Scotland has its own bands.
Claiming first-time buyer relief jointly when one buyer has owned a home before. All buyers must qualify.
Forgetting ADS when keeping your old home.
Missing the ADS refund deadline after selling your former main residence.
How LBTT differs from SDLT and LTT
Lower starting point: Scotland's nil-rate band (£145,000) is above England's £125,000 but well below Wales's £225,000.
First-time buyers: relief is small in Scotland (worth up to £600), larger in England (no SDLT up to £300,000 on homes up to £500,000) and absent in Wales.
Second homes: Scotland adds an 8% supplement on the whole price; England adds 5 points to every band; Wales uses a separate higher-rates table.

What to have ready
Before you run the numbers, gather the agreed price, whether you're a first-time buyer, and whether you'll own any other home worldwide on the date of completion. Owning another home usually triggers the Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS), payable on the whole price. If you're replacing your main home and sell the old one within the allowed period, you may be able to reclaim the ADS from Revenue Scotland, so keep completion dates and receipts. Frequently asked questions
How much LBTT will I pay on a £250,000 house?
£2,100 (2% on the £105,000 above £145,000), or £1,500 as a first-time buyer.
What is the first-time buyer relief in Scotland?
The nil-rate band rises from £145,000 to £175,000, saving up to £600.
What is the ADS rate?
8% of the total price from 5 December 2024.
Who pays LBTT?
The buyer.
Can ADS be refunded?
Yes, if you sell your previous main residence within the allowed period after buying the new one.
Does LBTT apply to the whole of the UK?
No. England and Northern Ireland have SDLT; Wales has Land Transaction Tax (see the LTT calculator).
