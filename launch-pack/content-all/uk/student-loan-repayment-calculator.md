H1: UK Student Loan Repayment Calculator
UK student loans aren't repaid like a normal loan. You repay a percentage of your income above a threshold, and any balance left after the write-off period is cancelled. That means the amount you owe doesn't change your monthly repayment. This calculator works out your 2026/27 repayments for each plan type from your salary, per payslip and per year.
How to use this calculator
1. Plan type: Plan 1, Plan 2, Plan 4 (Scotland), Plan 5, or Postgraduate Loan. Tick more than one if you have several. Your payslip or your online repayment account shows your plan.
2. Annual salary: gross pay before tax, including bonuses and overtime.
3. Pay frequency: monthly or weekly, since thresholds are applied per pay period.
4. Optional projection: current balance and expected pay rises, to estimate whether you'll repay in full before write-off.
Results: repayment per pay period, repayment per year and, for multiple plans, how the deduction is split.

Which plan? Plan 1: English or Welsh students who started before September 2012, and Northern Ireland students. Plan 2: English or Welsh students who started between September 2012 and July 2023. Plan 4: Scottish students. Plan 5: English students starting courses from 1 August 2023.
The formula
Repayment = Rate × (Income − Threshold), if income exceeds the threshold
Employers apply this to each pay period using the monthly or weekly threshold and round the deduction down to the pound. If you're on more than one undergraduate plan, you repay 9% over the lowest threshold, split between plans. A Postgraduate Loan is repaid at 6% on top.

Example 5: checking a monthly payslip
On a Plan 2 loan with £3,000 of pay in a month, the deduction is 9% × (3,000 − 2,448) = £49.68. Payroll works on each pay period separately, so a one-off bonus month can trigger a larger deduction even if your annual pay is below the threshold. If that happens and your total income for the year is under the threshold, you can ask the Student Loans Company for a refund.
Interest and write-off
Interest is added from the first payment, whether or not you're repaying. Current rates (from GOV.UK):
Plan 1, Plan 4 and Plan 5: 4.1%
Postgraduate: 6%
Plan 2: 4.1% if you earn £29,385 or less, rising on a sliding scale to 6% at £52,885 or more
Rates are linked to RPI and change each September, with caps applied when market rates are higher. Remaining balances are written off after a set period, for example 30 years after the April you were first due to repay for Plan 2 and 40 years for Plan 5 [VERIFY write-off terms for each plan].
Will you repay in full? Many Plan 2 borrowers won't. A £45,000 Plan 2 balance at 6% accrues about £2,700.00 of interest a year, while someone earning £60,000 repays £2,755.35. If your repayments won't clear the balance before write-off, overpaying voluntarily may not save you money. If they will, overpaying cuts total interest.
Understanding your results
It works like a tax. For a basic-rate taxpayer on Plan 2, each extra £1 above the threshold loses 20% income tax, 8% National Insurance and 9% student loan: 37% in total.
Self-employed or extra income: HMRC collects repayments through Self Assessment on your total income above the annual threshold.
Two jobs: each employer deducts only if that job's pay is over the threshold; Self Assessment may top up.
Refunds: if your annual income ends up below the threshold but deductions were taken in high-paid months, you can ask for a refund.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the wrong plan. Thresholds differ by thousands of pounds.
Counting the balance when budgeting. The monthly repayment depends only on income.
Overpaying without checking write-off. It's money you might never have needed to repay.
Forgetting salary sacrifice reduces repayments because it lowers your pay. See the salary sacrifice calculator.
Frequently asked questions
How much will I repay on Plan 2?
9% of income above £29,385 a year (£2,448 a month).
Does my balance change my monthly repayment?
No. Repayments depend on income only.
When are student loans written off?
After a period that depends on your plan, for example 30 years for Plan 2 and 40 years for Plan 5.
What happens if I move abroad?
You must tell the Student Loans Company; repayments are based on overseas income thresholds.
Should I overpay my student loan?
Only if you're likely to repay in full before write-off. Otherwise, overpaying may not reduce what you'll pay in total.
Is a postgraduate loan repaid separately?
Yes, at 6% above £21,000, alongside any undergraduate plan.
