H1: Personal Loan Calculator
A personal loan is a fixed sum, usually unsecured, that you repay in equal monthly installments over a set term. People use them to consolidate card debt, pay for home improvements, cover medical bills or make a large purchase. This calculator gives the monthly payment, the total you'll repay and the total interest, with a chart of how the balance falls.
How to use this calculator
1. Loan amount: how much you want to borrow. If the lender deducts an origination fee from the funds, see the fee section below.
2. Interest rate: the annual rate from your offer or prequalification. Personal loan rates depend heavily on credit score and income.
3. Loan term (years) and Loan term (months): most personal loans run 1 to 7 years.
Results: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest paid, the rate, and an amortization chart.
The formula
PMT = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Total interest = PMT × n − P
P: loan amount
r: annual rate ÷ 12
n: years × 12 + months
Worked examples
Example 1: $20,000 at 7.5% over 5 years
PMT = $400.76
Total of payments $24,045.54; interest $4,045.54
Example 2: $10,000 at 12.99% over 3 years with a 5% origination fee
PMT = $336.89; interest $2,128.09
The 5% fee ($500) is deducted, so you receive $9,500.00 but repay $10,000 plus interest. The real cost, as an APR, is about 16.62%.
To receive a full $10,000 after the fee, you'd need to borrow about $10,526.32 (10,000 ÷ 0.95), which raises the payment to $354.62.
Why people use personal loans to clear cards
Paying $336.89 a month against a $10,000 card balance at 24.99% would take 47 months and cost $5,736.69 in interest, versus $2,128.09 on the 3-year loan. The saving only holds if the cards aren't run up again.
Example 3: consolidating three cards
Balances of $3,000 at 22.99%, $4,500 at 19.99% and $2,500 at 26.99% total $10,000 at a weighted average rate of 22.64%. A 3-year personal loan at 11% would cost $327.39 a month and $1,785.94 in total interest. Paying the same $327.39 a month across the cards at 22.64% would take longer and cost more, so the loan helps, provided the cards aren't run up again.
Example 4: paying off early
On Example 1's $20,000 loan, the balance after 24 payments is about $12,883.56. Paying it off then saves the remaining interest of about $1,543.76 (the 36 remaining payments of $400.76 total $14,427.32), assuming no prepayment fee.

Understanding your results
Monthly payment is fixed for the term (for fixed-rate loans, the most common type).
Total interest is the cost of borrowing if you make every payment on schedule.
Fees aren't included unless you add them to the amount. Compare offers by APR with the APR calculator.
Early payoff: many personal loans can be paid off early with no penalty, which cuts the interest.
What affects your rate
Credit score and history: the biggest factor. Better credit means lower rates.
Debt-to-income ratio: lenders check whether the new payment is affordable; see the debt-to-income calculator.
Term: longer terms often carry slightly higher rates.
Secured vs unsecured: loans backed by savings or a vehicle can be cheaper.
Lender type: banks, credit unions and online lenders price differently. Prequalification with a soft credit check lets you compare without affecting your score.
Country notes
United States: federal credit unions can't charge more than an 18% APR on most loans under NCUA rules [VERIFY current cap]. Lenders must disclose the APR and finance charge (Truth in Lending). The CFPB warns about advance-fee loan scams: legitimate lenders don't ask for fees before approving a loan.
Canada: the federal criminal interest rate cap fell to 35% APR on 1 January 2025 [VERIFY]. Provinces regulate payday and high-cost lenders.
United Kingdom: personal loans are regulated under the Consumer Credit Act and FCA rules. Lenders advertise a "representative APR" that at least 51% of approved applicants receive; your rate may be higher. You have a 14-day right to withdraw from a credit agreement.
Tips and common mistakes
Comparing rates without fees. Use APR.
Borrowing more than you need because you're approved for it.
Choosing the longest term for the lowest payment. It costs the most interest.
Applying to many lenders at once with hard checks; use prequalification first.
Ignoring the reason for borrowing. For consolidation, have a plan to avoid new debt.
Quick check
Ask each lender whether an origination fee is deducted from the amount you receive.
Frequently asked questions
How is a personal loan payment calculated?
With the standard amortization formula using the monthly rate and the number of payments.
What is an origination fee?
A one-time fee, often a percentage of the loan, usually deducted from the money you receive. It raises the APR above the interest rate.
Is a personal loan better than a credit card?
For a planned expense or consolidation, a personal loan usually has a lower rate and a fixed payoff date. Cards can be cheaper if you can repay within a 0% promotional period.
Can I pay off a personal loan early?
Often yes, without penalty, but check the agreement for prepayment fees.
Does a personal loan affect my credit score?
Applying causes a hard inquiry; on-time payments build positive history; missed payments hurt it.
What term should I choose?
The shortest term with a payment you can comfortably afford.
