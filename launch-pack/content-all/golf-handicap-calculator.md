H1: Golf Handicap Calculator
A golf handicap lets players of different abilities compete fairly. Under the World Handicap System (WHS), used in the US, Canada, the UK and most golfing countries since 2020, each round produces a score differential that measures how well you played relative to the course's difficulty. This calculator works out that differential for a round, and shows how differentials build into a Handicap Index.
How to use this calculator
1. Score: your adjusted gross score for 18 holes (default 92).
2. Course rating: the expected score for a scratch golfer from your tees, printed on the scorecard (default 72.1).
3. Slope rating: how much harder the course plays for a bogey golfer, from 55 to 155; 113 is average (default 125).
4. Course par: default 72.
Results: score differential and score vs par.
The formula
score differential = (113 ÷ slope rating) × (adjusted gross score − course rating − PCC)
113: the slope of a course of standard difficulty
PCC (playing conditions calculation): an adjustment from −1 to +3 applied by your golf authority when conditions are unusually hard or easy. The calculator assumes 0.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
(113 ÷ 125) × (92 − 72.1) = 18.0; you finished +20 against par.
Example 2: an easier course
Score 85, rating 70.2, slope 118: (113 ÷ 118) × (85 − 70.2) = 14.2.
Example 3: from differentials to a Handicap Index
Your Handicap Index is the average of the lowest 8 of your most recent 20 differentials. Suppose your best eight are 15.8, 16.1, 16.4, 16.9, 17.2, 17.5, 17.9, 18.0. Their average is 17.0: your Handicap Index.
Example 4: Course Handicap
Before a round, convert your index to strokes for the tees you're playing:
Course Handicap = Handicap Index × (slope ÷ 113) + (course rating − par)
With index 17.0, slope 131, rating 73.4 and par 72: 21.11, rounded to 21 strokes.
Adjusted gross score
For handicap purposes, your score on any hole is capped at net double bogey: par + 2 + any handicap strokes you receive on that hole. One disastrous hole won't wreck your differential. Until you have an index, the maximum is par + 5 per hole.
Fewer than 20 rounds
New players can get an index after 54 holes (any combination of 9- and 18-hole rounds). With fewer than 20 differentials, a sliding scale applies: for example, the lowest one with an adjustment for 3 rounds, rising to the lowest 8 at 20 [VERIFY the full table with your national association].
Who runs handicaps
US: the USGA, through state and regional associations (GHIN).
Canada: Golf Canada.
UK: England Golf, Scottish Golf, Wales Golf and Golf Ireland, under the R&A.
Only an official index from your association is valid for competitions; this calculator is for estimates and tracking.
Understanding your results
Lower differentials are better. A scratch golfer averages around 0 on their best rounds.
Differentials reflect your better rounds, since the index averages the best 8 of 20; typical scores run a few shots above your index.
The maximum Handicap Index is 54.0.
Slope matters: the same score on a harder (higher slope) course gives a lower differential.
Tips and common mistakes
Using par instead of course rating in the formula.
Forgetting the net double bogey cap.
Using the wrong tees' ratings.
Mixing 9-hole and 18-hole scores without the official 9-hole method.
Frequently asked questions
How is a golf handicap calculated?
Average the lowest 8 of your last 20 score differentials.
What is a score differential?
(113 ÷ slope) × (adjusted score − course rating − PCC).
What is a slope rating?
A measure of how much harder a course is for a bogey golfer than for a scratch golfer.
What is net double bogey?
The maximum hole score for handicap purposes: par + 2 + strokes received.
How many rounds do I need?
Scores from 54 holes to get an initial index.
Is the index the same as my course handicap?
No; the course handicap adjusts the index for the tees you play.
