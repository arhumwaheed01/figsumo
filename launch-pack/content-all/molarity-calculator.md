H1: Molarity Calculator
Molarity is the most common way chemists express concentration: the number of moles of a substance dissolved per litre of solution. Enter the amount in moles and the solution volume in litres, and this calculator returns the molarity in mol/L (M).
How to use this calculator
1. Amount (mol): moles of solute (default 0.5).
2. Volume (L): total volume of the solution, not just the solvent (default 1).
Result: molarity in mol/L.
Have grams instead of moles? Divide the mass by the molar mass first (see the molecular weight calculator).
The formula
M = n ÷ V
n = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)
M: molarity (mol/L)
n: moles of solute
V: solution volume (L)
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
0.5 mol in 1 L = 0.5 M
Example 2: from grams (sodium chloride)
5.844 g of NaCl (molar mass 58.44 g/mol) is 0.1000 mol. Dissolved and made up to 250 mL (0.250 L): 0.1000 ÷ 0.250 = 0.4000 M.
Example 3: how much to weigh out
To make 500 mL of 0.1 M glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆, 180.16 g/mol): moles needed = 0.1 × 0.5 = 0.05 mol; mass = 0.05 × 180.16 = 9.008 g.
Example 4: dilution
To make 250 mL of 0.1 M solution from a 2.0 M stock: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂, so V₁ = 0.1 × 250 ÷ 2.0 = 12.5 mL of stock, topped up to 250 mL with water.
Preparing a solution correctly
1. Weigh the solute.
2. Dissolve it in less water than the final volume.
3. Transfer to a volumetric flask and add water up to the mark.
4. Mix thoroughly.
Adding solute to exactly 1 L of water gives slightly more than 1 L of solution, so the concentration would be a little low.
Related concentration units
Unit
Definition
Molarity (M)
mol solute per L solution
Millimolar (mM)
0.001 M; 0.25 M = 250 mM
Molality (m)
mol solute per kg solvent (doesn't change with temperature)
Mass percent (% w/w)
g solute per 100 g solution
% w/v
g solute per 100 mL solution
ppm
mg per kg (≈ mg/L for dilute water solutions)
Normality (N)
equivalents per L (e.g. 1 M H₂SO₄ = 2 N for acid–base)
Understanding your results
Temperature: volume changes with temperature, so molarity does too, slightly. Molality avoids this.
Significant figures: report to the precision of your measurements (see the rounding calculator).
Ionic compounds dissociate: 0.1 M CaCl₂ gives 0.1 M Ca²⁺ and 0.2 M Cl⁻.
Tips and common mistakes
Using mL as L. 250 mL is 0.250 L.
Using solvent volume instead of final solution volume.
Forgetting water of hydration in the molar mass (CuSO₄·5H₂O is 249.69 g/mol, not 159.61).
Mixing molarity and molality.
Safety
Follow your lab's safety rules and the substance's safety data sheet (SDS). Always add concentrated acid to water, not water to acid.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
UK GCSE and A-level papers write concentration as mol dm⁻³, which is identical to mol/L or M in US and Canadian textbooks (1 dm³ = 1 L). UK courses also use "g dm⁻³" for mass concentration.
Frequently asked questions
What is molarity?
Moles of solute per litre of solution.
How do I calculate molarity from grams?
Convert grams to moles with the molar mass, then divide by litres.
What does 1 M mean?
One mole of solute per litre of solution.
How do I dilute a solution?
Use C₁V₁ = C₂V₂.
What's the difference between molarity and molality?
Molarity uses litres of solution; molality uses kilograms of solvent.
Can I enter millilitres?
Not yet; divide by 1,000 to get litres.
