H1: Period Calculator
A period calculator predicts when your next period is likely to start and end, based on your last period and your usual cycle. It's useful for planning travel, sport and events, for noticing when a period is late, and for keeping a simple record to discuss with a doctor.
How to use this calculator
1. First day of last period: the first day of bleeding (not spotting).
2. Average cycle length: days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next (21–45; default 28).
3. Period length: how many days you usually bleed (2–10; default 5).
Results: next period start, next period end (estimate) and following period start.
How it works
Next period start      = last period start + cycle length
Next period end        = next start + (period length − 1)
Following period start = next start + cycle length
The end date subtracts one day because the start day counts as day 1 of bleeding.
Worked examples
Example 1: 28-day cycle, 5-day period, last period began 3 September 2026
Next period: 1 October 2026, ending about 5 October 2026
Following period: 29 October 2026
Estimated ovulation in that cycle: about 17 September 2026 (see the ovulation calculator)
Example 2: 33-day cycle, 6-day period, same start date
Next period: 6 October 2026, ending about 11 October 2026
Following period: 8 November 2026
Example 3: working out an average from several cycles
Suppose your last five cycles were 27, 31, 29, 33, 30 days long. The calculator takes a single average, so add them up and divide: 150 ÷ 5 = 30 days. With a last period starting on 3 September 2026, the next is predicted for 3 October 2026.
Because your cycles ranged from 27 to 33 days, a realistic window is 30 September 2026 to 6 October 2026. Run the calculator with your shortest and longest cycle as well as the average to see that window.
Example 4: 24-day cycle, 4-day period
Next period: 27 September 2026, ending about 30 September 2026
Following period: 21 October 2026
Shorter cycles mean periods come around more often: about 15 a year at 24 days, compared with 13 at 28 days.
What's a typical cycle?
Cycle length: in adults, cycles commonly last 21 to 35 days, and many people's cycles vary by a few days from month to month. In the first few years after periods begin, cycles of up to 45 days are common.
Period length: usually 2 to 7 days.
Blood loss: most people lose less than 80 ml per period; heavy bleeding means needing to change products every one to two hours, passing large clots or bleeding longer than 7 days.
Understanding your results
Predictions are estimates. Stress, illness, travel, weight changes, exercise, breastfeeding and some medicines can shift a period by days or weeks.
A late period can mean pregnancy; a home test is reliable from the first day of a missed period.
Using several months of data gives a better average cycle length.
When to see a doctor
Contact a doctor or nurse if you have:
periods that stop for more than three months (and you aren't pregnant)
cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days (adults)
very heavy or painful periods that affect daily life
bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause

Tips and common mistakes
Counting from the end of a period. Start from day 1 of bleeding.
Assuming 28 days. Only a minority of cycles are exactly 28 days.
Treating predictions as contraception. Period predictions don't reliably show safe days.
What to track
A note of a few details each month makes the predictions better and gives a doctor useful information:
Start date of each period (day 1 of full flow)
Number of bleeding days and which days are heaviest
Product changes on heavy days, as a rough measure of flow
Pain, mood changes or headaches, and on which days of the cycle they happen
Anything unusual: spotting between periods, a missed period, clots
Three to six months of records show your true average cycle and how much it varies.
Premenstrual symptoms
Many people notice symptoms such as bloating, breast tenderness, irritability or low mood in the week or two before a period. These usually ease once bleeding starts. Knowing when your next period is due helps you plan around them. If symptoms are severe enough to affect work, relationships or daily life, speak to a doctor: premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a recognised condition with effective treatments.
Period apps and this calculator
Period-tracking apps use the same basic method but average your cycle automatically as you log dates. This calculator doesn't store anything, so you'll need to work out your average and re-enter it. If you use an app, check its privacy policy, since cycle data is sensitive health information.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate my next period?
Add your average cycle length to the first day of your last period.
What counts as day 1?
The first day of full flow.
Is a 35-day cycle normal?
Yes, cycles from 21 to 35 days are typical for adults.
Why is my period late?
Stress, illness, weight change, exercise, contraception or pregnancy are common reasons.
Can I use this with irregular periods?
It will give an estimate, but it will be less reliable; tracking several months helps.
Does hormonal contraception change my cycle?
Yes; bleeds on the pill are withdrawal bleeds, so predictions may not apply.
