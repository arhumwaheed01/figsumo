H1: Electricity Cost Calculator
How much does it cost to run a space heater, a gaming PC or a window AC unit? This calculator turns an appliance's voltage and current into its power draw, the energy it uses over a period, and what that costs at your electricity rate.
How to use this calculator
1. Voltage (V): 120 V for standard US and Canadian outlets, 240 V for large US appliances and 230 V in the UK (default 120).
2. Current (A): from the appliance label or a plug-in meter (default 12).
3. Hours of use: how long it runs (default 2).
4. Electricity rate (per kWh): from your bill (default 0.16).
Results: power in watts, energy used in kilowatt-hours, and estimated cost.
The formulas
power (W) = voltage (V) × current (A)
energy (kWh) = power (W) × hours ÷ 1,000
cost = energy (kWh) × rate
A kilowatt-hour is 1,000 watts for one hour, the unit utilities bill in.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default (a space heater)
120 V × 12 A = 1,440 W
1,440 × 2 h ÷ 1,000 = 2.88 kWh
2.88 × $0.16 = $0.46
Run daily for a month (30 days), that's $13.82; all year, $168.19.
Example 2: a TV
A 100 W TV used 5 hours a day for a year at $0.16/kWh costs about $29.20.
Example 3: a UK kettle
A 3 kW kettle boiling for 15 minutes a day uses 0.75 kWh, about 20.2p a day at 27p/kWh. (Enter 230 V and 13 A for a similar result, since 230 × 13 ≈ 3,000 W.)
Example 4: charging an EV
Adding 60 kWh to a battery at $0.16/kWh costs $9.60 (plus roughly 10% charging losses). At about 3.3 miles per kWh, a typical figure for many EVs, that's around 200 miles [VERIFY per vehicle].
Example 5: standby power
A device drawing 15 W on standby uses 131.4 kWh a year if left on standby around the clock: about $21.02 at $0.16/kWh.
Where to find the numbers
Watts are printed on most labels and chargers. If you have watts, enter the voltage and divide watts by voltage for amps (1,500 W ÷ 120 V = 12.5 A).
Your rate is on your bill as cents or pence per kWh. Include delivery charges if they're per kWh.
Average residential rates are roughly 17–18¢/kWh in the US, vary widely across Canadian provinces (Quebec is among the lowest), and are set by the Ofgem price cap in Great Britain [VERIFY current rates].

Appliances that cycle on and off (fridges, AC units, heaters on a thermostat) use less than their rated power × hours. A plug-in energy meter gives the real figure.
Understanding your results
Volts × amps gives apparent power. For motors and some electronics, the real power can be lower (power factor), so this may slightly overestimate.
Time-of-use tariffs charge different rates by time of day; use the rate for when the appliance runs.
Standing charges (UK) and fixed monthly fees (US/Canada) aren't included.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering watts in the amps box.
Forgetting the ÷ 1,000 when working by hand (watt-hours vs kilowatt-hours).
Using cents instead of dollars (enter 0.16, not 16).
Assuming the label wattage runs constantly.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate electricity cost?
Watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × rate per kWh.
What is a kWh?
1,000 watts used for one hour.
How much does a 1,500 W heater cost per hour?
1.5 kWh × your rate: 24¢ at $0.16/kWh.
How do I convert amps to watts?
Multiply by the voltage.
What voltage should I use in the UK?
230 V.
Why is my bill higher than the estimate?
Fixed charges, taxes and other appliances.
