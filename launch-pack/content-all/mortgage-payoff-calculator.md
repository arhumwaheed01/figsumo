H1: Mortgage Payoff Calculator
Paying a little extra on your mortgage each month can take years off the loan and save a large amount of interest. This calculator compares your standard payment with the same payment plus an extra amount, and shows how many months the loan will now take and roughly how much interest you save.
How to use this calculator
1. Home price: the purchase price of the home.
2. Down payment: the percentage you paid (or will pay) up front. The calculator subtracts it to get the loan amount.
3. Interest rate: the annual rate on the mortgage.
4. Loan term (years): the original term, usually 30 or 15.
5. Extra monthly payment: the amount you will add to every monthly payment, applied to principal.
Results: standard monthly payment, payment with the extra amount, payoff time in months and years, and interest saved (estimate).
If you already have a mortgage, enter a price and down payment that give your original loan amount, and the original rate and term. (A mode that starts from your current balance is planned.)
The formula
The standard payment comes from the amortization formula:
PMT = L × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
L: loan amount = price × (1 − down%)
r: annual rate ÷ 12; n: term × 12
With extra payments, the calculator steps month by month:
Interest = Balance × r
Principal = (PMT + Extra) − Interest
Balance = Balance − Principal
until the balance reaches zero. Interest saved = interest on the standard schedule − interest with extra payments.

Understanding your results
Payoff time is how long the loan lasts with the extra payment, assuming you make it every month from the start.
Interest saved is an estimate. Real savings depend on when you start paying extra and whether your lender applies the money to principal straight away.
Your monthly obligation doesn't change. The extra is voluntary; you can usually stop if money gets tight.
Early years matter most. Extra principal paid early avoids interest for the rest of the loan.
Is paying off early right for you?
Paying extra gives a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate. Before doing it, many people check that they:
have an emergency fund of a few months' spending;
have no higher-rate debt, such as credit cards, which should usually be paid first;
are getting any employer match on retirement savings, which is a higher return.
If your mortgage rate is low and you could earn more elsewhere, investing instead may come out ahead, though with risk. There's no single right answer; compare the options with your own numbers.
Country notes
United States: most mortgages can be prepaid without penalty; the CFPB notes that some loans have prepayment penalties, which must be disclosed. Tell your servicer the extra amount is for principal. Paying down a conventional loan to 78% of the original value also ends PMI automatically (CFPB).
Canada: closed mortgages limit prepayments, typically 10% to 20% of the original principal a year plus payment increases, depending on the lender; going over can trigger a penalty of three months' interest or an interest-rate differential. Check your mortgage contract.
United Kingdom: many fixed-rate mortgages allow overpayments of up to 10% of the balance a year without an early repayment charge. Overpaying above the allowance during a fixed period can cost an ERC of several percent. Variable-rate deals often have no limit.
Tips and common mistakes
Not marking payments as principal. Some servicers hold extra money as a prepayment of the next instalment unless told otherwise.
Paying a "biweekly service" fee. You can get the same effect free by adding one-twelfth of a payment each month.
Draining savings. Money paid into the house is hard to get back without borrowing.
Ignoring prepayment limits in Canada and the UK.
Quick check
Check your statement for your exact remaining balance.
Frequently asked questions
How much interest can I save by paying extra?
It depends on your balance, rate and time left. On a 30-year loan, even $100–$200 a month often saves tens of thousands of dollars.
Is it better to pay a lump sum or extra every month?
Money paid earlier saves more interest. A lump sum today saves more than the same amount spread over the year.
Does paying extra lower my monthly payment?
Usually not; it shortens the term. Some lenders will recast (re-amortize) the loan after a large lump sum, which lowers the payment.
Should I pay off my mortgage or invest?
Compare your mortgage rate with the after-tax return you expect from investing, and consider risk and liquidity.
Do biweekly payments really help?
Yes, because 26 half-payments equal 13 full payments a year. Adding one-twelfth of a payment each month does the same.
Will paying extra hurt my credit?
No. Paying off a mortgage early may slightly change your credit mix, but it doesn't harm your payment history.
