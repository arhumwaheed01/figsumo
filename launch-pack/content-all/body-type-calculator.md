H1: Body Frame Size Calculator
"Body type" usually refers to the three somatotypes popularised in the 1940s, ectomorph (lean, narrow), mesomorph (muscular, broad) and endomorph (rounder, stores fat more easily), along with frame size, the size of your skeleton. This calculator estimates your frame size from your height and wrist circumference, calculates your BMI, and gives a descriptive body-type label. It's a rough, descriptive tool, not a scientific classification.
How to use this calculator
1. Sex.
2. Units: metric or imperial.
3. Weight and height.
4. Wrist circumference: measure around the wrist just above the wrist bone (toward the hand), using a flexible tape, in the same units as height.
Results: suggested body type, frame size (from the ratio of height to wrist), and BMI with its category.

A larger wrist relative to height gives a smaller r and a larger frame.
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)².
Body-type label:
BMI under 18.5 → "ectomorph-leaning"
BMI 25 or more → "endomorph-leaning"
Otherwise, a large frame → "mesomorph-leaning"; small or medium → "mesomorph / balanced"
Because the label is based on BMI and frame size, it describes your current size and build, not a fixed type you were born with.
Worked examples
Example 1: man, 178 cm, wrist 17 cm, 75 kg
r = 178 ÷ 17 = 10.47 → Medium frame
BMI = 75 ÷ 1.78² = 23.7 (healthy weight range)
Label: mesomorph / balanced
Example 2: woman, 163 cm, wrist 14.5 cm, 58 kg
r = 11.24 → Small frame
BMI = 21.8
Label: mesomorph / balanced (small frame)
Example 3: man, 180 cm, wrist 19.5 cm, 95 kg
r = 9.23 → large frame
BMI = 29.3 → endomorph-leaning label
A heavily muscled person with a high BMI may get an "endomorph" label even with low body fat. That's a limitation of using BMI. Check body fat with the body fat calculator for a better picture.
Example 4: two more profiles
Man, 185 cm, wrist 18 cm, 82 kg: r = 10.28 (medium frame), BMI 24.0 → mesomorph / balanced
Woman, 170 cm, wrist 15 cm, 80 kg: r = 11.33 (small frame), BMI 27.7 → endomorph-leaning
The woman's label comes from her BMI of 25 or more, not from her frame, which shows how strongly BMI drives the result.
What somatotypes can and can't tell you
The somatotype idea came from psychologist William Sheldon, whose claims linking body shape to personality have been discredited. Sports scientists later developed the Heath-Carter method, which rates each component from skinfolds, bone breadths and girths; it's used to describe athletes, not to predict health. Simple online body-type quizzes and calculators, including this one, give a broad description at best.
What matters more for health:
Body fat and where it's stored. Waist size and waist-to-height ratio are linked to health risks; see the waist-to-height ratio calculator.
Fitness and strength, which can be improved regardless of body type.
Diet and activity habits.
Using frame size
Frame size is sometimes used to adjust "ideal weight" ranges: a person with a large frame may sit toward the upper end of a healthy-weight range and a small frame toward the lower end. See the ideal weight calculator and the healthy weight calculator.
Measuring your wrist
Use a flexible tape (or a strip of paper you then measure with a ruler).
Wrap it around the smallest part of the wrist, just above the bony bump on the little-finger side, toward the hand.
Keep the tape snug but not tight. A 6¾-inch wrist is 17.1 cm.
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing units for height and wrist. The ratio only works if both are in the same units.
Treating the label as permanent. Training and diet change body composition.
Choosing a diet or workout by somatotype alone. There's little evidence that somatotype-specific diets work better.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
Frame size from wrist measurements was popularised in the US through the Metropolitan Life height-weight tables of the 1980s, which gave separate ranges for small, medium and large frames. Today the CDC, Health Canada and the NHS don't use frame size in public health advice. They combine BMI with waist circumference:
US (NIH) and Canada: health risk rises with a waist above 102 cm (40 in) for men and 88 cm (35 in) for women.
UK (NHS): risk is increased above 94 cm (37 in) for men and 80 cm (31.5 in) for women, and NICE also recommends keeping your waist below half your height.
Elbow breadth, measured with calipers across the bent elbow, is another way to estimate frame size and is less affected by body fat than the wrist, but it needs a measuring tool that most people don't have at home.
Frequently asked questions
How do I know my body type?
This calculator gives a rough description from your BMI and frame size. A formal somatotype assessment requires anthropometric measurements by a trained professional.
What is my frame size?
Divide your height by your wrist circumference: above about 11 is small and below 9.6 is large, with medium in between (thresholds vary by sex and source).
Can I change my body type?
Your skeleton and frame don't change, but body fat and muscle do, so a descriptive label can change.
Is mesomorph the best body type?
No body type is "best". Health depends on fitness, body fat distribution and habits.
Are somatotypes scientific?
The original theory isn't supported. The Heath-Carter method is a descriptive tool used in sports science.
Does frame size affect healthy weight?
It can shift where you fall within a healthy range, but BMI ranges themselves don't change with frame size.
