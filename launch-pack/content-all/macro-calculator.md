H1: Macro Calculator
Macronutrients, or "macros", are the three nutrients that supply calories: carbohydrate, protein and fat. This calculator estimates your daily calorie needs and splits them into grams of each macro using one of three preset ratios. It's useful for meal planning, tracking food in an app, or checking whether your current diet is roughly balanced.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric (kg, cm) or imperial (lb, ft and in).
2. Age, sex, weight and height: used to estimate your basal metabolic rate (BMR).
3. Activity level: sedentary, light (1–3 days a week), moderate (3–5 days), active (6–7 days) or very active (hard exercise daily).
4. Macro split:
Balanced: 40% carbohydrate, 30% protein, 30% fat
High protein: 30% carbohydrate, 40% protein, 30% fat
Low carb: 20% carbohydrate, 35% protein, 45% fat
Results: daily calories (TDEE) and grams of carbohydrate, protein and fat, with a chart.
How it works
Step 1: BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor equation)
Men:   BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161
Step 2: TDEE. BMR × activity factor (1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725 or 1.9).
Step 3: grams.
Carbohydrate g = TDEE × carb % ÷ 4
Protein g      = TDEE × protein % ÷ 4
Fat g          = TDEE × fat % ÷ 9
Carbohydrate and protein provide about 4 kcal per gram; fat about 9 kcal per gram. Alcohol (7 kcal per gram) isn't included.
The calories are your maintenance estimate. The calculator doesn't subtract a deficit for weight loss; use the calorie deficit calculator for a target and apply the same percentages to it.
Worked examples
Example 1: woman, 35, 65 kg, 165 cm, moderate activity, balanced split
BMR = 650 + 1,031.25 − 175 − 161 = 1,345.25 kcal
TDEE = 1,345.25 × 1.55 = 2,085 kcal
Carbohydrate: 2,085.14 × 0.40 ÷ 4 = 209 g
Protein: × 0.30 ÷ 4 = 156 g (about 2.4 g per kg of body weight)
Fat: × 0.30 ÷ 9 = 70 g
With the high-protein split, protein would be 209 g, or 3.2 g per kg. That's well above what most people need (see below).
Example 2: man, 40, 90 kg, 180 cm, light activity, low carb
BMR = 1,830.00 kcal; TDEE = 2,516 kcal
Carbohydrate 126 g, protein 220 g (2.4 g/kg), fat 126 g
Understanding your results
TDEE is an estimate. Mifflin-St Jeor is among the more accurate prediction equations for adults, but individuals can differ by 10% or more. Track your weight for two to four weeks and adjust.
Check protein per kilogram. Percentage splits can give very high or low protein. A common range is 0.8 g/kg for general health up to about 1.6–2.0 g/kg for strength training. The protein calculator works per kilogram directly.
Fat floor. Very low fat intakes make it harder to get essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins. All three splits here give 30% or more of calories from fat.
Grams are targets, not exact requirements. Hitting within 5–10 g most days is plenty for most goals.
Official guidance on macro ranges
The US National Academies set Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges (AMDRs) for adults:
Macro
AMDR (% of calories)
Carbohydrate
45–65%
Protein
10–35%
Fat
20–35%
The calculator's presets are higher in protein and lower in carbohydrate than the AMDRs. The low-carb split (20% carbohydrate, 45% fat) falls outside both ranges, which is common in diets designed for weight loss but worth knowing. For Example 1, carbohydrate within the AMDR would be 235–339 g a day.
Country notes
US: the Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020–2025 use the AMDRs and recommend limiting saturated fat to under 10% of calories.
Canada: Health Canada uses the same Dietary Reference Intakes as the US.
UK: government advice suggests carbohydrate should provide about 50% of energy (SACN 2015), total fat no more than 35% and saturated fat no more than 11% of food energy (COMA 1991).
Choosing a split
Balanced: a good default for most people who exercise.
High protein: may help with fullness and preserving muscle during weight loss, but check grams per kg.
Low carb: some people find it easier to control appetite; endurance athletes usually need more carbohydrate.
The best split is one you can stick to. Total calories matter most for weight change, and protein matters most for muscle.
Tips and common mistakes
Using maintenance calories when you want to lose weight.
Choosing "active" when you exercise moderately. Overestimating activity is the most common reason calorie targets don't work.
Ignoring fibre. Aim for whole grains, fruit, vegetables and legumes within your carbohydrate grams.
Forgetting cooking oils and drinks when tracking.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate my macros?
Work out your daily calories, multiply by each macro's percentage, then divide by 4 for carbohydrate and protein, or 9 for fat.
What macro ratio is best for weight loss?
A calorie deficit drives weight loss; higher protein (around 1.2–1.6 g/kg) can help preserve muscle and keep you full.
Why does fat have fewer grams?
Fat has more than twice the calories per gram (9 vs 4).
Do I count fibre as carbohydrate?
Yes, nutrition labels in the US and Canada include fibre in total carbohydrate; UK labels list it separately.
How accurate is TDEE?
It's a population-based estimate; adjust it based on your weight trend.
Is a high protein diet safe?
For healthy adults, intakes up to about 2 g/kg are generally considered safe. People with kidney disease should get medical advice.
Should I change macros on rest days?
Some people do, but keeping the same targets daily is simpler and works for most goals.
