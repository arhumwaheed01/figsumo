H1: Bra Size Calculator
A bra that fits depends on two measurements: the band (around your ribcage, just under the bust) and the bust (around the fullest part). This calculator uses both, in inches, to suggest a US bra size, and shows the difference that sets the cup.
How to measure
You'll need a soft tape measure. Wear a non-padded bra or none.
1. Underbust: wrap the tape snugly around your ribcage directly under the bust, keeping it level and exhaling normally.
2. Bust: measure loosely around the fullest part of the bust, tape level across the back.
Stand straight and look in a mirror to check the tape isn't riding up at the back.
How to use this calculator
1. Underbust (in): default 32.
2. Bust (in): default 36.
Results: suggested US size (band and cup) and cup difference in inches.
How the size is worked out
band = underbust, rounded to the nearest inch
cup difference = bust − underbust, rounded to the nearest inch

Worked examples
Example 1: the default
Underbust 32, bust 36: band 32, difference 4 in → 32D.
Example 2: a larger cup
Underbust 34, bust 39: difference 5 in → 34DD (US and UK).
Example 3: an odd band measurement
Underbust 31 in: bands are usually sold in even sizes, so try both 30 and 32. Going up a band usually means going down a cup to keep the same volume (see sister sizes below).
Sister sizes
Cup volume is tied to the band: a 32D, 34C and 36B hold about the same volume. If the cup fits but the band is loose, go down a band and up a cup (34C → 32D). If the band is tight, go up a band and down a cup.
Signs of a good fit
Band: level all round, firm on the loosest hook when new (it will stretch). It should provide most of the support.
Cups: no gaping or spilling over the edge.
Centre gore: lies flat against the breastbone on underwired styles.
Straps: stay put without digging in.
Understanding your results
A calculation is a starting point. Shape, brand, style (plunge, balconette, sports) and fabric all change fit.
Some fitters still use the older "plus four" method, adding 4–5 in to the underbust for the band, which tends to give a looser band and smaller cup letter than this method.
Bodies change with weight, pregnancy, breastfeeding and age; re-measure every year or so.
Tips and common mistakes
Pulling the tape too tight on the bust, or too loose on the band.
Measuring over a padded bra.
Sticking with one size across brands. Try the sister sizes too.
Assuming D is "large". Cup letters are relative to band size.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find my bra size?
Measure underbust and bust; the difference sets the cup.
What does 32D mean?
A 32-inch band with about a 4-inch difference between bust and band.
Is DD the same as E?
In UK sizing, E comes after DD. In US sizing, DDD (often labelled F) comes after DD.
What is a sister size?
A size with the same cup volume on a different band, such as 34C and 32D.
Can I measure in centimetres?
Convert to inches first (divide by 2.54), or use the unit converter.
How often should I re-measure?
Every 6–12 months, or after weight change or pregnancy.
