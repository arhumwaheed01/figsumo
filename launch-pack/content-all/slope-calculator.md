H1: Slope Calculator
Slope measures how steep a line is: how much it rises (or falls) for each unit it moves to the right. Enter two points and this calculator returns the slope and the angle the line makes with the x-axis.
How to use this calculator
1. x₁, y₁: the first point (default 1, 2).
2. x₂, y₂: the second point (default 4, 6).
Results: slope (m) to six decimal places and the angle from the x-axis in degrees. If both x values are equal, the line is vertical and the slope is shown as undefined.
The formulas
m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) = rise ÷ run
angle θ = arctan(m)
Once you have m, the line's equation is y = mx + b, where b = y₁ − m × x₁.
Worked examples
Example 1: (1, 2) and (4, 6)
Rise = 6 − 2 = 4; run = 4 − 1 = 3
m = 4 ÷ 3 = 1.333333
Angle = arctan(1.3333) = 53.13°
Intercept b = 2 − (4/3) × 1 = 0.666667, so y = 1.333333x + 0.666667.
Example 2: a falling line
(2, 5) and (6, −3): m = −8 ÷ 4 = -2. A negative slope falls from left to right; the angle is negative (below the x-axis).
Example 3: road gradient
A road rising 6 m over 100 m horizontal has a slope of 0.06, a 6% grade, at an angle of 3.43°.
Example 4: roof pitch
A 6-in-12 roof rises 6 inches per 12 inches of run: slope 0.5, angle 26.57°. See the roofing calculator.
Types of slope
Slope
Line
m > 0
Rises left to right
m < 0
Falls left to right
m = 0
Horizontal (y₁ = y₂)
Undefined
Vertical (x₁ = x₂)
Parallel lines have equal slopes.
Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is −1: a line with m = 2 is perpendicular to one with m = −0.5.
Slope, grade and angle
Grade (%) = slope × 100. A 100% grade is 45°, not vertical.
Ratio: a 1 : 12 slope means 1 unit up for 12 across (about 8.3%).
Angle: θ = arctan(m); slope = tan(θ).
Understanding your results
The order of the points doesn't matter, provided you subtract in the same order top and bottom.
Slope has units of "y units per x unit": dollars per month, metres per second, and so on. In a distance-time graph, slope is speed.
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing the order, such as (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₁ − x₂), which flips the sign.
Putting run over rise.
Confusing a zero slope with an undefined one.
Treating grade as angle. A 10% grade is about 5.7°, not 10°.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
Equation of a line: US and Canadian textbooks write y = mx + b; UK textbooks write y = mx + c. The slope (UK: "gradient") is m in both.
Road signs: UK hill signs show the grade as a percentage, such as 10%, while older signs used ratios such as 1:10. US and Canadian highway signs usually give percentages for steep grades.
Accessibility ramps: the US ADA standards set a maximum running slope of 1:12 (8.33%) for ramps. Canadian and UK guidance is similar but varies by province or building regulation, so check local rules [VERIFY].
Roof pitch: US and Canadian roofers speak of rise per 12 inches of run. UK roofers usually quote the angle in degrees.
Practice problems
1. Points (−2, 3) and (4, 6): m = 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5.
2. Points (0, 8) and (2, 0): m = −8 ÷ 2 = −4.
3. A ramp rising 0.5 m over 6 m: slope 0.0833, grade 8.33%, angle 4.76°.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find the slope between two points?
Divide the change in y by the change in x.
What does a negative slope mean?
The line goes down as x increases.
What is the slope of a vertical line?
Undefined, because the run is zero.
How do I convert slope to an angle?
Take the arctangent.
How do I find the equation of the line?
Calculate b = y₁ − m × x₁, then y = mx + b.
What is a 5% grade?
A rise of 5 units per 100 units horizontally (slope 0.05).
