H1: Exponent Calculator
An exponent says how many times to multiply a number (the base) by itself. 2¹⁰ means ten 2s multiplied together. Enter a base and an exponent and this calculator returns the result. Exponents can be whole numbers, negative numbers or decimals.
How to use this calculator
1. Base (b): any real number.
2. Exponent (n): whole, negative or fractional.
Result: bⁿ. If the result is too large for the calculator to represent (above about 1.8 × 10³⁰⁸), you'll see an overflow message.

Worked examples
Example 1: 2¹⁰
2 × 2 × … × 2 (ten times) = 1,024. That's why a kilobyte in binary terms is 1,024 bytes.
Example 2: a negative exponent
5⁻² = 1 ÷ 5² = 0.04.
Example 3: a fractional exponent
16^0.5 = √16 = 4; 2^0.5 = 1.414214.
Example 4: growth over time
Money growing 5% a year for 30 years is multiplied by 1.05³⁰ = 4.321942, so $10,000 grows to 43,219.42. The compound interest calculator handles deposits and compounding frequencies.
Special cases
Zero to a power: 0ⁿ = 0 for positive n. 0⁰ is conventionally 1 in algebra and in this calculator.
Negative base: (−2)³ = −8 and (−2)⁴ = 16 (even powers are positive). A negative base with a fractional exponent, such as (−8)^(1/3), has no real result in the calculator's arithmetic, even though −2 is a real cube root of −8. Use the root calculator for odd roots of negative numbers.
Very large results: the largest representable number is about 1.8 × 10³⁰⁸ (2¹⁰²³ ≈ 8.9885e+307). For exact huge integers, use the big number calculator.
e: Euler's number, about 2.718282, is the base of natural exponential growth.
Exponents, roots and logs
These are three views of the same relationship. If bⁿ = x:
the exponent gives x from b and n,
the root gives b from x and n (b = x^(1/n)),
the logarithm gives n from x and b (n = log_b x); see the log calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Treating −2² as (−2)². By convention −2² = −(2²) = −4.
Multiplying base by exponent. 3⁴ is 81, not 12.
Adding exponents when bases differ. 2³ × 3² can't be combined into one power.
Thinking negative exponents give negative answers. They give reciprocals.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US and Canadian classes say "exponent" or "power"; UK GCSE uses "index" (plural "indices"), so "the laws of indices" are the exponent rules above. A-level and university courses in all three countries use eˣ for exponential growth and decay.
Practice problems

2. 2⁻³ = 1 ÷ 2³ = 0.125.
3. 16^0.75 = (⁴√16)³ = 2³ = 8.
Frequently asked questions
What is any number to the power of 0?
1, except that 0⁰ is a special case (treated as 1 here).
How do I calculate a negative exponent?
Take the reciprocal of the positive power: b⁻ⁿ = 1/bⁿ.
What does a fractional exponent mean?
A root: b^(1/2) is the square root; b^(1/3) is the cube root.
What is 10 to the power of 6?
1,000,000.
Why does a negative base with a decimal exponent fail?
The result would be a complex number in most cases.
How big can the answer be?
Up to about 1.8 × 10³⁰⁸.
