H1: Hours Calculator
Payroll systems, invoices and timesheets usually want hours as a decimal (7.75), but clocks show hours and minutes (7:45). This calculator converts hours and minutes into decimal hours and total minutes, so you can multiply by an hourly rate without mistakes.
How to use this calculator
1. Hours: whole hours (default 8).
2. Minutes: 0 to 59 (default 30).
Results: decimal hours (to four decimals) and total minutes.
The formula
decimal hours = hours + minutes ÷ 60
total minutes = hours × 60 + minutes
To go the other way, multiply the decimal part by 60: 0.4 h × 60 = 24 minutes, so 6.4 h = 6 h 24 min.

Worked examples
Example 1: the default
8 h 30 min = 8.5 hours = 510 minutes
Example 2: getting paid
7 h 45 min at $22.50 an hour: 7.75 × 22.50 = $174.38. A common mistake is to multiply 7.45 × 22.50, which underpays by more than $6.
Example 3: a weekly total
You worked 38 h 50 min in a week: 38.8333 decimal hours × $18.75 = $728.12.
Example 4: short tasks
20 minutes = 0.3333 hours: useful for billing in fractions of an hour.
Rounding rules at work
US: the Department of Labor allows employers to round clock times to the nearest 5, 6 (tenth of an hour) or 15 minutes, provided rounding averages out and doesn't consistently underpay workers.
Canada and the UK: employment standards don't set a rounding rule in the same way, but employees must be paid for all time worked and at least the minimum wage on average for the pay period [VERIFY provincial rules].
Billing: many professionals bill in 6-minute (0.1 h) or 15-minute increments.
Overtime
US (FLSA): non-exempt employees earn 1.5× their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek; some states (such as California) add daily overtime.
Canada: thresholds vary by province (often 40 or 44 hours a week, and 8 hours a day in some provinces).
UK: there's no statutory overtime premium; it depends on the contract, but average pay must not fall below the National Minimum Wage.
Use the time card calculator to work out hours from clock-in and clock-out times.
Understanding your results
Repeating decimals: 20 min is 0.3333…; four decimal places keep pay calculations accurate to the cent for normal hours.
For payroll, check whether your employer rounds to two decimals before multiplying.
Tips and common mistakes
Treating 7:45 as 7.45 hours. Minutes are sixtieths, not hundredths.
Entering 90 minutes in the minutes box; use 1 hour 30 minutes.
Rounding each day and then summing, which can shift weekly totals.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
US and Canadian timesheets usually show the 12-hour clock with AM and PM, so check that a shift from 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is entered as crossing midnight. UK employers use 12- and 24-hour formats, and many rotas are written as 22:00–06:00. Overtime rules differ: US federal law sets overtime by the week, Canadian rules are provincial, and UK law has no general overtime premium.
Frequently asked questions
How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?
Divide the minutes by 60.
What is 7 hours 45 minutes in decimal?
7.75.
What is 0.25 hours in minutes?
15 minutes.
How do I calculate pay from hours and minutes?
Convert to decimal hours, then multiply by the hourly rate.
How many minutes is 8.5 hours?
510.
Can I add up several days?
Add the hours and minutes first, or convert each day and add the decimals.
