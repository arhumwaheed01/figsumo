H1: Loan Comparison Calculator
When you have more than one loan offer, the lowest monthly payment isn't always the cheapest loan. Rates, terms and fees all change the total cost. This calculator puts up to three loans side by side and shows each one's monthly payment, total interest, fees, total cost and APR, so you can see which is cheapest overall and which is easiest on your monthly budget.
How to use this calculator
For each loan (A, B and optionally C):
1. Loan amount
2. Interest rate (annual)
3. Term in months or years
4. Upfront fees: origination, arrangement or processing fees. Choose whether the fee is deducted from the money you receive, paid upfront, or added to the loan.
Results for each loan: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest, fees, total cost, APR, and the difference from the cheapest option.
How it works
Payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Total cost = Payment × n + Fees paid upfront or deducted
APR = the rate i that solves: Amount received = Payment × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i  (× 12 for annual)
APR folds fees into a single annual rate, which makes loans with different fees comparable. Total cost in dollars is the clearest comparison when the terms are the same; when the terms differ, look at both.

Rate versus fees on a mortgage
A $300,000 mortgage at 6.25% costs $1,847.15 a month; at 6.0% with $4,500 of extra points it costs $1,798.65, saving $48.50 a month. The points pay for themselves after about 93 months. If you might sell or refinance sooner, the higher rate with no points is cheaper.

Understanding your results
Compare like with like. If terms differ, the shorter loan usually has a higher payment and lower total cost.
Fees matter more on short loans and small amounts. A $750 fee on a two-year loan adds more to the APR than on a five-year loan.
Prepayment: if you plan to pay early, a loan with a large upfront fee loses its advantage; check prepayment penalties too.
Variable rates: the comparison assumes the rate stays fixed. For variable loans, run a higher-rate scenario.
What to check in each offer
APR and finance charge (US Truth in Lending disclosures; the UK and Canada also require APR in credit advertising and agreements).
Whether the rate is fixed or variable.
Origination, late and prepayment fees.
Any optional add-ons (insurance, protection products) included in the payment.
Whether the offer is a firm approval or a pre-qualification estimate.
Tips and common mistakes
Choosing on monthly payment alone.
Ignoring how fees are paid. A fee deducted from proceeds means you receive less.
Comparing a 5-year loan with a 7-year loan by total interest alone without considering affordability.
Forgetting the credit impact. Rate shopping within a short window is usually treated as a single inquiry for mortgages and auto loans [VERIFY scoring model rules].
Notes for US, Canada and UK borrowers
US: the Truth in Lending Act requires lenders to disclose the APR, finance charge, amount financed and total of payments in a standard box, which makes offers easy to compare side by side.
Canada: federally regulated lenders must give a cost of borrowing disclosure showing the APR and total cost, and you can usually prepay a closed personal loan, though check for fees.
UK: lenders must show a representative APR in adverts, and at least 51% of successful applicants must get that rate or better. Your personal rate can be higher, so compare the rate you're actually offered. You also have 14 days to withdraw from most credit agreements.
Frequently asked questions
How do I compare two loans?
Look at the total cost (payments plus fees) for the same amount, and the APR. Then check the payment fits your budget.
Is a lower interest rate always better?
Not if it comes with large fees or a longer term. Compare total cost and APR.
What is the difference between APR and interest rate?
The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal; APR also includes most fees, expressed as an annual rate.
Should I pay points to get a lower rate?
Only if you'll keep the loan past the breakeven month.
Can I compare loans with different terms?
Yes, but remember a longer term usually costs more overall even if the payment is lower.
Does the calculator include prepayment penalties?
No. Add them to fees if you expect to pay off early.
