H1: Speed Calculator
Average speed is distance divided by time. This calculator takes a distance in miles and a time in hours and minutes and returns your average speed in miles per hour and kilometres per hour. Use it for road trips, cycling and running splits, or physics homework.
How to use this calculator
1. Distance (mi): default 26.2 (a marathon).
2. Hours and Minutes: the elapsed time (default 0 h 45 min).
Results: average speed in mph and km/h.
The formulas
speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed × time
time = distance ÷ speed
km/h = mph × 1.609344
Time must be in hours: 45 minutes = 0.75 h.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
26.2 mi ÷ 0.75 h = 34.93 mph (56.22 km/h). That's a car on a suburban road, not a runner.
Example 2: a marathon runner
26.2 mi in 4 h 0 min → 6.55 mph (10.54 km/h), a pace of about 9:10 per mile. Pace (time per mile) is the inverse of speed: 60 ÷ mph minutes per mile.
Example 3: how far will I get?
Driving at an average of 65 mph for 2.5 hours covers 65 × 2.5 = 162.5 miles.
Example 4: how long will it take?
300 miles at an average of 70 mph takes 300 ÷ 70 = 4.29 h ≈ 4 h 17 min, before stops.
Example 5: the fastest human
Usain Bolt's 100 m world record of 9.58 s is an average of 10.44 m/s, or 23.35 mph.

Average speed vs average of speeds
If you drive 60 miles at 30 mph and another 60 miles at 60 mph, your average speed is not 45 mph. Total time is 2 h + 1 h = 3 h, so the average is 120 ÷ 3 = 40 mph. Always divide total distance by total time.
Speed limits in the US, Canada and UK
US: limits are in mph and set by states; rural interstates are often 65–75 mph, with some at 80 or more.
Canada: limits are in km/h; most highways are 100–110 km/h.
UK: limits are in mph: 70 mph on motorways and dual carriageways, 60 mph on single carriageways for cars, 30 mph in built-up areas (20 mph is the default in Wales since 2023).
Understanding your results
Average, not maximum: traffic, stops and hills mean your top speed was higher.
Moving time vs elapsed time: fitness trackers often show both; use the one that matches your question.
Precision: results show two decimals.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering 1.30 for 1 hour 30 minutes. Use the separate hours and minutes boxes.
Averaging speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
Mixing kilometres and miles.
Practice
10 km in 50 minutes is 10 ÷ (50/60) = 12 km/h, or about 7.46 mph.
At 12 km/h, a half marathon (21.1 km) takes about 1 h 45 min.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate average speed?
Divide total distance by total time.
How do I convert mph to km/h?
Multiply by 1.609344.
How do I find time from speed and distance?
Divide distance by speed.
How do I calculate running pace?
Divide time by distance (minutes per mile).
What is 100 km/h in mph?
About 62.1 mph.
Can I enter kilometres?
Not yet; divide km by 1.609344 to get miles.
