H1: Dice Roller
Need dice for a board game, a tabletop RPG or a classroom probability lesson and can't find any? This roller throws as many dice as you like with any number of sides, and shows each roll, the total and the average.
How to use the dice roller
1. Number of dice: at least 1 (default 2).
2. Sides per die: 2 or more (default 6). Use 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 20 for standard polyhedral dice, 2 for a coin flip, or 100 for percentile rolls.
3. Press Calculate to roll; press again to re-roll.
Results: total, the individual rolls, and the average per die.
How it works
Each die is rolled independently:
roll = 1 + floor(random × sides)
Every face has an equal 1 ÷ sides chance. The total is the sum of all rolls; the average is the total ÷ number of dice.
The maths of dice
Expected value
The average roll of a fair die with s sides is (s + 1) ÷ 2:
d6: 3.5
d20: 10.5
3d6: 3 × 3.5 = 10.5

Three dice
Rolling 18 on 3d6 (all sixes) has a 0.463% chance (1 in 216). Totals of 10 or 11 together come up about 25.0% of the time.
Worked examples
Example 1: Monopoly
2 dice, 6 sides. Doubles come up 6 times in 36, or 1 in 6.
Example 2: Dungeons & Dragons
1 die, 20 sides for an attack roll; a natural 20 has a 5% chance. Damage might be 2 dice, 8 sides (2d8), averaging 9.
Example 3: a coin
1 die, 2 sides: treat 1 as heads and 2 as tails.
Example 4: percentile
1 die, 100 sides gives 1–100, as rolled with two d10s in many games.

Is it fair?
The roller uses your browser's pseudo-random number generator, which is unbiased for practical purposes and has no memory: a six last roll doesn't make a six less likely next time. Physical dice can be slightly biased by manufacturing; precision casino dice are made to tight tolerances. For anything involving money, follow the venue's or regulator's rules.
Tips and common mistakes
Expecting results to "even out" quickly. Streaks are normal in short runs.
Confusing the average per die with the total.
Using a d6 roll for a d20 game. Set the sides to match.
Frequently asked questions
How do I roll 2d6?
Set 2 dice and 6 sides.
What is the most likely total with two dice?
7, with a 1 in 6 chance.
Can I roll a d20?
Yes; set sides to 20.
Is the roller random?
Yes, for games and teaching. Each roll is independent.
Can I add a modifier like +3?
Not yet; add it to the total yourself.
How many dice can I roll?
As many as you need for a game; very large numbers just make a long list.
