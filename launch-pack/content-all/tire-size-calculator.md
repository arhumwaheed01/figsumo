H1: Tire Size Calculator
The code on a tyre's sidewall, such as 225/45R17, describes its size. This calculator converts the three key numbers into the tyre's overall diameter, sidewall height and revolutions per mile, which you need when changing wheel or tyre sizes.
How to use this calculator
1. Section width (mm): the first number (default 225).
2. Aspect ratio (%): the second number, sidewall height as a percentage of width (default 45).
3. Wheel diameter (in): the number after "R" (default 17).
Results: overall diameter (in), sidewall height (each, in) and revolutions per mile.
Reading a tyre size
225/45R17 94W
225: section width in millimetres
45: aspect ratio (sidewall = 45% of 225 mm = 101.25 mm)
R: radial construction
17: wheel (rim) diameter in inches
94: load index; W: speed rating (up to 168 mph / 270 km/h)
LT (light truck) and P (passenger) prefixes appear on some US tyres.
The formulas
sidewall (in) = width × aspect ÷ 100 ÷ 25.4
diameter (in) = rim + 2 × sidewall
circumference = π × diameter
revolutions per mile = 63,360 ÷ circumference
(63,360 is the number of inches in a mile.)
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
Sidewall = 225 × 0.45 ÷ 25.4 = 3.99 in
Diameter = 17 + 2 × 3.99 = 24.97 in
Circumference = 78.45 in → 808 revolutions per mile
Example 2: plus-sizing to 245/40R18
Diameter = 25.72 in, 2.98% larger than 225/45R17. Your speedometer, calibrated for the original size, would read 60 mph when you're actually doing about 61.8 mph.
Example 3: a truck tyre
265/70R17: diameter 31.61 in, 638 revolutions per mile.
Changing tyre sizes safely
Keep the overall diameter within about 3% of the original to limit speedometer error and avoid problems with ABS, traction control and transmission shift points; many manufacturers and tyre retailers recommend this [VERIFY with your vehicle maker].
Check clearance at full steering lock and suspension compression.
Match or exceed the original load index and speed rating.
Use the same size on each axle (all four on AWD vehicles).
Tyre placard: the recommended sizes and pressures are on the driver's door jamb.
Speedometer error
If a new tyre is larger, each revolution covers more ground, so the speedometer reads low. If it's smaller, the speedometer reads high. Actual speed = indicated speed × (new diameter ÷ old diameter).
Regional notes
US: tyres carry a DOT code showing manufacture date (the last four digits: week and year).
Canada: winter tyres bearing the three-peak mountain snowflake are mandatory in Quebec from 1 December to 15 March, and required on many BC highways from 1 October to 30 April.
UK: the legal minimum tread depth for cars is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters of the tread.
Understanding your results
Actual diameter varies slightly between brands and with tread wear and pressure.
Revolutions per mile from a manufacturer's spec sheet may differ by 1–2% from this geometric figure because a loaded tyre deforms.
Tips and common mistakes
Treating the aspect ratio as a height in mm. It's a percentage of the width.
Mixing up width (mm) and rim (in).
Ignoring load index when choosing a larger wheel with a lower-profile tyre.
Frequently asked questions
What does 225/45R17 mean?
225 mm wide, sidewall 45% of the width, radial, for a 17-inch wheel.
How tall is a 225/45R17 tyre?
About 24.97 in.
How much can tyre diameter change?
Usually within about 3% of the original.
Will bigger tyres affect my speedometer?
Yes; it will read lower than your true speed.
How do I calculate revolutions per mile?
63,360 ÷ (π × diameter in inches).
Can I compare two sizes?
Run the calculator for each and compare diameters.
