H1: Property Tax Calculator
Property tax is the main source of funding for US local governments, schools and services. Your bill depends on your home's assessed value, any exemptions you qualify for and the combined tax rate set by your county, city, school district and special districts. This calculator estimates your annual and monthly property tax from those inputs and shows your effective tax rate.
How to use this calculator
1. Market value: the home's value, or the assessor's market value from your notice.
2. Assessment ratio (%): the share of market value that's taxed. Some states assess at 100%; others use a fraction (and some use different ratios for homes and businesses).
3. Exemptions ($): homestead, senior, veteran or disability exemptions that reduce taxable value.
4. Tax rate: either as a percentage or in mills (dollars per $1,000 of taxable value). Your tax bill or county website shows it.
Results: assessed value, taxable value, annual property tax, monthly amount (useful for escrow) and effective tax rate (tax ÷ market value).
The formulas
Assessed value = Market value × Assessment ratio
Taxable value = Assessed value − Exemptions
Annual tax = Taxable value × Rate          (rate as a decimal)
Annual tax = Taxable value × Mills ÷ 1,000 (rate in mills)
Effective rate = Annual tax ÷ Market value
Worked examples
Example 1: a 1.2% rate on a $350,000 home
Assessed at 100%: $350,000
Annual tax = $4,200.00 ($350.00 a month in escrow)
With a $50,000 homestead exemption, taxable value is $300,000 and tax is $3,600.00
Example 2: mills and a partial assessment ratio
Market value $300,000; assessment ratio 40%; rate 70 mills.
Assessed value = $120,000.00
Tax = 120,000 × 70 ÷ 1,000 = $8,400.00
Effective rate = 2.80%
A 70-mill rate sounds high, but on a 40% assessment the effective rate is lower. Always compare effective rates, not nominal ones.
Example 3: a large homestead exemption
Market value $400,000, assessed at 100%, a $100,000 homestead exemption and a 2.0% rate:
Taxable value = $300,000
Annual tax = $6,000.00 ($500.00 a month); effective rate 1.50%
Without the exemption, the bill would be $8,000.00, so it's worth checking you've applied.

How property tax changes
Reassessment: if your home is reassessed 8% higher at the same rate, the Example 1 bill rises by about $336.00.
Assessment caps: many states limit annual increases in assessed value for homesteads (for example, a 3% cap would keep the Example 1 taxable value from rising more than 3%, a bill of $4,326.00). Caps often reset when a home is sold, so a buyer may pay much more than the seller did.
Rates are set each year by local budgets.
Appealing your assessment
If the assessed value seems too high, compare it with recent sales of similar homes and check the property record for errors (square footage, bedrooms, lot size). Appeals have strict deadlines, often 30 to 60 days after the assessment notice [VERIFY your county].
Understanding your results
Escrow: most mortgage lenders collect 1/12 of the annual tax with each payment and pay the bill for you; the mortgage calculator can include it.
Federal deduction: if you itemize, property tax counts toward the state and local tax (SALT) deduction, which is capped [VERIFY 2026 SALT cap and phase-out]. At a 24% marginal rate, deducting $4,200 would be worth up to $1,008.00.
Buying a home: budget for the tax at the new assessed value, not the seller's bill.
Rentals: property tax is an operating expense in NOI; see the rental property calculator.
Canada and the UK
Canada: municipalities levy property tax as assessed value × a mill rate (municipal plus education), with assessments by provincial agencies such as MPAC in Ontario and BC Assessment. Some provinces offer homeowner grants or deferral programs.
UK: homes pay Council Tax based on valuation bands (in England, still based on 1991 values), not a percentage of current value. Commercial property pays business rates.
Worked comparisons for Canada and the UK
Canada: a home assessed at $600,000 in a municipality with a combined residential rate of 0.75% pays about $4,500 a year (illustrative rate; use your city's published rate).
UK: Council Tax doesn't depend on a percentage. In England, the average Band D bill for 2025/26 was about £2,280 [VERIFY], and bands A to H are charged as fixed fractions of Band D, from 6/9 to 18/9. Single occupants get a 25% discount.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the purchase price with the old rate without checking the assessment ratio.
Forgetting special assessments and district levies listed separately on the bill.
Missing exemptions. Homestead exemptions often require an application.
Comparing nominal mill rates across states. Use effective rates.
Frequently asked questions
How is property tax calculated?
Taxable value (assessed value minus exemptions) times the tax rate.
What is a mill?
One-tenth of a cent: $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable value.
What is a homestead exemption?
A reduction in taxable value (or a cap on increases) for your primary residence, available in many states.
Can I deduct property tax?
Yes, if you itemize, within the SALT cap.
Why did my property tax go up?
Your assessed value rose, local rates increased, an exemption expired, or a cap reset after a sale.
When is property tax paid?
It varies: annually, semi-annually or quarterly, often through your mortgage escrow.
