H1: Closing Cost Calculator
Closing costs are the fees and prepaid items you pay when a home purchase or refinance completes, on top of the down payment. They include lender charges, title and settlement fees, government recording fees and transfer taxes, and the first deposits into your escrow account. This calculator adds up the items you enter and shows your total closing costs and the cash you'll need to bring to closing.
How to use this calculator
Enter amounts from your Loan Estimate (which lenders must give you within three business days of applying) or quotes from title companies. Leave anything that doesn't apply at zero.
1. Loan amount and interest rate: used for points, origination and prepaid interest.
2. Lender charges: origination fee, discount points, application or underwriting fee.
3. Third-party services: appraisal, credit report, flood certification, survey, pest or home inspection.
4. Title and settlement: lender's title insurance, owner's title insurance (optional), settlement or escrow fee.
5. Government fees: recording fees and any state or local transfer taxes.
6. Prepaids: interest from closing to month end, the first year of homeowners insurance.
7. Initial escrow deposit: a few months of property tax and insurance.
8. Credits: seller concessions, lender credits and your earnest money deposit.
Results: total closing costs, closing costs as a percentage of the loan, and cash to close.
How it works
Closing costs = Lender charges + Third-party fees + Title/settlement + Government fees + Prepaids + Escrow deposits
Cash to close = Down payment + Closing costs − Seller and lender credits − Earnest money
Prepaid interest = Loan × Rate ÷ 365 × Days until the first of next month
There's no single national figure for closing costs; they depend on the state, the lender and the price. That's why the calculator uses your own itemised inputs rather than a percentage.

Should you pay discount points?
One point (1% of the loan, $3,200.00) might lower the rate from 6.5% to 6.25% [the actual trade-off is lender-specific]. That saves $52.32 a month, so you'd break even after about 61 months. Points make sense only if you'll keep the loan longer than that.
Understanding your results
Prepaids and escrow aren't really fees. They're your own tax, insurance and interest paid in advance, but you still need the cash at closing.
Compare Loan Estimates. Section A (origination charges) is controlled by the lender; sections B and C are third-party services, some of which you can shop for.
Closing Disclosure: you must receive it at least three business days before closing. Compare it with the Loan Estimate; certain fees can't increase at all, and others only within a 10% tolerance.
Wire fraud warning: confirm wiring instructions by phone with your title company using a number you already know.
Ways to reduce closing costs
Shop lenders and title services.
Ask for seller concessions (subject to loan program limits).
Accept a lender credit in exchange for a slightly higher rate.
Check state or local first-time buyer assistance programs.
Close near the end of the month to reduce prepaid interest (it doesn't change total interest, only when you pay it).
State differences
Transfer taxes, recording fees and title insurance rates vary widely by state and county. Some states have no transfer tax; others charge it on both buyer and seller, or split it by custom. In some states attorneys handle closings, adding a legal fee. Check your county recorder and state revenue department.
Tips and common mistakes
Budgeting only for the down payment.
Leaving out escrow deposits, which can be several thousand dollars.
Assuming the seller pays everything because of a concession; check the cap.
Skipping owner's title insurance without understanding what it covers.
Reading your Loan Estimate
Lenders must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days of your application. Section A lists origination charges, B the services you can't shop for, and C the services you can shop for, such as title and settlement. Compare section A and the total in section J across lenders on the same day, since rates change daily. Three business days before closing you'll receive the Closing Disclosure; compare it line by line with the estimate and ask about any increase.
Frequently asked questions
How much are closing costs?
They vary by location, lender and price. Your Loan Estimate gives the best figure.
Who pays closing costs?
The buyer pays most, but sellers can contribute through concessions, and sellers pay their own costs (such as agent commissions).
Can closing costs be rolled into the loan?
On a purchase, usually not, although lender credits can cover some. On a refinance, they often can.
What is cash to close?
The total you need at closing: down payment plus closing costs, minus credits and deposits already paid.
Are closing costs tax-deductible?
Most aren't for a home you live in. Points and prepaid mortgage interest may be deductible if you itemize, and property tax paid at closing may count toward the SALT deduction.
What's the difference between a Loan Estimate and a Closing Disclosure?
The Loan Estimate comes early with estimated costs; the Closing Disclosure shows the final terms before closing.
