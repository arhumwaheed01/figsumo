H1: US Estate Tax Calculator
This calculator estimates the US federal estate tax due on an estate that is larger than the federal exemption (the basic exclusion amount). Enter the taxable estate and the exemption that applies. For deaths in 2026, the basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per person (IRS, IR-2025-103). Very few estates owe federal estate tax, but for those that do, the tax on the amount above the exemption is 40%.
How to use this calculator
1. Taxable estate value: the gross estate (everything the person owned or controlled at death, including real estate, investments, business interests, retirement accounts and certain life insurance) minus allowable deductions: debts, funeral and administration expenses, bequests to a surviving spouse (the marital deduction) and to charity. Add back lifetime taxable gifts made after 1976, because they use up exemption.
2. Federal exemption: the basic exclusion amount for the year of death ($15,000,000 for 2026), plus any unused exclusion inherited from a spouse who died earlier (DSUE, "portability") if it was elected on that spouse's estate tax return.
Results: taxable amount above exemption, estimated federal estate tax (40%) and a note that the figure is simplified.
The formula
Amount above exemption = max(0, Taxable estate − Exemption)
Estimated estate tax   = 40% × Amount above exemption
The actual Form 706 computation applies graduated rates from 18% to 40% to the whole tax base and then subtracts the unified credit, which equals the tax on the exclusion amount. Because the exclusion is far above $1 million (where the 40% bracket starts), the result is the same as taxing only the excess at 40%. The calculator uses that shortcut.
Worked examples
Example 1: $18,000,000 taxable estate, death in 2026
Amount above exemption = 18,000,000 − 15,000,000 = $3,000,000.00
Estimated estate tax = 40% × $3,000,000.00 = $1,200,000.00
Example 2: a married couple with $28,000,000, using portability
The first spouse leaves everything to the survivor; the unlimited marital deduction means no tax at the first death, and the executor elects portability of the unused $15,000,000.
At the survivor's death (assuming 2026 values), the exemption is 2 × 15,000,000 = $30,000,000.00
Amount above = $0, so estimated tax: $0.00
Without the portability election, the survivor's exemption would be $15,000,000 and the estimated tax would be $5,200,000.00
Portability has to be elected on a timely filed Form 706 for the first spouse, even if no tax is due. This is the most common expensive mistake in this area.
Understanding your results
Taxable amount above exemption is the part of the estate exposed to tax.
Estimated federal estate tax is the 40% shortcut. The real figure can differ if there are credits (for example for foreign death taxes or state death taxes as a deduction) or valuation discounts.
Who pays: the estate, before assets pass to heirs. Heirs don't pay federal estate tax personally, and inherited assets generally receive a "stepped-up" basis for capital gains.
Filing: an estate tax return (Form 706) is due within 9 months of death if the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the filing threshold, with a 6-month extension available.
The exemption over time
The exclusion is indexed for inflation. It was $13,610,000 in 2024 and $13,990,000 in 2025 [VERIFY 2025 figure in Rev. Proc. 2024-40]. The 2025 tax law (the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act") set it at $15,000,000 for 2026, indexed afterwards, replacing the scheduled drop that would otherwise have roughly halved it. Always use the figure for the year of death. The IRS estate tax page lists the amounts.
Gift tax and the annual exclusion
Gifts and estates share one lifetime exemption. Gifts up to the annual exclusion per recipient don't use it ($19,000 per recipient for 2025 [VERIFY 2026 annual exclusion in Rev. Proc. 2025-32]). Larger gifts are reported on Form 709 and reduce the exemption left at death.
State estate and inheritance taxes
Some states levy their own estate tax, often with much lower exemptions than the federal one, and a few levy inheritance tax on the people who receive assets [VERIFY current list of states and their exemptions with each state's revenue department]. This calculator covers federal tax only.
Canada and the UK
Canada has no estate tax. Instead, a person is generally treated as having sold their capital property at fair market value at death (a "deemed disposition"), and the resulting capital gains are taxed on the final return, unless the property passes to a spouse or partner. Provinces also charge probate fees. See the Canadian capital gains tax calculator.
United Kingdom charges Inheritance Tax at 40% above the £325,000 nil-rate band, with an extra residence nil-rate band of up to £175,000 when a home passes to direct descendants, tapered for estates over £2 million. Both bands are frozen until April 2030 [VERIFY]. Transfers between spouses and civil partners are exempt, and unused bands can pass to the survivor. From April 2027, most unused pension funds are due to be brought into the estate for IHT [VERIFY].
Tips and common mistakes
Using the wrong year's exemption. Check the year of death.
Forgetting life insurance. Policies you own are in your gross estate unless held in a properly structured trust.
Missing the portability election. File Form 706 for the first spouse to preserve the unused exemption.
Ignoring state tax. A state threshold can be far lower than the federal one.
Mixing up estate and income tax. Inherited IRAs and 401(k)s can also trigger income tax for the beneficiary.
Frequently asked questions
What is the federal estate tax exemption for 2026?
$15,000,000 per person, according to IRS release IR-2025-103. Married couples can effectively shelter up to $30,000,000 with portability.
What is the estate tax rate?
Graduated rates run from 18% to 40%, but because of the size of the exemption, any taxable excess is taxed at 40%.
Do heirs pay estate tax?
Not federally. The estate pays before distributing assets. Some states have inheritance taxes paid by recipients.
Is life insurance included in the estate?
If the deceased owned the policy or had "incidents of ownership", the death benefit is usually included in the gross estate.
Does the estate tax apply to non-US citizens?
Different rules apply. Non-resident non-citizens get a much smaller exemption on US-situated assets; transfers to a non-citizen spouse do not get the unlimited marital deduction.
Can I reduce estate tax?
Common legal approaches include lifetime gifts, charitable bequests and trusts. These are complex; take advice from an estate planning attorney.
