H1: Marriage Tax Calculator
Getting married changes how you file your federal income tax return, and that can raise or lower the total tax the two of you pay. This calculator compares the tax two partners would owe filing as single people with the tax they'd owe filing jointly as a married couple, using the 2026 federal brackets. The difference is the "marriage bonus" (you pay less) or "marriage penalty" (you pay more).
How to use this calculator
1. Partner 1 taxable income: income after deductions (standard or itemized) for the year. On a past return, this is the "taxable income" line of Form 1040.
2. Partner 2 taxable income: the same for the other partner.
Results: tax if both file single, tax if married filing jointly, and the marriage bonus / penalty (joint tax minus the combined single tax; a negative number is a bonus).
Because the inputs are taxable income, the standard deduction is not applied by the tool. For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 single and $32,200 married filing jointly (exactly double), so subtracting each person's deduction before entering incomes gives a like-for-like comparison.
The formula
Tax(single) = T_single(Income1) + T_single(Income2)
Tax(joint)  = T_joint(Income1 + Income2)
Bonus (−) or penalty (+) = Tax(joint) − Tax(single)

Joint brackets are exactly twice the single brackets up to the 32% band, so for most couples the bracket effect is zero. Differences appear when incomes are very unequal (a bonus) or when combined income reaches the 35% and 37% bands (a possible penalty).
Worked examples
Example 1: $90,000 and $60,000
Single: $14,512.00 + $7,912.00 = $22,424.00
Joint on $150,000: $22,424.00
Bonus/penalty: $0.00. Both incomes sit within brackets that double exactly.
Example 2: one earner with $150,000, the other with none
Single (the earner alone): $28,598.00
Joint: $22,424.00
Marriage bonus: $6,174.00. The joint brackets let the higher earner use the wider bands.
Example 3: high incomes, $500,000 and $400,000
Single total: $252,538.50
Joint on $900,000: $255,164.50
Marriage penalty: $2,626.00. The joint 35% band ends at $768,700, well below twice the single threshold of $640,600.
Understanding your results
A bonus usually appears when one partner earns much more than the other.
A penalty from brackets alone only arises at high combined incomes. Below that, real-world penalties usually come from other rules (next section).
This is a bracket comparison. It doesn't include credits, other taxes or state tax.
Other sources of marriage penalties and bonuses
These aren't in the calculator but often matter more than brackets:
Earned Income Tax Credit: income limits for married couples are not double the single limits, so two low-to-moderate earners can lose some or all of the credit.
State and local tax (SALT) deduction cap: the cap is the same for single and joint filers [VERIFY 2026 amount and phase-down under the 2025 law], which can penalize two itemizing homeowners.
Net Investment Income Tax and Additional Medicare Tax: thresholds are $200,000 single and $250,000 joint, not double (IRS Topic 559 and Topic 560).
Head of household: an unmarried parent may file as head of household, which has better brackets than single. Marrying gives up that status.
Child Tax Credit: $2,200 per child for 2026; phase-out thresholds are $200,000 single and $400,000 joint [VERIFY], so it is usually neutral.
State taxes: some states' brackets for couples are not double those for singles.
Filing separately
Married couples can also file separately (MFS). For 2026, MFS brackets match single up to $256,225, then the 35% band ends at $384,350. MFS rarely lowers total tax and blocks several credits, but it can help in specific cases such as income-driven student loan repayment or separating liability.
Country notes
The US is unusual in taxing married couples jointly. Canada taxes each person individually; spouses can share some credits and split eligible pension income. The UK also taxes individuals; the Marriage Allowance lets a non-taxpayer transfer £1,260 of personal allowance to a basic-rate spouse. Use the Canada and UK income tax calculators for those systems.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering gross salary. Use taxable income, after deductions.
Forgetting the year. Your filing status for the whole year depends on whether you're married on 31 December.
Ignoring withholding. After marrying, update your Form W-4 so your paychecks withhold the right amount; see the W-4 withholding calculator.

Frequently asked questions
Is there a marriage penalty in 2026?
From brackets alone, only at high combined incomes (around the 35% and 37% bands). Other rules, such as the EITC and some thresholds, can create penalties at lower incomes.
Why do most couples see $0?
Because 2026 joint brackets are exactly double the single brackets up to the 32% band, and the joint standard deduction is double the single one.
Do I have to file jointly when married?
No. You can file jointly or separately. Joint filing usually costs less.
When does marriage count for tax?
If you're married on the last day of the year, you're treated as married for the whole year.
Does this include state taxes?
No. State rules vary; some have their own marriage penalties.
What about Social Security and Medicare taxes?
Payroll taxes are calculated per worker and are unaffected by marriage, except the Additional Medicare Tax threshold.
