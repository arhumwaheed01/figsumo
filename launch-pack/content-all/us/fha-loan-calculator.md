H1: FHA Loan Calculator
FHA loans are US mortgages insured by the Federal Housing Administration, popular with first-time buyers because they allow a small down payment and more flexible credit standards. This calculator estimates the monthly payment on an FHA loan, including the two kinds of FHA mortgage insurance: the upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP), which is usually added to the loan, and the annual MIP, which is paid monthly.
How to use this calculator
1. Home price: the purchase price.
2. Down payment (%): FHA's minimum is 3.5% with a credit score of 580 or higher, and 10% with a score from 500 to 579 [VERIFY in HUD Handbook 4000.1].
3. Base interest rate: the note rate on your Loan Estimate.
4. Term (years): usually 30 or 15.
5. Upfront MIP (%): a percentage of the base loan; 1.75% for most forward purchase loans [VERIFY]. The calculator adds it to the loan balance, which is how most borrowers pay it.
6. Annual MIP (%): a yearly percentage of the loan, divided into monthly payments. For most 30-year loans with less than 5% down it is 0.55% [VERIFY HUD Mortgagee Letter 2023-05 table].
Results: base loan, loan with upfront MIP, principal and interest (P&I) payment, estimated monthly MIP and total monthly (estimate).
The formula
Base loan        = Price × (1 − Down% ÷ 100)
UFMIP            = Base loan × Upfront MIP%
Loan with UFMIP  = Base loan + UFMIP
P&I              = L × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n),  L = loan with UFMIP, r = rate ÷ 12, n = years × 12
Monthly MIP      ≈ Base loan × Annual MIP% ÷ 12
Total monthly    = P&I + Monthly MIP
HUD calculates annual MIP on the average outstanding balance for each year, so the real premium falls slightly each year. The calculator uses the base loan, which gives the first-year figure.
Worked examples
Example 1: $300,000 home, 3.5% down, 6.5%, 30 years
Down payment: $10,500.00; base loan $289,500.00
UFMIP at 1.75%: $5,066.25, financed → loan $294,566.25
P&I: $1,861.86
Annual MIP at 0.55%: $132.69 a month
Total: $1,994.55 a month, before property tax and insurance
Example 2: $250,000 home, 10% down, 6.0%, 15 years
Base loan $225,000.00; UFMIP $3,937.50; loan $228,937.50
P&I: $1,931.90
With 10% down on a 15-year loan, the annual MIP rate is much lower (0.15% in the 2023 table [VERIFY]): $28.12 a month
Total: $1,960.03 a month
Understanding your results
P&I is the loan payment. It is calculated on the loan including the financed UFMIP, so you pay interest on the premium too.
Monthly MIP is the FHA insurance premium. It protects the lender, not you.
Total monthly excludes property tax, homeowners insurance and any HOA dues. Add these for the full housing payment lenders use in debt-to-income checks.
How long you pay MIP
For FHA loans with case numbers assigned since June 2013, annual MIP lasts for the life of the loan if your original down payment was under 10%, and for 11 years if it was 10% or more [VERIFY in HUD Mortgagee Letter 2013-04 and later letters]. Many borrowers remove MIP by refinancing into a conventional loan once they have about 20% equity. Conventional PMI, by contrast, can be cancelled at 80% LTV and ends automatically at 78% (CFPB). Compare with the PMI calculator.
FHA requirements in brief
Loan limits vary by county and are updated every year; check HUD's FHA mortgage limits lookup.
Property standards: the home must be appraised by an FHA-approved appraiser and meet minimum property requirements.
Primary residence: FHA loans are for homes you live in.
Debt-to-income: FHA allows higher ratios than many conventional loans when other factors compensate [VERIFY current limits].
Seller concessions: sellers can contribute toward closing costs up to a limit.
FHA or conventional?
FHA often suits borrowers with lower credit scores or small down payments, because its rates and insurance don't rise as steeply with lower scores. Borrowers with strong credit and 5% or more down often pay less with a conventional loan, mainly because PMI can be cancelled. Run both: this calculator for FHA, and the mortgage calculator plus PMI for conventional.
Country notes
FHA loans are US-only. In Canada, the closest equivalent is mortgage default insurance from CMHC or private insurers, required when the down payment is under 20%; see the CMHC insurance calculator. In the UK, there's no government-insured mortgage of this kind; high-LTV lending is offered by lenders directly, and the government has run mortgage guarantee schemes at times.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting the UFMIP. It's added to the loan unless you pay it in cash.
Using the wrong MIP rate. It depends on LTV, term and loan amount.
Comparing payment only. FHA's lifetime MIP can make it costlier over the long run.
Ignoring county limits. In expensive areas, the price you want may exceed FHA limits.
Frequently asked questions
What is the minimum down payment for an FHA loan?
3.5% with a credit score of at least 580; 10% with scores from 500 to 579 [VERIFY].
What is UFMIP?
A one-time FHA insurance premium, usually 1.75% of the base loan, normally added to the loan balance.
Can I remove FHA MIP?
If you put down 10% or more, annual MIP ends after 11 years. Otherwise it usually lasts for the life of the loan, unless you refinance.
Is MIP the same as PMI?
Both protect the lender, but MIP is FHA's government-backed insurance with set rates and duration, while PMI applies to conventional loans and can be cancelled earlier.
Does the payment include property tax and insurance?
No. Add them separately for the full monthly cost.
Are FHA loans only for first-time buyers?
No. Any eligible borrower buying a primary residence can use one.
