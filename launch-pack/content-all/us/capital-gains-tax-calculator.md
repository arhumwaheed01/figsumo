H1: US Capital Gains Tax Calculator
When you sell an investment, a second home or other capital asset for more than you paid, the profit is a capital gain and is subject to federal income tax. The rate depends on how long you held the asset and your taxable income. This calculator estimates federal tax on short-term and long-term gains for 2026, including the 3.8% net investment income tax for higher earners.
How to use this calculator
1. Filing status: single, married filing jointly, married filing separately or head of household.
2. Taxable ordinary income: your taxable income excluding the gains (wages, interest and so on, after the standard or itemized deduction).
3. Short-term gains: net gains on assets held one year or less.
4. Long-term gains: net gains on assets held more than one year (include qualified dividends here; they're taxed at the same rates).
5. Modified adjusted gross income (MAGI): for the net investment income tax test.
Results: tax on long-term gains by rate band, tax on short-term gains, net investment income tax, total federal tax on gains and the effective rate on the gains.
How it works
Gain = Sale price − Selling costs − Cost basis (purchase price plus commissions and improvements).
Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income at your marginal rate (10% to 37%).
Long-term gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%. They're "stacked" on top of your ordinary income: ordinary income fills the brackets first, and the gains use what's left.

Net investment income tax (NIIT): 3.8% on the lesser of net investment income or MAGI above $200,000 (single, head of household), $250,000 (married filing jointly) or $125,000 (married filing separately). These thresholds aren't indexed for inflation.
Worked examples
Example 1: single, $60,000 ordinary taxable income, $20,000 long-term gain
Ordinary income already exceeds the $49,450 0% threshold, so all the gain is in the 15% band.
Tax = 15% × 20,000 = $3,000.00
If the same gain were short-term, it would be taxed at the 22% ordinary rate: $4,400.00, $1,400.00 more. Holding for more than a year can matter.
Example 2: single, $35,000 ordinary taxable income, $30,000 long-term gain
0% band: 49,450 − 35,000 = $14,450.00 of gain taxed at 0%
15% band: the remaining $15,550.00
Tax = $2,332.50
Example 3: married filing jointly, $500,000 ordinary taxable income, $200,000 long-term gain
15% on $113,700.00 (up to $613,700) and 20% on $86,300.00: $34,315.00
NIIT: MAGI about $700,000, so 3.8% × the lesser of $200,000 or $450,000 = $7,600.00
Total federal tax on the gain: $41,915.00
Example 4: selling a day too early
Single filer, $60,000 of ordinary taxable income, $10,000 gain:
Held one year or less (short-term): taxed as ordinary income at 22% = $2,200.00
Held more than one year (long-term): 15% = $1,500.00
Waiting until the holding period passes one year saves $700.00 here, if the price holds up in the meantime.
Example 5: harvesting a loss
The same filer also has a $4,000 loss on another holding. Selling it nets against the gain, cutting the long-term taxable gain to $6,000 and the tax to $900.00. Net losses beyond gains can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income a year, with the rest carried forward. Buying the same security back within 30 days triggers the wash-sale rule and disallows the loss.
Special cases
Selling your home: up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly) is excluded if you owned and lived in the home for at least two of the five years before the sale. A couple selling for $620,000 a home with a basis of $280,000 has a $340,000 gain, fully excluded.
Collectibles (art, coins, precious metals) are taxed at up to 28%, and depreciation recapture on real estate (unrecaptured Section 1250 gain) at up to 25%. This calculator doesn't model those rates.
Losses: capital losses offset gains, and up to $3,000 of net loss a year ($1,500 married filing separately) offsets ordinary income, with the rest carried forward.
Wash sales: a loss is disallowed if you buy substantially identical securities within 30 days before or after the sale.
Inherited assets generally get a stepped-up basis to their value at death.
Understanding your results
Federal only. Most states tax capital gains as ordinary income; see the state income tax calculator.
Effective rate on gains is total tax ÷ total gains, useful for comparing scenarios.
Timing: spreading sales over two tax years, or selling in a low-income year, can keep more of the gain in the 0% band.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting cost basis adjustments such as reinvested dividends and commissions, which overstates the gain.
Counting from the wrong date. Long-term means held more than one year, counted from the day after purchase.
Ignoring NIIT once income passes the thresholds.
Assuming 0% means no tax anywhere. States may still tax the gain.
Frequently asked questions
What is the long-term capital gains rate for 2026?
0%, 15% or 20% depending on taxable income and filing status.
How long do I need to hold for long-term treatment?
More than one year.
Are short-term gains taxed differently?
Yes, at ordinary income rates.
What is the net investment income tax?
An extra 3.8% on investment income for people with MAGI above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).
Do I pay capital gains tax on my house?
Not on the first $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for couples) if you meet the ownership and use tests.
Can capital losses reduce my taxes?
Yes. They offset gains, then up to $3,000 of other income each year.
