H1: HSA Calculator
A health savings account (HSA) is a tax-advantaged account for medical costs, available if you're covered by a high-deductible health plan (HDHP). It's the only account in the US tax code with a triple advantage: contributions are deductible (or pre-tax through payroll), growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. This calculator checks your 2026 contribution limit, estimates your tax savings and projects your balance.
How to use this calculator
1. Coverage type: self-only or family HDHP.
2. Age 55 or older: adds a $1,000 catch-up contribution.
3. Months of HDHP coverage in the year, if not the full year.
4. How you contribute: through payroll (also avoids FICA) or directly (deducted on your tax return).
5. Employer contribution: counts toward the limit.
6. Federal and state marginal tax rates.
7. Balance, expected growth, years and annual medical spending for the projection.
Results: your 2026 limit, room left after employer contributions, tax savings this year, and the projected balance.

How it works
Limit = Annual limit (by coverage) + Catch-up (if 55+), pro-rated by months eligible
Your room = Limit − Employer contributions
Tax savings = Contribution × (Federal rate + State rate + FICA 7.65% if via payroll)
FICA savings apply only to payroll contributions through a cafeteria plan; direct contributions are deducted for income tax but not FICA. Employer contributions are also free of income tax and FICA.
Worked examples
Example 1: family coverage, maximum contribution through payroll
22% federal bracket, 5% state tax, contributions through payroll:
Tax savings = 8,750 × (22% + 5% + 7.65%) = $3,031.88
The $3,031.88 saved means the $8,750 deposit costs you about $5,718.12 of take-home pay.
Contributing directly instead saves $2,362.50 (no FICA saving).
If your employer puts in $1,000, you can contribute $7,750.00 yourself.

Example 3: investing the HSA for retirement
Contributing $4,400 a year for 25 years and investing it at 6% gives about $241,403.85, from $110,000.00 of contributions, if you pay medical costs from other money and keep receipts. Qualified medical withdrawals stay tax-free at any time, including for expenses incurred years earlier (after the HSA was opened).
Example 4: direct contributions in a state with no income tax
Self-only coverage, 24% federal bracket, no state income tax, contributing from your bank account rather than payroll:

Partial-year coverage
With family HDHP coverage for 7 months, the pro-rated limit is about $5,104.17, unless you use the last-month rule (coverage on 1 December lets you contribute the full amount, provided you stay eligible through the following year).
Using HSA money
Qualified expenses: deductibles, copays, prescriptions, dental, vision and many other medical costs (IRS Publication 502).
Non-qualified withdrawals are taxed as income plus a 20% penalty before 65; after 65 the penalty doesn't apply, so the HSA works like a traditional IRA for non-medical spending.
No use-it-or-lose-it: balances roll over every year and stay with you if you change jobs.
Medicare: once you enroll in Medicare, you can't contribute, but you can still spend the balance (including on Medicare premiums other than Medigap).
Understanding your results
Eligibility matters. You must be covered by an HDHP and have no disqualifying coverage (such as a general-purpose health FSA or Medicare), and you can't be claimed as someone else's dependent.
State tax: a few states don't follow federal HSA rules and tax contributions or earnings [VERIFY your state].
Compare with a 401(k). After capturing any 401(k) match, many people prioritise the HSA for its tax treatment; see the 401(k) calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Over-contributing when you and your employer both contribute. Excess contributions face a 6% excise tax each year until removed.
Leaving the balance in cash if you're saving long term.
Losing receipts. Keep records to justify tax-free withdrawals.
Contributing after enrolling in Medicare.
Notes for Canadian and UK readers
There's no direct equivalent of an HSA in either country, because most medical care is publicly funded. In Canada, some employers offer a health spending account, a benefit that reimburses medical and dental costs tax-free, and individuals can claim eligible medical expenses as a non-refundable tax credit. In the UK, NHS care is free at the point of use; private medical insurance provided by an employer is normally a taxable benefit in kind, and there's no tax-free personal medical savings account. People moving to the US from either country can open an HSA only once they're covered by a qualifying HDHP.
Frequently asked questions
What is the HSA limit for 2026?
$4,400 self-only and $8,750 family, plus $1,000 if you're 55 or older.
Do I need a high-deductible plan?
Yes, an HSA-eligible HDHP.
Can I invest my HSA?
Most providers let you invest once your balance passes a minimum.
What happens to my HSA if I leave my job?
It's yours to keep.
Can I use my HSA for my spouse's expenses?
Yes, for your spouse and tax dependants, even if they aren't on your plan.
What is the deadline for contributions?
The tax filing deadline for the year, usually April 15 of the following year.
