H1: PMI Calculator
Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is usually required on a conventional mortgage when your down payment is less than 20% of the home's value. It protects the lender if you default, and you pay for it, typically as a monthly charge added to your mortgage payment. This calculator estimates your monthly PMI, your total payment, and when PMI can be cancelled under federal rules.
How to use this calculator
1. Home price: the purchase price or appraised value, whichever is lower (the "original value").
2. Down payment: in dollars or as a percentage.
3. Interest rate and loan term.
4. Annual PMI rate (%): from your Loan Estimate. There's no official rate; it depends on credit score, down payment, loan type and insurer. Rates commonly fall somewhere between about 0.3% and 1.5% of the loan a year [VERIFY with your lender].
Results: loan amount, loan-to-value (LTV), monthly PMI, principal and interest, total monthly payment, the month PMI can be requested off (80% LTV) and automatically ends (78% LTV), and total PMI paid.
How it works
Loan = Price − Down payment
LTV = Loan ÷ Original value
Monthly PMI = Loan × Annual PMI rate ÷ 12
Cancellation (Homeowners Protection Act, for most loans closed on or after 29 July 1999):
You can ask your servicer to cancel PMI when the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the original value, or earlier if extra payments get you there.
PMI ends automatically when the balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the original value, if you're current on payments.
It also ends at the midpoint of the loan term (for example, after 15 years of a 30-year loan) if it hasn't already.
The calculator uses the original amortization schedule to find these dates.
Worked examples
Example 1: $400,000 home, 10% down
Loan $360,000 at 6.5% for 30 years; PMI 0.5% a year.
Monthly PMI = 360,000 × 0.005 ÷ 12 = $150.00 ($1,800.00 a year)
Principal and interest: $2,275.44; total $2,425.44 before taxes and insurance
Balance reaches 80% of $400,000 ($320,000) at payment 95 (about 7.9 years); you can request cancellation then.
Balance reaches 78% ($312,000) at payment 109 (about 9.1 years), when PMI ends automatically.
Total PMI paid to automatic termination: $16,350.00 ($14,250.00 if you request removal at 80%).
With $300 a month extra, the balance reaches $320,000 at payment 56 (about 4.7 years), saving roughly $5,850.00 of PMI on top of the interest savings.
Example 2: 5% down
A $400,000 home with $20,000 down needs a $380,000 loan. If the PMI rate is 0.8% because of the higher LTV, PMI is $253.33 a month.
Example 3: when does PMI end?
Example 1's $360,000 loan on a $400,000 home, at 6.5% over 30 years, with no extra payments:
You can ask to cancel PMI when the balance reaches 80% of the original value, $320,000: after about 95 months (7.9 years)
PMI ends automatically at 78%, $312,000: after about 109 months (9.1 years)
At $150 a month, that's roughly $14,250 of PMI before you can request cancellation. Extra principal payments, or a new appraisal showing the home has risen in value, can bring the date forward; lenders set their own rules for value-based cancellation.
Understanding your results
PMI rate drivers: a higher credit score and bigger down payment lower the rate.
Monthly PMI is constant under the most common borrower-paid monthly plan, based on the original loan.
Other PMI types: single-premium (paid upfront), split-premium, and lender-paid PMI (built into a higher interest rate, which doesn't cancel).
Rising home values: some servicers allow cancellation based on a new appraisal showing enough equity, usually after a minimum period [VERIFY servicer and investor rules].
PMI vs other mortgage insurance
FHA loans have an upfront and annual mortgage insurance premium (MIP), and for most loans with less than 10% down MIP lasts for the life of the loan; see the FHA loan calculator.
VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance but a one-time funding fee; see the VA loan calculator.
USDA loans charge a guarantee fee.
Is PMI worth it?
PMI lets you buy sooner with less cash. Compare the PMI cost with the cost of waiting to save 20% (rent paid and possible price changes). On Example 1, PMI totals $16,350.00 over about 9.1 years. Some buyers use a second mortgage ("piggyback" 80-10-10) to avoid PMI, at the cost of a higher-rate second loan.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting to request cancellation at 80%. Automatic termination comes later, at 78%.
Assuming PMI is tax-deductible. The mortgage insurance premium deduction expired after 2021 [VERIFY whether restored for 2026].
Confusing PMI with homeowners insurance. PMI protects the lender; homeowners insurance protects your home.
Using the current value for automatic termination; the rules use the original value.
Quick check
Ask your servicer for the original value it uses for cancellation.
Frequently asked questions
When is PMI required?
On most conventional loans with less than 20% down.
How much does PMI cost?
It depends on your loan and credit; your Loan Estimate shows the exact amount.
When does PMI go away?
You can request removal at 80% LTV based on the original value; it ends automatically at 78% or at the loan's midpoint.
Can I remove PMI early?
Yes, by paying down the balance to 80% of the original value, or in some cases with a new appraisal.
Does refinancing remove PMI?
If the new loan is 80% or less of the current value, a new conventional loan won't need PMI.
Is FHA mortgage insurance the same as PMI?
No. FHA MIP has different rules and often can't be cancelled.
