H1: US State Income Tax Calculator
On top of federal income tax, most US states tax income too, and the rules differ a lot: some have no income tax, some charge one flat rate, and others use graduated brackets with their own deductions and credits. This calculator estimates your state income tax for 2026 from your state, filing status and income, and shows your effective state tax rate.
How to use this calculator
1. State of residence. If you lived in more than one state during the year or work in a different state, see the notes below.
2. Filing status: most states follow your federal status.
3. Income: wages and other income. Many states start from federal adjusted gross income (AGI).
4. State deductions and exemptions: the calculator applies the state's standard deduction or personal exemption where it has one; you can override it.
5. Custom rate mode: enter a flat rate or your own brackets if you want to model a state from its tax form.
Results: state taxable income, state income tax, effective rate (tax ÷ income) and marginal rate.
How state income taxes work
States with no tax on wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. (Washington taxes some long-term capital gains; New Hampshire's tax on interest and dividends has been repealed [VERIFY].)
Flat-tax states apply one rate to taxable income. A growing number of states have moved to flat rates in recent years.
Graduated-tax states use brackets like the federal system, with each slice of income taxed at its own rate.
State taxable income = Income (often federal AGI) ± State adjustments − Deductions/exemptions
State tax = Σ (income in each bracket × bracket rate) − State credits
Worked examples

Example 1: a flat-rate state
$80,000 of income, a flat 4.95% rate and a $2,850 personal exemption (Illinois-style [VERIFY 2026 figures]):
Tax = (80,000 − 2,850) × 4.95% = $3,818.93
A flat 3.07% rate with no standard deduction (Pennsylvania-style [VERIFY]):
Tax = 80,000 × 3.07% = $2,456.00
Example 2: graduated brackets
A hypothetical state taxes 2% on the first $10,000, 4% to $40,000 and 6% above, with $70,000 of taxable income:
2% × 10,000 = $200.00
4% × 30,000 = $1,200.00
6% × 30,000 = $1,800.00
Tax = $3,200.00; effective rate 4.57%, marginal rate 6%

Example 4: credit for tax paid to another state
You live in State A (flat 5%) and work in State B, which taxes your $90,000 salary as a nonresident at 4%:
State B tax: 90,000 × 4% = $3,600.00
State A tax before credit: 90,000 × 5% = $4,500.00
Credit for tax paid to B: $3,600.00, so you owe A $900.00
Total state tax: $4,500.00, the higher of the two states' amounts, not both added together
Rules vary, and some neighbouring states have reciprocity agreements so only the home state taxes wages [VERIFY your states].
Multi-state situations
Moving during the year: you usually file part-year resident returns in both states, each taxing income earned while you lived there.
Living in one state and working in another: the work state may tax your wages as a nonresident, and your home state usually gives a credit for tax paid to the other state. Some neighbouring states have reciprocity agreements so you pay only your home state.
Remote work: a few states tax remote workers based on the employer's location ("convenience of the employer" rules) [VERIFY current states].
Understanding your results
Local income taxes are extra in some places, such as New York City, many Ohio and Pennsylvania municipalities, and Maryland counties. They aren't included yet.
Federal deduction: state income tax counts toward the federal SALT deduction if you itemize, subject to the cap [VERIFY 2026 cap]. At a 24% federal rate, deducting $3,200.00 could save up to $768.00 of federal tax.
Capital gains: most states tax them as ordinary income.
Retirement income: many states exempt Social Security and some exempt pension income.
Tips and common mistakes
Assuming no state tax means no tax. States without income tax often rely more on sales and property taxes.
Using federal taxable income when your state starts from AGI, or the other way round.
Forgetting the credit for taxes paid to another state.
Ignoring local taxes on your paycheck.
Notes for Canadian and UK readers
Canada has a similar two-level system: every province and territory charges its own income tax on top of federal tax, and residence on 31 December decides which province taxes the whole year's income. In the UK, income tax is national except in Scotland, which sets its own bands and rates on earnings; Wales has the power to vary rates but has so far matched England. Frequently asked questions
Which states have no income tax?
Alaska, Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and Wyoming have no broad income tax, and New Hampshire and Washington don't tax wages.
What is a flat tax state?
A state that taxes all taxable income at a single rate.
Do I pay state tax where I live or where I work?
Generally your home state taxes all your income, and a work state may tax wages earned there, with a credit to avoid double taxation.
Is state income tax deductible on my federal return?
Yes, if you itemize, within the SALT cap.
Do states tax Social Security benefits?
Most don't; a handful do, often with exemptions [VERIFY current list].
How accurate is the estimate?
It uses each state's published 2026 brackets and standard deductions; state credits and special adjustments may change your final figure.
