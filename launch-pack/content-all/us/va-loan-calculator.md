H1: VA Loan Calculator
VA home loans are mortgages guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible veterans, active-duty service members, National Guard and Reserve members, and some surviving spouses. They usually need no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance, but most borrowers pay a one-time VA funding fee. This calculator estimates the funding fee, the total loan amount when the fee is financed, and the monthly principal and interest payment.
How to use this calculator
1. Home price: the purchase price.
2. Down payment ($): optional; VA loans allow 0%. A down payment of 5% or 10% of the loan lowers the funding fee.
3. Interest rate: the annual rate from your lender.
4. Term (years): usually 30 or 15.
5. VA funding fee (%): use the rate from the table below that applies to you, or 0% if you're exempt.
Results: base loan, funding fee, total loan amount (base loan plus the financed fee) and monthly payment (principal and interest).
The formula
Base loan = Home price − Down payment
Funding fee = Base loan × Fee %
Total loan = Base loan + Funding fee
Monthly payment = L × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
L: total loan amount
r: annual rate ÷ 12
n: years × 12
The calculator assumes the fee is financed. You can also pay it in cash at closing.

Other loan types: cash-out refinance 2.15% first use and 3.3% after; interest rate reduction refinance (IRRRL) 0.5%; loan assumption 0.5%; Native American Direct Loan 1.25% for purchase. These rates have been in effect since 7 April 2023. The fee applies to the loan amount, not the home price.
You don't pay the fee if you receive (or are eligible for but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead of) VA compensation for a service-connected disability, receive Dependency and Indemnity Compensation as a surviving spouse, have a pre-discharge rating before closing, or are an active-duty member who has received a Purple Heart.
Worked examples
Example 1: $350,000 home, no down payment, first use
Funding fee = 350,000 × 2.15% = $7,525.00
Total loan = $357,525.00
Monthly payment at 6.25% over 30 years = $2,201.34
Financing the fee adds $46.33 a month compared with $2,155.01 on $350,000. Over 30 years, total interest is $434,958.45.
Example 2: $400,000 home with 10% down
Base loan $360,000.00; fee at 1.25% = $4,500.00
Total loan $364,500.00; payment at 6% = $2,185.36
For comparison, a subsequent-use borrower with nothing down on $400,000 would pay a 3.3% fee: $13,200.00.
Example 3: second use, no down payment
The same $350,000 home with no down payment on a subsequent use:
Funding fee = 350,000 × 3.3% = $11,550.00
Total loan = $361,550.00
Monthly payment at 6.25% over 30 years = $2,226.13, $24.79 more than first use
Putting 5% down instead cuts the fee to 1.5% of the smaller loan: $4,987.50 on $332,500.
Example 4: an exempt veteran
Veterans receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability, and some surviving spouses, don't pay the funding fee. For Example 1, that removes the $7,525 fee, and the payment at 6.25% falls to $2,155.01. Your Certificate of Eligibility shows whether you're exempt.
Understanding your results
Monthly payment is principal and interest only. Add property tax, homeowners insurance and any HOA dues for the full housing cost. VA lenders count these when checking affordability.
No PMI. A conventional loan with under 20% down usually needs private mortgage insurance; an FHA loan has mortgage insurance premiums. See the PMI calculator and the FHA loan calculator.
Residual income test. VA underwriting checks the money left over each month after major expenses, by family size and region, as well as debt-to-income.
VA loan basics
Eligibility: based on length and type of service. You need a Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which lenders can usually request for you.
Entitlement: with full entitlement there's no VA loan limit; if you have a VA loan already, remaining entitlement and county limits affect how much you can borrow with no down payment.
Primary residence: VA purchase loans are for homes you'll live in.
Seller concessions: VA limits seller concessions to 4% of the home's reasonable value; normal closing-cost credits don't count toward this limit.
Closing costs: only the funding fee can be financed into a purchase loan. Other closing costs must be paid at closing or covered by seller credits; see the closing cost calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the wrong fee rate. Check first use vs subsequent use and your down payment band.
Assuming the payment is the whole cost. Add taxes and insurance.
Missing an exemption. If you have a disability rating, the fee may be zero, and you may be eligible for a refund if a rating is later made retroactive.
Not comparing lenders. VA sets the fee, but rates, points and lender fees vary.
Frequently asked questions
What is the VA funding fee?
A one-time fee paid on most VA-backed loans, set as a percentage of the loan amount.
Can I roll the funding fee into the loan?
Yes. The calculator assumes you do.
Do VA loans need a down payment?
Usually not, though putting 5% or more down reduces the funding fee.
Do VA loans have mortgage insurance?
No monthly mortgage insurance; the funding fee takes its place.
Who is exempt from the funding fee?
Mainly veterans receiving or eligible for VA disability compensation, eligible surviving spouses, and Purple Heart recipients on active duty.
Can I use a VA loan more than once?
Yes. Subsequent use has a higher fee when you put down less than 5%.
