H1: US Paycheck Calculator (2026)
Your offer letter quotes gross pay. Your bank account receives net pay. This calculator estimates the gap for the 2026 tax year: federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and pre-tax retirement contributions, spread across your pay schedule. State and local income tax are not yet included (see Country notes), so add them from your state's figures if your state has one.
How to use this calculator
1. Gross pay: your annual salary, or an hourly rate with hours per week.
2. Pay frequency: weekly (52), bi-weekly (26), semi-monthly (24) or monthly (12).
3. Filing status: single, married filing jointly or head of household. This sets the standard deduction and the tax brackets.
4. Pre-tax deductions: traditional 401(k)/403(b) as a percent or an amount, plus health premiums and HSA contributions paid through payroll.
5. Other adjustments (optional): extra withholding from line 4(c) of your W-4, or deductions you expect to claim.
The formula
Annual gross                      G
Pre-tax retirement (401k etc.)    D401
Section 125 deductions (health)   D125
Federal taxable income            T = G − D401 − D125 − standard deduction
Federal income tax                = progressive brackets applied to T
Social Security (6.2%)            = 0.062 × min(G − D125, $184,500)
Medicare (1.45%)                  = 0.0145 × (G − D125)
Additional Medicare (0.9%)        = 0.009 × wages above $200,000 (withholding rule)
Net pay per period                = (G − D401 − D125 − taxes) ÷ periods per year
Traditional 401(k) contributions lower federal income tax but not Social Security and Medicare. Most Section 125 health premiums lower both.

Worked examples
Example 1: $75,000 salary, single, 5% to a 401(k), paid bi-weekly.
401(k): 5% × 75,000 = $3,750
Taxable income: 75,000 − 3,750 − 16,100 = $55,150
Federal income tax: 10% × 12,400 + 12% × 38,000 + 22% × 4,750 = 1,240 + 4,560 + 1,045 = $6,845
Social Security: 6.2% × 75,000 = $4,650; Medicare: 1.45% × 75,000 = $1,087.50; FICA total $5,737.50
Annual net: 75,000 − 3,750 − 6,845 − 5,737.50 = $58,667.50
Per paycheck (26): gross $2,884.62 − federal tax $263.27 − FICA $220.67 − 401(k) $144.23 ≈ $2,256.44. Per-period rounding can shift this by a cent.
Example 2: $210,000 salary, single, no pre-tax deductions.
Taxable income: 210,000 − 16,100 = $193,900 → federal tax $39,134 (top bracket reached: 24%)
Social Security stops at the wage base: 6.2% × 184,500 = $11,439
Medicare: 1.45% × 210,000 = $3,045
Additional Medicare: 0.9% × (210,000 − 200,000) = $90
Annual net $156,292; bi-weekly about $6,011.23
Once your wages for the year pass $184,500, the 6.2% Social Security deduction stops. This usually happens late in the year, so your last paychecks are larger.
Understanding your results
Net pay per period: an annualised estimate divided evenly. Your employer withholds using IRS Publication 15-T and your W-4, so individual paychecks can differ by a few dollars. Paychecks also change once you pass the Social Security wage base.
Marginal rate: the federal rate on your next dollar. In Example 1 it is 22% plus 7.65% FICA.
Withholding is not the final tax. Your final 2026 tax is settled on your return, filed in 2027. That return can include credits (such as the child tax credit) and deductions (such as the new temporary deductions for qualified tips and overtime) that payroll may not reflect.
Country notes
State and local tax: most states tax wages. A few do not, including Texas and Florida. State calculations are on our roadmap; each state's rules will be checked against that state's revenue department before launch.
Canada and UK: see the Canadian take-home pay calculator (CPP, EI, federal and provincial tax) and the UK take-home pay calculator (PAYE, National Insurance, student loans).
Tips and common mistakes
Comparing gross offers without benefits. Health premiums and 401(k) matches change take-home pay and total value.
Forgetting state and local taxes. They vary widely by location.
Ignoring pre-tax deductions. 401(k), HSA and Section 125 deductions lower taxable pay.
Not updating your W-4 after a life change.
Frequently asked questions
Why is my paycheck different from this estimate?
Employers use Publication 15-T withholding tables and your W-4 settings, including dependents, other income and extra withholding. Your paycheck may also include deductions this page doesn't know about, such as state tax, insurance or union dues.
Do 401(k) contributions reduce Social Security tax?
No. Traditional 401(k) deferrals reduce federal income tax withholding, but they are still subject to Social Security and Medicare. Roth 401(k) deferrals reduce neither.
What is the 2026 standard deduction?
$16,100 for single filers and married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for heads of household. Additional amounts apply if you are 65 or older or blind.
When does the Additional Medicare Tax apply?
Employers must withhold an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000 in a calendar year, regardless of filing status. The tax you actually owe is based on combined wages and filing status ($250,000 for married filing jointly) and is reconciled on your return.
Are tips and overtime tax-free in 2026?
Not entirely. Federal law created temporary deductions for 2025–2028: up to $25,000 for qualified tips, and up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint) for the overtime premium, with income limits. They are claimed on your return, and these amounts still have Social Security and Medicare taken out.
Does this include state income tax?
Not yet. It's planned for each state, with values checked against each state's revenue agency.
How do I convert an hourly wage?
Hourly rate × hours per week × 52 gives an annual figure. At $25/hour and 40 hours, that's $52,000 a year.
