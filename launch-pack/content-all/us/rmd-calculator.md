H1: RMD Calculator
Once you reach a certain age, the IRS requires you to take a minimum amount out of most tax-deferred retirement accounts each year: a required minimum distribution (RMD). This calculator works out the RMD for a traditional IRA (and similar accounts) from your age and the account balance on December 31 of the previous year, using the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.
How to use this calculator
1. Your age: your age on your birthday in the distribution year (not today's age if your birthday hasn't arrived).
2. Prior Dec. 31 account balance: the account's fair market value at the end of last year. Your custodian reports it on Form 5498 or the year-end statement.
Results: distribution period (the IRS life-expectancy factor) and the required minimum distribution for the year.
The formula
RMD = Prior December 31 balance ÷ Distribution period
The distribution period comes from Table III (Uniform Lifetime Table) in IRS Publication 590-B, which applies to most account owners. It shrinks each year as you age, so the share of the account you must withdraw rises. Selected factors [VERIFY each against Pub 590-B Appendix B before publishing]:

When RMDs start
Under the SECURE 2.0 Act:
Age 73 if you were born from 1951 to 1959.
Age 75 if you were born in 1960 or later.
Your first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the year after you reach RMD age, but then you must also take the second RMD by December 31 of that year, which can push you into a higher bracket. Later RMDs are due by December 31 each year.
Which accounts have RMDs
Traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs: yes. You can total the RMDs for all your IRAs and take the amount from any one or more of them.
401(k), 403(b) and 457(b) plans: yes, calculated separately for each plan (403(b)s can be aggregated with each other). If you still work for the employer and don't own 5% or more, you can usually delay that plan's RMD until you retire.
Roth IRAs: no RMDs for the original owner. Since 2024, designated Roth accounts in workplace plans also have no lifetime RMDs.
Inherited accounts follow different rules, including the 10-year rule for many non-spouse beneficiaries.
Understanding your results
The RMD is a minimum. You can withdraw more, but extra withdrawals don't count toward next year's RMD.
It is taxable as ordinary income (except any after-tax basis). Plan your withholding; custodians can withhold federal tax from the distribution. The income tax calculator helps estimate the tax.
Missing an RMD triggers an excise tax of 25% of the shortfall, reduced to 10% if corrected within the correction window (SECURE 2.0).
Ways to manage RMDs
Qualified charitable distributions (QCDs): from age 70½, you can give directly from an IRA to charity (up to an annual limit, indexed [VERIFY 2026 QCD limit]). QCDs count toward the RMD and aren't taxable income.
Roth conversions before RMD age reduce future RMDs, at the cost of tax on the conversion now.
Timing: taking the RMD early in the year is simple; late in the year leaves more time invested. Either is allowed.
Country notes
RMDs are a US rule. Canada has a similar requirement for RRIFs, with prescribed minimum withdrawal percentages starting the year after the RRIF is opened; RRSPs must be converted by the end of the year you turn 71. The UK has no compulsory withdrawals from pensions; you can leave a pension invested for as long as you like, although from April 2027 unused pension funds are due to fall within the estate for Inheritance Tax [VERIFY].
Tips and common mistakes
Using the wrong balance. It's the prior December 31 value, not today's.
Forgetting accounts. Each workplace plan has its own RMD.
Delaying the first RMD without planning for two taxable distributions in one year.
Ignoring the spouse exception. A much younger spouse beneficiary means a lower RMD.
Frequently asked questions
At what age do I have to take RMDs?
73 if born 1951–1959; 75 if born in 1960 or later.
How is the RMD calculated?
Prior year-end balance divided by your factor from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.
Do Roth IRAs have RMDs?
Not for the original owner.
What is the penalty for missing an RMD?
An excise tax of 25% of the amount not taken, which can drop to 10% if corrected promptly.
Can I take more than the RMD?
Yes. The RMD is only the minimum.
Can I take my IRA RMD from one account?
Yes, for IRAs: calculate each IRA's RMD, add them up and withdraw the total from any of your IRAs. Workplace plans are different.
