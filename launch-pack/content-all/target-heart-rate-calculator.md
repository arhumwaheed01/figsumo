H1: Target Heart Rate Calculator
Your target heart rate zone is the range of beats per minute (bpm) that gives a moderate to vigorous workout. Exercising in this zone improves heart and lung fitness without pushing to your limit. This calculator estimates your maximum heart rate from your age and gives a target zone of 50% to 85%, using either a simple percentage of maximum or the Karvonen method if you enter your resting heart rate.
How to use this calculator
1. Age (1–120).
2. Resting heart rate (optional): your pulse when fully at rest, ideally first thing in the morning before getting up (30–120 bpm).
Results: estimated maximum heart rate, target zone low and high, and the method used.
How it works
Maximum heart rate:
Max HR = 220 − age
Without resting heart rate (percent of max):
Low = 0.50 × Max HR;  High = 0.85 × Max HR
With resting heart rate (Karvonen, heart rate reserve):
HRR  = Max HR − Resting HR
Low  = Resting HR + 0.50 × HRR
High = Resting HR + 0.85 × HRR
The Karvonen method personalises the zone: two people of the same age with different resting heart rates get different targets.
Worked examples
Example 1: age 40, no resting heart rate
Max HR = 220 − 40 = 180 bpm
Target zone: 90–153 bpm
Moderate intensity (50–70%): 90–126 bpm; vigorous (70–85%): 126–153 bpm
Example 2: age 40, resting heart rate 60
HRR = 180 − 60 = 120
Low = 60 + 0.50 × 120 = 120 bpm
High = 60 + 0.85 × 120 = 162 bpm
Example 3: age 65, resting heart rate 72
Max HR 155; Karvonen zone 114–143 bpm (percent-of-max gives 78–132)
Example 4: comparing 220 − age with Tanaka at 60
220 − 60 = 160 bpm; Tanaka: 208 − 0.7 × 60 = 166 bpm
50–85% zone: 80–136 bpm with 220 − age, or 83–141 bpm with Tanaka
The difference grows with age, which is why the choice of formula matters more for older exercisers. The calculator currently uses 220 − age.

Understanding your results
220 − age is a rough estimate. Real maximum heart rates vary by 10 to 12 bpm or more either way. The Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age) is an alternative that tends to be more accurate for older adults; for a 40-year-old it gives 180 bpm.
Moderate vs vigorous. The American Heart Association describes moderate intensity as about 50–70% of maximum and vigorous as about 70–85%. The CDC uses slightly higher bands (64–76% moderate, 77–93% vigorous), so the boundary between "moderate" and "vigorous" depends on the source.
Talk test. At moderate intensity you can talk but not sing; at vigorous intensity you can say only a few words without pausing for breath.
Medicines matter. Beta blockers and some other drugs lower heart rate, so zones based on age won't apply. Ask your doctor.

Notes for US, Canada and UK users
US: the American Heart Association publishes target heart rate tables using 220 − age and 50–85%.
Canada: Heart & Stroke and the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines describe intensity mainly with the talk test and perceived effort rather than heart rate numbers.
UK: the NHS describes a normal adult resting heart rate as 60 to 100 bpm and recommends the talk test for judging moderate and vigorous activity.
Measuring your heart rate
Find your pulse on the inside of your wrist or the side of your neck.
Count beats for 15 seconds and multiply by 4 (or 30 seconds × 2).
Chest-strap monitors are more accurate than wrist-based optical sensors during intense exercise.
How much exercise?
US, Canadian and UK guidelines all recommend adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days.
Tips and common mistakes
Measuring resting heart rate after coffee or exercise. Measure on waking.
Chasing the top of the zone every session. Most training should be at easy to moderate intensity.
Ignoring warning signs. Stop and seek help if you have chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or an irregular heartbeat.
Frequently asked questions
What is a good target heart rate?
For most adults, 50% to 85% of maximum heart rate.
How do I calculate my maximum heart rate?
The common estimate is 220 minus your age.
What is a normal resting heart rate?
For adults, 60 to 100 bpm; fit people often have lower rates.
What is the Karvonen formula?
It uses your heart rate reserve (max minus resting) to set the target zone.
Is it dangerous to exceed my max heart rate?
The estimate isn't a hard limit, but if you feel unwell, slow down. Talk to a doctor before vigorous exercise if you have heart disease.
Which zone burns the most fat?
Lower intensities use a higher proportion of fat, but higher intensities burn more total calories.
