H1: Ovulation Calculator
Ovulation is when an ovary releases an egg. It usually happens about 14 days before your next period, and pregnancy is most likely from sex in the few days before and on the day of ovulation. This calculator estimates your ovulation date and fertile window from the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length.
How to use this calculator
1. First day of last period (LMP): the first day of full bleeding, not spotting.
2. Average cycle length: the number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next (21–45; default 28). Average your last three to six cycles if they vary.
Results:
Estimated ovulation date
Fertile window: five days before ovulation to one day after
Next period (estimate)
How it works
Ovulation   = LMP + (cycle length − 14) days
Fertile window = ovulation − 5 days to ovulation + 1 day
Next period = LMP + cycle length
The method assumes the luteal phase (the time from ovulation to your next period) is 14 days. That is an average; it typically ranges from about 11 to 17 days. Sperm can survive up to five days in the reproductive tract, and the egg lives about 12 to 24 hours, which is why the window starts five days before ovulation.

Understanding your results
The most fertile days are the two or three days before ovulation and the day itself.
Dates are estimates. Ovulation can shift from cycle to cycle because of stress, illness, travel, breastfeeding or hormonal conditions.
Irregular cycles: if your cycle varies by more than about seven days, a calendar estimate is unreliable. Ovulation predictor kits or temperature tracking work better.
Not contraception. Calendar-based estimates are not a reliable way to avoid pregnancy.
Confirming ovulation
Ovulation (LH) tests: detect the hormone surge 24 to 36 hours before ovulation.
Cervical mucus: becomes clear, slippery and stretchy near ovulation.
Basal body temperature: rises by about 0.2–0.5 °C after ovulation, so it confirms ovulation has happened rather than predicting it.
Using ovulation tests with this calculator
Many test makers suggest starting LH tests about 17 days before your next period is due, which gives a couple of days' margin before the estimated ovulation date. For a 28-day cycle, that's 11 days after the first day of your last period (12 September 2026 in Example 1); for a 32-day cycle, 15 days after it. Test at the same time each day and keep testing until you see a positive result. If you never see a surge over several cycles, mention it to a doctor.
Things that can change ovulation timing
Coming off hormonal contraception: cycles can take a few months to settle.
Breastfeeding: often delays the return of ovulation after birth, but not reliably enough to use as contraception.
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and thyroid conditions: can cause irregular or absent ovulation.
Low body weight or very intensive training: can suppress ovulation.
Perimenopause: cycles become less predictable in the years before periods stop.
If your cycles are regularly shorter than 21 days or longer than 35, a calendar estimate won't be reliable, and it's worth a check-up.
Country notes
US: ACOG and the Office on Women's Health provide guidance on fertility awareness.
UK: the NHS advises that couples having regular unprotected sex every two to three days don't need to time sex to ovulation.
Canada: the Society of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists of Canada (SOGC) gives similar advice.
In the US, ACOG advises seeing a doctor if you haven't conceived after 12 months of trying, or after 6 months if you're 35 or over. The NHS advises seeing a GP after a year of trying, or sooner if you're 36 or over or know of a fertility problem.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting from the last day of your period. Use the first day.
Assuming everyone ovulates on day 14. That's only true for a 28-day cycle with a 14-day luteal phase.
Using one unusual cycle. Average several cycles.
Frequently asked questions
When am I most fertile?
In the two to three days before ovulation and on ovulation day.
Can I get pregnant outside the fertile window?
It's less likely but possible, because ovulation dates vary.
How long does ovulation last?
The egg survives 12 to 24 hours after release.
Can I ovulate twice in a cycle?
More than one egg can be released within 24 hours (leading to non-identical twins), but not days apart in a typical cycle.
Does the pill affect this calculator?
Hormonal contraception usually stops ovulation; the calculator isn't meaningful while you're using it.
What if my cycles are irregular?
Use ovulation tests and talk to your doctor, especially if cycles are shorter than 21 or longer than 35 days.
