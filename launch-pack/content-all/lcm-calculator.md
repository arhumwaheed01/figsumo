H1: LCM Calculator
The least common multiple (LCM) of two integers is the smallest positive number that both divide into exactly. It's the number you need for adding fractions with different denominators and for working out when repeating events line up. Enter two integers to get their LCM.
How to use this calculator
1. First integer.
2. Second integer.
Result: LCM. Negative inputs are treated by absolute value. If either number is 0, the result is 0.
How it works
The calculator uses the relationship with the greatest common factor:
LCM(a, b) = |a × b| ÷ GCF(a, b)
For 12 and 18: GCF = 6, so LCM = 216 ÷ 6 = 36. See the GCF calculator for how the GCF is found.
Other methods
Listing multiples
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, …
Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54, …
First common one: 36
Prime factorisation
Take every prime that appears, each to its highest power:
12 = 2² × 3
18 = 2 × 3²
LCM = 2² × 3² = 36
For three numbers, 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, so LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120. (This calculator takes two numbers at a time: find LCM(8, 12) = 24, then LCM(24, 15) = 120.)
Worked examples
Example 1: adding fractions
5/12 + 7/18: the least common denominator is LCM(12, 18) = 36.
5/12 = 15/36; 7/18 = 14/36
Sum = 29/36
Example 2: schedules
One bus comes every 12 minutes and another every 20 minutes. If they leave together, they next coincide after LCM(12, 20) = 60 minutes.
Example 3: 21 and 6
GCF = 3, so LCM = 126 ÷ 3 = 42.
Example 4: coprime numbers
For 8 and 15 (GCF 1), the LCM is simply 8 × 15 = 120.
Understanding your results
The LCM is at least as large as the larger number.
If one number divides the other, the LCM is the larger number (LCM(6, 24) = 24).
For coprime numbers, LCM = their product.
GCF × LCM = a × b for two positive integers.
Uses of the LCM
Common denominators in fraction arithmetic; see the fraction calculator.
Gear and wheel rotations: two gears with 12 and 18 teeth return to the same alignment after 36 teeth have passed.
Repeating events: shift rotas, traffic lights, planetary alignments in simple models.
Packaging: hot dogs in packs of 10 and buns in packs of 8 match up at LCM(10, 8) = 40, so 4 packs of hot dogs and 5 of buns.
Practice problems
1. LCM(4, 6)? (Answer: 12.)
2. LCM(9, 12)? (Answer: 36.)
Tips and common mistakes
Multiplying the numbers when they share a factor; 12 × 18 = 216 is a common multiple but not the least.
Using the lowest powers of primes (that's the GCF).
Confusing LCM and GCF.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
"Least common multiple" (LCM) is the usual US and Canadian term. UK schools say "lowest common multiple", with the same abbreviation, and pair it with HCF (highest common factor) rather than GCF. Both terms appear in Key Stage 3 and GCSE papers, often together in one question: for 12 and 18, the HCF is 6 and the LCM is 36, and HCF × LCM = 12 × 18 = 216.
That product rule (only for two numbers) is a quick check: LCM(a, b) = a × b ÷ GCF(a, b). For 84 and 126, the GCF is 42, so the LCM is 84 × 126 ÷ 42 = 252.
Frequently asked questions
What is the LCM of 12 and 18?
36.
How do I find the LCM of three numbers?
Find the LCM of two, then of that result and the third.
What's the difference between LCM and LCD?
The least common denominator is the LCM of the denominators.
What is the LCM of two prime numbers?
Their product.
Can the LCM be smaller than the numbers?
No; it's at least the larger number.
What is the LCM if one number is 0?
The calculator returns 0.
