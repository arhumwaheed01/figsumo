H1: Lease Calculator
This lease calculator estimates the regular monthly payment on a lease of a vehicle, machine, IT equipment or other asset. You enter the asset's value at the start, its expected residual (buyout) value at the end, the financing rate and the term. The payment covers the value the asset loses while you use it, plus a finance charge on the money tied up in it.
How to use this calculator
1. Asset value: the price the lease is based on (the capitalized cost), after any discount or upfront payment that reduces it.
2. Residual / buyout: the value the lessor expects the asset to have at the end of the term, or the agreed buyout price.
3. Financing rate (APR): the annual interest rate built into the lease. For car leases quoted as a money factor, multiply by 2,400 to get an approximate APR.
4. Lease term (months): the length of the lease.
Results: estimated monthly lease payment and total lease payments.
The formula
Depreciation charge = (Asset value − Residual) ÷ Term
Finance charge      = (APR ÷ 12) × (Asset value + Residual) ÷ 2
Payment             = Depreciation charge + Finance charge
The depreciation charge spreads the lost value evenly over the term.
The finance charge applies the monthly rate to the average amount tied up in the asset, (start value + end value) ÷ 2.
This is the same as the car-lease "money factor" method, because APR ÷ 2,400 × (value + residual) = (APR ÷ 12) × (value + residual) ÷ 2. A 6% APR is a money factor of 0.0025.
Payments are treated as monthly, with no tax added.
Worked examples
Example 1: $40,000 asset, $15,000 residual, 6% APR, 48 months
Depreciation = (40,000 − 15,000) ÷ 48 = $520.83
Finance = 0.005 × (40,000 + 15,000) ÷ 2 = $137.50
Payment = $658.33 a month
Total lease payments = $31,600.00, plus the $15,000 buyout if you choose to keep the asset
Example 2: $120,000 of equipment, 10% residual, 7.5%, 60 months
Residual = $12,000
Depreciation = 108,000 ÷ 60 = $1,800.00; finance = $412.50
Payment = $2,212.50; total $132,750.00
Equipment lessors often price with an exact annuity formula instead of the average-balance shortcut. With payments at the end of each month, the exact payment would be $2,239.10; with payments in advance (at the start of each month, which is common), $2,225.19. The shortcut is a good first estimate but can differ from a lessor's quote by a few percent on long terms.
Understanding your results
Monthly payment excludes sales tax, VAT, insurance, maintenance and fees.
Total lease payments is the cost of using the asset for the term. Add the buyout (residual) if you plan to keep it; compare that total with buying outright or financing with a loan.
Higher residual = lower payment, but a higher buyout price if you keep the asset.
Lease or buy?
Leasing keeps cash free, can match costs to the asset's useful life, and may include maintenance. Buying (with cash or a loan) usually costs less over the asset's full life if you keep it a long time. To compare:
1. Add up total lease payments plus any end-of-lease costs.
2. Compare with the loan payments on the same asset (use the loan calculator) minus its expected resale value at the end of the same period.
3. Consider tax treatment, which differs by country and lease type.
Types of leases
Operating lease (or true lease): the lessor keeps the risks of ownership; you return the asset at the end.
Finance (capital) lease: most of the risks and rewards pass to you; it works much like a loan.
Lease with a $1 buyout or bargain purchase option: effectively a purchase financed over time.
Under current accounting standards (ASC 842 in the US, IFRS 16 internationally), most leases longer than 12 months appear on the lessee's balance sheet as a right-of-use asset and a lease liability.
Country notes
United States: for tax, a true lease is usually deductible as a rental expense, while a lease that is in substance a purchase is treated like a financed asset (depreciation plus interest). The IRS looks at the substance of the agreement, not its name.
Canada: lease payments on business assets are generally deductible; for passenger vehicles the CRA caps deductible lease costs [VERIFY 2026 monthly limit].
United Kingdom: business contract hire and finance leases have different VAT and tax treatment. VAT is usually charged on each rental; businesses can often reclaim it, with restrictions on cars.
Tips and common mistakes
Using list price as the asset value. Negotiate the price first; the lease is built on it.
Ignoring end-of-term charges. Return conditions, excess use and wear can add costs.
Comparing rate-free quotes. Ask for the implied interest rate or money factor.
Forgetting tax. Add sales tax or VAT to the payment where it applies.
Frequently asked questions
How is a lease payment calculated?
Depreciation over the term plus a finance charge on the average amount of value tied up in the asset.
What is a residual value?
The asset's expected value (or agreed buyout price) at the end of the lease.
What is the difference between APR and money factor?
They express the same thing in different forms: APR ≈ money factor × 2,400.
Can I use this for a car lease?
Yes. For car-specific inputs like down payment and a direct money factor, the auto lease calculator is more convenient.
Are lease payments paid in advance?
Many leases require the first payment at signing (in advance). That slightly lowers each payment compared with payments in arrears at the same rate.
Is leasing cheaper than buying?
Not usually over the full life of an asset, but it can be better for cash flow, flexibility or assets that become obsolete quickly.
