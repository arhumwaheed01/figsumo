H1: Debt Payoff Calculator
This calculator tells you how many months it will take to clear a debt with a fixed monthly payment, and how much interest you will pay along the way. It works for credit cards, personal loans, overdrafts, medical bills or any balance that charges interest. Change the payment to see how much time and money a larger payment saves.
How to use this calculator
1. Debt balance: what you owe today. Use the payoff figure from your lender if it differs from the statement balance.
2. APR: the annual interest rate on the debt, from your statement or loan agreement.
3. Monthly payment: the fixed amount you will pay every month. It must be more than one month's interest, or the debt will never shrink.
Results: months to payoff and total interest. The calculator assumes no new borrowing on the debt.
The formula
The calculator steps through each month:
Interest = Balance × APR ÷ 12
Balance = Balance + Interest − Payment
It counts months until the balance reaches zero (the last payment is smaller). There's also a direct formula for the number of payments:
n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ PMT) ÷ ln(1 + r)
B: balance; r: APR ÷ 12; PMT: monthly payment
If PMT ≤ B × r, the logarithm is undefined: the payment never covers the interest.
Worked examples
Example 1: $8,000 at 12% with $250 a month
First month's interest: 8,000 × 0.01 = $80, so $170 reduces the balance
Months to payoff: 39
Total interest: $1,689.92
Raising the payment to $350 clears it in 27 months with $1,128.36 of interest, a saving of $561.56.
To be debt-free in 24 months instead, you would need to pay about $376.59 a month (the standard loan payment formula).
Example 2: $3,200 on a card at 24.99% with $120 a month
First month's interest: $66.64
Months to payoff: 40; total interest: $1,518.67
A high APR means a large share of each early payment goes to interest. This is why high-rate debts are usually the ones to clear first.
Understanding your results
Months to payoff assumes the rate stays the same and you make every payment on time.
Total interest is the extra you pay on top of the balance. Compare it across payment amounts to see the value of paying more.
"Payment must exceed monthly interest" means your payment doesn't even cover interest. Increase it, or look at a lower rate.
Paying off several debts: avalanche vs snowball
The calculator handles one debt at a time. With several, keep making the minimum on all of them and put every spare dollar towards one target:
Avalanche: target the highest APR first. This minimizes total interest.
Snowball: target the smallest balance first. You clear accounts sooner, which many people find motivating.
When the target is paid off, roll its whole payment into the next one. To plan this here, run each debt in turn: work out how long the first takes, then add its payment to the next debt from that month on.
Other ways to cut the cost
Consolidate high-rate debts into a single lower-rate loan; compare totals with the debt consolidation calculator.
Balance transfer to a 0% card, if the fee and promotional period make sense and you stop using the old card.
Negotiate. Lenders may lower rates or agree a payment plan, especially if you are struggling.
Find the money. The budget calculator shows where extra payments could come from.
Country notes
United States: non-profit credit counseling agencies (look for NFCC membership) can set up debt management plans. Debt settlement companies may charge high fees; the FTC warns about upfront-fee scams.
Canada: non-profit credit counselling is available, and formal options include a consumer proposal or bankruptcy through a Licensed Insolvency Trustee, regulated by the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy.
United Kingdom: free debt advice is available through MoneyHelper, StepChange, National Debtline and Citizens Advice. Formal options include debt management plans, IVAs and, in England and Wales, Debt Relief Orders, with the Breathing Space scheme pausing most enforcement for 60 days.
Tips and common mistakes
Stop adding to the debt. New charges undo the plan.
Automate the payment so it happens every month.
Keep a small emergency fund, or the next surprise bill goes on the card.
Check for prepayment penalties on loans before paying extra.
Don't ignore the smallest debts with fees. A debt that incurs a monthly fee can cost more than its APR suggests.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find out how long it will take to pay off my debt?
Enter the balance, APR and the amount you will pay each month. The calculator gives the number of months and total interest.
What if my payment is less than the interest?
The debt will grow. Increase the payment, ask the lender for a lower rate or seek free debt advice.
Is it better to pay off debt or save?
Paying off a high-interest debt is often a guaranteed "return" equal to its APR. Many people still keep a small emergency fund first so they don't need to borrow again.
Should I use the avalanche or snowball method?
Avalanche saves the most interest; snowball can be easier to stick with. The best method is the one you will follow.
Does this work for a mortgage or car loan?
Yes, for the time and interest of a fixed payment. For a full schedule, use the loan calculator.
Can I see a payment plan to finish by a date?
Not directly yet. Example 1 shows the formula; the loan calculator can find the payment for a given term.
