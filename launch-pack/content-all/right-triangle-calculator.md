H1: Right Triangle Calculator
A right triangle has one 90° angle. Its two shorter sides, the legs, meet at that angle; the longest side, the hypotenuse, is opposite it. Enter the two legs and this calculator finds the hypotenuse, the area and both acute angles in degrees.
How to use this calculator
1. Leg a: one side forming the right angle (default 5).
2. Leg b: the other side forming the right angle (default 12).
Both must be positive and in the same unit.
Results: hypotenuse (six decimal places), area (in square units), angle opposite a and angle opposite b (degrees, two decimals).
The formulas
c = √(a² + b²)                 (Pythagorean theorem)
Area = a × b ÷ 2
Angle A = arctan(a ÷ b)        (opposite leg a)
Angle B = 90° − A
The two acute angles always add up to 90°.
Trigonometric ratios (SOH CAH TOA)
For angle A:
sin A = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = a ÷ c
cos A = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse = b ÷ c
tan A = opposite ÷ adjacent = a ÷ b
Worked examples
Example 1: legs 5 and 12
c = √(25 + 144) = 13
Area = 5 × 12 ÷ 2 = 30
Angle opposite a = arctan(5/12) = 22.62°; opposite b = 67.38°
Perimeter (by hand) = 30
Example 2: legs 7 and 24
Hypotenuse 25, area 84, angles 16.26° and 73.74°
Example 3: a staircase
Each step rises 7 in and runs 11 in. The stair angle is arctan(7/11) = 32.47°, and the sloped distance per step is 13.04 in. See the stair calculator.
Example 4: an accessibility ramp
A 1 : 12 ramp (1 unit up for every 12 along) rises at 4.76°. The US ADA and many building codes use 1 : 12 as the maximum slope for ramps [VERIFY local code].

Understanding your results
Angle labels: "angle opposite a" is the angle across from leg a, at the end of leg b.
Units: sides and hypotenuse share your input unit; area is that unit squared.
Degrees vs radians: results are in degrees. Multiply by π/180 for radians.
For triangles without a right angle, use the triangle calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering the hypotenuse as a leg. If you know the hypotenuse and one leg, use the Pythagorean theorem calculator to find the other leg first.
Mixing up which angle is which. The smaller angle is always opposite the shorter leg.
Calculator in radian mode when checking by hand.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US students learn the trig ratios as SOH CAH TOA in geometry and Algebra 2; Canadian students meet them in Grade 10 math; UK students cover them for GCSE, where the "exact values" of sin, cos and tan for 30°, 45° and 60° must be known without a calculator. US building plans give lengths in feet and inches, so convert 7 ft 6 in to 7.5 ft before entering it.
Another check
Legs 9 and 40 give a hypotenuse of √(81 + 1,600) = 41 and a smaller angle of 12.68°. Whole-number sets like 9, 40, 41 are Pythagorean triples, useful for setting out a square corner on site.
Frequently asked questions
How do I find the hypotenuse?
c = √(a² + b²).
How do I find the angles of a right triangle?
Angle A = arctan(a/b); the other is 90° minus A.
What is the area of a right triangle?
Half the product of the legs.
What is SOH CAH TOA?
A memory aid for sine, cosine and tangent ratios.
What are the sides of a 30-60-90 triangle?
In the ratio 1 : √3 : 2.
Can I enter a leg and an angle?
Not yet. Use tan: the other leg = known leg × tan(angle opposite the other leg).
