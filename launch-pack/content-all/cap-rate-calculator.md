H1: Cap Rate Calculator
The capitalization rate (cap rate) links a rental property's net operating income to its value. Investors use it to compare properties quickly and to estimate what a property is worth at the yields typical for its market. This calculator takes the annual net operating income and a cap rate and returns the implied property value and the monthly NOI.
How to use this calculator
1. Net operating income (NOI): the property's annual income after operating expenses but before mortgage payments and income tax. See below for how to work it out.
2. Cap rate: the yield you want to value the property at, usually taken from recent sales of similar properties in the same area.
Results: estimated property value and monthly NOI.
The formula
Cap rate = NOI ÷ Property value
Property value = NOI ÷ Cap rate
NOI = Effective gross income − Operating expenses
Effective gross income: rent and other income (parking, laundry) minus an allowance for vacancy and bad debt.
Operating expenses: property tax, insurance, maintenance and repairs, property management, utilities paid by the owner, HOA or service charges, and reserves for replacements.
Not included in NOI: mortgage principal and interest, depreciation, income tax, and major capital improvements.
To find the cap rate of a property listed for sale, divide its NOI by the asking price. The calculator works in the other direction; for the reverse you can do the division by hand.
Worked examples
Example 1: valuing a property at a 6% cap rate
NOI $48,000 a year
Value = 48,000 ÷ 0.06 = $800,000.00
Monthly NOI = $4,000.00
Example 2: building NOI for a duplex and finding its cap rate
Gross scheduled rent: $36,000.00 a year
Less 5% vacancy: effective gross income $34,200.00
Operating expenses: property tax $6,400, insurance $3,200, repairs and maintenance $2,600, reserves $1,800 = $14,000.00
NOI = $20,200.00
Asking price $350,000 → cap rate = 5.77%
What the same NOI is worth at other market cap rates:
At 5.5%: $367,272.73
At 6.5%: $310,769.23
Small changes in the cap rate move the value a lot. Raising NOI matters too: an extra $1,200 a year of NOI adds about $20,000.00 of value at a 6% cap rate.
Understanding your results
Higher cap rate = lower price relative to income. It usually signals higher perceived risk, weaker growth prospects or a less desirable location.
Lower cap rate = higher price relative to income. Common for prime locations and stable tenants.
Cap rate ignores financing. Two buyers paying the same price have the same cap rate whether they pay cash or borrow 80%. For your return on the cash you put in, use cash-on-cash return in the rental property calculator or the ROI calculator.
Cap rate is a snapshot. It uses one year's NOI and doesn't account for rent growth, a future sale or major repairs. For multi-year analysis, use the IRR calculator.
Getting NOI right
Sellers' figures often leave out vacancy, management (even if you'll self-manage, price your time) and reserves for roofs, boilers, appliances and parking surfaces. Use actual tax bills and insurance quotes. Check whether the listed NOI is "pro forma" (projected) or actual trailing 12-month income. An inflated NOI produces an inflated value.
Cap rates in context
Market cap rates vary by property type (apartments, offices, retail, industrial), location and interest-rate conditions. When interest rates rise, buyers generally demand higher cap rates, so values fall if NOI doesn't rise. Compare the cap rate with your mortgage rate: if the cap rate is below the rate on your loan, borrowing reduces your cash return (negative leverage).
Country notes
United States: property tax is often the largest operating expense; use the actual local rate (see the property tax calculator). Commercial lenders use NOI to set the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR).
Canada: budget for municipal property tax and, in some cities, vacancy taxes on empty homes. Rental income is taxable; capital cost allowance is a tax item and stays out of NOI.
United Kingdom: investors often talk about "yield" (gross yield = annual rent ÷ price; net yield ≈ cap rate). Include service charges, ground rent, letting agent fees and licensing costs in expenses. Mortgage interest relief for individual landlords is restricted to a basic-rate tax credit, which affects after-tax returns but not NOI.
Tips and common mistakes
Including the mortgage payment in expenses. NOI is before debt service.
Using gross rent instead of NOI. That's gross yield, which overstates returns.
Comparing cap rates across different markets or property types. They aren't like-for-like.
Ignoring capital expenditure. A property that needs a new roof has a lower true yield.
Quick check
Before relying on a cap rate, recompute NOI from the rent roll and the expense statements yourself rather than using the seller's figure.
Frequently asked questions
What is a good cap rate?
It depends on the market and risk. A "good" cap rate is one that reflects the property's risk and beats your cost of capital; compare with recent sales nearby.
How do I calculate cap rate?
Divide annual NOI by the purchase price or current value.
Does cap rate include mortgage payments?
No. It's an unleveraged measure.
Why do cap rates change with interest rates?
Investors compare property yields with bond yields and borrowing costs. Higher rates usually push cap rates up.
What is the difference between cap rate and ROI?
Cap rate measures income relative to price. ROI measures your total gain relative to the money you invested, including financing and appreciation.
Is a higher cap rate always better?
Not necessarily. It means more income per dollar paid, but often comes with more risk.
