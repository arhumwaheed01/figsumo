H1: Password Generator
Strong, unique passwords are the simplest defence against account takeovers. This generator creates random passwords from the character types you choose, from 4 to 128 characters long, in your browser.
How to use the generator
1. Password length: 4 to 128 (default 16).
2. Uppercase (A–Z), Lowercase (a–z), Numbers (0–9), Symbols: include or exclude each set. Symbols are ! @ # $ % ^ & * ( ) - _ = + [ ] { }.
3. Press Calculate for a new password, then copy it into your password manager.
Result: the generated password.
How strong is it?
Strength is measured as entropy, the number of guesses an attacker would need, expressed in bits:
entropy (bits) = length × log₂(number of possible characters)
With all four sets there are 26 + 26 + 10 + 18 = 80 possible characters.

Each extra bit doubles the work. Even at a trillion guesses a second, exhausting half the space of a 16-character password would take on the order of 4.5e+10 years. Length matters more than symbols: a longer password with letters and digits beats a short one with every character type.
Why random beats clever
People pick predictable patterns: words with capital first letters, numbers at the end, "!" for a symbol, letter swaps like "@" for "a". Cracking tools try these first. A randomly generated password has no pattern to exploit.
Good password habits
Use a password manager to generate, store and fill passwords, so you only need to remember one strong master passphrase.
Never reuse passwords. A breach at one site then exposes the others.
Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA), ideally with an authenticator app or passkey rather than SMS.
Check for breaches with a service such as Have I Been Pwned.
Change a password when there's a breach or sign of compromise, not on a fixed schedule; that's the current advice of NIST in the US and the UK's NCSC.
What the authorities recommend
NIST SP 800-63B (US): at least 8 characters for user-chosen passwords (15 when it's the only authentication factor), allow at least 64, and block known-breached passwords. No forced composition rules or periodic resets [VERIFY current revision numbers].
NCSC (UK): "three random words" for memorable passwords, plus a password manager.
Canadian Centre for Cyber Security: passphrases of at least 4 words / 15 characters, or complex passwords of 12+ characters.
Privacy
Passwords are generated on your device and aren't sent to FigSumo. Don't share them over email or chat.
Tips and common mistakes
Making it memorable by editing it. Changing a random password back into something familiar weakens it.
Excluding all but one set to fit a site's rules; increase the length instead.
Sites with odd symbol rules: turn symbols off and add four or more characters.
Saving passwords in a plain document.
Frequently asked questions
How long should my password be?
At least 12–16 characters; longer for important accounts.
Are symbols necessary?
They help, but length helps more.
Is it safe to generate passwords online?
This generator runs in your browser; use a password manager to store the result.
What is password entropy?
A measure in bits of how hard a password is to guess.
What's a passphrase?
Several random words, easier to remember and type.
How often should I change passwords?
When there's a reason to suspect compromise.
