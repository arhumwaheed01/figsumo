H1: Tile Calculator
Ordering tile means working out how many pieces cover the floor or wall, plus extra for cuts, breakage and future repairs. This calculator takes the area to cover, the tile size and a waste allowance, and tells you how many tiles to buy.
How to use this calculator
1. Area to cover (ft²): from the square footage calculator if needed (default 120).
2. Tile size (in): the side of a square tile (default 12 for 12 × 12 in).
3. Waste (%): extra for cuts and breakage (default 10).
Results: tiles needed (rounded up) and tile area (ft² per tile).
The formulas
tile area (ft²) = (tile size in ÷ 12)²
area with waste = area × (1 + waste ÷ 100)
tiles = area with waste ÷ tile area, rounded up
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
12 in tiles are 1 ft² each. 120 × 1.10 = 132 ft² → 132 tiles. In boxes of 10, that's 14 boxes.
Example 2: smaller tiles
Same floor with 6 in tiles (0.25 ft² each) → 528 tiles.
Example 3: rectangular tiles
Not yet built in: divide by the tile's area. 12 × 24 in tiles cover 2 ft² each, so 132 ÷ 2 = 66 tiles.
Example 4: a metric bathroom (UK, Canada)
11 m² with 30 × 30 cm tiles (0.09 m² each) and 10% waste → 135 tiles.
Example 5: a backsplash
30 ft² of 3 × 6 in subway tiles (0.125 ft² each) with 15% waste → 276 tiles.
How much waste to allow
Layout

Straight lay, simple room
5–10%
Many cuts, small rooms, obstacles
10–15%
Diagonal or herringbone
15–20%
Large-format tiles (24 in+)
10–15% (breakage is costly)
Keep a few spare tiles after the job. Dye lots vary, and an exact match later can be impossible.
Grout lines
The calculation ignores grout joints, which slightly reduces the number of tiles needed (a ⅛ in joint on 12 in tiles is about 2%). The waste allowance usually covers the difference. Common joint widths are 1/16–⅛ in for rectified wall tiles and ⅛–¼ in for floors.
Other materials
Thinset or adhesive: coverage depends on trowel notch size; check the bag (often 40–95 ft² per 50 lb bag for floor tile [VERIFY product]).
Grout: depends on tile size, joint width and depth; manufacturers publish calculators.
Underlayment, membranes and trim: measure separately.
Understanding your results
Tiles are rounded up, so you never under-order a whole tile.
Order full boxes; most suppliers won't split them.
The chart compares the coverage area with the waste buffer.
Tips and common mistakes
Using nominal sizes: a "12 × 12" tile may actually be 11¾ in. For large areas, use the actual size.
Forgetting waste for patterns.
Buying from different dye lots. Check the lot number on every box.
Tiling under fixed cabinets. Usually unnecessary; subtract that area.
Quick check
12 m² plus 10% waste is 13.2 m² of tile.
Frequently asked questions
How many tiles do I need for 100 ft²?
With 12 × 12 in tiles and 10% waste, 110.
How much extra tile should I buy?
Usually 10%, more for diagonal or herringbone layouts.
How many 12 × 24 tiles are in a square foot?
Each covers 2 ft², so half a tile per ft².
Should I count grout lines?
Not usually; the waste allowance covers them.
Can I use metric sizes?
Not yet. Convert to inches and ft², or use the metric example above.
How many tiles are in a box?
It varies; check the box's coverage in ft² or m².
