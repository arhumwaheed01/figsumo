H1: Simple Interest Calculator
Simple interest is charged or earned only on the original principal, never on interest already added. It's used for many short-term loans, some auto loans, accrued interest on bonds, and certain savings products. This calculator gives the interest and the final amount from the principal, the annual rate and the time in years.
How to use this calculator
1. Principal: the amount borrowed or deposited.
2. Annual rate (%): the yearly interest rate.
3. Time (years): the length of time. Use decimals for part years (6 months = 0.5).
Results: simple interest and final amount (principal plus interest).
The formula
I = P × r × t
A = P + I = P × (1 + r × t)
P: principal
r: annual rate as a decimal (4.5% = 0.045)
t: time in years
Rearranged:
Rate: r = I ÷ (P × t)
Time: t = I ÷ (P × r)
Principal: P = I ÷ (r × t)
For days, use t = days ÷ 365 (some lenders use 360).
Worked examples
Example 1: $10,000 at 4.5% for 2 years
I = 10,000 × 0.045 × 2 = $900.00
A = $10,900.00
Example 2: a 90-day loan
$5,000 at 6% for 90 days, on a 365-day basis:
I = 5,000 × 0.06 × 90 ÷ 365 = $73.97
Example 3: finding the rate or the time
You paid $600 interest on $8,000 over 18 months. Rate = 600 ÷ (8,000 × 1.5) = 5.00%.
How long for $12,000 at 5% to earn $1,500? t = 1,500 ÷ (12,000 × 0.05) = 2.5 years.
Example 4: £2,000 at 7% for 9 months
t = 9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 years
I = 2,000 × 0.07 × 0.75 = £105.00
Example 5: does the day count matter?
$50,000 at 8% for 120 days:
365-day basis: 50,000 × 0.08 × 120 ÷ 365 = $1,315.07
360-day basis: 50,000 × 0.08 × 120 ÷ 360 = $1,333.33
The 360-day convention, common in US commercial lending, adds $18.26 here. Check the loan agreement.

Converting to a compound annual rate
$10,000 growing to $10,900 over 2 years is 4.5% simple, but only (10,900 ÷ 10,000)^(1/2) − 1 = 4.40% a year compounded. Use the compound figure to compare it with a savings account's AER or APY.

Where simple interest is used
Auto loans and some personal loans use simple interest computed daily on the outstanding balance. At 6.9% on a $25,000 balance, interest accrues at about $4.73 a day, so paying early in the cycle saves a little and paying late costs a little. Because the balance falls with each payment, the interest charged also falls.
Bond accrued interest: when you buy a bond between coupon dates, you pay the seller the interest accrued since the last coupon. For $1,000 of a 5% bond 73 days after a coupon, accrued interest is about $10.00 (actual/365 basis; conventions vary).
Short-term business credit and promissory notes.
Some certificates of deposit that pay interest out rather than reinvesting it.
Understanding your results
The final amount is principal plus interest for the full period, assuming no payments in between.
For loans with regular payments, total interest is less than P × r × t because the principal falls over time. Use the loan calculator for amortized loans.
Rates are annual. A monthly rate of 1% is 12% a year in simple terms.
Country notes
United States: the Truth in Lending Act requires lenders to disclose the APR and total finance charge, so you can compare simple-interest loans with others.
Canada: the Interest Act requires that when a rate is stated for a period shorter than a year, the equivalent annual rate must also be disclosed.
United Kingdom: consumer credit agreements must show the APR under the Consumer Credit Act. Some UK savings accounts pay a "gross" (AER) rate that already reflects compounding.
Short-term loans: a warning
Fees on payday loans are often quoted per $100 for two weeks, which looks like simple interest but is very expensive. The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's example is a $15 fee per $100 borrowed for two weeks, an APR of almost 400% (15% × 26 two-week periods = 390% before any compounding). Canada caps the cost of payday loans by regulation and the UK caps them at 0.8% a day, but both are still far costlier than mainstream credit [VERIFY current Canadian cap].
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing months and years. Convert months to years first (18 months = 1.5).
Using a percentage as a whole number. 4.5% is 0.045.
Assuming simple interest on a credit card. Cards compound daily or monthly.
Ignoring the day-count basis (360 vs 365) on short-term loans.
Quick check
Confirm whether your agreement uses a 360-day or a 365-day year.
Frequently asked questions
What is the simple interest formula?
I = P × r × t.
Is simple interest better than compound interest?
For borrowers, yes, at the same rate. For savers, compound interest pays more.
How do I calculate simple interest for months?
Divide the months by 12 to get years: I = P × r × (months ÷ 12).
Do car loans use simple interest?
Most US auto loans charge simple interest on the daily balance.
How do I calculate interest for days?
I = P × r × days ÷ 365 (or 360, depending on the lender).
