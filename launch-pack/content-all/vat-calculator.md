H1: VAT Calculator
Value-added tax (VAT) is a consumption tax charged at each stage of the supply chain and borne by the final consumer. It's used in the UK, across the EU and in more than 170 countries. This calculator adds VAT to a net price or extracts the VAT from a gross price that already includes it.
How to use this calculator
1. Amount: the price you have.
2. VAT rate (%): the rate that applies. The UK standard rate is 20%.
3. Amount is: choose Excluding VAT to add VAT to a net price, or Including VAT to find the net price and VAT inside a gross price.
Results:
Excluding VAT: VAT amount and total including VAT
Including VAT: net (ex-VAT) and VAT amount
The formulas
Adding VAT:
VAT = Net × Rate
Gross = Net × (1 + Rate)
Removing VAT:
Net = Gross ÷ (1 + Rate)
VAT = Gross − Net
At 20%, the VAT inside a gross price is 1/6 of the gross (16.6667%), not 20% of it. This is the most common VAT mistake.
Worked examples
Example 1: adding 20% VAT to £100
VAT = 100 × 0.20 = £20.00
Gross = £120.00
Example 2: extracting VAT from £150
Net = 150 ÷ 1.20 = £125.00
VAT = £25.00
Taking 20% of £150 would give £30.00, which is wrong.
Example 3: the reduced rate
Home energy in the UK is charged at 5%. On a £80 net bill, VAT is £4.00, total £84.00.
Example 4: a quarterly VAT return
A VAT-registered business sells £30,000 (net) of standard-rated services in a quarter and buys £8,000 (net) of standard-rated supplies:
Output VAT: 30,000 × 20% = £6,000.00
Input VAT: 8,000 × 20% = £1,600.00
VAT to pay HMRC = £4,400.00
Example 5: removing 5% VAT
A £63.00 energy bill including 5% VAT: net = 63 ÷ 1.05 = £60.00, VAT £3.00.

How VAT moves through the supply chain
A manufacturer sells goods to a retailer for £1,000 + VAT and charges £200.00 output VAT. The retailer sells them to consumers for £1,600 + VAT, charging £320.00. The retailer pays HMRC the difference between the VAT it charged and the VAT it paid: £120.00. Each business pays VAT only on the value it adds, and the consumer bears the full £320.00.
Understanding your results
Prices for consumers include VAT in the UK and EU. Business-to-business prices are often quoted ex-VAT ("+ VAT").
Rounding: HMRC allows VAT to be rounded to the nearest penny per line or invoice, so small differences are normal.
VAT vs sales tax: US sales tax is charged once at the final sale and is added at the till. See the sales tax calculator.
UK VAT rates and rules
Standard rate 20%: most goods and services.
Reduced rate 5%: for example home energy, children's car seats, some energy-saving installations.
Zero rate 0%: most food, children's clothes, books and newspapers. Zero-rated goods are still VAT-taxable (businesses can reclaim input VAT), unlike exempt items.
Exempt: for example most financial services, insurance, education and health services; no VAT is charged and input VAT generally can't be reclaimed.
Registration threshold: businesses must register when VAT-taxable turnover exceeds £90,000.00 in a rolling 12 months (from 1 April 2024) [VERIFY current threshold].
Flat Rate Scheme: small businesses can pay a fixed percentage of gross turnover instead of calculating input and output VAT; the percentage depends on the trade.
Since 1 January 2025, VAT applies to private school fees in the UK.
Other countries
EU: each member state sets its own rates, with a standard rate of at least 15%. Rates range up to 27% (Hungary) [VERIFY].
Canada: GST/HST is a value-added tax at 5% to 15% depending on the province; see the GST/HST calculator.
United States: no VAT; states and localities levy sales tax instead.

Tips and common mistakes
Taking 20% of a gross price to find the VAT. Divide by 1.2 first.
Quoting business prices ex-VAT to consumers. UK consumer prices must include VAT.
Confusing zero-rated with exempt. They affect reclaiming differently.
Forgetting to register on time once you pass the threshold.
Checking an invoice
A valid UK VAT invoice shows the supplier's VAT registration number, the date, a description of the goods or services, the net amount, the VAT rate and the VAT charged. Use the calculator to check that net plus VAT equals the gross total. If you're VAT-registered, you need a valid invoice to reclaim input VAT, so ask the supplier for a corrected invoice when the figures don't add up.
Frequently asked questions
How do I add 20% VAT?
Multiply the net price by 1.2.
How do I remove VAT from a price?
Divide the gross price by 1 plus the rate (1.2 for 20%), then subtract to get the VAT.
What is the UK VAT rate?
20% standard, 5% reduced, 0% zero rate.
Why isn't the VAT 20% of the total?
Because the total already includes the VAT. VAT is 20% of the net price, which is 1/6 of the gross.
When do I need to register for VAT?
When taxable turnover passes the registration threshold in any rolling 12-month period, or you expect it to in the next 30 days.
Can I reclaim VAT?
VAT-registered businesses can usually reclaim VAT on business purchases. Consumers can't, except in schemes such as tourist refunds abroad.
