H1: Carb Calculator
Carbohydrates are the body's main fuel, particularly for the brain and for hard exercise. How much you need depends on your total energy needs, how active you are and your goals. This calculator estimates a daily carbohydrate target either as a share of your daily calories or in grams per kilogram of body weight, the method sports dietitians use.
How to use this calculator
1. Method: choose % of calories from carbs or grams per kg of body weight.
2. For the percentage method: enter daily calories (from the TDEE calculator or calorie calculator) and the carbohydrate %. The default is 45%.
3. For the g/kg method: enter body weight and carbs (g per kg). The default is 3 g/kg.
Results: daily carbohydrates in grams and the matching calories or share of calories.
The formulas
% method:   Carbs (g) = Daily calories × Carb % ÷ 4
g/kg method: Carbs (g) = Body weight (kg) × g per kg
Calories from carbs = Carbs (g) × 4
Carbohydrate provides about 4 kcal per gram.
Guideline ranges
General adults: the US Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range (AMDR) is 45% to 65% of calories from carbohydrate. The RDA is 130 g a day, the average minimum the brain uses.
Added sugars: less than 10% of calories (US Dietary Guidelines; UK free sugars limit is 5% of energy).
Fibre: 14 g per 1,000 kcal in the US guidelines (about 25 to 34 g a day for adults); the UK recommends 30 g a day.
Athletes (sports nutrition position statements): about 3 to 5 g/kg for moderate exercise of about an hour a day, 5 to 7 g/kg for moderate-to-high intensity training of 1 to 3 hours, and 6 to 10 g/kg or more for very high volumes [VERIFY current ranges].
Worked examples
Example 1: 2,000 kcal a day
45%: 2,000 × 0.45 ÷ 4 = 225 g
55%: 275 g
65%: 325 g
Fibre target: about 28 g. Keep added sugars under about 50 g.
Example 2: endurance training, 65 kg
At 5 g/kg: 325 g of carbohydrate a day (1,300 kcal). If total intake is 2,600 kcal, that's 50% of calories, within the general range.
Example 3: imperial units
150 lb (68.0 kg) at 3 g/kg → 204 g a day.
Example 4: heavy training, 70 kg
At 7 g/kg: 70 × 7 = 490 g a day (1,960 kcal). On a 3,200 kcal intake, that's 61% of calories, toward the top of the general range, which is normal for endurance athletes in heavy training.

Understanding your results
Quality matters as much as quantity. Whole grains, legumes, fruit, vegetables and dairy provide fibre, vitamins and minerals; refined grains and sugary drinks don't.
Low-carbohydrate diets (often under 26% of calories, or under 130 g) can work for weight loss or blood glucose for some people. At 1,800 kcal, 20% of calories is about 90 g. Discuss very low-carb plans with a clinician, particularly if you take diabetes medicines.
Diabetes: carbohydrate counting is used to match insulin doses; targets should be set with your diabetes care team.
Timing: for training, carbohydrate before and after sessions supports performance and recovery.
Country notes
United States: AMDR 45–65%; Nutrition Facts labels list total carbohydrate, fibre, total sugars and added sugars.
Canada: Health Canada uses the same Dietary Reference Intakes; Canada's food guide emphasises whole grains and vegetables, and labels show % Daily Value for fibre and sugars.
United Kingdom: the NHS recommends starchy foods make up just over a third of what you eat and that carbohydrate provides about 50% of energy; front-of-pack traffic-light labels flag high sugar.
Tips and common mistakes
Using grams of food instead of grams of carbohydrate. 100 g of cooked rice contains about 28 to 30 g of carbohydrate.
Forgetting carbohydrate in drinks such as juice, smoothies and sports drinks.
Choosing a percentage that doesn't fit protein and fat needs. Check the full split with the macro calculator.
Not adjusting on rest days if you train hard.
Frequently asked questions
How many carbs should I eat a day?
For most adults, 45% to 65% of calories, which is about 225 to 325 g on 2,000 kcal.
What is the minimum carbohydrate intake?
The RDA is 130 g a day for adults, based on the brain's glucose use.
How many grams of carbs per kg for athletes?
Roughly 3 to 10 g/kg depending on training volume and intensity.
How many calories are in a gram of carbohydrate?
About 4 kcal.
Do fibre grams count as carbs?
Fibre is a carbohydrate on labels, but it provides fewer usable calories. Some people count "net carbs" (total minus fibre).
Is a low-carb diet healthy?
It can be for some people in the short term; long-term effects depend on what replaces the carbohydrate.
