H1: Pregnancy Week Calculator
"How far along am I?" is one of the first questions in pregnancy. Doctors and midwives count pregnancy from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), not from conception, so at conception you're already about two weeks pregnant. This calculator works out your gestational age today, your estimated due date and your trimester.
How to use this calculator
Enter the first day of your last menstrual period. The date must be today or earlier.

How it works
Gestational age (days) = today − LMP
Weeks = days ÷ 7 (rounded down); days = the remainder
Due date = LMP + 280 days (40 weeks)
This is Naegele's rule, which assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your cycles are longer or shorter, your dating scan may move the due date.
The calculator shows trimesters as weeks 1–12, 13–26 and 27 onwards. Sources differ slightly: ACOG counts the third trimester from week 28, and the NHS describes the first trimester as weeks 1 to 12.

Understanding your results
"20 weeks, 3 days" means you've completed 20 weeks and are 3 days into week 21.
Due dates are estimates. Only about 4% of babies arrive on the due date [VERIFY]; most are born between 37 and 42 weeks.
Your scan date takes priority. A first-trimester ultrasound measuring the baby (crown-rump length) is the most accurate way to date a pregnancy; if it differs from your LMP date by more than a set amount, your care team will use the scan date.
IVF pregnancies are dated from the embryo transfer date, not LMP.

How many months pregnant am I?
Care teams count in weeks, but people often think in months. Forty weeks is a little over nine calendar months, so there's no exact conversion. A rough guide: weeks 1–13 cover months 1–3, weeks 14–27 months 4–6, and weeks 28–40 months 7–9.
Country notes
US: ACOG defines early term as 37w0d–38w6d, full term 39w0d–40w6d, late term 41w0d–41w6d and post-term 42w0d onward.
UK: the NHS offers a dating scan at 10 to 14 weeks and an anomaly scan at 18 to 21 weeks.
Canada: the SOGC recommends first-trimester ultrasound dating where available.
Tips and common mistakes
Using the date of conception. Use the first day of your last period.
Ignoring irregular cycles. If your cycles are long, your due date may be later.
Changing dates after the scan. Once your care team sets your due date, stick to it.
What the trimesters cover
First trimester (to week 12 or 13): the main organs form. Nausea, tiredness and breast tenderness are common. Most early miscarriages happen in this period, which is one reason many people wait until after the dating scan to share news.
Second trimester (to week 26 or 27): nausea often eases. The anatomy scan checks the baby's development, and many people first feel movement between about 16 and 24 weeks; it's often earlier in a second pregnancy.
Third trimester (to birth): the baby gains most of its weight. Antenatal appointments become more frequent, and your care team will ask about the baby's movements. Contact your maternity unit or doctor straight away if movements slow down or change; don't wait for the next appointment.
Gestational age is the measure your care team uses on scan reports and maternity notes, so quoting weeks and days in that format, such as 19+2, avoids confusion. UK maternity notes typically write it that way, and US and Canadian charts often write 19w2d.
Frequently asked questions
How many weeks pregnant am I?
Count the days since the first day of your last period and divide by 7.
Why is pregnancy 40 weeks?
It's counted from your last period, about two weeks before conception; the baby's actual age is about 38 weeks at the due date.
How accurate is the due date?
It's an estimate; most babies arrive within two weeks either side.
When does the second trimester start?
Around week 13 or 14, depending on the source.
What if I don't know my LMP?
A dating ultrasound can estimate gestational age.
Is this calculator right for IVF?
Not exactly; IVF dating uses the transfer date and embryo age.
