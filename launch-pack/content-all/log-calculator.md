H1: Log Calculator
A logarithm answers the question "what power do I raise the base to, to get this number?" Because log₁₀ 100 = 2, ten squared is 100. This calculator finds the logarithm of any positive number to any valid base and shows the result to eight decimal places.
How to use this calculator
1. Base (b): a positive number other than 1. Use 10 for the common log, 2 for the binary log, and 2.718281828 (e) for the natural log (ln).
2. Value (x): a positive number.
Result: log_b(x).
The definition and formula
log_b(x) = y  means  bʸ = x
The calculator uses the change-of-base formula:
log_b(x) = ln(x) ÷ ln(b)
Log rules
log_b(xy)  = log_b x + log_b y
log_b(x/y) = log_b x − log_b y
log_b(xⁿ)  = n × log_b x
log_b(1)   = 0
log_b(b)   = 1
Worked examples
Example 1: log₁₀ 100
10² = 100, so log₁₀ 100 = 2.
Example 2: log₂ 1000
ln 1000 ÷ ln 2 = 9.96578428
So 2^9.96578428 ≈ 1,000; you'd need about 10 bits to count to 1,000.
Example 3: log₃ 50
3.56087680: 3 raised to that power is 50.
Example 4: natural log of 10
With base e: ln 10 = 2.30258509.
Example 5: how long to double money at 7%?
Solve 1.07ᵗ = 2: t = ln 2 ÷ ln 1.07 = 10.24 years. (The "rule of 72" gives 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10.3.)
Where logarithms appear
pH: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]. A hydrogen-ion concentration of 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L gives pH 4.49.
Sound (decibels): a sound 1,000 times more intense is 10 × log₁₀ 1000 = 30 dB louder.
Earthquakes: each whole step on magnitude scales means about 10 times more ground motion and roughly 32 times more energy.
Computing: binary search on n items takes about log₂ n steps.
Growth and decay: solving for time in compound interest or half-life problems; see the half-life calculator.
Understanding your results
log of a number between 0 and 1 is negative (log₁₀ 0.01 = −2).
Logs of zero or negative numbers aren't defined in real numbers, so the calculator rejects them.
Base 1 isn't allowed because 1 raised to any power is 1.
Eight decimals are shown; the last digit may be rounded.
Practice problems
1. log₁₀ 0.001? (Answer: -3.)
2. log₃ 81? (Answer: 4, because 3⁴ = 81.)
Tips and common mistakes
Writing log(x + y) = log x + log y. The product rule is for log(xy).
Assuming "log" means the same base everywhere. In maths and science "log" is often base 10, in computer science base 2, and in many programming languages log() is the natural log.
Entering e as 2.7. Use at least 2.718281828 for accuracy.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
On most calculators sold in all three countries, the log key means base 10 and ln means base e. Some university textbooks, especially in computer science and pure maths, write "log" for the natural log or for base 2, so check the convention in your course. UK A-level papers write logₐ x with the base as a subscript; US and Canadian high-school texts do the same, but often drop the 10 for common logs.

Frequently asked questions
What is a logarithm?
The exponent that gives a number from a base: log_b x = y when bʸ = x.
How do I calculate ln on this calculator?
Enter base 2.718281828 (e).
What is log base 2 of 8?
3.
Why can't I take the log of a negative number?
No real power of a positive base gives a negative number.
What is the change-of-base formula?
log_b x = ln x ÷ ln b (any base works in place of ln).
What is the inverse of a log?
Exponentiation: if y = log_b x, then x = bʸ. See the exponent calculator.
