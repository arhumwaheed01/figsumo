H1: Boat Loan Calculator
Use this calculator to estimate the monthly payment, total interest and payoff pattern of a loan to buy a boat, personal watercraft or other recreational vessel. Enter the amount you will borrow, the interest rate and the term in years and months. It returns the payment, the total of all payments and an amortization chart showing how the balance falls.
How to use this calculator
1. Loan amount: the amount you will finance. Work it out as purchase price + any taxes and fees you finance − down payment − trade-in value. Lenders commonly ask for a down payment on boat loans [VERIFY typical percentage with lenders; it varies].
2. Interest rate: the annual rate on the loan offer. If you have the APR from a disclosure, using it gives a cost closer to reality.
3. Loan term (years) and Loan term (months): the repayment period. Enter 10 years and 6 months as 10 and 6. Boat loans often run longer than car loans; terms of 10 to 20 years are common for larger boats.
Results: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest paid and the rate used, plus a chart of the balance, cumulative interest and cumulative payments over time.
The formula
The payment is a standard fixed-rate amortizing payment:
PMT = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
P: loan amount
r: monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12
n: total months = years × 12 + months
Each month, interest is charged on the remaining balance (balance × r) and the rest of the payment reduces the balance. Total interest = PMT × n − P.
Worked examples
Example 1: $30,000 over 5 years at 7.5%
r = 0.075 ÷ 12 = 0.00625; n = 60
PMT = $601.14 a month
Total of 60 payments: $36,068.31; total interest: $6,068.31
The first payment is $187.50 interest and $413.64 principal
Example 2: $85,000 boat, 15% down, 8.25% for 15 years vs 10 years
Loan amount: 85,000 × 0.85 = $72,250.00
15 years (180 months): $700.93 a month, total interest $53,916.75
10 years (120 months): $886.17 a month, total interest $34,089.83
Choosing the shorter term saves $19,826.93 of interest, at the cost of a higher monthly payment
Understanding your results
Monthly payment covers principal and interest only. It does not include insurance, mooring or storage, which can be significant.
Total interest paid is the cost of borrowing over the full term. Long terms cut the payment but raise this figure sharply.
The chart shows the balance falling slowly at first, because early payments are mostly interest, then faster later. Boats lose value, and on a long loan the balance can exceed the boat's resale value for years.
The real cost of owning a boat
The loan is only part of the budget. Typical ongoing costs include:
Insurance, often required by the lender.
Mooring, slip or dry storage, plus winter storage in cold climates.
Maintenance, including engine servicing, antifouling, and winterizing.
Fuel, which for a powerboat can be large; use the fuel cost calculator for an estimate.
Registration, licensing and safety equipment, which vary by state, province or country.
Trailer and towing vehicle for trailerable boats.
Add these to the payment in the budget calculator to see whether the full cost fits.
Paying cash vs financing
Financing keeps savings available for emergencies but costs interest. Paying cash avoids interest but ties up money that could earn a return elsewhere. A simple comparison: if your savings earn less after tax than the loan rate, paying cash (or a bigger down payment) usually costs less overall. Keep an emergency fund either way, because boats bring surprise repair bills.
Country notes
United States: a boat with a galley, sleeping berth and toilet can qualify as a second home, in which case loan interest may be deductible as qualified mortgage interest if you itemize (IRS Publication 936). Sales tax and registration are set by each state.
Canada: pleasure craft with engines of 10 hp (7.5 kW) or more must be licensed with Transport Canada, and operators need proof of competency. Sales tax (GST/HST and any PST) applies to the purchase.
United Kingdom: marine finance is usually an unsecured personal loan or a secured marine mortgage. Boats on many inland waterways need a licence (for example from the Canal & River Trust), and VAT status matters when buying a used boat.
Tips and common mistakes
Compare the APR, not just the rate, if there are lender fees.
Don't stretch the term to afford a bigger boat. You pay more interest on a depreciating asset.
Check prepayment terms. Some marine loans charge for early payoff.
Get a marine survey on used boats before committing to a loan; many lenders require one for larger vessels.
Budget for running costs, not just the payment.
Frequently asked questions
How long can a boat loan last?
It depends on the lender, the amount and the boat's age. Small loans may run a few years; loans on large new boats can run 15 to 20 years.
Is a boat loan secured?
Often, yes: the boat is the collateral. Secured loans usually carry lower rates than unsecured personal loans.
Can I enter the term in months only?
Yes. Leave years at 0 and enter the months, or combine both fields.
Does the payment include insurance or taxes?
No. The result covers principal and interest only.
Will paying extra each month help?
Yes, if the lender applies it to principal. It shortens the loan and cuts total interest. Use the amortization calculator to model extra payments.
Is boat loan interest tax-deductible?
In the US it can be, if the boat qualifies as a second home and you itemize; see IRS Publication 936. In Canada and the UK, interest on a personal boat loan is generally not deductible.
