H1: Tip Calculator
Work out the tip, the total and each person's share in one step. Enter the bill, choose a tip percentage and the number of people splitting it, and the calculator does the rest.
How to use this calculator
1. Bill amount: default $85.
2. Tip (%): default 18.
3. Split between (people): default 2.
Results: tip amount, total with tip and per person, with a chart of bill versus tip.
The formulas
tip = bill × tip % ÷ 100
total = bill + tip
per person = total ÷ people
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
$85 × 18% = $15.30 tip; total $100.30; $50.15 each.
Example 2: quick mental maths
10% of $85 is $8.50. Double it for 20% ($17.00); add half again for 15% ($12.75).
Example 3: tip before or after tax?
A $72.00 meal in New York City with 8.875% sales tax ($6.39): 20% of the pre-tax amount is $14.40; 20% of the total with tax is $15.68. Etiquette guides usually suggest tipping on the pre-tax amount, but many people tip on the total for simplicity. Enter whichever figure you prefer as the bill.
Example 4: UK service charge
A £64 bill with a 12.5% discretionary service charge adds £8.00, for £72.00. If the service charge is already on the bill, there's no need to tip again.
Example 5: Canada
A $120 restaurant bill at 15% tip is $18.00. In Ontario, the 13% HST on that bill is a separate $15.60; many card terminals suggest tips calculated on the after-tax total.
Tipping customs
United States
Tipping is expected in sit-down restaurants because many servers earn a lower tipped minimum wage. Typical ranges:

Canada
Similar to the US: 15–20% in restaurants is the norm, and card machines often default to 18–25% suggestions.
United Kingdom
Tipping is smaller and less expected. In restaurants, 10–12.5% is common if service isn't already included. Since October 2024, employers in England, Scotland and Wales must pass all tips to workers without deductions (the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023). Tipping in pubs for drinks at the bar isn't customary.
Customs are guidelines, not rules; tip according to the service and your budget.
Understanding your results
Rounding: per-person amounts are shown to the cent; round up if you're paying cash.
Uneven splits: if diners ordered very different amounts, calculate each person's share separately.
Service charges for large groups are often added automatically; check the bill.
Tips and common mistakes
Tipping twice when a service charge is already included.
Typing 0.18 for 18%.
Including gift-card or coupon discounts: many people tip on the original pre-discount amount.

Frequently asked questions
How much should I tip in a US restaurant?
15–20% for table service is typical.
Do I tip on tax?
Either is acceptable; etiquette guides usually suggest the pre-tax amount.
Is tipping expected in the UK?
Not always. 10–12.5% in restaurants if no service charge is added.
How do I split a bill evenly?
Divide the total with tip by the number of people.
What is 20% of $50?
$10.
Should I tip on takeout?
It's optional; some people leave 10%.
