H1: Wind Chill Calculator
Wind chill is how cold it feels on exposed skin when wind strips away the thin layer of warm air around your body. It's why a breezy 20 °F day can feel like single digits. Enter the air temperature and wind speed to get the wind chill temperature used by US and Canadian weather services.
How to use this calculator
1. Air temperature (°F): default 20.
2. Wind speed (mph): measured at about 33 ft (10 m), as in forecasts (default 15).
Results: wind chill in °F and a note on whether the formula applies. It's defined for temperatures at or below 50 °F and winds of at least 3 mph; outside that range the calculator returns the air temperature.
The formula
The 2001 wind chill index, adopted jointly by the US National Weather Service and the Meteorological Service of Canada:
WC = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75V^0.16 + 0.4275T·V^0.16
T: air temperature (°F)
V: wind speed (mph)
The metric version (used in Canada):
WC = 13.12 + 0.6215T − 11.37V^0.16 + 0.3965T·V^0.16
with T in °C and V in km/h.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
20 °F with a 15 mph wind → 6.2 °F.
Example 2: a bitter day
0 °F with 20 mph → -22.0 °F. Frostbite on exposed skin is possible within about 30 minutes.
Example 3: extreme cold
−10 °F with 30 mph → -39.4 °F: frostbite in 10 minutes or less.
Example 4: Canada (metric)
−20 °C with a 30 km/h wind → wind chill -32.6, reported by Environment Canada as a unitless index that equals what that temperature would feel like in calm conditions.
Example 5: a UK winter day
2 °C with a 20 mph wind (about 32 km/h) → about -4.1 °C. The Met Office's "feels like" temperature uses a related approach.

Staying safe in the cold
Dress in layers: a wicking base layer, an insulating middle layer and a windproof outer shell.
Cover exposed skin: hat, gloves or mittens (warmer than gloves) and a face covering.
Stay dry; wet clothing loses insulation quickly.
Watch for frostbite: numbness and white or greyish-yellow skin. Warm the area gradually; don't rub it.
Watch for hypothermia: shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness. It's a medical emergency (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK).
Understanding your results
Wind chill affects people and animals, not objects. A car or a pipe can't get colder than the air temperature, though wind cools them faster.
Sunshine can make it feel up to about 10–18 °F warmer; the formula assumes no sun.
Use forecast wind speeds at standard height; ground-level gusts vary.
Tips and common mistakes
Applying the formula above 50 °F.
Using gust speeds instead of sustained wind.
Entering km/h in the mph box; divide km/h by 1.609 first.
Frequently asked questions
What is wind chill?
How cold it feels on exposed skin, given temperature and wind.
When does wind chill apply?
At 50 °F (10 °C) or colder with winds of at least 3 mph (5 km/h).
Can wind chill freeze water?
No. Water freezes only when the air temperature is at or below 32 °F (0 °C).
What is a dangerous wind chill?
Below about −18 °F (−28 °C), frostbite can occur in 30 minutes.
Does Canada use the same formula?
Yes, in metric units.
Can I enter Celsius?
Not yet; convert first with the unit converter.
