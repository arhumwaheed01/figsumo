H1: Mass Calculator
Mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms. If you know what something is made of (its density) and how big it is (its volume), you can work out its mass without a scale: useful for shipping estimates, lifting plans, engineering and science homework.
How to use this calculator
1. Density (kg/m³): for the material (default 7,850, typical carbon steel).
2. Volume (m³): the object's volume in cubic metres (default 0.01).
Result: mass in kilograms.
Volume in litres? Divide by 1,000 (10 L = 0.01 m³). In cubic centimetres? Divide by 1,000,000.
The formula
m = ρ × V
m: mass (kg)
ρ: density (kg/m³)
V: volume (m³)
Worked examples
Example 1: the default steel block
7,850 × 0.01 = 78.5 kg (about 173.1 lb).
Example 2: a steel ball
A solid steel ball 10 cm across has radius 0.05 m: V = 4/3 × π × 0.05³ = 0.000524 m³; mass = 4.11 kg. See the volume calculator for other shapes.
Example 3: water in a tank
A tank 2 m × 1.5 m filled to 0.4 m holds 1.2 m³ of water. At 1,000 kg/m³, that's 1,200 kg, more than a tonne; check floor loading before installing.
Example 4: an aluminium sheet
A 2.4 m × 1.2 m sheet, 3 mm thick: volume 0.00864 m³ × 2,700 = 23.33 kg.

Weight = mass × gravitational acceleration (9.80665 m/s² standard on Earth). The 78.5 kg block weighs 769.8 N on Earth but only about 127.2 N on the Moon (g ≈ 1.62 m/s²), while its mass stays 78.5 kg.
In everyday language in the US, Canada and the UK, "weight" in pounds or kilograms usually means mass. Use the weight converter for kg ↔ lb.

Understanding your results
Hollow objects: subtract the hollow volume, or the mass will be far too high.
Loose materials (sand, gravel, grain) need bulk density, which includes air gaps.
Precision: results are only as good as the density figure; real materials vary by a few percent.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering litres as cubic metres (1,000 times too heavy).
Mixing g/cm³ and kg/m³. 7.85 g/cm³ = 7,850 kg/m³.
Ignoring hollow sections and holes.
Treating newtons and kilograms as the same.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
US engineers and fabricators often work in pounds and cubic inches. Steel's density is about 0.284 lb/in³ (7,850 kg/m³), so a 1 in × 2 in × 36 in steel bar weighs about 20.4 lb. Canadian and UK workshops use kg and m³ (or g/cm³), which avoids conversion. The UK spelling "aluminium" and the US "aluminum" are the same metal at about 2,700 kg/m³.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate mass from density and volume?
Multiply them: m = ρ × V.
What's the difference between mass and weight?
Mass is matter; weight is the force of gravity on it.
How heavy is a cubic metre of water?
About 1,000 kg.
How do I convert g/cm³ to kg/m³?
Multiply by 1,000.
Does mass change on the Moon?
No, only weight does.
What is the density of steel?
About 7,850 kg/m³ for common carbon steel.
