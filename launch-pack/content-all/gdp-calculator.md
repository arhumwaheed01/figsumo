H1: GDP Growth Calculator
Gross domestic product (GDP) is the total value of finished goods and services produced in an economy over a period. This calculator projects how GDP grows at a steady annual rate: enter a starting value, a growth rate and a number of years to see the future GDP, the total increase and the growth multiple. It works just as well for a business's revenue or a city's output.
How to use this calculator
1. Starting GDP ($): any currency amount (default 1,000,000).
2. Annual growth rate (%): real or nominal, as you choose (default 3). Negative rates model contraction.
3. Years: default 5.
Results: future GDP, total growth and growth multiple (future ÷ starting).
The formula
future GDP = starting GDP × (1 + g)ⁿ
g: annual growth rate as a decimal
n: number of years
This is compound growth: each year's growth builds on the last.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
1,000,000 × 1.03⁵ = $1,159,274.07; total growth $159,274.07; multiple 1.159.
Example 2: a decade at 2.5%
The growth multiple is 1.025¹⁰ = 1.280, a 28.0% increase.
Example 3: doubling time
At 2% a year, an economy doubles in ln 2 ÷ ln 1.02 = 35.0 years. The rule of 70 gives a quick estimate: 70 ÷ 2 = 35 years.
Example 4: backing out a growth rate
If an economy grows from $21.4 trillion to $27.0 trillion in 5 years, the compound annual growth rate is (27.0 ÷ 21.4)^(1/5) − 1 = 4.76% (nominal). Figures are illustrative.
How GDP is measured
The expenditure approach, used for headline figures in the US, Canada and UK:
GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)
Component
Meaning
C
Household consumption
I
Business investment and housing
G
Government spending on goods and services
X − M
Exports minus imports
Illustration: C 15.0, I 3.8, G 3.4, X 2.4, M 3.0 (trillions) → GDP = 21.6 trillion.
The income approach adds wages, profits, rents and taxes less subsidies; the output approach sums value added by each industry. In principle all three give the same total.
Real vs nominal GDP
Nominal GDP is measured at current prices.
Real GDP removes inflation, so it measures changes in volume.
Real growth ≈ (1 + nominal growth) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. With 5% nominal growth and 3% inflation, real growth is 1.94%. Use real rates to compare living standards over time; see the inflation calculator.
Who publishes GDP
US: Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), quarterly at an annualised rate.
Canada: Statistics Canada, monthly and quarterly.
UK: Office for National Statistics (ONS), monthly and quarterly (UK quarterly growth is usually reported as a non-annualised rate).
That last point matters: a US "2% growth" quarter means about 0.5% that quarter, while a UK "0.5%" quarter is roughly the same pace.
Understanding your results
Constant growth is a simplification. Real economies have recessions and recoveries.
GDP per capita divides by population and better reflects living standards.
Currency comparisons between countries need exchange rates or purchasing power parity.
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing real and nominal rates in the same projection.
Comparing annualised and non-annualised quarterly rates.
Using simple growth (rate × years) instead of compounding.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate GDP growth?
(New GDP ÷ old GDP) − 1, or use this calculator to project forwards.
What is the GDP formula?
C + I + G + (X − M).
What's the difference between real and nominal GDP?
Real GDP is adjusted for inflation.
What is the rule of 70?
70 ÷ growth rate ≈ years to double.
Can I use this for company revenue?
Yes; it's compound growth for any amount.
Can growth be negative?
Yes; enter a negative rate.
