H1: Student Loan Calculator
Student loans are usually repaid in fixed monthly installments over a set term, like other amortizing loans. This calculator shows the monthly payment, total of payments and total interest for a student loan, so you can see what a balance will cost and how the term changes it. It works for US federal and private loans on a standard (level-payment) plan, Canadian student loans and private education loans elsewhere.
How to use this calculator
1. Loan amount: your current balance, or the total you expect to borrow. Include any unpaid interest that has been or will be capitalized (added to the principal).
2. Interest rate: your loan's annual rate. If you have several loans with different rates, run each separately or use a weighted average rate.
3. Loan term (years) and (months): 10 years is the US federal standard repayment term; private loans are often 5 to 20 years.
Results: monthly payment, total of payments, total interest paid and an amortization chart.
The calculator assumes a fixed rate and equal payments. It doesn't model income-driven repayment, forgiveness or deferment.
The formula
Payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
P: loan balance
r: annual rate ÷ 12
n: number of monthly payments
Worked examples
Example 1: $20,000 at 7.5% over 5 years
Payment = $400.76
Total paid $24,045.54, of which interest $4,045.54
Example 2: $30,000 at 6.39% on a 10-year plan
Payment = $338.97; total interest $10,676.07
Stretching to 20 years: payment $221.73, total interest $23,216.01
Paying $100 a month extra on the 10-year schedule clears the loan in 86 months (about 7.2 years), cutting interest to $7,395.92, a saving of $3,280.15. Enter the higher payment as a shorter term to see this, or use the debt payoff calculator.
Example 3: $45,000 at 5.5% over 15 years
Payment = $367.69
Total interest $21,183.76
On a 10-year term instead: $488.37 a month and $13,604.19 of interest
Example 4: a weighted average rate
Loans of $10,000 at 4.5% and $20,000 at 6.5% have a weighted average rate of (10,000 × 4.5 + 20,000 × 6.5) ÷ 30,000 = 5.83%, not the simple average of 5.5%. Enter $30,000 at 5.83% for a close estimate of the combined payment.
Interest before repayment
Unsubsidized loans accrue interest while you're in school. $30,000 at 6.39% accrues about $5.25 a day; over four and a half years of study and grace, that's roughly $8,626.50 of simple interest. If it's capitalized, your repayment balance starts higher. Paying the interest as it accrues avoids this.

Understanding your results
Total interest assumes you pay on schedule for the full term.
Longer terms lower the payment but raise total interest.
Refinancing a federal loan into a private loan can lower the rate but gives up federal protections (income-driven plans, deferment, forgiveness programs).
Country notes
United States: federal Direct Loan rates are fixed for the life of each loan and reset every July 1 for new loans, based on the 10-year Treasury auction. For 2025–26 the undergraduate rate was 6.39% [VERIFY 2026–27 rates on studentaid.gov]. Federal loans also charge an origination fee deducted from each disbursement. Repayment plan rules changed under 2025 legislation (including a new Repayment Assistance Plan) [VERIFY]; this page doesn't model income-driven plans. Up to $2,500 of student loan interest a year may be deductible, subject to income limits.
Canada: Canada Student Loans have no interest on the federal portion since April 2023. Repayment starts six months after you finish school, and the Repayment Assistance Plan can reduce or pause payments based on income. Provincial portions may still charge interest, which varies by province [VERIFY].
United Kingdom: UK student loans work differently: repayments are a percentage of income above a threshold, not a fixed installment, and remaining balances are written off after a set period. Use the UK student loan repayment calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting capitalized interest in the balance.
Averaging rates without weighting by balance.
Assuming a longer term is cheaper because the payment is lower.
Refinancing federal loans without weighing lost protections.
Missing autopay discounts. Many servicers and lenders cut the rate by 0.25 percentage points for autopay.
Before you borrow or refinance
List each loan's balance, rate and servicer before using the calculator, because a single blended rate can hide one expensive loan. Pay the highest-rate loan first when you have spare money. Federal US loans come with income-driven plans and forgiveness options that private loans don't, so think hard before refinancing them privately. Set up autopay if your servicer offers a rate reduction for it, and check your loan statements once a year.
Frequently asked questions
How is my student loan payment calculated?
Using the amortization formula with your balance, rate and term.
What is the standard repayment term?
10 years for most US federal loans.
Should I pay extra on my student loans?
Extra payments reduce interest and shorten the loan. Make sure they're applied to principal, ideally on the highest-rate loan.
Does this calculator include income-driven repayment?
No. It models fixed payments only.
Is student loan interest tax deductible?
In the US, up to $2,500 a year, phased out at higher incomes. In Canada, interest on government student loans can qualify for a non-refundable tax credit.
What happens if I can't afford my payment?
Contact your servicer. Federal and Canadian government loans have options to reduce or pause payments.
