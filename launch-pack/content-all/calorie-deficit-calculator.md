H1: Calorie Deficit Calculator
A calorie deficit means eating fewer calories than your body uses, so it draws on stored energy, mostly fat. This calculator estimates your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), then works out a daily calorie target for the rate of weight loss you choose and roughly how long it would take to reach a goal weight. It also flags targets that are lower than generally advised without medical supervision.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric or imperial.
2. Age, sex, height and current weight.
3. Activity level: sedentary to very active.
4. Goal weight.
5. Rate of loss: 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 or 1 kg a week (about 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 lb).
Results:
TDEE (maintenance calories)
Daily deficit for your chosen rate
Daily calorie target
Estimated weeks to goal and a goal date
A warning if the target falls below about 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men), or the deficit exceeds 1,000 kcal a day
How it works
Step 1: BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation:
Men:   10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5
Women: 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age − 161
Step 2: TDEE = BMR × activity factor (1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725 or 1.9).
Step 3: daily deficit
Daily deficit = weekly loss (kg) × 7,700 ÷ 7
Target        = TDEE − daily deficit
Weeks to goal = (current weight − goal weight) ÷ weekly loss
7,700 kcal per kg (about 3,500 kcal per lb) is a long-standing rule of thumb for the energy in body fat. A 0.5 kg-a-week rate needs 550 kcal a day; 1 lb a week needs about 500 kcal a day.

Understanding your results
Weight loss slows over time. As you lose weight, your TDEE falls, and your body adapts. The 7,700 kcal rule doesn't account for this, so the "weeks to goal" figure is an optimistic minimum. The NIH Body Weight Planner uses a dynamic model that's more realistic for long periods.
Early losses are faster. The first week or two often includes water and glycogen, so the scale may drop faster at first.
Daily weights fluctuate by 1–2 kg. Use a weekly average.
Adjust after three to four weeks. If you're losing more slowly than planned, reduce intake by 100–200 kcal or increase activity.
How fast should you lose weight?
The NHS suggests aiming to lose 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week.
The CDC says people who lose weight gradually and steadily (about 1 to 2 lb a week) are more successful at keeping it off.
For many people with excess weight, losing 5% to 10% of body weight brings health benefits; for an 80 kg person that's 4–8 kg.
Very low-calorie diets (800 kcal a day or less) should only be followed with medical supervision.
Making a deficit work
Protein: around 1.2–1.6 g per kg helps preserve muscle and keeps you full. See the protein calculator.
Strength training two or more days a week helps keep muscle as you lose fat.
Fibre and volume: vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains add fullness for few calories.
Drinks: sugary drinks and alcohol are easy calories to cut.
Sleep: short sleep is linked with increased appetite.
Country notes
US: the CDC and the NIDDK Body Weight Planner are the main public resources.
UK: the NHS Weight Loss Plan is a free 12-week programme; the NHS uses the 0.5 to 1 kg a week guideline.
Canada: Obesity Canada's clinical practice guideline focuses on health improvements rather than a target weight.
Tips and common mistakes
Overestimating activity, which inflates TDEE and makes the target too high.
Underestimating food intake. Weighing food for a week or two helps calibrate.
Cutting too hard. Large deficits are harder to sustain and increase muscle loss.
Not recalculating after losing 5 kg or more.
Frequently asked questions
How big should my calorie deficit be?
For most people, 300 to 500 kcal a day, and rarely more than 1,000 kcal a day.
How many calories should I eat to lose 1 kg a week?
About 1,100 kcal a day below your TDEE, which may be too low for many people.
Is 1,200 calories a day safe?
It's often used as a lower limit for women without supervision, but needs vary; talk to a doctor or dietitian.
Why did my weight loss stop?
Your TDEE falls as you lose weight, and intake tends to creep up. Recalculate and review portions.
Do I need to count calories?
Not necessarily; any approach that creates a consistent deficit works.
Does exercise matter if I'm in a deficit?
Yes; it helps preserve muscle and improves health even when weight doesn't change.
Can I target belly fat?
No. Fat loss happens across the body, though waist size often falls as you lose weight.
