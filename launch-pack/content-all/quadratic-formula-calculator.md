H1: Quadratic Formula Calculator
A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a isn't zero. Its solutions (roots) are where the parabola y = ax² + bx + c crosses the x-axis. Enter the coefficients a, b and c and this calculator returns the discriminant and both roots, including complex roots when the parabola doesn't cross the axis.
How to use this calculator
1. Coefficient a: the number in front of x² (not zero).
2. Coefficient b: the number in front of x (include the sign).
3. Coefficient c: the constant term.
Rearrange your equation into standard form first: 2x² = 7 − 3x becomes 2x² + 3x − 7 = 0, so a = 2, b = 3, c = −7.
Results: discriminant, root 1 and root 2 (or a complex pair a ± bi).
The quadratic formula
x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a
The discriminant D = b² − 4ac tells you what kind of roots to expect:
Discriminant
Roots
D > 0
Two different real roots
D = 0
One repeated real root (the vertex touches the axis)
D < 0
Two complex conjugate roots (no x-intercepts)
Worked examples
Example 1: x² − 3x + 2 = 0
D = 9 − 8 = 1
x = (3 ± 1) ÷ 2 → 2 and 1
Check by factoring: (x − 2)(x − 1) = 0.
Example 2: 2x² + 3x − 7 = 0
D = 9 + 56 = 65
x = (−3 ± √65) ÷ 4 → 1.265564 and -2.765564
Example 3: x² + 2x + 5 = 0
D = 4 − 20 = −16
x = (−2 ± 4i) ÷ 2 → −1 ± 2i
Example 4: 2x² − 4x + 2 = 0
D = 16 − 16 = 0 → one repeated root, x = 1
Example 5: a thrown ball
Height h = −4.9t² + 20t + 15 (metres, seconds). It hits the ground when h = 0: a = −4.9, b = 20, c = 15. The positive root is t ≈ 4.73 s; the negative root has no physical meaning here.
The vertex and the shape
The parabola opens upward if a > 0 and downward if a < 0.
Vertex: x = −b ÷ 2a. For x² − 3x + 2, the vertex is at x = 1.5, y = -0.25 (the minimum).
The roots are symmetric about the vertex.
Sum of roots = −b/a; product = c/a (Vieta's formulas): for Example 1, 2 + 1 = 3 and 2 × 1 = 2.
Other solving methods
Factoring: quickest when roots are whole numbers.
Completing the square: rewrite as a(x − h)² + k = 0; it's how the formula is derived.
Graphing: read off x-intercepts.
The formula always works, which is why the calculator uses it.
Understanding your results
Rounding: results show six decimals; exact forms like (−3 ± √65)/4 are often preferred in algebra.
Complex roots come in conjugate pairs for real coefficients.
a = 0 isn't quadratic; the calculator shows an error. Solve bx + c = 0 instead (x = −c/b).
Tips and common mistakes
Dropping the sign of b. If b = −3, then −b = 3.
Dividing only part of the numerator by 2a. The whole expression −b ± √D goes over 2a.
Not rearranging to = 0 before reading off a, b and c.
Squaring a negative b incorrectly. (−3)² = 9.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
UK GCSE calls b² − 4ac the discriminant, as US and Canadian courses do.
Frequently asked questions
What is the quadratic formula?
x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a.
What does the discriminant tell me?
Whether the roots are two real, one repeated, or two complex.
Can a quadratic have no solution?
No real solution if D < 0, but it always has two complex solutions.
What if a is negative?
The formula still works; the parabola opens downward.
How do I check my roots?
Substitute them back into the equation.
Why are there two answers?
Because of the ± in front of the square root.
