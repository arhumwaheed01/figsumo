H1: Stair Calculator
Building or replacing a staircase starts with simple arithmetic: how many steps fit the height between floors, how tall each riser is, and how far the stairs will run. This calculator takes the total rise and your target step sizes and returns the number of risers, the exact riser height, the total run and an approximate stringer length.
How to use this calculator
1. Total rise (in): finished floor to finished floor (default 108, a 9 ft rise).
2. Target riser height (in): the step height you'd like (default 7.5).
3. Target tread depth (in): the horizontal depth of each step (default 10).
Results: number of steps (risers), actual riser height, total run and approximate stringer length.
The formulas
risers = total rise ÷ target riser, rounded to the nearest whole number
actual riser = total rise ÷ risers
treads = risers − 1        (the top floor acts as the last step)
total run = tread depth × treads
stringer ≈ √(riser² + tread²) × risers
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
108 ÷ 7.5 = 14.4 → 14 risers
108 ÷ 14 = 7.714 in each (about 7 ¹¹⁄₁₆ in)
13 treads × 10 in = 130 in run (10 ft 10 in)
Stringer ≈ 176.82 in. The straight-line diagonal from bottom to top is 169.01 in; the calculator's figure includes one extra step's diagonal to allow for the top connection.
Stair angle ≈ 37.6°
Example 2: comfort check
A common rule of thumb is 2 × riser + tread ≈ 24–25 in. Here: 2 × 7.714 + 10 = 25.43 in. That's a little short; a 10.5 in or 11 in tread would feel more natural if space allows.
Example 3: metric
A UK or Canadian rise of 2,700 mm with 180 mm target risers and 250 mm treads: 15 risers of 180.0 mm, a 3,500 mm run, and a pitch of 35.8°. Convert to inches (÷ 25.4) to use the calculator, or work it by hand like this.

Terms
Rise / riser: vertical height of one step.
Run / tread / going: horizontal depth of one step (the "going" excludes the nosing overhang).
Nosing: the front edge that overhangs the riser below, typically ¾–1¼ in.
Stringer: the sloped board that supports the treads.
Headroom: vertical clearance above the stair's pitch line.
Understanding your results
Every riser must be equal. Even small differences trip people; codes limit variation to ⅜ in (9.5 mm) in the US.
Measure finished heights, including flooring on both levels.
Stringer stock length: buy the next standard length up from the stringer figure and allow for the cut at top and bottom.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting treads as risers. There's one fewer tread than risers.
Ignoring flooring thickness, which changes the first and last riser.
Forgetting the landing on long flights; codes limit the total rise of a single flight.
Leaving out the nosing when checking tread depth.
Frequently asked questions
How many stairs do I need for a 9 ft rise?
About 14 risers of 7.7 in.
What is the maximum riser height?
7¾ in under the US IRC; 200 mm in Canada and 220 mm for English private stairs.
What is a comfortable stair angle?
About 30–37°.
Why is there one fewer tread than riser?
The upper floor forms the last step.
How long should my stringer be?
Roughly the diagonal of the total rise and run, plus a little for the cuts.
