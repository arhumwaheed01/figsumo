H1: Mulch Calculator
A layer of mulch keeps soil moist, suppresses weeds and evens out soil temperature. To order the right amount, you need the bed's area and the depth you want. This calculator converts length, width and depth into cubic yards (how bulk mulch is sold in the US and Canada) and cubic feet (how bags are labelled).
How to use this calculator
1. Bed length (ft) and bed width (ft): default 20 × 8.
2. Depth (in): default 3.
Results: mulch volume in cubic yards and cubic feet, with a chart of whole yards plus leftover cubic feet.
The formulas
ft³ = length × width × depth ÷ 12
yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27
bags = ft³ ÷ bag size (ft³), rounded up
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
20 × 8 × (3 ÷ 12) = 40 ft³ = 1.48 yd³. In bags: 20 × 2 ft³ bags or 14 × 3 ft³ bags.
Example 2: a round bed
A circular bed 6 ft across (radius 3 ft), 3 in deep: π × 3² × 0.25 = 7.07 ft³ → 4 two-cubic-foot bags. Enter the area of irregular beds by splitting them into rectangles.
Example 3: metric (UK and Canada)
A border 5 m × 1.2 m at 75 mm deep needs 0.45 m³ = 450 litres, or 8 × 60 L bags.
Example 4: what a cubic yard covers
One cubic yard (27 ft³) covers about 108 ft² at 3 in deep (27 ÷ 0.25), 162 ft² at 2 in and 81 ft² at 4 in.
How deep should mulch be?
Situation
Depth
Fine mulch (shredded bark, compost)
2–3 in
Coarse mulch (wood chips, nuggets)
3–4 in
Topping up an existing layer
1–2 in
Around tree trunks
Keep 2–6 in clear of the trunk; never pile up a "mulch volcano"
Deeper than about 4 in can starve roots of oxygen and keep the soil too wet.
Types of mulch
Shredded hardwood: knits together, stays put on slopes.
Pine bark nuggets: long-lasting, but can float in heavy rain.
Wood chips: inexpensive, often free from arborists.
Compost and leaf mould: feed the soil, break down within a season.
Straw: for vegetable beds (choose weed-free straw).
Rubber and stone: don't break down or improve soil; stone heats up in sun. For stone, use the gravel calculator.
Bulk or bags?
Bulk delivery is usually cheaper above about 2–3 cubic yards, though delivery fees apply [VERIFY with local suppliers]. A 2 ft³ bag is 1/13.5 of a cubic yard, so 1 yd³ equals about 14 bags.
Understanding your results
Settling: fresh mulch settles 10–20%; add a little extra.
Plants take up room: you can subtract large shrubs or paths from the area.
The chart shows whole yards plus extra cubic feet, matching how some suppliers sell in whole yards.
Tips and common mistakes
Entering depth in feet.
Piling mulch against stems and trunks, which invites rot and pests.
Spreading over weeds without clearing them first.
Laying mulch on dry soil: water first.
Frequently asked questions
How much mulch do I need?
Length × width × depth (in) ÷ 12 gives cubic feet; divide by 27 for cubic yards.
How many bags are in a yard of mulch?
About 13.5 two-cubic-foot bags, or 9 three-cubic-foot bags.
How deep should mulch be?
2–4 inches, depending on the material.
How much area does a yard of mulch cover?
About 108 ft² at 3 in deep.
When should I mulch?
Spring, after the soil warms, and autumn for winter protection are common.
Should I remove old mulch?
Only if it's thick and matted; otherwise top it up.
