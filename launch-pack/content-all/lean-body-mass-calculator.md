H1: Lean Body Mass Calculator
Lean body mass (LBM) is everything in your body that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs, skin and water. Knowing it helps you track whether weight changes come from fat or muscle, set protein targets, and use equations such as Katch-McArdle that estimate metabolic rate from lean mass. This calculator works out lean body mass and fat mass from your weight and body fat percentage.
How to use this calculator
1. Units and weight.
2. Body fat %: from a body composition scan (DEXA), bioimpedance scale, skinfold measurement or a circumference estimate such as the body fat calculator.
Results: lean body mass and fat mass in kg and lb.
The formula
Fat mass = Weight × Body fat % ÷ 100
Lean body mass = Weight − Fat mass = Weight × (1 − Body fat % ÷ 100)
Worked examples
Example 1: 80 kg at 22% body fat
Fat mass = 80 × 0.22 = 17.6 kg
Lean body mass = 62.4 kg (137.6 lb)
Example 2: 150 lb at 30% body fat
Fat mass = 45.0 lb; LBM = 105.0 lb
Example 3: tracking a weight change
The person in Example 1 loses 6 kg, of which an estimated 4.5 kg is fat and 1.5 kg lean. New weight 74 kg; fat mass 13.1 kg; body fat about 17.7%. Monitoring lean mass shows whether a diet is preserving muscle.
Example 4: 65 kg at 32% body fat
Fat mass = 65 × 0.32 = 20.8 kg
Lean body mass = 44.2 kg (97.4 lb)
That's close to the Boer estimate of 46.1 kg for a 65 kg, 165 cm woman (below), which shows the formula is reasonable for someone of average build.
Estimating LBM without body fat %
When body fat isn't known, formulas estimate LBM from height and weight. The Boer formula (1984):
Men:   LBM = 0.407 × Weight (kg) + 0.267 × Height (cm) − 19.2
Women: LBM = 0.252 × Weight (kg) + 0.473 × Height (cm) − 48.3
For a man of 80 kg and 180 cm, that's 61.4 kg; for a woman of 65 kg and 165 cm, 46.1 kg. These are population averages and less accurate than a measured body fat percentage.

W is weight in kg and H height in cm. The spread of about 4.9 kg is typical, and it's why a measured body fat percentage gives a better answer when you have one. The James formula is known to give implausible results at high body weights, so it's rarely used for people with obesity.
Fat-free mass index
Lean mass is easier to compare between people when it's scaled by height, like BMI: FFMI = LBM (kg) ÷ height (m)². For 62.4 kg of lean mass at 1.80 m, FFMI = 19.3. Higher values mean more lean mass for your height, but there's no single official healthy range, so use it to track your own trend.
Using lean body mass
Metabolic rate: the Katch-McArdle equation estimates BMR = 370 + 21.6 × LBM (kg). With 62.4 kg of LBM, that's about 1,718 kcal a day. See the BMR calculator.
Protein: some sports nutritionists set protein per kg of lean mass; at 1.6 g/kg of LBM, 62.4 kg of LBM suggests about 100 g a day. See the protein calculator.
Medication dosing: some anaesthetic and other drugs are dosed on lean body weight; that's a clinical decision.
Understanding your results
Accuracy depends on the body fat estimate. Home scales and tape methods can be several percentage points off, which changes LBM by a few kilograms.
LBM isn't the same as muscle. It includes bone, organs and water, so it changes with hydration.
Typical body fat ranges vary by age and sex, and different organisations publish different ranges. Use trends over time rather than a single reading.
Strength training and adequate protein help preserve lean mass when losing weight, and build it over time.
Measurement tips
Measure at the same time of day under similar conditions (for example, morning, before eating).
Bioimpedance readings change with hydration, recent exercise and food.
DEXA scans are among the more accurate widely available methods, but they're not available everywhere and involve a small dose of radiation.
Tips and common mistakes
Mixing kg and lb. Use one unit throughout.
Comparing results from different devices.
Assuming all weight loss is fat. Rapid weight loss often includes water and some lean tissue.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
There's no national guideline for lean body mass itself in any of the three countries; health services use BMI and waist measurements for screening. In the US, body-composition DEXA scans are widely sold direct to consumers at private clinics and gyms. In Canada and the UK, DEXA is mainly used for bone density testing through the public health system, and body-composition scans are usually private and paid for. Home bioimpedance scales are sold everywhere; treat their readings as estimates.
Frequently asked questions
What is lean body mass?
Your total weight minus fat mass.
How do I calculate lean body mass?
Weight × (1 − body fat % ÷ 100).
What is a healthy lean body mass?
There's no single healthy figure; it depends on height, sex and age. What matters is having enough muscle for strength and function.
Is lean body mass the same as muscle mass?
No. LBM includes muscle, bone, organs and water.
How can I increase lean body mass?
Resistance training with enough protein and calories.
Why use LBM for calorie estimates?
Lean tissue uses more energy than fat, so equations based on LBM can be more accurate for people with very high or low body fat.
