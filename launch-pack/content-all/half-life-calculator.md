H1: Half-Life Calculator
Half-life is the time it takes for half of a quantity to decay. It describes radioactive isotopes, the elimination of drugs and caffeine from the body, and any process of exponential decay. Enter the starting amount, the half-life and the time that has passed, and this calculator gives the amount remaining and the amount that has decayed.
How to use this calculator
1. Initial amount (N₀): in any unit: grams, atoms, milligrams, becquerels or percent.
2. Half-life (t½): in any time unit.
3. Elapsed time (t): in the same time unit as the half-life.
Results: remaining amount and amount decayed (N₀ − N).
The formula
N(t) = N₀ × (1/2)^(t ÷ t½)
Equivalent forms using the decay constant λ and the mean lifetime τ:
N(t) = N₀ × e^(−λt),  λ = ln 2 ÷ t½,  τ = 1 ÷ λ = t½ ÷ ln 2
Solving for other values:
Time: t = t½ × ln(N ÷ N₀) ÷ ln(1/2)
Half-life: t½ = t × ln(1/2) ÷ ln(N ÷ N₀)
Worked examples
Example 1: the default values
100 units, half-life 5, elapsed 10: that's two half-lives.
N = 100 × (1/2)² = 25 remaining; 75 decayed.
Example 2: carbon-14
Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years. After 10,000 years, 29.83% of the original C-14 remains. For C-14, λ = 0.00012097 per year and the mean lifetime is about 8,267 years.
Example 3: radiocarbon dating (solving for time)
A sample has 30% of its original C-14:
t = 5,730 × ln(0.30) ÷ ln(0.5) ≈ 9,953 years
Example 4: iodine-131
I-131, used in thyroid treatment, has a half-life of about 8.02 days. 80 MBq after 24 days becomes 10.0520 MBq.
Example 5: caffeine
Caffeine's half-life in healthy adults is often quoted as roughly 5 hours, although it varies widely between people. 200 mg at 8 a.m. would leave about 50.0 mg by 6 p.m. (10 hours, two half-lives).
Example 6: finding a half-life
A substance falls from 100 g to 40 g in 6 hours:
t½ = 6 × ln(0.5) ÷ ln(0.4) = 4.539 hours
Understanding your results
Each half-life halves what's left, not the original: after 1, 2, 3 and 4 half-lives, 50%, 25%, 12.5% and 6.25% remain.
It never quite reaches zero mathematically. After about 7 half-lives, less than 1% remains; after 10, less than 0.1%.
Half-life is constant for radioactive decay regardless of the amount or conditions. Biological half-lives vary between individuals.
Units must match. If the half-life is in years, enter time in years.
Practice problems
1. What fraction remains after 3 half-lives? (Answer: (1/2)³ = 0.1250, or 1/8.)
2. 1,000 mg of a drug has a 12-hour half-life. How much remains after 30 hours? (Answer: 176.78 mg.)
Tips and common mistakes
Subtracting half the original each period (linear decay). Decay is exponential.
Mismatched time units.
Confusing half-life with mean lifetime; τ is about 1.44 times t½.
Treating biological half-lives as exact. Use them only as rough guides, and follow medical advice for medications.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
Iodine-131, used in thyroid treatment in all three countries, has a half-life of about 8.02 days. After 30 days, about 7.5% of the original activity remains.
Frequently asked questions
What is half-life?
The time for half of a quantity to decay or be eliminated.
How much remains after two half-lives?
25%.
How is carbon dating calculated?
From the remaining fraction of C-14 using t = t½ × ln(N/N₀) ÷ ln(1/2).
Does half-life depend on the starting amount?
No.
What is the decay constant?
λ = ln 2 ÷ t½, the fraction decaying per unit time in the continuous model.
Can I use this for drug elimination?
For first-order elimination, yes, as an estimate.
