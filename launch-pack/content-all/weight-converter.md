H1: Weight Converter
Kilograms are the standard almost everywhere, but body weight is still commonly given in pounds in the US and Canada and in stones and pounds in the UK. This converter switches between kilograms and pounds, and the tables below cover stones, ounces and grams.
How to use this converter
1. Weight: the amount to convert (default 70).
2. Convert: "Kilograms → pounds" or "Pounds → kilograms".
Result: the converted weight to two decimal places.
The conversion factors
1 lb = 0.45359237 kg (exact, by international agreement)
1 kg ≈ 2.2046226 lb
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
70 kg × 2.2046 = 154.32 lb
Example 2: pounds to kilograms
154 lb × 0.45359 = 69.85 kg
Example 3: stones and pounds (UK)
11 st 4 lb = 11 × 14 + 4 = 158 lb → 71.67 kg. Going the other way, 80 kg is about 12 st 8.4 lb.
Example 4: a newborn
3.4 kg = 7.50 lb, or 7 lb 7.9 oz: birth weights in the US, Canada and UK are often announced in pounds and ounces.
Example 5: luggage
A 23 kg checked-bag limit (common on transatlantic flights) is 50.7 lb, usually rounded to 50 lb.
Example 6: cooking
500 g = 17.64 oz (1 oz = 28.35 g).

Mental shortcuts
kg to lb: double it and add 10% (70 → 140 + 14 = 154).
lb to kg: halve it and subtract 10% (150 → 75 − 7.5 = 67.5; exact is 68.0).
Understanding your results
Two decimal places are shown; round to the nearest pound or 0.1 kg for body weight.
Mass vs weight: strictly, kilograms and pounds measure mass; in everyday use they're called weight. See the mass calculator.
Health tools: the BMI calculator and calorie calculator accept either unit.
Tips and common mistakes
Using 2.2 in precise work (it's 2.20462…).
Treating stones as 10 lb. A stone is 14 lb.
Confusing US tons, UK tons and metric tonnes.
Entering pounds and ounces as a decimal: 7 lb 8 oz is 7.5 lb, not 7.8.
Notes for US, Canada and UK users
US: pounds and ounces are used for body weight, food labels (alongside grams) and shipping. Note that a US "ton" is the 2,000 lb short ton.
Canada: metric is official, and groceries are priced per kg and per 100 g, but many people still give their body weight in pounds, and doctors' offices may record either. Store flyers often show both, such as "$4.39/lb ($9.68/kg)".
UK: food is sold in grams and kilograms, but body weight is often given in stones and pounds, and babies' birth weights in pounds and ounces. Pub and road signs keep some imperial units, but weight on packaging is metric.
The legal definition is the same in all three countries: since the 1959 international yard and pound agreement, 1 lb is exactly 0.45359237 kg.
Check the price example: $4.39 per lb × 2.20462 lb per kg = $9.68 per kg.
