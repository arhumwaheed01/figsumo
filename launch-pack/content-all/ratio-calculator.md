H1: Ratio Calculator
A ratio compares two quantities, such as 3 : 4. Two ratios that are equal form a proportion. This calculator solves proportions: enter A, B and C in A : B = C : D, and it finds the missing fourth value, D. It's the tool for scaling recipes, resizing images, reading map scales and converting rates.
How to use this calculator
1. A and B: the known ratio (default 3 : 4). A can't be zero.
2. C: the value that corresponds to A in the new ratio (default 75).
Results: the completed proportion A : B = C : D and the missing value D.
To find a different missing term, rearrange: if you know B and D but not C, enter B, A and D in the A, B, C boxes (B : A = D : C).
The formula
A ÷ B = C ÷ D   →   D = B × C ÷ A
This is cross-multiplication: A × D = B × C.
Worked examples
Example 1: the default
3 : 4 = 75 : D → D = 4 × 75 ÷ 3 = 100
Example 2: resizing an image
A 16 : 9 video is 1,920 pixels wide. Height = 9 × 1,920 ÷ 16 = 1,080 px.
A 16 : 9 image that must be 1,200 px tall needs a width of 1,200 × 16 ÷ 9 = 2,133.33 px (enter A = 9, B = 16, C = 1,200).
Example 3: scaling a recipe
A recipe uses 150 g of flour for 2 people. For 5: 2 : 150 = 5 : D → D = 150 × 5 ÷ 2 = 375 g.
Example 4: map scale
On a 1 : 50,000 map, 3.2 cm on paper is 3.2 × 50,000 = 160,000 cm = 1.6 km on the ground.
Simplifying ratios
Divide both terms by their greatest common factor: 12 : 18 → ÷ 6 → 2 : 3. For decimals, multiply first: 1.5 : 2.5 → 15 : 25 → 3 : 5. The GCF calculator helps with large numbers.
Ratios, fractions and percentages
The ratio 3 : 4 means 3 parts to 4 parts, so the first quantity is 3 ÷ 7 of the total (about 42.9%), not 3 ÷ 4.
Comparing part to part (3 : 4) is different from part to whole (3 : 7).
To split an amount in a ratio, divide by the total parts: £700 in the ratio 3 : 4 is £300 and £400.

Understanding your results
D is shown to six decimal places; round to suit the context (whole pixels, grams, people).
Units must match within each side: if A is in cups, C must be too.
Proportions assume a straight-line relationship. Cooking times and some chemistry don't scale linearly.
Tips and common mistakes
Swapping the order. A must correspond to C, and B to D.
Confusing part : part with part : whole.
Scaling non-linear quantities, such as baking time or pan area (doubling a round pan's diameter quadruples its area).
Frequently asked questions
How do I solve a proportion?
Cross-multiply: D = B × C ÷ A.
How do I simplify a ratio?
Divide both parts by their greatest common factor.
What does 16:9 mean?
16 units wide for every 9 units high.
How do I split a number in a ratio?
Divide by the sum of the parts, then multiply by each part.
Can a ratio have more than two parts?
Yes (for example, 1 : 2 : 3 for concrete mixes). Scale each term by the same factor.
Why can't A be zero?
D = B × C ÷ A would divide by zero.
