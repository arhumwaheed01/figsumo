H1: Ideal Weight Calculator
"Ideal body weight" formulas were developed for clinical use, mainly to dose certain medicines, not as personal weight goals. They give a single number for your height and sex. This calculator uses the Robinson formula (1983); this page also explains the other common formulas and how they compare with the healthy BMI range, which is usually a better guide for setting a goal.
How to use this calculator
1. Sex.
2. Units: metric (cm) or imperial (ft and in).
3. Height: without shoes.
Results: ideal weight (Robinson formula) in kg and lb.

Understanding your results
One number isn't a target. Different formulas give different answers, and none accounts for muscle mass, frame size, age or ethnicity.
Healthy range: a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 is the standard adult guide; see the healthy weight calculator.
Frame size: people with a large frame may sit toward the upper end of a range; see the body type calculator.
Body composition: two people of the same weight can have very different body fat. The body fat calculator and lean body mass calculator give a fuller picture.
Where ideal body weight is used
In hospitals, ideal body weight (often the Devine formula) is used to calculate doses of some drugs, set ventilator tidal volumes and estimate kidney function in certain equations. For people with obesity, clinicians may use an "adjusted body weight" (ideal weight plus 40% of the excess). These uses are clinical decisions, not something to calculate for yourself.
Adjusted body weight, for reference
For a man of 175 cm who weighs 100 kg, the Devine ideal weight is 70.5 kg, so the adjusted body weight is 70.5 + 0.4 × (29.5) = 82.3 kg. Clinicians use figures like this only for specific drugs and calculations; it isn't a recommended weight.
Setting a personal goal
If you want to change your weight, a goal based on health markers is more useful than a formula. For many people with excess weight, losing 5% to 10% of current weight brings measurable health benefits. Use the calorie deficit calculator to plan a gradual change, and talk to your doctor if you have health conditions.
Country notes
The formulas use inches and kilograms because they came from US clinical practice. The NHS, Health Canada and the CDC all use BMI ranges rather than ideal-weight formulas for public health advice. NICE recommends lower BMI thresholds for some ethnic groups (overweight from 23).
Tips and common mistakes
Treating the result as the only healthy weight.
Using it for children. These formulas are for adults.
Comparing results from different formulas as if one were "correct".
Frequently asked questions
What is my ideal weight?
Formulas give a single estimate, but a healthy weight is a range: BMI 18.5 to 24.9 for adults.
Which ideal weight formula is most accurate?
None is clearly best. Robinson and Miller tend to give lower values for tall people than Hamwi.
Why do the formulas start at 5 feet?
They were built as a base weight at 5 feet plus an amount per inch.
Does muscle change my ideal weight?
Muscular people may be healthy above the formula weight; the formulas don't consider body composition.
Is ideal weight the same for men and women?
No, the formulas use different constants by sex, while BMI ranges are the same.
Why do doctors use ideal body weight?
For dosing certain medications and some clinical calculations.
