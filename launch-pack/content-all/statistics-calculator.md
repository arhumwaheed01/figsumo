H1: Statistics Calculator
This calculator gives a full set of descriptive statistics for a list of numbers in one go: count, sum, mean, median, mode, minimum, maximum, range and sample standard deviation. It's useful for homework, survey results, lab readings or quick checks on business data.
How to use this calculator
1. Numbers (comma-separated): for example 3, 7, 7, 2, 9, 4. Decimals and negatives are fine.
Results: mean (highlighted), count, sum, median, mode, min / max / range and sample standard deviation, plus a summary table you can copy.
What each statistic means
Statistic
Definition
Count (n)
How many values
Sum
All values added
Mean
Sum ÷ count
Median
Middle value when sorted (average of the middle two if n is even)
Mode
Most frequent value ("No mode" if every value appears once)
Range
Max − min
Sample SD
√(Σ(x − mean)² ÷ (n − 1))
Worked example: 3, 7, 7, 2, 9, 4
Sorted: 2, 3, 4, 7, 7, 9
Count 6, sum 32
Mean = 32 ÷ 6 = 5.333333
Median = (4 + 7) ÷ 2 = 5.5
Mode = 7 (appears twice)
Min 2, max 9, range 7
Sample SD = 2.73252 (population SD would be 2.49444)
Example 2: when the mean misleads
Eight house prices in $1,000s: 32, 35, 38, 40, 41, 44, 47, 250. The mean is 65.88 but the median is 40.5. One extreme value pulls the mean up, so for skewed data such as incomes and house prices, the median better describes a "typical" value.
Example 3: averaging growth rates
An investment returns +10%, −5% and +20% in three years. The arithmetic mean of the returns is 8.33%, but the actual average yearly growth is the geometric mean of the growth factors 1.10, 0.95 and 1.20: 1.0784, or 7.84% a year. Use the geometric mean for rates that compound. (The calculator doesn't show geometric mean yet.)
Choosing a measure of centre
Mean: uses every value; best for symmetric data without outliers.
Median: robust to outliers; best for skewed data.
Mode: the only option for categories (most common shoe size or colour).
Measuring spread
Range is quick but depends only on the two extremes.
Standard deviation uses every value; see the standard deviation calculator for sample vs population and the 68-95-99.7 rule.
Interquartile range (middle 50%) resists outliers.
Understanding your results
Mode with ties: if two values are equally common, the calculator currently shows one of them. Check the data if it matters.
Rounding: results show up to six decimals.
Sample SD assumes your numbers are a sample; for a complete population use the population figure from the standard deviation calculator.
Tips and common mistakes
Forgetting to sort when finding the median by hand.
Using the mean for skewed data.
Averaging percentages or averages without weighting by group size.
Pasting with stray characters such as currency signs.
Notes for US, Canada and UK readers
US and Canadian courses usually use s for the sample standard deviation and σ for the population. UK GCSE and A-level papers use the same symbols but also ask for the interquartile range and box plots. Quartile methods vary between textbooks, so check which one your course uses.
Frequently asked questions
What's the difference between mean and median?
The mean is the arithmetic average; the median is the middle value.
What if there's no repeated number?
There is no mode.
Can a data set have more than one mode?
Yes (bimodal or multimodal).
What standard deviation does this show?
The sample standard deviation (n − 1).
How many numbers can I enter?
Paste the whole list. For thousands of values, a spreadsheet is more practical.
When should I use the geometric mean?
For growth rates and ratios that multiply over time.
