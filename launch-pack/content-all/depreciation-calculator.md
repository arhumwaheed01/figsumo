H1: Depreciation Calculator
Depreciation spreads the cost of an asset (a vehicle, machine, computer or building) over the years you use it. This calculator uses the straight-line method: the same amount is written off each year until the asset reaches its salvage value. Enter the cost, the salvage value and the useful life to get the annual and monthly depreciation.
How to use this calculator
1. Asset cost: what you paid to acquire the asset and get it ready for use, including delivery and installation.
2. Salvage value: what you expect it to be worth at the end of its useful life (resale or scrap value). Enter 0 if you expect nothing.
3. Useful life (years): how long you expect to use the asset. Accounting policies or tax rules often set typical lives by asset class.
Results: straight-line annual depreciation, monthly depreciation, and book value after full life (which equals the salvage value).
The formula
Annual depreciation = (Cost − Salvage value) ÷ Useful life
Monthly depreciation = Annual depreciation ÷ 12
Book value after t years = Cost − t × Annual depreciation
Cost − Salvage is the depreciable amount.
Book value is the asset's carrying value in the accounts, not its market value.
Worked examples
Example 1: $50,000 machine, $5,000 salvage, 7 years
Depreciable amount = 50,000 − 5,000 = $45,000
Annual depreciation = 45,000 ÷ 7 = $6,428.57
Monthly = $535.71
Book value after 3 years = $30,714.29; after 7 years = $5,000.00

All three write off the same $10,000 in total; they differ only in timing. Accelerated methods put more expense in the early years, which matches assets like vehicles and computers that lose value fastest when new.
Part-year example
If the Example 1 machine is put into service on 1 April, it is used for 9 months of the first financial year (to 31 December). A prorated first-year charge is $6,428.57 × 9 ÷ 12 = $4,821.43. Depreciation then continues at the full annual amount, and the remaining 3 months fall into an eighth year. The calculator gives the full-year figure, so prorate the first and last years yourself.
Understanding your results
Annual depreciation is the expense recorded each year. It is not a cash payment; the cash was spent when you bought the asset.
Monthly depreciation is useful for monthly management accounts.
Book value falls in a straight line to the salvage value. It can differ widely from what the asset would sell for.
Accounting depreciation vs tax depreciation
Businesses usually keep two sets of figures:
Financial statements use a method that reflects how the asset is used up (straight-line is the most common), under GAAP, IFRS or the relevant standards.
Tax returns follow the tax authority's rules, which can be quite different and often faster.
The calculator gives the accounting straight-line figure. For tax, use the rules for your country:
Country notes
United States: most business property placed in service after 1986 is depreciated for tax using MACRS, with recovery periods and conventions set out in IRS Publication 946. Section 179 expensing and bonus depreciation can allow much faster write-offs; check current limits and percentages for the tax year [VERIFY 2026 Section 179 limit and bonus depreciation rate].
Canada: the CRA uses capital cost allowance (CCA): assets are grouped into classes, each with a prescribed declining-balance rate, and a half-year rule usually applies in the year of purchase. Accelerated investment incentives have applied to some classes [VERIFY current status].
United Kingdom: depreciation in the accounts is not deductible for tax. Instead, businesses claim capital allowances, such as the Annual Investment Allowance (£1 million a year) and full expensing for companies on qualifying new plant and machinery [VERIFY against GOV.UK for 2026/27].
Tips and common mistakes
Using market value as salvage without thought. Salvage is your best estimate at the end of the useful life, not today's resale value.
Depreciating land. Land is generally not depreciated; separate it from the building's cost.
Mixing tax and book figures. Use the right method for the right purpose.
Forgetting part-year use. If you buy an asset mid-year, the first year's depreciation is often prorated by months.
Frequently asked questions
What is straight-line depreciation?
A method that spreads the depreciable amount (cost minus salvage) evenly over the asset's useful life.
How do I choose a useful life?
Base it on how long you realistically expect to use the asset. Accounting policies often set standard lives for each asset type; tax rules prescribe their own.
Can salvage value be zero?
Yes. Then the full cost is depreciated over the useful life.
Which depreciation method is best?
For financial reporting, the one that best reflects how the asset's benefits are used up. Straight-line is simplest; declining-balance fits assets that lose value quickly early on.
How do I depreciate a rental property?
In the US, residential rental buildings are depreciated over 27.5 years using straight-line under MACRS (land excluded). Canada and the UK use different rules. See the rental property calculator for returns.
Is depreciation a cash expense?
No. It allocates a past cash outlay over time, which is why it is added back in cash-flow calculations.
What is accumulated depreciation?
The total depreciation charged on an asset so far. Cost minus accumulated depreciation equals book value. After 3 years, the Example 1 machine has accumulated 3 × $6,428.57 of depreciation.
