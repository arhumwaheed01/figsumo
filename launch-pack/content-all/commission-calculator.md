H1: Commission Calculator
This commission calculator works out the commission on a sale at a flat percentage rate, and what is left of the sale amount after the commission is paid. It is useful for salespeople checking their pay, for home sellers estimating an agent's fee, and for businesses budgeting the cost of a sales team.
How to use this calculator
1. Sale amount: the value of the sale the commission is based on. For a home sale, the sale price; for a sales role, the revenue the plan counts (check whether it is before or after discounts and tax).
2. Commission rate: the percentage paid, as written in the commission plan or listing agreement.
Results: commission earned and net to seller (before other fees), which is the sale amount minus the commission.
The formula
Commission = Sale amount × Rate ÷ 100
Net to seller = Sale amount − Commission
To find the rate when you know the commission: Rate = Commission ÷ Sale amount × 100.
Worked examples
Example 1: $450,000 home sale at 3%
Commission = 450,000 × 0.03 = $13,500.00
Net to seller = $436,500.00, before other closing costs, taxes and mortgage payoff
Example 2: a tiered sales plan (done by hand)
Many sales plans pay a higher rate as sales grow. Suppose the plan pays 5% on the first $10,000 of monthly sales, 7% on $10,000–$25,000 and 10% above $25,000, and you sell $32,000:
First $10,000 × 5% = $500.00
Next $15,000 × 7% = $1,050.00
Last $7,000 × 10% = $700.00
Total commission = $2,250.00, an effective rate of 7.03%
The calculator handles one flat rate at a time. For tiers, run each band separately and add the results, as above.
Understanding your results
Commission earned is gross pay from that sale, before income tax and payroll deductions.
Net to seller is what remains for the seller from the sale price after commission. In real estate, other selling costs (transfer taxes, legal fees, repairs, mortgage payoff) come out as well.
Effective rate: on tiered or capped plans, divide total commission by total sales to compare plans fairly.
Common commission structures
Straight commission: pay is only commission. Income swings with sales.
Base salary plus commission: a fixed salary plus a smaller rate. For example, a $3,000 monthly base with 4% on $85,000 of sales pays $3,400.00 commission, for $6,400.00 in total.
Tiered (graduated): higher rates on higher bands of sales, as in Example 2.
Draw against commission: an advance on future commissions that is repaid from them.
Gross margin commission: paid on profit rather than revenue, so discounts reduce pay.
Residual commission: ongoing payments for as long as a customer keeps paying, common in insurance and subscriptions.
Real estate commissions
In a typical US home sale, the total commission is shared between the listing brokerage and the buyer's brokerage, and each brokerage then pays its agent a share. For example, on a $600,000 sale with a 5% total commission ($30,000.00), each side's brokerage might receive $15,000.00, and an agent on a 70% split would get $10,500.00 before expenses. The seller's net would be $570,000.00 before other costs. Commission rates are negotiable. Since August 2024, following the National Association of Realtors settlement, buyers' agent compensation is agreed separately in writing and is no longer offered through MLS listings [VERIFY wording against NAR/DOJ guidance].
Commission as a business cost
For a business, commission is a variable cost of each sale. A 10% commission on a product sold with a 30% gross margin takes a third of the profit on that sale. Before setting rates, check the margin on what is being sold with the margin calculator, and the number of sales needed to cover fixed costs with the break-even calculator. Paying commission on gross margin instead of revenue keeps sales staff from discounting away the profit.
Country notes
United States: commission is taxed as supplemental wages for employees. Employers may withhold federal income tax on supplemental wages at a flat 22% (37% above $1 million) [VERIFY in IRS Publication 15]. Independent contractors receive Form 1099-NEC and pay self-employment tax.
Canada: commissions are employment income subject to CPP, EI and income tax deductions. Commissioned employees can sometimes deduct certain expenses using Form T2200.
United Kingdom: commission is taxed through PAYE like salary, with Income Tax and National Insurance. Estate agents typically charge sellers a percentage plus VAT; the buyer usually pays no agent fee.
Tips and common mistakes
Check the commission base. Is it calculated on the price before discounts, after discounts, or on margin?
Watch for clawbacks. Commission may be reversed if a customer cancels or doesn't pay.
Add VAT or GST/HST where it applies to agent fees.
Compare total pay, not just the rate. A lower rate with a higher base can pay more.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate commission?
Multiply the sale amount by the commission rate as a decimal. A 6% commission on $20,000 is $1,200.
How do I find my commission rate?
Divide the commission by the sale amount and multiply by 100.
Does the calculator handle tiered rates?
Not automatically. Split the sale into bands, calculate each at its rate, and add them up.
Is commission taxed differently from salary?
It is taxable income in the same way, but US employers may withhold tax on it at a flat supplemental rate, so your paycheck withholding can look different.
Are real estate commission rates fixed?
No. They are negotiable between the seller (or buyer) and the brokerage.
What does "net to seller" include?
Only the sale amount minus the commission entered. Other costs of selling are not deducted.
