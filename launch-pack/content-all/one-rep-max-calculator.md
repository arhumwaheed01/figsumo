H1: One Rep Max Calculator
Your one-rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. Testing it directly is tiring and carries some risk, so most lifters estimate it from a heavier set of several reps. This calculator uses the Epley formula to turn a set of 1 to 12 reps into an estimated 1RM, which you can use to plan training loads.
How to use this calculator
1. Units: metric or imperial.
2. Weight lifted: the load for the set, in kg or lb.
3. Repetitions: how many full reps you completed with good form (1–12).
Result: estimated one-rep max in kg and lb.
The Epley formula
1RM = w × (1 + r ÷ 30)
w = weight lifted
r = reps completed
If you enter 1 rep, the calculator returns the weight itself. Estimates become less reliable above about 10 reps, which is why entries are capped at 12.
Worked examples
Example 1: 100 kg for 5 reps
1RM = 100 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 116.7 kg (257.2 lb).
The Brzycki formula (w × 36 ÷ (37 − r)) gives 112.5 kg for the same set, so formulas differ by a kilogram or two at low reps.
Example 2: 185 lb for 8 reps
1RM = 185 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30) = 234.3 lb.

Understanding your results
It's an estimate. Accuracy is best with sets of 3 to 6 reps taken close to failure.
Exercise matters. Formulas fit compound lifts (squat, bench press, deadlift) better than isolation or machine exercises.
Formulas diverge at high reps. For 60 kg × 10 reps, Epley gives 80.0 kg and Brzycki 80.0 kg.
Training status matters. Endurance-trained lifters can usually do more reps at a given percentage than strength athletes.
Safety tips
Warm up with gradually heavier sets before your working set.
Use a spotter or safety bars for bench press and squats.
Stop the set when form breaks down; count only clean reps.
Beginners don't need to test a true 1RM; estimates are enough to set loads.
Tips and common mistakes
Counting reps with poor form, which inflates the estimate.
Using a set that wasn't close to failure. If you could have done three more reps, the estimate will be low.
Mixing units. Enter the weight in the unit you selected.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate my one-rep max?
Multiply the weight by (1 + reps ÷ 30), using the Epley formula.
Which 1RM formula is most accurate?
Epley and Brzycki are the most widely used and agree closely at low reps; no single formula is best for everyone.
How many reps should I use?
A set of 3 to 6 reps near failure gives the most reliable estimate.
Should I test my real 1RM?
Experienced lifters sometimes test it with a spotter; it isn't necessary for most training.
How often should I recalculate?
Every 4 to 8 weeks, or after a training block.
Does it work for bodyweight exercises?
Not directly; you'd need to include body weight in the load.
