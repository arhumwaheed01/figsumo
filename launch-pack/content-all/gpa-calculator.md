H1: GPA Calculator
Your grade point average (GPA) is a credit-weighted average of your course grades, used by US and Canadian colleges and many high schools for honours, scholarships, graduate admissions and academic standing. This calculator gives your term GPA from your courses' grade points and credit hours.
How to use this calculator
1. Course grade (4.0 scale): enter the grade points for each course (for example A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3).
2. Course credits: credit hours for each course.
3. Leave a course's credits blank or at 0 to skip it.
Results: term GPA (two decimals), credit hours, and a table showing each course's quality points.
The formula
GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ credits
Each course's grade points × credits are its quality points. Courses with more credits count more.

Example 2: cumulative GPA
You have a 3.20 GPA over 60 credits and earn the term above. Cumulative = (3.20 × 60 + 37.0) ÷ 70 = 3.27. (A cumulative mode is planned; for now, combine by hand like this.)
Example 3: what you need
To graduate with 3.50 over 90 credits, starting from 3.20 over 60, you'd need (3.50 × 90 − 3.20 × 60) ÷ 30 = 4.10 in the last 30 credits, which is above 4.0 and so impossible on this scale. Raising a GPA late is hard because earlier credits carry weight.
Weighted vs unweighted GPA
US high schools often report a weighted GPA that adds points for honours, AP or IB courses (for example +0.5 or +1.0), so it can exceed 4.0. Colleges often recalculate on their own scale. This calculator is unweighted unless you enter the boosted points yourself.
Canada and the UK
Canada: scales vary by university: 4.0 or 4.33 at many, a 9-point scale at York and a 12-point scale at McMaster. Many report percentages too.
UK: universities award degree classifications (First, 2:1, 2:2, Third) based on weighted module marks, not GPA. Some institutions now also report a GPA alongside, on a 4.25 scale [VERIFY current UK GPA scheme]. US graduate schools usually convert UK results themselves.
Understanding your results
Pass/fail and withdrawn courses usually don't count in GPA; check your school's rules.
Repeated courses may replace or average the earlier grade.
Rounding: schools may truncate rather than round (3.696 → 3.69).
Tips and common mistakes
Averaging grades without weighting by credits.
Entering percentages instead of grade points.
Mixing scales (4.0 vs 4.3).
Including pass/fail courses.
Frequently asked questions
How do I calculate GPA?
Multiply each grade's points by its credits, add them up, and divide by total credits.
What is a good GPA?
Many scholarships and honours programmes set minimums around 3.0–3.5; competitive graduate programmes often look for 3.5 or higher.
What's a 3.7 GPA as a letter grade?
A−.
How do I calculate cumulative GPA?
Combine total quality points and total credits across all terms.
Can GPA be above 4.0?
Yes, on weighted or 4.3 scales.
How many courses can I enter?
Three at present; combine more by hand as in Example 2.
